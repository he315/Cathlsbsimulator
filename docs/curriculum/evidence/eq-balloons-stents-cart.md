## Notes for checkers

- **Cutting balloon ceiling: Brilakis disagrees with himself.** 9.7.9 (p.179): 6–8 atm, not to exceed 12 atm; 23.2.3 (p.417): >14 atm should be avoided. Both given.
- **Non-compliant pressure ceilings.** 9.7.9.3.1 (p.180) lists high inflation pressures (>20 atm) as a cause of rupture and says avoid them; 23.2.2 (p.417) uses NC balloons "often up to 26–28 atm" and says know RBP and diameter growth; 10.2.9 (p.191) allows up to 26–28 atm for NC; Oxford p.218 defines rated pressure as the design limit. The lesson presents these together and recommends reading the chart and changing tool; it does not recommend exceeding the rated pressure.
- **Very-high-pressure balloon rupture rate.** Brilakis p.373 quotes a 2%–3% risk of vessel rupture for the series of 326 lesions; the Secco abstract reports coronary rupture in 3 patients but gives no patient count, so no percentage is computed. Both given. Raja 2010 is a four-case report (not called the first series).
- **Magnesium resorption rate: Oxford contradicted by trial reports.** Oxford p.225 says magnesium resorbs at a lower rate than polymers; BIOSOLVE-II 3-year (PMID 30803936) and pooled BIOSOLVE-II/III (PMID 32917533) describe 3-year follow-up as two years after scaffold resorption (resorption within about a year), against about 36 months for the polymer scaffold (oxford p.225). Lesson gives both and says not to rely on Oxford; q8 no longer tests resorption speed.
- **Malapposition threshold.** Brilakis p.243: ≥400 µm OR ≥1 mm length. The Academy lessons phy-ivus-optimisation and phy-oct-stent (tracks/physiology-imaging.ts, citing eapci-imaging-2018) use >0.4 mm over >1 mm length. The lesson attributes Brilakis's rule and names the other; the EAPCI text itself was not reread for this module.
- **Brachytherapy: the books disagree.** Brilakis p.403 (recurrent ISR: brachytherapy or DCB best), p.597; Oxford p.222 (largely superseded by repeat DES). Both given.
- **Compliance terminology.** Brilakis 30.9.1 (p.569) semicompliant/noncompliant; 9.7.3.2.3 (p.169) and 23.1.1 (p.407) "compliant"; Oxford p.217 three classes. Taught as loose terminology.
- **Lithotripsy energy description.** Brilakis p.573 "ultrasonic shockwaves", p.420 "ultrasound shockwaves"; Oxford p.271 "pulsatile mechanical energy ... 1 Hz"; Hill 2020 "acoustic pressure waves". All three given.
- **Fig. 10.1 reading (p.186).** For the 3.5–4.0 mm band: dashed post-dilatation limit lines end at about 4.5 (one platform), about 4.75 (two platforms) and about 5.5 mm (one platform); one platform charts 3.0–3.5 and 4.0 mm bands instead. Lesson says "from about 4.5 mm to 5.5 mm on the platforms that chart that band". Text p.404 confirms 5.5 mm for one platform's 3.5/4.0 mm stents and 5.75 mm for another's 4.0 mm and for the 5.0 mm platform.
- **Device names (rule 7) replaced by classes**: Angiosculpt → spiral scoring balloon; Chocolate → caged balloon; Wolverine → cutting balloon; Blimp → wire-alongside scoring balloon; OPN NC → very-high-pressure balloon; Shockwave → lithotripsy balloon; Sapphire Pro/Ikazuchi Zero/Ryurei → 1.0 mm low-profile balloons; NIC Nano → 0.85 mm balloon; Threader → microdilation catheter; Glider → skived-tip semi-compliant balloon; Trapper/Trap-it/Kusabi → dedicated trapping balloons; Trapliner → guide extension with built-in trapping balloon; Ostial Flash → dual-balloon ostial flaring catheter; Graftmaster → sandwich PTFE covered stent; PK Papyrus → single-layer polyurethane-covered stent; BeGraft → single-layer PTFE covered stent; Absorb → first scaffold tested widely in humans; Ostial Pro → aorto-ostial positioning device; CrossBoss → stiff metallic re-entry catheter; Stingray → flat re-entry balloon; Amplatzer Vascular Plug II → type II plug; Magmaris → main magnesium scaffold; Resolute Onyx/Synergy/Xience Sierra → "one platform", "another", "a third"; Dyevert → contrast-diversion device; Zero Gravity → suspended shielding system; Finecross → "only one standard CTO microcatheter". Trial names (ISAR-STEREO-2, ABSORB III, AIDA, PREPARE-CALC, Disrupt CAD III, DIVA, LEADERS FREE) are kept.
- **Possible issues in existing Academy lessons (not edited)**: (a) `pci-bs-specialty-balloons` (pci-core) table: "Very-high-pressure NC balloon ... designed for very high pressures (about 30–40 atm)": Brilakis says it can deliver >40 atm and is used at 30–35 atm (p.573, p.179); the range is not wrong but understates the capability. Same table: "Cutting balloon: NC balloon carrying longitudinal microblades": neither manual says the cutting balloon is non-compliant; and "Scoring balloon ... usually somewhat less aggressive" has no source in either manual. (b) `pci-bs-balloons` defines RBP as "at least 99.9% of balloons will not burst, with 95% confidence": not in either manual (Oxford p.218 gives only the design-limit definition); needs a cited source. ## Lesson eq-balloons-stents-cart-standard

- Balloons main method of plaque modification; also expand stents, deliver drugs, trap equipment, lithotripsy: brilakis p.568 (30.9)
- Hypotube carries inflation pressure from the inflation device to the balloon: oxford p.218. Balloons usually polyethylene polymer, behaviour depends on material: oxford p.217
- Sizing the balloon: dilate maximally before stenting (cracking after stenting very difficult); diameter no larger than vessel at lesion (oversizing dissection/rupture, undersizing incomplete preparation); length appropriate (too short melon-seeds, too long damages normal wall): oxford p.218. Balloon shorter than estimated lesion length: brilakis p.169 (9.7.3.2.2); 1:1 to distal reference and ≤1.5 mm for crossing: p.169 (9.7.3.2.1)
- Getting more from NC balloons: undersize ~0.5 mm, shorter balloons, cycle pressure, two undersized balloons side by side, buddy wires (makeshift scoring), cover ballooned segments, sticky after high-pressure inflation remove under fluoroscopy, 1:1 or lower: brilakis p.417 (23.2.2); buddy wires: p.359 (19.9.3); sticky with >20 atm: p.182 (9.7.12.3.1)
- Stent deployment row: 12–14 atm; longer inflation or up to 20 atm SC / 26–28 atm NC: p.191 (10.2.9; 'semi-compliant delivery balloon' wording removed)
- Post-dilatation exceptions (excellent expansion on imaging, thrombus/plaque embolisation concern, large jailed branch): brilakis p.191 (10.2.9); also case undilatable s5 feedback
- Check 3 explanation: first step for acute underexpansion NC >18 atm at least stent diameter: p.243 (cutting-balloon sentence removed)
- Balloon catheter parts: low-profile hypotube with inflation connection, monorail segment, balloon with radio-opaque markers, chamfered (tapered) nosecone to prevent local trauma: oxford p.217
- Hypotube damage/kink causes failure to deflate; avoid kinking shaft: brilakis p.181 (9.7.10.3.1)
- Monorail in the vast majority (simpler, no long wires, stiffer shaft easier to deliver): brilakis p.169 (9.7.3.2.4); OTW needs exchange-length wires, now usually restricted to CTO: oxford p.217
- Single-marker small balloons; profile highest at the marker segment; longer (20–30 mm) balloons penetrate deeper: brilakis p.407 (23.1.1)
- Lesion entry profile and crossing profile definition ("maximum diameter found between the proximal end of the balloon and the distal tip of the catheter"); Threader 1.20 × 12 mm, 0.017 in. entry, 0.024 in. crossing, 150 cm: brilakis p.570 (Fig. 30.81)
- Compliant/semi-compliant/non-compliant definitions; compliant the standard predilatation balloon, unpredictable at high pressure; NC for post-dilating stents; semi-compliant "controlled compliance": oxford p.217
- Standard balloons semicompliant and noncompliant; SC more flexible and easier to deliver; NC harder to deliver, little expansion at high pressure, for post-dilation and balloon-undilatable lesions: brilakis p.569 (30.9.1)
- NC: higher pressures with lower risk of dissection or rupture, higher crossing profile, less flexible: brilakis p.359 (19.9.3)
- "Compliant" used for flexible balloons: brilakis p.169 (9.7.3.2.3), p.179 (9.7.9), p.407 (23.1.1)
- Nominal pressure definition with 2.5 mm example; rated/burst pressure definition, rupture with consequent dissection: oxford p.218
- Pressure table: NC ≤1:1 12–20 atm, up to 20–26 atm; compliant 12–14 atm; inflation 3–5 s; duration 10–20 s: brilakis p.179–180 (9.7.9, image checked); stent deployment 12–14 atm, high-pressure (≥20 atm) post-dilation with shorter NC, up to 20 atm SC and 26–28 atm NC: p.191 (10.2.9); underexpansion >18 atm NC at least stent diameter; malapposition ≥400 µm or ≥1 mm length → SC at low pressure: p.243 (13.3.6.2.3); undilatable NC often up to 26–28 atm, 30–60 s or more, know RBP and % growth: p.417 (23.2.2); calcified ≥20 atm, 1–2 min, decreasing pressure = expansion: p.359 (19.9.3); falling stent-balloon pressure = continued expansion: p.191; SC balloon for perforation sized to vessel, ≤8–10 atm, at least 10–15 min: p.447 (26.2)
- Rupture causes include high pressures >20 atm; avoid >20 atm: brilakis p.180 (9.7.9.3.1)
- Balloons lose original profile after inflation; reshape with fingers under vacuum, new small balloon or another manufacturer's: brilakis p.408 (23.1.1); rotate no more than 2–3 times each way: p.408
- RX more pushability; RX Threader more penetrating (stiffer shaft); OTW allows wire changes and contrast injection: brilakis p.407–408; RX Threader preferred, more support: p.570
- Brilakis prefers NC for predilatation (reusable for post-dilation) but less deliverable: p.169 (9.7.3.2.3); standard balloons first in calcium, then plaque modification: p.359
- Small balloons: Sapphire Pro 1.0 mm lowest profile in US and first choice for uncrossable; Ikazuchi Zero and Ryurei 1.0 mm outside US; Nano 0.85 mm lowest profile in Europe: brilakis p.569 (30.9.2); small balloons for uncrossable lesions and jailed side branches: p.569
- Threader kink-resistant shaft, RX and OTW, RX preferred: p.570; hydrophilic coating: p.407

- Small balloons ≤1.5 mm to modify entry point: p.169 (9.7.3.2.1); 1.0, 1.20, 1.25, 1.5 mm, single marker, RX, compliant, long: p.407; larger 2.5–3.0 mm RX balloon listed as an alternative, sometimes inflated just proximal to the lesion to disrupt entry: p.408 (check explanation)

## Lesson eq-balloons-stents-cart-specialty

- Eight balloon categories (standard compliant/noncompliant; small; plaque modification; trapping; Ostial Flash; DCB; very-high-pressure; IVL): brilakis p.568–569 (30.9, Fig. 30.78)
- Plaque modification balloons score/modify the lesion to facilitate expansion and prevent watermelon seeding; four designs (three US, one Europe only): brilakis p.570 (30.9.3)
- Plaque modification intro: designed to score/modify lesion to facilitate expansion and prevent watermelon seeding: p.570 (30.9.3); focused pressure: p.417 (23.2.3) ('lower pressure' and 'grip' wording removed)
- Spiral scoring: semicompliant balloon, three nitinol spiral struts; lower crossing profile and more scoring marks per mm than cutting balloon: p.570 (30.9.3.1) (reworded: 'crosses more easily ... denser scoring')
- Caged balloon: nitinol cage, pillows and grooves; pillows create small dissections; grooves relieve stress and stop propagation: p.571 (30.9.3.2)
- Cutting balloon: atherotomes protrude 0.005 in., three or four radial incisions through fibrocalcific tissue; used to release intramural haematomas post stenting, after re-entry, spontaneous dissection; very slow inflation 1 atm every 5 s; high crossing profile: p.571 (30.9.3.3); 6–8 atm not to exceed 12 atm: p.179; avoid >14 atm: p.417; slow inflation and deflation for blades to exit folds and rewrap: p.359; slow inflation and deflation: p.169
- Wire-alongside scoring balloon (Blimp): short distal monorail segment, 0.6 mm, 5 mm balloon adjacent to wire; nominal 25 atm, RBP 30 atm; scores through the guidewire, back-loaded, positioned along the balloon: p.571 (30.9.3.4); not available in US; may help uncrossable lesions: p.407–408
- No slow inflation needed for spiral and caged (contrast "In contrast to Angiosculpt and Chocolate"): p.359
- Oxford cutting balloon: one or more atherotomes attached longitudinally; incise fibrotic or calcified lesions; sharper than scalpels; may perforate gloves/skin; damage vessels if not fully deflated before withdrawal: oxford p.218
- Tight valve deforms balloons; cutting balloons at higher risk: brilakis p.171 (9.7.6.3.1)
- Uses: undilatable lesions: p.417 (23.2.3); aorto-ostial: p.276 (15.1.9: facilitate expansion, prevent excessive balloon movement); branch ostial: p.288 (15.2.9); watermelon seeding: p.180; ISR compacting restenotic intima: oxford p.222
- Less deliverable and more costly; anchor and guide extension may be needed: brilakis p.417 (23.2.3); PREPARE-CALC 16% crossover: p.356 (19.9.1)
- Complications: loss of wire/guide position, rupture/perforation, entrapment, cutting blade fracture, stent strut avulsion: p.417; previously placed stents extracted while retrieving cutting balloons: p.476 (27.3.1.1); cutting balloon in SCAD/IMH perforation risk: p.401 (22.1.9)
- Check 1 (watermelon seeding treatment: deflate, lower pressure, slower inflation, buddy wire, plaque modification balloon, longer balloons; causes include rapid inflation): brilakis p.180 (9.7.9.3.2)
- VHP balloon: specialised NC delivering >40 atm, not in US: brilakis p.573 (30.9.7), p.420 (23.2.10); usually 30–35 atm: p.179; Secco max 45–50 atm, 47% >40 atm: ref secco-opn-2019; series 326 undilatable lesions >90% expansion, 2%–3% rupture: p.373 (19.9.7); RBP 35 atm, twin-layer, uniform expansion, no dog-boning: ref raja-opn-high-pressure-2010; 3 patients ruptured, OPN alone >90%: ref secco-opn-2019
- IVL: ultrasonic shockwaves; not approved in US as of 2020; peripheral balloon used off label in large coronary arteries; its smallest size 2.5 × 40 mm; useful for undilatable lesions: brilakis p.573–574 (30.9.8); "ultrasound shockwaves", undilatable lesions with circumferential calcification: p.420 (23.2.9); Oxford: pulsatile mechanical energy 1 Hz via emitters along a semi-compliant RX balloon; vibrates superficial and deeper calcium; main limitation crossing tight lesions; most promising, potential default: oxford p.271; Disrupt CAD III: ref disrupt-cad3-2020; 2023 EAPCI consensus selects tools by calcium morphology/location, including lithotripsy: ref barbato-calcium-consensus-2023
- Fig. 23.7 lists IVL and very high-pressure balloon as "Future techniques": brilakis p.416
- DCB: paclitaxel and sirolimus most common; released on inflation; decreases restenosis; ISR most often, bifurcations, small vessels; not available in US; peripheral DCB off label in large vessels: brilakis p.573 (30.9.6); first ISR repeat DES after confirming well dilated, recurrent ISR brachytherapy or DCB: p.403 (22.2.10); AGENT IDE first US randomised coronary DCB trial, TLF 17.9% vs 28.6%: ref yeh-agent-ide-2024; no class effect: ref jeger-dcb-consensus-2020
- Trapping balloons: dedicated ones without wire lumen, resistant to kinking/deformation, lower profile: brilakis p.572 (30.9.4); smaller profiles trap in smaller guides; monorail preferred over OTW (larger profiles, may not fit with other equipment); 2.5 mm for 6 or 7 Fr, 3.0 mm for 7 and 8 Fr; ≥20 mm, especially polymer-jacketed wires; used balloon deformed, exchange or use stylet; Trapliner 6–8 Fr; 6 Fr cannot trap OTW balloons, CrossBoss, Stingray; 0.071 in. 6 Fr guides trap low-profile microcatheters: p.160 (8.9.2.1); trapping preferred (minimises wire movement and radiation), Trapliner best with 135 cm microcatheters (150 cm too long): p.160; trapping multiple devices more likely to fail; advance beyond distal marker of microcatheter at/near primary curve; short/shortened guides shaft markers unreliable: p.161
- Ostial Pro (product name omitted): nitinol, self-expanding legs advanced just distal to guide tip, prevent guide entry, mark aortic wall plane, align guide tip with aorto-ostial plane, accurate stent placement: brilakis p.584 (30.13.1)
