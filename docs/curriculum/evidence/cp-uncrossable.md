- Disagreements taught explicitly: (1) microcatheter rotation: up to 10 turns before unwinding (23.1.4, p.411) vs no more than 20 rotations in any direction for the stranded-wire plaque modification type (30.6.5, p.558); the lesson gives both and advises the lower when unsure. (2) Cutting balloon ceiling: avoid >14 atm (23.2.3, p.417) vs not to exceed 12 atm (9.7.9, p.179); both given. (3) Side-branch anchor pressure: usually 6–8 atm (23.1.3.1.2, p.409) vs 4–8 atm with 1:1 sizing (9.7.8, p.175); both given. (4) Prolonged inflation: 30–60 s or more (23.2.2, p.417) vs 1–2 min for calcified lesions (19.9.3, p.359); both given. (5) Very-high-pressure balloon: capable of >40 atm (23.2.10, p.420; 30.9.7, p.573) vs usual inflation 30–35 atm (9.7.9, p.179); both given. (6) Rupture rate with that balloon: Brilakis summarises Secco's series as ">90% expansion with 2%–3% risk of vessel rupture" (p.373); the Secco abstract (PMID 30902652, checked again in round 1) reports coronary rupture in 3 patients among 326 lesions (under 1%); the lesson now states both figures and says explicitly that Brilakis's 2–3% is higher than the abstract reports. (7) Non-compliant balloon pressure: often up to 26–28 atm in undilatable lesions (23.2.2, p.417) vs usual 12–20 atm, up to 20–26 atm for suboptimal expansion, >20 atm listed as a cause of rupture and to be avoided, atherectomy or lithotripsy first in heavy calcium (9.7.9, p.179–180); Oxford defines rated burst pressure as the pressure beyond which the balloon is not designed to be inflated and may rupture (oxford p.218). The lesson, keypoint, tip and LAD case (now 'at its rated burst pressure') give both positions and no longer imply that 26–28 atm is within a standard rating.
- Power microcatheter and power guide extension (topic `pci-power-techniques`): the brief maps this topic to Brilakis 8.1, and the video title is "8.5 Manual of PCI - Power microcatheter and power guide extension" (src/content/videos.ts). ## Lesson cp-uncrossable-first-line

- Definition (wire crossed, balloon cannot): brilakis p.407 (23.1)
- Algorithm and five lines, step numbering 1–9, combinations: brilakis p.407 (Fig. 23.1, page image); yellow = plaque modification (steps 1–2, 4–5, 6–7, external crush), green = increase support (step 3 guide and wire, distal anchor): p.407 legend and image
- Causes of failure to reach/cross (proximal lesions, tortuosity, calcification, highly stenotic lesion, high-profile, non-compliant or used balloons) and the five-group menu including deep breath: brilakis p.174 (Fig. 9.6), p.179
- Small balloon first; single-marker RX compliant, 1.0/1.20/1.25/1.5 mm, 20–30 mm long; profile highest at marker so longer allows deeper penetration: brilakis p.407
- 1.0 mm balloons and the 0.85 mm balloon lowest profile, first choice: brilakis p.407; 0.85 mm lowest profile available in Europe: p.569
- Compliant balloons more flexible, lower profile; used balloons less deliverable: brilakis p.178
- Marker in the middle for 1.0–1.5 mm balloons, so longer preferred: brilakis p.178, p.173
- Micro-dilation catheter: low profile, stiff shaft, hydrophilic, 0.017 in. lesion entry profile; RX preferred to OTW (more penetrating, likely stiffer shaft); OTW allows wire change and contrast: brilakis p.407–408; 1.20 × 12 mm balloon, kink-resistant shaft: p.570
- Bevelled-tip balloon, designed for stent struts, can be torqued to present alternative tip configurations: brilakis p.408, p.570
- Scoring device with 0.6 mm balloon beside the wire may help: brilakis p.408 (Blimp), p.571 (0.6 mm, 5 mm, wire along the balloon), p.570 (available only in Europe); p.407 (not available in the US)
- Stop advancing if guide backs out or distal wire position compromised: brilakis p.408
- Balloon wedge (inflate while maintaining forward pressure; may cross with same balloon): brilakis p.408
- Rotation ≤2–3 times each direction to avoid getting stuck on the wire: brilakis p.408
- Reshape with fingers under vacuum; new balloon (lose profile after inflation); another manufacturer; RX more pushability: brilakis p.408
- Larger 2.5–3.0 mm RX balloon; inflation just proximal disrupts entry for small balloon or microcatheter: brilakis p.408
- Check 1 (new 1.0 mm long balloon; rotation limit; NC less deliverable): p.408, p.178
- What can go wrong: guide/wire loss; forceful back-and-forth → ostial dissection esp pre-existing ostial lesion; see-saw distal injury esp stiff or polymer-jacketed wires; balloon entrapment highly unlikely: brilakis p.408
- Grenadoplasty = BAM; simple, safe, often effective, increasingly used: brilakis p.408
- Vo 2016: 17 balloon-uncrossable CTOs, two centres, procedural success 94%, BAM facilitated success in approximately one half, no major complication: ref vo-bam-2016 (abstract)
- How: 1.0–1.5 mm balloon as far as possible into lesion, high pressure until rupture, immediate suction through inflating device to avoid barotrauma; modifies plaque; new balloon crosses: brilakis p.408
- Fig. 23.2 example (distal RCA CTO, 8 Fr Amplatz guide and anchor balloon failed; 1.2 mm balloon ruptured; microcatheter advanced, wire exchange, 2.0 mm balloon): brilakis p.408
- Complications: proximal dissection/perforation extremely unlikely with 1.0–1.5 mm; ≥2.0 mm should NOT be used: p.408; meticulous preparation to remove air (air embolism); watch indeflator not screen → faster deflation, less pinhole injury; ruptured balloon hard to remove, may entangle wire → both removed, wire position lost: brilakis p.409
- Check 2 (2.0 mm forbidden): p.408
- Pearl (round 1 rewrite: inflated balloons lose their profile; another maker's balloon differs in profile and tip; a fresh small balloon is the next try): brilakis p.408
- Drill: angio-pci calcified mid-RCA scenario (exists per MANUAL_AUTHORING)

## Lesson cp-uncrossable-support-microcatheter

- Increasing support critical; key elements guide catheter and guidewire: brilakis p.409 (23.1.3)
- Guide support: larger guides, supportive shapes, femoral access, forward push or deep intubation, extensions, anchoring: brilakis p.409; full menu p.175–178 (taught in pm-prep-and-stent)
- Changing guide after wire crossing may lose wire; may not be acceptable (CTOs): brilakis p.409
- Guide extension enhances support and pushability: p.409; DOCA-TRI described as 5-in-6 more effective and efficient than buddy wire or balloon anchoring for transradial complex PCI: brilakis p.409; ref zhang-doca-tri-2012
- Extension complications: position loss/distal injury; ostial or mid-vessel dissection; wedged → damped → avoid guide injections (hydraulic dissection); distal marker dislodgement; stent deformation/stripping at proximal collar around subclavian curve with radial → 40 cm instead of 25 cm cylinder: brilakis p.409
- Extension delivered over balloon/microcatheter rather than bare wire: brilakis p.533, p.440
- Side-branch anchor: workhorse wire into side branch (conus or acute marginal for RCA, diagonal for LAD), 1.5–2.0 mm balloon depending on branch size, usually 6–8 atm, chest pain possible: brilakis p.409; 4–8 atm and 1:1 sizing: p.175
- Buddy-wire stent anchor: brilakis p.409; antegrade and retrograde anchoring in retrograde CTO: p.409
- Anchor complications: position loss/distal injury during anchor delivery: p.409; branch injury/dissection infrequent, usually without significant consequences; perforation rare; avoid oversizing; workhorse wire; buddy wire stent anchor risks (cannot remove buddy wire, cannot pass proximal stent): brilakis p.410
- Second wire alongside original, sometimes through second guide: brilakis p.410
- Support wire, wiggle wire, deep wiring facilitate crossing: brilakis p.410; wiggle wire three deflections centring the wire, useful through stents, tortuosity, calcification, not for primary wiring, exchanged over microcatheter: p.561; support wires have strong bodies and soft tips: p.561; deep wiring perforation risk: p.177
- Check 3 (guide exchange risk; larger guides more support): p.409, p.175
- Microcatheter concept (advancement modifies the occlusion, enabling balloon crossing): brilakis p.410
- Consider microcatheter in any complex lesion: brilakis p.149 (8.1); five categories: p.149, p.541
- Plaque modification microcatheter (eight stainless-steel wires stranded; 2.1 and 2.6 Fr, latter more support; no inner polymer so no contrast; counterclockwise to advance, clockwise to withdraw; ≤20 rotations any direction to avoid kinking and unravelling): brilakis p.558; counterclockwise/clockwise also p.410
- Threaded coil microcatheters advanced clockwise, withdrawn counterclockwise (opposite to the stranded type): brilakis p.410, p.546
- Coil microcatheters rotated either direction: p.410; large ones can be spun, overspinning causes damage: p.542
- Small braided microcatheters without coils: mainly pushed, rotation challenging, risk of tip dislodgement if aggressively torqued: brilakis p.410, p.548
- Very low-profile braided microcatheter not designed for aggressive torque, generally not to be used in uncrossable lesions (tip fracture, separation): brilakis p.410, p.550
- Keep wire inside (prevent kinking, entrapment); rotation not exceed 10 turns before unwinding; overtorque → tip entrapment, tip/shaft fracture: brilakis p.411
- Position loss with aggressive pushing; distal injury from uncontrolled wire movement: brilakis p.411
- After crossing: balloon often crosses, or exchange for supportive or atherectomy wire: brilakis p.410
- Device and wire locked together → both withdrawn; polymer-jacketed wire through established track: brilakis p.411
- Wire-cutting steps 1–4: brilakis p.411
- Seesaw wire-cutting (two balloons, alternate, repeated until one crosses): brilakis p.411; retrospective study of 80 patients, higher device and procedural success, shorter time vs plaque modification microcatheter: p.411–412; numbers (40 vs 40 matched; device success 87.5% vs 45.0%; procedural success 92.5% vs 72.5%; 90.5 vs 141.5 min; no difference in complications): ref xue-seesaw-2017 (abstract)
- Wire-cutting complication (deep guide intubation on withdrawal, ostial or proximal dissection; watch and back out guide; dissection could be used for subintimal techniques if experienced): brilakis p.412
- Wire puncture (stiff wire to lesion over microcatheter or OTW balloon; punctured/jabbed several times; supportive second wire through lesion may itself help): brilakis p.412; stiff tapered wires with strong penetrating power: p.561
- Perforation unlikely even if wire exits the architecture unless followed by balloon or microcatheter: brilakis p.412
- Check 4 (back the guide out): p.412

## Lesson cp-uncrossable-ablation-subintimal

- Laser increasingly used for uncrossable and undilatable; over any standard 0.014 in. wire, unlike RA and OA which need dedicated wires: brilakis p.412, p.582
- Oxford: inability to cross with a microcatheter is a main limitation for RA; alternatives such as laser may be necessary; direct wiring with the RA wire practically challenging: oxford p.270
- Laser can modify the proximal cap without crossing: brilakis p.582
- 5-minute warm-up and calibration; set up early: brilakis p.412
- 0.9 mm catheter, max repetition and fluence (80 Hz, 80 mJ/mm²): brilakis p.412; coronary catheter programmed for slow continuous saline infusion; peripheral 0.9 mm catheter generally preferred because it does not stop after 10 s: p.412; coronary catheters lase 10 s then off 5 s; off-label use of the peripheral catheter: p.582
- Contrast during laser (via guide or OTW side port) → profound modification via acoustomechanical effect of exploding bubbles; increases risk of injury/perforation; generally only inside stents: brilakis p.412
- Complications: perforation esp tortuous vessels; higher at high settings and with contrast; lower in stents: p.412; polymer-jacketed wire polymer damage, sticky: brilakis p.413
- Atherectomy can greatly facilitate crossing; requires dedicated wire exchange which may not be feasible: brilakis p.413
- Bury microcatheter as far as possible, pull original wire, rewire with atherectomy wire: brilakis p.413
- RA advantageous over current OA (end-on vs sideways): brilakis p.413; RA may be preferred for uncrossable lesions: p.359, Fig. 19.4 p.358
- Differential cutting, not elastic tissue (adventitia): brilakis p.413
- Complications: loss of wire position and failure to recross; perforation → small burr 1.25–1.50 mm; higher in subintimal space; burr entrapment on forceful advancement, repetitive gentle advancement, avoid wedging with deceleration: brilakis p.413
- Check 5: p.413; oxford p.270
- Subintimal techniques require experience in CTO techniques: brilakis p.413
- Second wire (antegrade or retrograde) subintimally around the segment, or dissection catheter then exchanged: brilakis p.413
- External crush: balloon over subintimal wire next to lesion, usually 8–10 atm, crushing plaque from outside, balloon then passes over true-lumen wire: brilakis p.413; ref christopoulos-external-crush-2017; Vo 2014 named as Brilakis's ref 18 (p.421)
- Subintimal distal anchor: second wire subintimally distal, balloon distal inflated, anchors true-lumen wire: brilakis p.413; ref michael-subintimal-anchor-2013
- Complications: subintimal crossing may not be feasible (side branches): p.413; confirm within vessel architecture (side branches, pericardium) with contralateral injection in two orthogonal projections; large subintimal haematoma compressing distal true lumen: brilakis p.414
- Check 6: p.414
- Videos: titles only

## Lesson cp-uncrossable-undilatable-balloons

- Definition (cannot be expanded despite multiple high-pressure inflations); often severe calcification; often also uncrossable: brilakis p.415
- Diagnosis: waist on the balloon during inflation on fluoroscopy: brilakis p.181, p.192
- Stent underexpansion predisposes to stent thrombosis and restenosis: brilakis p.418; underexpansion much harder to treat than undilatable de novo lesion: p.359
- Oxford: maximally dilate before stent deployment; cracking a resistant lesion after stenting can be very difficult; balloon diameter no larger than the vessel; oversizing → dissection or rupture: oxford p.218
- More frequent in CTOs: brilakis p.415; Tajti: 52/425 (12%) of CTOs with wire crossing; longer; more moderate/severe calcification; lower technical and procedural success; more in-hospital MACE due to perforation: ref tajti-undilatable-cto-2018 (abstract)
- Avoid stenting undilatable lesion; predilate with balloon sized to reference diameter, esp severe calcification; imaging for balloon size and features needing atherectomy; resistant → more predilation or atherectomy: brilakis p.415
- De novo vs ISR key determinant (same procedure or prior); OA not in recently implanted stents; RA risk burr entrapment and stent distortion in recent stents; laser with contrast (ESLAP, Extra-Stent Laser-Assisted Plaque modification) can expand in-stent lesions; infrequently used de novo (dissections, perforations): brilakis p.415
- Algorithm table: brilakis p.416 (Fig. 23.7, page image: de novo 1 → yes 2 / no 4; 2 failure → 3; 3 failure → 4; 4 yes → 5, no → 2 (arrow) and → 6 (left arrow); 5 failure → 6; 6 failure → 7; 7 failure → 8; successes → lesion expansion; future techniques 9, 10; 11 imaging bracket. ISR: 2 → 3 → 6 → 5 → 7 → 8; same future techniques and imaging)
- Likely to respond: heavily calcified less likely; imaging; three OCT findings (>180°, >0.5 mm, >5.0 mm) associated with underexpansion: brilakis p.417; ref fujino-calcium-2018
- Heavy circumferential calcification favours early atherectomy; less calcified start with balloon: brilakis p.420
- Check 9 (in-stent: laser before atherectomy; OA not in recent stents; RA risk; larger burrs ≥1.75 mm for undilatable stents): p.415, p.416, p.418
- High-pressure technique items 1–7 (NC often up to 26–28 atm; know rated burst pressure and growth; prolonged 30–60 s or more; pressure cycling; slightly undersized usually by 0.5 mm; avoid injuring non-stented segments, all ballooned segments stented (geographic miss); shorter balloons; two undersized balloons side by side; one or more buddy wires, makeshift cutting/scoring balloon): brilakis p.417
- 1–2 min, falling indeflator pressure suggests expansion: brilakis p.359
- Complications: perforation avoided by conservative sizing (1:1 or lower) and air removal; rupture → immediate angiography for perforation, air embolization, flow-limiting dissection; sticky balloons, fluoroscopy during removal: brilakis p.417
- Rupture: suction, remove, aspirate guide, angiography; balloon at perforation: brilakis p.180; p.359
- Balloon entrapment prevention (primary atherectomy for very heavy calcium; avoid very high pressures that cause rupture and entrapment): brilakis p.477 (27.3.2)
- Check 10: p.417; p.359 (underexpansion harder)
- Sim pci-bench task: exists

## Lesson cp-uncrossable-undilatable-beyond

- Imaging critical at all stages; calcified lesions may appear expanded during inflation but small MLA: brilakis p.420
- Plaque modification balloons (focused pressure; less deliverable, more costly): brilakis p.417; scoring balloon with three nitinol spiral struts: p.570; nitinol-caged balloon forming pillows and grooves: p.571; cutting balloon atherotomes: p.571; standard balloons first in most cases: p.359
- Complications: position loss, enhanced support needed; rupture/perforation; entrapment esp with rupture; blade fracture, strut avulsion, entrapment; cutting balloon inflated slowly, avoid >14 atm: brilakis p.417; not to exceed 12 atm: p.179; 1 atm every 5 s: p.359, p.571
- Atherectomy suitability: risks (dissection, embolization, perforation, equipment loss or entrapment) increase with tortuosity (relative contraindication), thrombus, dissections, bypass grafts: brilakis p.418; Oxford contraindications severe tortuosity and dissection: oxford p.269
- RA and OA can expand undilatable lesions; RASER for highly resistant lesions: brilakis p.418; Egred: three patients, severely calcified undilatable lesions unresponsive to either alone: ref egred-raser-2012 (abstract)
