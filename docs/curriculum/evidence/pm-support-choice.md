## Lesson pm-support-choice-when (Deciding whether a PCI needs haemodynamic support)

- PCI may reduce cardiac output and tissue perfusion or be done with decreased or absent perfusion (shock, arrest); need for support before, during or after PCI, sometimes unexpected (acute vessel closure, left main dissection): brilakis p.249 (14.1, 14.1.1). (Round 1: the earlier 'every PCI interrupts coronary flow' and 'decision made before the patient is on the table' were removed.)
- No randomised trial has shown lower in-hospital mortality or major complications with support devices; they may increase the safety of high-risk PCI and potentially improve outcomes of shock or arrest: brilakis p.249 (14.1, refs 1 and 2 = PROTECT II, BCIS-1 long-term).
- Oxford: no trial has shown a convincing net benefit on survival; devices increasingly used: oxford p.285.
- PROTECT II: 452 patients, complex 3-vessel or unprotected LM disease, severely depressed LV function; Impella 2.5 better haemodynamic support; 30-day MAE 35.1% vs 40.1% (P = 0.227, ITT); 90-day trend favouring Impella: ref protect-ii-2012 (PMID 22935569 abstract).
- BCIS-1: 301 patients, EF ≤30%, extensive disease; MACCE at discharge 15.2% vs 16.0%: ref perera-bcis1-2010 (note). Long-term: median 51 months, fewer deaths with elective IABP (42 vs 58), HR 0.66: ref perera-bcis1-longterm-2013 (PMID 23224207).
- 1.14 summary rephrased in original words (round 1, copied-wording finding). Checklist 1.14: history (CHF symptoms, low EF), examination (raised JVP, leg oedema, crackles, femoral and radial pulses), labs (BNP; lactate in shock), imaging (echo EF, LV and RV size, valves; access-site imaging for feasibility of support), haemodynamics (RHC if available: high RA, PA or PCWP, low CO, low CPO, low PAPi); consider support with reduced EF, poor haemodynamics and/or complex or high-risk interventions: brilakis p.36 (1.14).
- Baseline haemodynamics are the most important factor; arrest: VA-ECMO essential (supports circulation and oxygenation); many shock patients need support; stable elective patients: depends on PCI risk: brilakis p.249 (14.1.1).
- RHC strongly recommended in low EF with complex PCI or unclear haemodynamic status; PCWP >18 mmHg suggests LV failure; PaPi <0.9 suggests RV failure; PCWP >25 mmHg with SvO2 <50% high risk of periprocedural pulmonary oedema and shock; Swan Ganz can be left in during the procedure to monitor PA pressure and decide escalation or de-escalation: brilakis p.249 (14.1.1).
- Four groups normal / abnormal but stable / shock / cardiac arrest: brilakis p.250 (Fig 14.1).
- Swan Ganz in hypotension, heart failure or shock aids the support decision and detects new changes: brilakis p.38 (2.3).
- Hypotension or acute pulmonary oedema during or after PCI; anticipated or unexpected (acute vessel closure, left main dissection): brilakis p.249 (14.1.1).
- PCI risk list (unprotected LM especially with occluded/diseased RCA; last remaining vessel; severe calcification and tortuosity; high-grade stenosis with risk of acute occlusion during wiring; bifurcation with large SBs with high-grade stenoses hard to protect; large thrombus; atherectomy; retrograde CTO PCI): brilakis p.249 and p.251 (14.1.2).
- Oxford CHIP: complex anatomy (unprotected LMS; multivessel disease with SYNTAX >33; last patent vessel), comorbidity list (heart failure, severe valvular disease, COPD, kidney failure, diabetes, elderly, PVD, prior cardiac surgery, acute presentation, electric instability), haemodynamic compromise (EF <35%, SBP <100 mmHg, CI <2.2 L/min/m²); coexistence defines CHIP: oxford p.286.
- Left main: support often used, especially low EF, high LVEDP or PCWP, occluded RCA, prolonged ischaemia such as atherectomy; RHC helps decide beforehand and monitor: brilakis p.333 (17.14) and p.329 (17.1).
- Atherectomy: consider prophylactic support with poor baseline haemodynamics, low EF, large myocardium at risk, severe valve disease, severe pulmonary hypertension: brilakis p.360 (19.9.4); atherectomy favours support: brilakis p.374 (19.14).
- Complex LM bifurcation stenting with depressed LV, especially occluded/severely diseased RCA: brilakis p.327 (16.2.14).
- Prior CABG patients: low EF, CHF, complex lesions: brilakis p.351 (18.14).
- ACS more likely to need support than stable angina: brilakis p.386 (20.14).
- About 4% of CTO PCI in the US: brilakis p.395 (21.14).
- SCAD: access to devices such as VA-ECMO recommended: brilakis p.401 (22.1.14).
- Ostial LM rarely needs support: brilakis p.287 (15.1.14); Medina 0.0.1 rarely unless large territory, atherectomy and significant LV dysfunction: brilakis p.292 (15.2.14).
- Prophylactic support in high-risk patients as prevention of hypotension: brilakis p.487 (28.1.2).
- Availability of support devices part of complex PCI programmes; prophylactic, after complication, or in shock: brilakis p.597 (30.20).
- Decision table: abnormal haemodynamics + high-risk → prophylactic support in most cases if feasible; abnormal + low-risk or normal + high-risk → not prophylactic in most, immediately available; many operators insert a 4 Fr femoral sheath; upfront support may be considered in some: brilakis p.251 (14.1.2); normal + low-risk → no support; instability during case → device selection; no instability → no support: brilakis p.250 (Fig 14.1).
- Equipment Table 30.1 category 20: IABP, Impella CP, Tandem Heart, VA-ECMO, external compression devices: brilakis p.514.
- Sheath kept if a support device is likely in the next few hours: brilakis p.195 (11.1.2). ACS: femoral sheath sometimes left a few hours in borderline haemodynamics, increased bleeding risk: brilakis p.385 (20.11).
- Oxford downward spiral (myocardial dysfunction → low output, high EDP → hypotension, congestion → low coronary perfusion, hypoxia → worse ischaemia → worse dysfunction): oxford p.285 (Fig 9.7). MCS aims (normalise CO, MAP, decrease PCWP, optimise O2 saturation, improve coronary flow, reduce workload and O2 demand, bridge to definitive VAD/transplant): oxford p.283 and p.285. Three indications (protected PCI in CHIP, cardiogenic shock, salvage in MI): oxford p.285.
- Rihal 2015 consensus (cited for prophylactic support recommendation): brilakis p.251 ref [4]; ref rihal-mcs-consensus-2015 (PMID 25861963).
- Pitfall "elective balloon pump for every left main case": Oxford's LM advice is now also taught here (below); BCIS-1 as above.
- Pitfall "support will lower the risk of dying": brilakis p.249 (no RCT showing lower in-hospital mortality or major complications); BCIS-1 long-term mortality benefit at 51 months: ref perera-bcis1-longterm-2013; CHIP-BCIS3: ref perera-chip-bcis3-2026.
- CHIP-BCIS3 (round 1, clinical finding): 300 patients, severe LV dysfunction, extensive CAD, planned complex PCI; elective microaxial unloading vs standard care; hierarchical composite (death, disabling stroke, spontaneous MI, CV hospitalisation, periprocedural injury) at minimum 12 months, median 22; win ratio 0.85 (95% CI 0.63–1.15); deaths 47 vs 33, HR 1.54 (0.99–2.41): ref perera-chip-bcis3-2026 (PMID 41910380 abstract). Substudy 97 patients: fewer inotropes (RR 0.57), more loss of pulse pressure (RR 7.83), stunning not prevented: ref ezad-chip-bcis3-haemodynamics-2026 (PMID 42554527 abstract).
- Round 2: CHIP-BCIS3 population 'overlaps heavily' with Brilakis's prophylactic-support group, not identical: Brilakis recommends prophylactic support for abnormal haemodynamics (right heart data: wedge >18, PAPi <0.9, wedge >25 with SvO2 <50%, brilakis p.249) plus high-risk PCI (p.251); CHIP-BCIS3 selected by severe LV dysfunction and extensive coronary disease having complex PCI (ref perera-chip-bcis3-2026).
- Brilakis's prophylactic-support recommendation cites the 2015 SCAI consensus (ref [4]): brilakis p.251; ref rihal-mcs-consensus-2015. "No randomised trial stood behind it when he wrote": brilakis p.249 (no RCT showed benefit; PROTECT II and BCIS-1 cited). "Individual decision; standby with access, device and PA catheter a valid default": inference from CHIP-BCIS3 and Brilakis's own standby description (p.249, p.251); the Academy lesson cx-high-risk (pci-complex.ts) already states that guidelines do not recommend routine prophylactic support.
- Ostial LM: support could be considered if a high likelihood of haemodynamic compromise: brilakis p.287 (15.1.14).
- Stent thrombosis as a setting needing support: brilakis p.403 (22.2.14, Stent failure). Acute closure of large vessels → arrhythmias and haemodynamic compromise needing support: brilakis p.445 (25.3).
- Left main: early vasopressors such as norepinephrine if hypotension develops, defibrillation pads on: brilakis p.329 (17.2). Balloon inflations as brief as possible, especially without support: brilakis p.332 (17.9).
- Oxford LMS: IABP electively with impaired LV function, normal LV tolerates LMS inflations; haemodynamic compromise: short inflations and prophylactic IABP: oxford p.261 (ch 8, Left main stem PCI). No-reflow: consider IABP to maximise coronary perfusion pressure if hypotension persists: oxford p.267. LMS dissection: femoral access ready for IABP/Impella: oxford p.323 (ch 10).
- Equipment chapter: 'must have' criterion is at least one item for each requisite step, including haemodynamic support: brilakis p.513 (ch 30 introduction); Table 30.1 category 20 options (IABP, Impella CP, Tandem Heart, VA-ECMO, external compression devices): brilakis p.514. 'Many operators insert a 4 French femoral sheath'; support 'immediately available': brilakis p.251.

## Lesson pm-support-choice-which (Choosing the device)

- Six devices (IABP, Impella, VA-ECMO, Tandem Heart, Protek Duo, Impella RP): brilakis p.249 (14.1). Four LV devices, two RV devices: brilakis p.252 (14.2).
- IABP smallest, least support; usually via 7–8 Fr femoral sheath; helium inflation in diastole increases coronary perfusion, displaces blood, increases CO, reduces LVEDP and afterload: brilakis p.252 (14.2).
- Impella non-pulsatile axial flow, through aortic valve LV → aorta, LV unloading with lower LVEDP and volume; 2.5 and CP percutaneous; 5.0 surgical cut-down: brilakis p.252. Oxford: does not need native LV pulsation; the more dysfunctional the LV the more functional the Impella; larger access, prolonged anticoagulation, more expensive, longer learning curve: oxford p.295.
- Tandem Heart: centrifugal LA → femoral artery: brilakis p.252.
- VA-ECMO: centrifugal pump, membrane oxygenator, venous drainage, arterial return; large cannulae and perfusionist; increases LVEDP and volume and myocardial O2 demand, may impair recovery: brilakis p.252 (14.2, ref 7 Tomasello).
- Comparison table (availability, access, CO increase, arrhythmia, RV dependence, respiratory failure, transseptal, limb ischaemia, bleeding, haemolysis, stroke, difficulty; footnotes a–d): brilakis p.254 (Table 14.1, read from PDF).
- Contraindications, Brilakis: Table 14.1 p.254; Impella (mechanical AV, LV thrombus, severe AR, ASD or VSD): p.251 (14.1.3); VA-ECMO or IABP (severe AR, aortic dissection, uncontrolled bleeding; can run without anticoagulation briefly in active bleeding): p.251 (14.1.6). Transcaval for arterial cannula in severe PAD: p.254 footnote b.
- Contraindications, Oxford: IABP (significant AR, significant AS, HOCM with significant gradient, known dissection, significant PVD relative): oxford p.290 (Box 9.1). Impella (LV thrombus, mechanical AV, severe AS, moderate/severe AR, significant PVD, contraindication to anticoagulation): oxford p.295. ECMO (moderate-severe AR, significant PVD, contraindication to anticoagulation): oxford p.297.
- Algorithm steps 3–6 (Impella contraindications; femoral access adequate: iliofemoral >5 mm, no severe tortuosity; alternative access feasible → Impella, else IABP; Impella contraindicated → contraindications for IABP or ECMO? yes → no support; no → IABP or VA-ECMO): brilakis p.250 (Fig 14.1).
- Impella CP most commonly used in high-risk PCI where available: brilakis p.251 (14.1.3).
- 14 Fr sheath; iliofemoral >5 mm without severe calcification, tortuosity or obstructive lesions; iliac stenting; intravascular lithotripsy: brilakis p.251 (14.1.4).
- Alternative sites axillary and transcaval, require large-bore expertise: brilakis p.251 (14.1.5); detailed description beyond scope: brilakis p.93–94 (4.4.3, 4.4.4).
- Sheath size discrepancies: Brilakis 14.2 IABP 7–8 Fr (p.252); Table 14.1 (p.254); 30.1.1.1 IABP 7–9 Fr (can be inserted without sheath), Impella 2.5 13 Fr, CP 14 Fr, VA-ECMO arterial 15–19 Fr, venous 25 Fr (p.515, read from PDF). Oxford: IABP 7.5 F or larger, sheathed or sheathless (p.291, Fig 9.10 p.290); Impella 12 F/2.5 L/min, 14 F/3.5, 21 F/5.0 (p.296 Fig 9.18); ECMO venous 18–21 F, arterial 14–16 F, 3–7 L/min vs max 5 L/min Impella 5.0 (p.297).
- "Check the instructions for use": AUTHORING.md rule; no source claim.
- Oxford selection factors (amount of support, cardioprotection: VA ECMO best haemodynamic support, very poor cardioprotection; ease of use; complications; contraindications): oxford p.287. Fig 9.8 severity × chronicity: oxford p.286.
- ECMO disadvantages (larger access, often cut-down; continuous anticoagulation; higher systemic thromboembolism; poor coronary perfusion; ventricular distension; complex; ECMO team): oxford p.297.
- All PCI labs should have immediate IABP availability: oxford p.289. IABP main device in the vast majority of labs: oxford p.252.
- Tomasello 2015: 12 patients, all procedures successful, no in-hospital MACCE: ref tomasello-ecmo-chip-2015 (PMID 25913808); cited by brilakis p.252 ref [7].
- Oxford: IABP less effective in tachycardia, especially irregular rhythm: oxford p.289.

- Acute MR or AR often poorly tolerated, may need urgent surgery; acute MCS such as IABP for acute MR may improve haemodynamics: brilakis p.488 (28.1.3.2). IABP lowers LVEDP and afterload, raises output: brilakis p.252.
- VSD contraindicates Impella except as bridge to surgery: brilakis p.254 (Table 14.1 footnote a).
- Impella complications (device migration, device thrombosis, limb ischaemia, vascular trauma, haemolysis, stroke, infection): oxford p.295. ECMO complications (bleeding, limb ischaemia, vascular trauma, compartment syndrome, AKI, haemolysis, thrombus and air embolism, infection, neurological injury): oxford p.297. VA vs VV ECMO; ECMO probably the most adopted ECLS in the lab because of simpler insertion: oxford p.297.

## Lesson pm-support-choice-shock (Profiling cardiogenic shock and matching the device)

- 24.2 introduction (identify early, understand cause, stabilise, revascularise; shock team; algorithms): brilakis p.428.
- Five ingredients (early diagnosis; Swan Ganz; revascularisation culprit-only; mechanical support before vs after PCI, device by haemodynamic need and local availability/expertise; subsequent care: dedicated team, haemodynamic monitoring, adjust support, advanced therapies): brilakis p.428 (Fig 24.6, read from PDF); ref brilakis-eckman-shock-2018 (PMID 29460404).
- Early identification before refractory and metabolic shock improves survival: brilakis p.428 (24.2.1).
- Tehrani algorithm (SBP <90 >30 min or drugs; end-organ hypoperfusion; lactate >2 mmol/L; one-call shock team: interventional cardiology, cardiac surgery, advanced HF, critical care; transfer to lab or CICU; ADHF-CS: RHC + echo; AMI-CS: RHC, angiography + revascularisation, peripheral vascular assessment; haemodynamic criteria Fick CI <1.8 without or <2.2 with drugs, PCWP >15, CPO <0.6 W, PAPi <1.0; consider PMCS; CICU daily echo, neurovascular assessment, serial CPO, PAPi, lactate, weaning vs escalation): brilakis p.429 (Fig 24.7, read from PDF). CPO and PAPi formulas: brilakis p.429 (Fig 24.7 legend) and p.251 (PAPI formula).
- Tehrani outcomes: 204 patients; 30-day survival 47% (2016), 57.9%, 76.6%; observational: ref tehrani-shock-team-2019 (PMID 30947919).
- Oxford severe shock (BP <90, HR >120, lactate >4 mmol/L, cool extremities, obtunded, ≥2 inotropes/vasoactive; CI <1.5, PCWP >30, LVEDP >30, cardiac power <0.6 W): oxford p.287.
- Maintain CPO >0.6 W while limiting escalating vasopressors and inotropes; assess RV failure (RA, PAPI); serial lactate and RHC: brilakis p.428 (24.2.2).
- PAPi <0.9 from Korabathina (acute inferior MI): brilakis p.249 ref [3]; ref korabathina-papi-2012.
- Worked examples and check/case numbers: own arithmetic from the formulas above.
- Shock branch of Fig 14.1 (hypoxaemia → VA-ECMO; RV failure → LV failure? yes → VA-ECMO; no → Protek Duo, Impella RP; LV failure: pre-shock → IABP; severe shock → Impella, Tandem Heart; refractory → VA-ECMO): brilakis p.250. Text: hypoxaemia → VA-ECMO (14.1.7), RV failure by echo (may not be available in lab) or RA >12 mmHg or PAPI <0.9 (14.1.8): brilakis p.251; isolated RV failure → Protek Duo, Impella RP; biventricular → VA-ECMO usually, or LV + RV device: brilakis p.252 (14.1.8); pre-shock CI >2.0 IABP may suffice with continuous monitoring ideally Swan Ganz; severe isolated LV → Impella CP (Tandem Heart in experienced centres); refractory despite Impella CP or Tandem Heart → VA-ECMO or Impella 5.0; Impella often left for LV venting during VA-ECMO: brilakis p.252 (14.1.9).
- NCSI algorithm (inclusion: AMI STEMI/NSTEMI, hypotension <90/60 or drugs to keep SBP >90, end-organ hypoperfusion: cool extremities, oliguria, lactic acidosis; exclusions incl. anoxic brain injury, unwitnessed OHCA or arrest without ROSC in 30 min, IABP before Impella, septic/anaphylactic/haemorrhagic/neurologic causes, non-ischaemic causes, active bleeding, recent major surgery, mechanical complications, known LV thrombus, no revascularisation, contraindication to anticoagulation, mechanical aortic valve; access: femoral arterial with ultrasound and fluoroscopy, venous femoral or IJ, Fick CI or LVEDP; LVEDP >15 or CI <2.2 and suitable anatomy → Impella; TIMI III in all major epicardial vessels other than CTO, intracoronary vasodilators if not; post-PCI CPO and PAPi; CPO >0.6 and PAPi >0.9 wean drugs, decide Impella removal in lab or ICU; CPO <0.6: PAPi <0.9 right-sided support, PAPi >0.9 additional support, local practice: more robust MCS or LVAD/transplant centre; CPO >0.6 and PAPi <0.9 right-sided support if clinical suspicion; vascular assessment: femoral angiogram and Doppler, external bypass if indicated; quality measures: Impella pre-PCI, door to support <90 min, TIMI III, RHC, wean drugs, CPO >0.6 W, survival >80%): brilakis p.430 (Fig 24.8, read from PDF).
- NCSI outcomes: 171 patients, 35 sites; 74% MCS before PCI; door-to-support 85 ± 63 min; survival to discharge 72%; predictors creatinine ≥2, lactate >4, CPO <0.6 W, age ≥70; single-arm: ref basir-ncsi-2019 (PMID 31025538).
- Femoral access may be preferred in shock: brilakis p.429 (24.2.4).
- Vasopressors/inotropes increase myocardial O2 consumption, arrhythmias; alpha agonists add afterload without inotropy; early MCS reduces need: brilakis p.428–429 (24.2.3).
- FFR affected by coronary afterload, may be inaccurate in shock; use with caution: brilakis p.431 (24.2.12).
- About two thirds multivessel disease; culprit-only (CULPRIT-SHOCK); staged: brilakis p.431 (24.2.7). Oxford culprit-only: oxford p.252.
- Limited data that support improves outcomes in shock: brilakis p.431 (24.2.14).

## Lesson pm-support-choice-rv (Right ventricular support, escalation, weaning and the exit)

- RV assessment by echo (may not be immediately available in the lab) or RA >12 mmHg or PAPI <0.9: brilakis p.251 (14.1.8); Tehrani PAPi <1.0: brilakis p.429.
- Suction alarm causes (hypovolaemia, RV failure, cannula under papillary muscles); prevention: optimise volume while keeping RA <10 mmHg, minimise device movement, cross the aortic valve with a pigtail; treatment: reduce P-level by 1 or 2, fluids if hypovolaemic, check and correct position, support RV if needed: brilakis p.260 (14.4.10.3.2). Round 1: prevention and treatment now taught separately.
- Opening paragraph: IABP, Impella and Tandem Heart require adequate RV function, VA-ECMO does not: brilakis p.254.
- IABP, Impella, Tandem Heart require adequate RV function; VA-ECMO does not: brilakis p.254 (Table 14.1).

## Lesson pm-support-choice-tandemheart (Left atrial-to-femoral bypass: the transseptal pump)

- Centrifugal pump LA → femoral artery; 21 Fr transseptal cannula and 15–19 Fr arterial cannula; full perfusion with appropriately sized arterial cannula; continuous monitoring to prevent displacement of transseptal cannula into RA; no oxygenation alone, oxygenator can be added for full cardiopulmonary support: brilakis p.252 (14.2).
- Table 14.1 features (availability +, difficulty ++++, limb ischaemia +++, bleeding/haemolysis/stroke ++, needs RV function, affected by arrhythmias, transseptal yes, 4–5 L/min depending on arterial cannula; contraindications high bleeding risk, severe AR, severe PAD (transcaval footnote), LA thrombus): brilakis p.254.
- Oxford has no TandemHeart section: search of the Oxford extract for "Tandem" found no entry.
- Round 1: steps renumbered to Brilakis's own sequence (14.6.1, steps 1–8 with the transseptal needle generic); 'venous access usually in the right groin'; LA verification moved to prevention (14.6.2); continuous monitoring attributed to the device description (14.2, p.252). The positive intrathoracic pressure and low cannula end are taught as prevention items under 14.6.2.1 (anatomic variations), not as air measures. The three TandemHeart lab tips were removed (no device field in TipTrigger; no console in the labs).
- Insertion similar to VA-ECMO except venous cannula in LA; 15, 17 or 19 Fr arterial cannula; arterial and venous access; venous right groin for transseptal; Brockenbrough needle under fluoroscopy, ICE or TEE; heparin immediately after LA entry; extra-support wire in LA; skin dilated gradually, 21 Fr atrial septal dilator; LA cannula with fenestrated end fully inside LA; rest as VA-ECMO: brilakis p.266–267 (14.6).
- Challenges: anatomical variation → failed transseptal, aortic entry, atrial wall puncture; prevention TEE/ICE, verify LA access before introducing sheath, transseptal under positive intrathoracic pressure, lower proximal cannula end below the heart; treatment: inadvertent aortic or other puncture usually well tolerated if transseptal sheath not advanced: brilakis p.267 (14.6.2).
- Severe shock with isolated LV failure: Tandem Heart at experienced centres; refractory → VA-ECMO or Impella 5.0: brilakis p.252 (14.1.9).
- Review: up to 4 L/min LA to descending aorta; reduces LV filling pressure (advantage over VA-ECMO); usable with mechanical AV or LV thrombus (limitation of Impella); transseptal needs longer time and expertise; vs IABP enhanced CO, lower PCWP and PA pressures, no 30-day mortality improvement, more vascular complications; impractical during cardiac arrest because of LA access: ref yadav-cath-lab-arrest-2018 (PMC6088444 full text).
- Rihal consensus abstract names the left atrial-to-femoral bypass pump: ref rihal-mcs-consensus-2015.
- Comparison with IABP: randomised, 41 patients with infarct-related CS and intended PCI of the infarct artery (IABP 20, bypass pump 21); cardiac power index improved more; severe bleeding 19 (bypass pump) vs 8 (IABP), limb ischaemia 7 (bypass pump) vs 0; 30-day mortality 45% (IABP) vs 43% (bypass pump), P = 0.86; round 2: the ref note now names the group for every number: ref thiele-tandemheart-iabp-2005 (PMID 15734771 abstract). The review's summary sentence (higher output, lower PCWP and PA pressures, no 30-day mortality gain, more vascular complications): ref yadav-cath-lab-arrest-2018.

## Lesson pm-support-choice-cpr (Mechanical chest compression in the catheter laboratory)

- Three systems: LUCAS, LifeStat, Autopulse; facilitate management of arrest in the lab; risk of rib fracture and even cardiac laceration: brilakis p.267 (14.7, refs 11 Yadav and 12 Elison).
- Oxford: compression depth at least 5 cm, rate 100 per minute, allow recoil; rarely met by human rescuers, especially prolonged arrest; piston-based (Lucas) and load-distributing band (AutoPulse); piston plunger compressed gas or electric, predefined rate, suction cup to facilitate decompression; band devices: backboard, band around armpits compressing sternum; used by ambulance crews, ED, cath lab; American and European guidelines do not recommend routine use due to limited outcome data; reasons: internal organ damage, more sternal (Lucas) and rib (AutoPulse) fractures, interruption during positioning, device shifting to vulnerable places: oxford p.283.
- Radiographer moves the C-arm to facilitate CPR: oxford p.63.
- CPR ideally with automated system such as Lucas, then VA-ECMO if no prompt recovery: brilakis p.488 (28.1.3.1). Arrest → VA-ECMO: brilakis p.250 (Fig 14.1) and p.249 (14.1.1).
- Review (Yadav and Truong): manual compression problems in lab (space, lead apron, C-arm, fatigue, interruptions, radiation to hands, visualisation and cardiac motion for operator); device advantages (no rescuer hazard, frees rescuer, no interruption for rotation, continues during defibrillation, consistent quality); PA projection obscured, coronary work in oblique projections, minor inconvenience; delay while applying; median 7 s to first mechanical compression with training; LifeStat piston; LINC n=2589 no difference at 4 h or 6 months; PARAMEDIC n=4471 no improvement in 30-day survival; ASPIRE and CIRC band trials no survival benefit; lab evidence: retrospective n=53 higher ROSC, prospective n=42; IABP ineffective in arrest (needs intrinsic function); Tandem Heart impractical in arrest: ref yadav-cath-lab-arrest-2018 (PMC full text). LINC and PARAMEDIC in refs.ts (linc-2014, paramedic-2015).
- Elison: RV and coronary artery perforation from mechanical CPR during primary PCI: ref elison-mechanical-cpr-trauma-2019 (PMID 34316838).
- External compression devices in Brilakis's support equipment: brilakis p.514 (Table 30.1, item 20e).
- Compression figures: Oxford gives at least 5 cm, 100/min, recoil, citing the American and European guidelines of its time: oxford p.283. The module tells the reader to take current figures from the current resuscitation guideline; the ERC 2025 text could not be opened (publisher pages returned 403), so no current numbers are given.
- Round 1 evidence additions (review full text, PMC6088444): ASPIRE n=1071 and CIRC n=4753 no improvement in survival to discharge; ASPIRE associated the band device with worse neurological outcomes; 2015 AHA IIb as reasonable alternative to manual compression; in the cath lab supported by the AHA (Class IIb) and 'strongly recommended' by the European guidelines; prospective n=42 suggested improved survival with good neurological recovery; VA-ECMO: refractory VF/VT 42% vs 15% favourable survival; Arlt 14-patient lab series (PCI and TAVR) ROSC in all, 50% survival to discharge; AHA and ERC give weak recommendations for VA-ECMO in lab arrest; Impella a possible option but clinical data lacking: ref yadav-cath-lab-arrest-2018.
- ASPIRE: stopped after first interim analysis; primary population survival to discharge 5.8% vs 9.9% (P = 0.06), CPC 1–2 3.1% vs 7.5% (P = 0.006): ref hallstrom-aspire-2006 (PMID 16772625 abstract).
- Device names made generic (round 1): Brilakis names three systems (two piston, one band; p.267); Oxford names one piston and one band device (p.283).
