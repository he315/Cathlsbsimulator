## Lesson cp-prior-cabg-svg-find

- Three challenges after CABG (grafts engaged/visualised/treated; native lesions challenging because grafting accelerates native atherosclerosis; older, comorbid, higher baseline risk); longer procedural and fluoroscopy time, higher dose, more contrast; higher risk of CI-AKI and possibly haemodialysis: brilakis p.335
- Most patients in the last decade: combination of arterial and SVG or total arterial; mammary patency higher; common operation LIMA to LAD with SVGs to Cx and RCA; bilateral IMAs in total arterial revascularisation with RIMA usually to RCA; radial patency variable particularly when bypassed stenosis <70%: oxford p.187
- Table 7.1: LSV 1-year 80–90%, late 60% at 10 years; LIMA 98%, 90% at 10 years; RIMA 96% to LAD, 75% to Cx/RCA, late 90% at 5 years; radial highly dependent on target vessel: oxford p.187
- 8% of grafts obstructed within 3 months; up to 20% of SVGs at 5 years; at 10 years only half patent; >90% of LIMA-LAD patent 10–20 years, significant stenoses uncommon: oxford p.187
- SVG ~98% patent immediately; <88% at 1 month due to acute graft thrombosis; 50–60% patent after 10 years: oxford p.194
- Review surgical report rather than relying on assumed anatomy; mistakes frequent, details lost in translation; saves time, contrast, dose, catheters; in non-urgent cases delay angiography to obtain the report: brilakis p.335
- Oxford: check previous angiogram report (view films if possible) and operation records for type of grafts and location of proximal and distal anastomoses; check history justifies invasive assessment: oxford p.189; operation note to be made available: oxford p.41
- Prior angiograms show proximal anastomosis locations and the catheters that engaged them: brilakis p.335; planning checklist "Issues with LIMA or other graft engagement" and catheters used: brilakis p.32; studying location of bypass grafts on previous angiograms expedites engagement (radiation reduction): brilakis p.489
- Prior chest CT, thinner slices (≤1 mm) enough for origin and number of grafts; CCTA excellent for native anatomy, location and patency of grafts; distal vessels and anastomoses can be hindered by calcification and clips: brilakis p.335–336
- Oxford: cardiac CT assesses SVG patency (less mobile, larger, tend not to develop calcified atherosclerosis): oxford p.29
- CKD and PAD common; risk score for CI-AKI; high risk: pre-procedural hydration, iso-osmolar contrast, contrast <3.7 × eGFR, careful planning and contrast-saving devices: brilakis p.336
- Heart failure: non-emergent cases deferred if volume overloaded for haemodynamic optimisation, often guided by RHC: brilakis p.336
- Severe PAD: access selection facilitated by pre-procedure CT angiography: brilakis p.336
- Check 1 (delay for the report; aortogram not a substitute; CT): brilakis p.335, p.338; oxford p.29
- Femoral or radial possible, femoral generally preferred (shorter time, fewer catheters, lower contrast and dose): brilakis p.337; ref michael-radial-cabg-2013 for the RADIAL-CABG figures (128 patients; contrast 171 vs 142 mL; procedure time 34.2 vs 21.9 min; crossover 17.2% vs 0%; higher operator dose)
- Femoral preferred in prior CABG patients even in ACS, where radial generally preferred: brilakis p.377 (20.4)
- Left radial in most cases if radial; right radial LIMA challenging; Simmons via right radial (Fig 18.2: LIMA, SVG to RCA, SVG to diagonal); Bartorelli-Cozzi via left radial; early femoral conversion when engagement challenging; bilateral IMA → upfront femoral: brilakis p.337–338; also brilakis p.61 (4.1 step 2: femoral usually preferred with patent grafts, left radial for LIMA "which nearly all patients have", bilateral IMA femoral strongly favoured)
- Oxford: femoral or left radial if LIMA; LIMA from right radial possible but challenging, may injure ostium; vein grafts challenging from right radial due to catheter shape entering ascending aorta from right brachiocephalic; RIMA may only be selectively intubated from right radial: oxford p.67
- Check 2: brilakis p.337; oxford p.67
- Catheters table, Brilakis: MP most common for right grafts, AL1 or LCB for left-sided grafts; JR4 often engages both, tried immediately after RCA angiography to save time and cost: brilakis p.338; JR4 engages most left-sided grafts for diagnostic angiography; AL for PCI or radial access gives stronger support; MP and LCB less support than AL: brilakis p.523; right-sided grafts usually inferiorly directed take-off, JR4 or multipurpose, dedicated RCB: brilakis p.524
- Catheters table, Oxford: JR4 usually used for SVGs and occasionally LIMA; JL4 and JR4 often complete study; RCA vein grafts at acute angle → MPA1 or MPA2; LCB and RCB help localisation; AL occasionally, particularly left-sided vein grafts; left then right coronary leaves a JR4 in situ: oxford p.189
- Location: most SVG proximal anastomoses on anterior wall of ascending aorta just above sinuses; surgeon-dependent; Cx grafts usually most superior, RCA most inferior; markers largely abandoned; clips give clues: oxford p.189; LAD and diagonal grafts just above LCA ostium: oxford p.191; RCA graft ostium just above and anterior to native RCA: oxford p.193; markers ~10%: brilakis p.338
- Right graft engagement: LAO; withdraw slightly from RCA ostium; gentle clockwise above RCA; acute-angle graft not suitable for JR4 → steeper distal tip: oxford p.193; MP or RCB rotated to face the right side of aorta in LAO and advanced up and down: brilakis p.117 (5.7.2.4)
- LAD/diagonal graft: LAO, RAO to determine anterior direction; gentle clockwise: oxford p.191
- Cx graft: most cranial; LAO or RAO; pull back above level of other grafts and rotate clockwise; in RAO tip away from spine: oxford p.192
- Brilakis left-sided sweep: up and down with left hand over several cm from cusp to origin of brachiocephalic trunk, gentle rotation after each movement with right hand: brilakis p.117 (5.7.2.3)
- Slow purposeful manipulation; engagement = resistance or slight jump; check pressure not damped, slow withdrawal and rotation if it is; slow injection confirms; occluded graft = small indentation (stump), record an image; increased risk of VF with vein graft cannulation: oxford p.189
- View note (rewritten round 1, no 'in practice' rule): Oxford p.191 (LAO cannulation, RAO check of anterior direction); brilakis p.125 (SVG usual initial projection LAO); Brilakis p.275 (15.1: RAO = least foreshortened ostium of left-sided SVG for aorto-ostial lesion PCI, not an engagement view); Academy dx-graft-plan uses RAO for left grafts
- Check 3 (Cx graft highest, clockwise, tip away from spine; RCA graft lowest): oxford p.189, p.192, p.193
- Graft not found: continue until sources of flow to all territories identified; bilateral subclavian angiography for IMA use; clips along IMA; aortography ~60 mL at 20 cc/s, cine in LAO: brilakis p.338
- Oxford aortography: before graft localisation when unclear which grafts, occluded graft, origin; LAO; loop through, freeze clearest frame as road map providing the same projection is used; sternal sutures and rib spaces as markers: oxford p.187; 40–60 mL at 20 mL/s in LAO; for grafts the catheter end slightly higher with prolonged acquisition to trace origin, location and direction: oxford p.126
- Failure-to-engage algorithm (cusp angiography or aortography; 0.035/0.038 wire inside catheter, long sheath, diagnostic catheter, 1 Fr smaller catheter, change access; larger or smaller curve or guide extension (step 3, now in the lesson); other shapes; stop and CT if large contrast volume and no emergency, e.g. no STEMI or shock): brilakis p.118–119 (5.7.3.6)
- Collateral and competitive filling as clues: oxford p.189, p.197–198
- Pearl: states Brilakis's rule (brilakis p.338, continue until sources of flow to all territories are identified) and labels the sketch as a teaching suggestion, not a manual step
- Monitoring step 7 (careful monitoring, higher baseline risk, complex anatomy): brilakis p.337 (18.2)
- Check 1 explanation: aortogram helps locate origins and Oxford may do it before localisation when unclear which grafts were placed: oxford p.187; Brilakis delays for the report: brilakis p.335
- ACS femoral trade-off (radial: lower bleeding and mortality in prior studies; SAFARI-STEMI no difference; femoral preferred after CABG): brilakis p.377 (20.4)
- Sim: cathlab movements per oxford p.193, brilakis p.117

## Lesson cp-prior-cabg-svg-image

- Bypass angiography after thorough evaluation of native anatomy; examine all three territories for collaterals and competitive filling, clues to graft patency: oxford p.189
- High CTO prevalence after CABG; long cine runs without panning for collaterals: brilakis p.339 (18.6.1)
- Competitive filling: washout of contrast distal to anastomosis when a patent native artery also receives a graft; occasional back filling of the graft: oxford p.197
- Collateral filling: collaterals suggest reduced supply (blocked native or graft); no collaterals with an occluded native vessel implies the supplying graft may still be patent: oxford p.198
- Check 1: oxford p.198
- Optimal engagement critical for image quality; prolonged runs for long grafts supplying a large territory: brilakis p.339 (18.6.2)
- Grafts may require larger contrast volumes; typical 5–10 mL LCA and 3–5 mL RCA: oxford p.180
- At least two views per SVG; entire length, ostium and insertion; radiographer pans for distal vessel and collaterals: oxford p.191
- Panning best avoided as it degrades the image: brilakis p.126 (6.6.2 step 4)
- Views table (Brilakis row round 1: ostium text moved to 'Ostium and body'; anastomosis column 'Not given separately'): LAD graft LAO and RAO (ostium, body), LAO cranial and lateral (distal anastomosis, native LAD): oxford p.191; Cx graft LAO and RAO, RAO caudal: oxford p.192; RCA graft LAO and RAO, LAO cranial: oxford p.193; Brilakis SVG and radial grafts initial LAO: brilakis p.125; LAO, RAO: brilakis p.127; ostium right-sided SVG LAO, left-sided SVG RAO: brilakis p.275
- Vein graft failure table: high availability, suitable length, not influenced by vasospasm; causes (intimal hyperplasia, atherosclerosis, thrombosis due to plaque rupture); aneurysmal dilatation usually >5 years with distal thrombotic embolisation, fistula, compression, rupture; patient risk factors (DM, hypercholesterolaemia, CKD, younger age); procedural (poor vein quality, increased vein diameter, multiple distal anastomoses or poor distal vessel, surgical handling, grafting non-ischaemic target): oxford p.194
- Majority of SVG failures clinically silent; symptoms depend on territory, native disease, other grafts/collaterals; prevention lifestyle, risk factors, aspirin and statins; treatment depends on timing, symptoms, risk/benefit: oxford p.195
- FFR limited for SVG lesions: FFR reflects native flow (significant SVG lesion may have normal FFR); intermediate SVG lesions progress rapidly; FFR-CT not validated with stents and/or grafts: brilakis p.351 (18.12); FFR of SVG = SVG flow + native (unless occluded) + collaterals; additional lesions develop elsewhere in the same SVG; recommend against physiological assessment of SVG lesions: brilakis p.227 (12.3.3)
- Check 2: brilakis p.227, p.351
- Drill and sim: angio-real collaterals drill (oxford p.198 logic); views sim (oxford p.191–193)

## Lesson cp-prior-cabg-arterial

- LIMA in nearly all CABG patients: brilakis p.61
- LIMA ostium anteroinferiorly from left subclavian a few cm beyond the vertebral artery: oxford p.199
- Non-selective proximal subclavian angiography before selective IMA to identify lesions causing steal; otherwise compare subclavian with central aortic waveform; subclavian steal rare cause of recurrent ischaemia and vertebrobasilar insufficiency such as vertigo; caused by significant ipsilateral subclavian stenosis; suspect with >20 mmHg systolic difference between arms: brilakis p.339 (18.6.3)
- Severe proximal subclavian lesions → ischaemia, even ACS; subclavian stenting effective: brilakis p.341 (18.7.3)
- Oxford: record pressure; damped may suggest subclavian stenosis: oxford p.199
- Check 1: brilakis p.339, p.341
- Catheters, Brilakis: JR4 engages most IM grafts; IM catheter bigger distal bend; VB1 and Bartorelli-Cozzi even larger distal bend (BC for left radial): brilakis p.523 (30.2.6.3); IM or IM-VB1 shaped like proximal two-thirds of a pigtail curve; pigtail with coronary wire or cutting a small part of its tip: brilakis p.339 (18.5.3); Simmons via right radial, BC via left radial: brilakis p.337; general list IM or occasionally VB1, JR4, Williams, Bartorelli-Cozzi, JIM: brilakis p.117 (5.7.2.5)
- Catheters, Oxford: femoral JR4 or LIMA catheter; left radial 3DRC (Williams) or BC: oxford p.199; LIMA catheter longer tip than JR4, easier access; long exchange wire without losing access: oxford p.199; right radial LIMA challenging, may injure ostium: oxford p.67
- Engagement steps 1–7: oxford p.199 (catheter over 0.035 J wire just past left subclavian ostium; AP or LAO; withdraw wire slightly; anticlockwise rotation with slow withdrawal; elderly or calcified aorta wire just out of the tip before rotation; non-selective injection if difficult; advance wire, halt on resistance; towards left arm, not vertebral; hydrophilic J wire for tortuosity; exchange for LIMA catheter if needed; catheter past vertebral; withdraw wire, aspirate, pressure; good trace before manipulation; record, damped may suggest subclavian stenosis; slow withdrawal with anticlockwise rotation bringing the tip anteriorly with small non-selective injections; slight jump or dip; check damping; small test injection)
- Brilakis: advance over 0.035 wire past IM graft origin, withdraw with counterclockwise rotation, preferably RAO; severe proximal tortuosity → kinking; long sheaths improve handling: brilakis p.117 (5.7.2.5); 0.035 or 0.038 wire inside catheter stiffens it and reduces kinking: brilakis p.117 (5.7.3.3); 0.014 wire into vessel can facilitate engagement, often done for IMA grafts: brilakis p.118 (5.7.3.5)
- Warn patient of warm/burning sensation over left chest and arm: oxford p.199; localised discomfort in the arm during IMA angiography: oxford p.100
- LIMA fragile, dissection particularly in elderly, slow selective injections: oxford p.201; LIMA ostium more prone to dissection: oxford p.321; carotid or vertebral dissection during LIMA cannulation as a cause of stroke: oxford p.303
- Views: Oxford AP (ostium), RAO cranial (mid LAD), LAO cranial and left lateral (distal anastomosis); table moved; pre-position for lateral; ensure catheter not displaced: oxford p.200; Brilakis initial AP: brilakis p.125; AP, lateral, AP cranial or RAO cranial: brilakis p.127; LIMA and RIMA ostium AP with shallow cranial: brilakis p.275
- Cannot cannulate: non-selective acquisition from subclavian; BP cuff on left arm; 5 F; left radial: oxford p.201
- Check 2 (exchange to IM over long wire; non-selective with cuff; 5 F; left radial; deep intubation and guide extensions may dissect or perforate IMA): oxford p.199, p.201; brilakis p.339 (18.5.3)
- RIMA: occasionally in situ, or dissected free from subclavian and used as a free graft from the aorta; cannulated like LIMA but challenging; JR4 or LIMA catheter past innominate; withdraw with slow anticlockwise rotation; avoid right common carotid; clockwise to bring the tip anteriorly; slow injections to avoid ostial dissection; clips show path and insertion (Fig 7.12): oxford p.201; right radial may be the only selective route: oxford p.67
- Terminology: 'glyceryl trinitrate (GTN)' for Brilakis's 'nitroglycerin' (house style)
- RIMA cross-reference now points to Step 2 of lesson 'Before the first graft injection' (cp-prior-cabg-svg-find)
- Radial graft: anti-spasm drugs improved survival but patency not as high as hoped; usually free grafts on the aorta like vein grafts; occasionally jump or sequential from LIMA or RIMA; smaller and smoother than SVGs; accessed like vein grafts: oxford p.202; engaged as SVGs, NTG important as very prone to spasm: brilakis p.339 (18.5.3)
- Gastroepiploic: not routine, used when IMA and vein grafts unavailable; distal RCA; cobra into common hepatic via coeliac; hydrophilic wire into gastroduodenal then right gastroepiploic; exchange for MPA1 or JR4: oxford p.202

## Lesson cp-prior-cabg-target

- Three or four options (native, SVG, arterial graft, redo): brilakis p.340 (18.7); redo: brilakis p.139 (7.1.3)
- Perforation previously considered innocent because adhesions prevent effusion and tamponade; now known lethal; loculated haematomas, chamber compromise, haemodynamic collapse (dry tamponade); surgery or CT-guided drainage; prompt identification and treatment: brilakis p.335; loculated effusions compress left atrium or right ventricle; may be impossible to drain percutaneously; CT-guided drainage or emergency surgery; immediately treat (covered stents or coils): brilakis p.462 (26.6); loculated effusion especially in prior CABG: brilakis p.447
- Algorithm (Fig 18.6): brilakis p.340, image read; ref xenogiannis-prior-cabg-2019
- SVG lesions usually treated with PCI because redo high risk; redo an option especially without LIMA-LAD and when LIMA-LAD feasible; native PCI if native easily treated; SVG PCI if native challenging (complex CTO) and SVG simple; native CTO recanalisation after SVG PCI fails or recurrent SVG failure at experienced centres: brilakis p.340 (18.7.2)
- Native PCI preferred: lower periprocedural risk, better long-term outcomes; native lesions challenging (calcification, CTO): brilakis p.340 (18.7.1)
- Redo considered with severely diseased or occluded grafts, native not amenable to PCI, depressed LVEF, absence of patent arterial grafts; IMA should be used in reoperation: brilakis p.139 (7.1.3)
- Redo usually avoided with patent IMA to LAD: brilakis p.341 (18.7.3)
- Oxford: redo increased mortality, may not be appropriate; new vein conduits more distally or onto existing graft if distal anastomosis preserved: oxford p.195; native vessel PCI alternatives (CTOs, protected LMS with patent LIMA, via patent grafts): oxford p.265
- Check 1: brilakis p.340
- Acute SVG occlusion: large thrombus, diffuse degeneration, high recurrence; aggressive thrombectomy and EPDs; low long-term patency: brilakis p.340 (18.7.2.1); ad hoc or staged PCI of the bypassed native artery may give better outcomes but challenging, needing dedicated equipment and expertise: brilakis p.341; ACS from failure of highly diseased SVG → native CTO preferred if feasible: brilakis p.389 (21.1)
- Chronic SVG occlusion: generally not recanalised (class III, LOE C) unless no other options; occluded SVG for retrograde crossing if favourable morphology: brilakis p.341 (18.7.2.2); guideline is ref 15 of ch 18 (2011 ACCF/AHA/SCAI PCI guideline): brilakis p.352; that guideline replaced by the 2021 ACC/AHA/SCAI revascularisation guideline: ref lawton-revasc-2021 (no current class quoted)
- Acute SVG occlusion row: native CTO preferred in ACS if feasible, ad hoc or staged, depends on dedicated expertise and equipment: brilakis p.341 (18.7.2.1), p.389 (21.1)
- Elective occlusion of an SVG with multiple failures after native recanalisation using vascular plugs; type II plug used in most SVG cases (general statement, not 'his practice'); braided nitinol mesh discs on a delivery cable, released by counterclockwise rotation, retrievable and repositionable before release; size 30–50% larger than vessel at occlusion site; sufficient length: brilakis p.592 (30.18.3)
