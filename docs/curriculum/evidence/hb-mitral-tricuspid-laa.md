# Evidence: hb-mitral-tricuspid-laa (Mitral, tricuspid and left atrial appendage interventions)

Module file: `src/content/manual/cath-handbook/08-mitral-tricuspid-laa.ts`. Page numbers are PDF pages (Brilakis PDF page as in the extracts; Oxford PDF page = printed page + 21).

## Sources and how they were read

The two manuals cover these procedures only at the edges. Oxford ch 5 'Mitral regurgitation' (oxford p.135–136) and 'Tricuspid valve' (oxford p.138) are haemodynamic (v waves, ventriculographic grading); ch 4 gives the transseptal route to the left heart (oxford p.107) and the atrial waveforms (oxford p.110, p.114). Brilakis has transseptal puncture only for the TandemHeart left atrial cannula (brilakis p.252, 14.2; p.254, Table 14.1; p.266–267, 14.6). Neither book describes TEER, tricuspid devices or LAAO. The procedural teaching therefore comes from these references, each checked on PubMed (authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages) and read in full on the publisher's open-access page (EuroIntervention; NCBI does not release the XML) unless stated:

- `russo-transseptal-2021`: Russo G, Taramasso M, Maisano F. EuroIntervention 2021;17:720–727. PMID 33775929, PMC9724844. Full text read. Cited below as "Russo".
- `hausleiter-mitral-teer-2023`: Hausleiter J et al. EuroIntervention 2023;18:957–976. PMID 36688459, PMC9869401. Full text read, including Tables 1–5 as rendered in HTML. Cited as "Hausleiter".
- `praz-tricuspid-2021`: Praz F et al. EuroIntervention 2021;17:791–808. PMID 34796878, PMC9724890. Full text read; Tables 1–3 are images and were not readable, so nothing is taken from them. Cited as "Praz".
- `laao-consensus-2020` (existing registry entry, Glikson M et al. EuroIntervention 2020;15:1133–1180): full text read on the publisher's page, chapters 3, 4, 5 (leaks and DRT), 7 and 8, plus chapter 9 (centre requirements). Cited as "Consensus".
- `fukutomi-laao-transseptal-2022`: Fukutomi M et al. EuroIntervention 2022;17:e1435–e1444. PMID 34483092, PMC9900448. Abstract and results read. Cited as "Fukutomi".
- `lakkireddy-amulet-ide-2021`: Lakkireddy D et al. Circulation 2021;144:1543–1552. PMID 34459659, PMC8570346. Abstract read.
- Added in fixer round 1, each checked on PubMed (authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages):
  - `dukkipati-pdl-2022`: Dukkipati SR et al. J Am Coll Cardiol 2022;80:469–483. PMID 35902169. Abstract read: pooled PROTECT AF, PREVAIL, CAP2 (1,054 patients); leak ≤5 mm at 1 year associated with 5-year ischaemic stroke/SE, adjusted HR 1.94.
  - `alkhouli-ncdr-leak-2022`: Alkhouli M et al. JACC Clin Electrophysiol 2022;8:766–778. PMID 35387751, PMC9233062. Abstract read: 51,333 NCDR patients; small (>0–5 mm) leaks: stroke/TIA/SE adjusted HR 1.152, major bleeding higher.
  - `lurz-mithras-2021`: Lurz P et al. JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2021;14:2685–2694. PMID 34949392. Abstract read: MITHRAS, 80 patients with iASD and Qp:Qs ≥1.3 at 30 days after TEER, closure vs conservative; death/HF hospitalisation at 12 months 35% vs 50% (P = 0.26); iASD cohort worse than no-iASD cohort (43% vs 17%), driven by HF hospitalisation.
  - `adamo-vhd-update-2026`: Adamo M et al. Eur Heart J Suppl 2026;28(Suppl 4):iv83–iv96. PMID 42064867, PMC13128274. Full text read on PMC: summary of the 2025 ESC/EACTS guideline (`esc-vhd-2025`, PMID 40878295, existing registry entry) by task-force members. Cited as "Adamo".
  - `esc-ph-2022` (existing registry entry): cited only for the haemodynamic definition of pre-capillary PH (mean PAP >20 mmHg, PAWP ≤15 mmHg, PVR >2 WU), the same definition taught in hb-cardiac-catheterisation.
- `katsiampoura-pitloc-2022`: J Cardiothorac Vasc Anesth 2022;36:3257–3264. PMID 35153136. Abstract read; cited only for the six PITLOC stages.
- `lo-russo-transseptal-occluder-2025`: Interv Cardiol 2025;20:e33. PMID 41450850. Abstract read; cited only for planning with TOE/CT, native-septum or through-device puncture, balloon-assisted tracking and RF, residual shunt and device deformation.
- Existing registry entries used: `triluminate-2023` (abstract re-read on PubMed, PMID 36876753), `triscend2-2025` (abstract re-read, PMID 39475399), `esc-vhd-2021` (cited only as summarised by Praz and Hausleiter; the guideline itself was not re-read: the OUP page is behind a bot check), `everest2-2011`, `coapt-2018`, `mitra-fr-2018` (cited as background and as summarised by Hausleiter), `reddy-laao-5y-2017` (background for the trial regimen, as summarised by the Consensus).

Device names are not used; implants are described as the clip system, the paddle-and-spacer system, the single-lobe (plug) occluder and the lobe-and-disc occluder. Device-specific numbers are those the cited source gives for the devices it describes and are labelled as such (for example the plug occluder's 8% minimum compression and 5 mm marker offset are from the 2020 Consensus, which describes the earlier generation).

## Notes for checkers

- Tip book locations: the validator (src/content/validate.ts, validateTips) requires at least one manual location per tip. Where neither manual contains a tip's technique, the location string itself now states its limited role, so the lab does not imply the manual supports the tip: `ch 4: Accessing the left heart (transseptal route only; technique from the references)` for the transseptal technique tips (pacing-lead-wire, pulldown-orient, two-movements, resistant-wire-first, vagal-st, posterior-delayed-tamponade), teer-height-coaptation and all structural-laao-* tips except fill-before-sizing; `ch 5: Mitral regurgitation (the condition only; technique from the references)` for teer-area-budget, gantry-arms, steer-views and second-device; `ch 5: Tricuspid valve (the condition only; ...)` for tteer-diurese-first; `ch 4: Left heart pressures and waveforms (pressure measurement only; fluid challenge from the references)` for laao-fill-before-sizing. Brilakis 14.6 was removed from those tips (its section format cannot carry a label and it holds none of their technique). The technique itself is traced to the refs listed on each tip and below. Tips whose content the manuals do support keep plain locations: ts-both-groins-low (brilakis p.266: venous access in the right groin for transseptal puncture; oxford p.107 route), four-confirmations (oxford p.110, p.114), air-hub-low and aortic-puncture-hold and heparin-after-crossing (brilakis p.267), la-vwave (oxford p.135), planimetry (oxford p.133: mean gradients vary with heart rate).
- Disagreements and differences taught explicitly:
  - Needle orientation at pull-down: Russo 5 or 6 o'clock (6 for more posterior); Consensus 5–6 pm with noon to the ceiling and 6 to the floor. Both given; no viewpoint 'from the feet' is claimed. A 'Two sources, two conventions' paragraph in ts-setup names the Academy's 4–5 o'clock (viewed from below, 12 anterior) and its 'withdraw to the SVC and repeat' advice, attributed to the Academy lesson, alongside Russo's combined movements for small adjustments.
  - TEER height: Russo measures puncture to annulus (at least 4 cm degenerative, 3 cm acceptable functional); Hausleiter gives about 4.0–4.5 cm from puncture to coaptation point. Now given as two separately labelled measurements; the Academy's 3–3.5 cm is named as the Academy's figure.
  - Iatrogenic ASD: Russo (expert review) closes for bidirectional/right-to-left shunt or >8 mm, highly advisable after TMVI; MITHRAS (randomised, left-to-right only) found no benefit from closure at 1 month after TEER. Both taught and attributed; q4 now tests a right-to-left defect with hypoxaemia, where Russo and the Academy (structural.ts 'close if significant right-to-left shunting with hypoxia') agree.
  - Peri-device leak: Consensus 2020 (no link to events; 5 mm from PROTECT AF, not validated) is now followed by Dukkipati 2022 and Alkhouli 2022 (small leaks linked to modestly more thromboembolism). Procedural seal target: Consensus ch 3 'minimal peri-device leak (<3 mm)' in 0/45/90/135 before release; Amulet section: detach when 'no device leak is present'. Both taught.
  - Heparin: Russo 2,000 U at access, 1,000–2,000 IU before puncture, full 100 U/kg after, ACT 250–300 s; Consensus 70–100 IU/kg (100 U/kg example), ACT ≥250 s or >250 s (some >300 s), timing varies; Brilakis heparin immediately after entering the LA (brilakis p.267). All given and attributed; "follow local protocol" stated.
  - Aortic puncture: Russo "once confirmed... not to pull back anything", call surgeon, rule out tamponade; Brilakis "usually well tolerated if the transseptal sheath is not advanced" (brilakis p.267). Taught together, consistent.
- Author reasoning, labelled or obvious: the forward-flow formula restates Hausleiter Table 2 arithmetic; the pearls (rehearsing the views aloud, saying the four confirmations, naming the deployment aloud, writing numbers on the planning sheet, TAPSE/PASP beside the TR grade) are teaching devices built from the cited facts; case narratives and vitals are synthetic.
- Possible issues in existing Academy lessons (not edited; for the checkers and fixer to route):
  - `sh-transseptal` (structural.ts) orients the needle "about 4 to 5 o'clock viewed from below"; Russo gives 5 or 6 o'clock and the Consensus 5–6.
  - `sh-transseptal` says that after a needle-only aortic puncture you "withdraw it and watch with echo"; Russo advises pulling nothing back once aortic puncture is confirmed and calling the surgeon first (Brilakis p.267 only says needle-only entry is usually tolerated).
  - `sh-laao-imaging` pitfall: "many operators give fluid to bring the mean LA pressure above about 10 mmHg"; the 2020 Consensus gives >12 mmHg for procedural measurement and fluid when below 11–12 mmHg.
  - `sh-laao-procedure` release table: "for the current-generation lobe-only device this is about 10–30%" compression; not in the 2020 Consensus it cites (which gives a minimum of 8% for the device it describes). Needs its own source (device IFU or a FLX trial) or removal.
  - `sh-toe-views`: LVOT view "about 120–140°"; Hausleiter uses an LVOT view at 140–160° as the x-plane partner during TEER. Not necessarily wrong (generic TOE view vs TEER x-plane), but worth reconciling. Its 3D en-face convention (aorta at 12 o'clock) is the surgeon's view; Hausleiter prefers the cardiologist's view (aorta at 6 o'clock) for TEER steering.
  - `sh-teer-procedure` cites `hanna-pcm` for TEER height 3.5–4.5 cm; the sources here give ≥4 cm (degenerative) and 3 cm acceptable (functional) to the annulus (Russo), 4.0–4.5 cm to the coaptation point (Hausleiter).
  - Registry (refs.ts, not mine): id `respect-2017` carries the PROTECTED TAVR citation (Kapadia 2022) and id `close-2017` carries the BHF PROTECT-TAVI citation (Kharbanda 2025); the ids do not match the papers.

## Lesson hb-mitral-tricuspid-laa-ts-setup

- LHC usually retrograde; alternative transseptal route from RA to LA across PFO, ASD or transseptal puncture: oxford p.107
- Uses (mitral repair/replacement, LAAO, paravalvular leak, LV assist positioning): Russo (introduction lists electrophysiology, mitral repair/implantation, PFO closure, LAAO, paraprosthetic leak repair, LVAD positioning); left atrial cannula for mechanical support: brilakis p.252 (14.2, TandemHeart 21 Fr cannula via transseptal puncture), brilakis p.254 (Table 14.1 transseptal puncture required)
- True septum ~20% of total septal area; anatomical vs true septum; fossa in lower posterior part, oval or round, thin fibrous; four quadrants: Russo
- Quadrant table: MitraClip-type TEER superior-posterior or inferior-posterior; height ≥4 cm degenerative, 3 cm acceptable functional; annuloplasty band superior-anterior or inferior-anterior; LAAO inferior, usually inferior-posterior: Russo. 4.0–4.5 cm puncture-to-coaptation: Hausleiter (M-TEER optimisation). Inferior-central/anterior predicted in about a quarter (16% + 8%): Fukutomi
- Brockenbrough modified Ross needle, distal 1.5 cm from 18 to 21 gauge; stainless steel; stylet prevents friction/scraping; arrow on handle shows orientation; standard 19°, BRK-1 53°; hand-shapeable: Russo
- Sheaths 8–12 Fr; braided SL more push and torque, curve points at left superior PV; Mullins curve ideal to reach mitral valve; dilator protrudes beyond sheath: Russo
- Energy/wire alternatives (RF, cautery to needle, sharp nitinol wire, back of coronary wire): Russo (thick septum); Consensus (ch 4: RF catheter, Bovie on needle arrow, stylet or back of 0.014/0.018 wire, dedicated wire)
- Pressure line on needle: Russo (after stylet removal connect to pressure line); Consensus (manifold/power injector with transducer for continuous pressure)
- Pre-procedural TOE recommended in all; thrombus or tumour contraindications; CT for TMVI/direct annuloplasty; software maps fossa vs LAA ostium, mitral annulus, cavae; height, orientation, C-arm angle can be simulated: Russo
- Three views (bicaval, short-axis, four-chamber): Russo (Figure 4 and text); Academy lesson sh-toe-views
- PITLOC: Practice, Identification of needle, Tracking tip, Localisation in fossa, Optimising indentation, Crossing: ref katsiampoura-pitloc-2022 (abstract)
- Right femoral preferred for linear trajectory; prepare both groins; left for arterial monitoring, complication access, TSP if right venous anatomy unfavourable; reduced trackability, more curved needle: Russo
- Low puncture: femoral vein does not bifurcate and is reasonable size until a few cm from inguinal ligament; reduces retroperitoneal bleeding; US with linear probe avoids arterial puncture; fluoroscopic landmark medial to femoral head and below its equator (AP); alternative 4 cm below ligament, medial to pulse, 45°: Russo
- Micropuncture needle, ~7 mm skin incision, optional pre-close; pre-closure 'has not been demonstrated to improve outcomes or reduce vascular access complications': Consensus ch 4
- Brockenbrough puncture under fluoroscopic, ICE or TOE guidance: brilakis p.267 (14.6.1 step 3)
- ~2,000 U heparin (~¼ total) during access phase: Russo; timing strategies vary (full before, after, split): Consensus ch 4
- Sheath and dilator over wire to SVC under AP fluoro and bicaval TOE; pacing electrodes: wire posterior, in LAO wire appears on right side of screen vs electrodes; sheath tip facing left (aorta) not right (caval free wall); venous tortuosity: larger sheath, double stiff wires: Russo
- Needle advanced with stylet inside until 4 cm from the tip; then stylet removed and Luer lock connected to the pressure line; needle advanced under AP fluoroscopy to the sheath tip, not beyond; arrow rotates freely: Russo (Needle insertion and its tips)
- Grip: first three fingers on sheath at de-airing line, fourth and fifth on needle arrow; de-airing line and arrow in same direction before pullback: Russo
- Pullback arrow 5 or 6 o'clock, 6 for posterior; anatomy varies: Russo. 5–6 pm, noon to ceiling, 6 pm to floor: Consensus ch 4. Academy 4–5 o'clock viewed from below and 'withdraw to the SVC and repeat': structural.ts sh-transseptal (attributed, not asserted)
- Jump from SVC into RA and another when engaging fossa: Consensus ch 4
- Bicaval monitors tenting; supraventricular extrasystoles as needle tents superior rim; AP fluoro monitors rotation and avoids pulling into IVC: Russo
- Paint and poke (contrast to show tenting); tenting must also be confirmed by echo (TOE or ICE): Consensus ch 4
- SAX: posterior in most mitral procedures for working distance; four-chamber measures height (tent to mitral annulus) for MitraClip: Russo. To coaptation point: Hausleiter
- Isolated clockwise rotation rotates sheath without changing contact point; movements in two directions at once; too anterior → clockwise plus slight pull back; clockwise moves away from mitral plane and gains height, counter-clockwise toward the valve: Russo
- More posterior by more clockwise and sometimes slight withdrawal; slight withdrawal more inferior: Consensus ch 4
- Posterior rotation gains height; bending (rarely straightening) needle helps contact in distorted anatomy: Hausleiter (M-TEER optimisation)
- Table rows: anterior tent fix (Russo); slight withdrawal for lower site (Consensus); posterior/clockwise for height (Russo, Hausleiter); large atrium: add curve, lower and less posterior (Russo, challenging septa)
- ICE: probe in RA, rotated clockwise and retroflexed/tilted to see fossa cranio-caudally; clockwise to see posterior fossa: Consensus ch 3 (procedural imaging)
- Fusion imaging: integrates fluoro and TOE; markers; C-arm angle; limits (distracting, same vendor, limited C-arm rotations for some views, markers do not follow heart motion); no data on time, dose, complications, confidence: Russo
- Pitfalls: needle protrusion (Russo: avoid pushing needle over sheath tip; stylet); alignment of arrow/side arm (Russo); rotation alone (Russo); IVC (Russo); pre-TOE (Russo)
- Check (pacing leads in LAO): Russo
- Sims: TEER lab FossaPicker and LAAO lab Transseptal step (src/ui/structural/FossaPicker.tsx, TeerLab.tsx, LaaoLab.tsx; src/simulation/teer.ts assessTransseptal flags danger and coaxial-for-LAA)
- Video uvgLPY2gqmQ: title only

## Lesson hb-mitral-tricuspid-laa-ts-cross

- Cross with TOE SAX and AP fluoro; advance needle keeping sheath in place; slow movements; tenting release on TEE; confirm LA pressure before advancing sheath; no trace or aortic pressure → do not advance: Russo
- Four checks (TEE, aspiration, pressure curve, contrast through needle): Russo
- LA v wave usually slightly higher than a wave: oxford p.110; RA v wave usually slightly smaller than a wave and RA pressure slightly lower than LA: oxford p.114 (Fig. 4.18 legend; 'further clue' is author reasoning from it); LA v more prominent than a, RA the opposite, used to confirm LA position: Consensus ch 4
- Heparin immediately after entry into LA (TandemHeart): brilakis p.267 (14.6.1 step 4). Full heparinisation 100 U/kg after puncture, ACT 250–300 s for most procedures: Russo. 70–100 IU/kg, ACT ≥250 s; therapeutic ACT as soon as possible because of low-flow LA and thrombogenic equipment: Consensus ch 4 and ch 8
- Check (aortic trace): Russo (aorta puncture management); brilakis p.267 (14.6.2.1 treatment)
- Sheath and needle 1–2 cm into LA under TEE; holding needle, advance over needle; needle and dilator steady, sheath over dilator; near edge of heart shadow retrieve needle and eventually dilator: Russo
- Cover proximal hub with thumb, hold hub low between legs or lateral to leg, particularly with low LA pressure; continuous slow aspiration through dilator during removal; aspirate, flush, connect to transducer for mean LA pressure: Consensus ch 4
- Positive intrathoracic pressure and lowering proximal end of cannula below heart (TandemHeart prevention): brilakis p.267 (14.6.2.1)
- 0.032-inch wire into superior left PV with slight clockwise rotation; four-chamber view confirms not in appendage: Russo. Confirm stiff wire in left PV (usually upper) or LA: Hausleiter
- Mullins: do not retrieve dilator completely (kinking); very posterior puncture: rotate slightly counter-clockwise before advancing to avoid roof: Russo
- Extra-support wire into LA, skin then 21 Fr atrial septal dilator, fenestrated end fully inside LA: brilakis p.267 (14.6.1 steps 5–7); continuous monitoring to prevent displacement of the transseptal cannula into the RA: brilakis p.252 (14.2). 'In some cases, especially when performing TMVI, septal dilatation using a 12-16 mm peripheral balloon is needed': Russo
- Resistance at dilator–sheath transition; mild forward tension and slight rotation; stiffest wires or 4 mm balloon; sheath tracks as balloon deflates; first a J wire deep in PV (usually left superior) or Inoue-shaped wire in LA/LV to avoid posterior wall injury from sudden jump: Consensus ch 4
- Enlarged atria: more inferior and less posterior to avoid pericardial exit; extra curvature: Russo
- Thick/resistant septum after surgery or radiation: back of 0.014-inch exchange wire; RF to proximal needle by electrosurgical generator; sharp nitinol needle-free wire that becomes J when unsupported; RF catheter 5 W for 2–5 s, 1–4 pulses; overshoot phenomenon into superior or contralateral LA wall: Russo. Bovie or RF 15–20 W, 1 s to needle arrow; stylet or back of 0.014/0.018 wire: Consensus ch 4
- Floppy septum: height reduced during steering; higher punctures; diathermy or RF for safety and precision (no 'extreme tenting' claimed): Russo. Difficult crossing (thickened or floppy): electrocautery on needle or dedicated device; stiff end of PCI wire: Hausleiter
- Prior occluder: ref lo-russo-transseptal-occluder-2025 (abstract)
- PFO/ASD use when LAA orientation lateral/posterior; tight long-tunnel PFO may misalign and need separate puncture: Consensus ch 4
- Posterior TSP at border with muscular septum crosses pericardial space; delayed tamponade after catheter removal; TEE atrial haematoma; pericardial drainage first, then ASD closure device; early diagnosis by keeping wire across and waiting minutes with arterial pressure, ECG and TEE: Russo
- Brilakis lists inadvertent entry into the aorta or puncture of the atrial wall from anatomical variation: brilakis p.267 (14.6.2.1)
- Aortic puncture 0.05%; anterior puncture, difficult anatomy, inexperience; pressure line or injection to confirm; do not advance dilator and sheath in doubt; once confirmed do not pull back anything; call surgeon; if unstable rule out tamponade first; needle only may have no consequences; 'if the dilator and sheath have also been advanced, massive haemopericardium may occur' (taught as the risk of having advanced them, not of removal); occluders described: Russo. Usually well tolerated if transseptal sheath not advanced: brilakis p.267
- Air embolism if large devices not de-aired: Russo
- Thrombus: 1,000–2,000 IU heparin before TSP; full heparin ACT >250 s; aspiration especially before crossing; cerebral protection if in left heart; surgery for large thrombus: Russo
- ST elevation: Bezold–Jarisch-like reflex, inferior leads, vagal signs (hypotension, bradycardia, diaphoresis), 'probably caused by' parasympathetic fibres in septum leading to cholinergic vasospasm (hedge kept); recovery in minutes without troponin rise; atropine seldom necessary; persisting → air or thrombus: Russo
- Iatrogenic ASD common after large-bore sheaths; sheath size strongest predictor; closure for bidirectional or right-to-left shunt and large ASD (>8 mm); highly advisable after TMVI; immediate closure advisable; conservative: attention to right-to-left shunt and DVT risk, empiric anticoagulation: Russo (presented as an expert review)
- MITHRAS: 80 patients, relevant left-to-right iASD (Qp:Qs ≥1.3) at 30 days after TEER, closure vs conservative; death/HF hospitalisation 35% vs 50% at 12 months, not significant; iASD patients had more HF hospitalisation than those without: ref lurz-mithras-2021
- Complications table has six rows (posterior exit, aortic puncture, air, thrombus, vagal ST elevation, iASD): intro says six
- Learning the puncture: supervision by experienced operators during the learning curve; training including echocardiography and tamponade management: Russo (Conclusions). At least 10 TSPs in other procedures or guided by an expert before unproctored LAAO: Consensus ch 9
- Keypoint 5 split: needle will not cross a thick septum (RF, cautery, sharp nitinol wire, back of 0.014-inch wire; overshoot): Russo (Thick septum); sheath will not follow the dilator (J wire deep in PV or Inoue-shaped wire first, then stiffest wires or 4 mm balloon): Consensus ch 4
- TOE/ICE guidance and verifying LA access before sheath (prevention): brilakis p.267
- Checks: aortic trace (Russo, brilakis p.267); overshoot (Russo); vagal reflex (Russo)
- Keypoint ACT "at least 250 s (250–300 s)": Consensus ch 4/8; Russo

## Lesson hb-mitral-tricuspid-laa-teer-select

- Clinical evaluation (NYHA, HF admissions, hepatic and renal function, QoL questionnaires, 6-minute walk, CPET): Hausleiter
- TTE key; TOE required in almost all candidates; pharmacological or, better, physiological exercise echo might be useful for changes in RVol and pulmonary pressures at peak exercise, 'but this concept needs further investigation': Hausleiter
- 'Coronary angiography is recommended' (all candidates); CTCA alternative in selected low-likelihood, stable sinus rhythm; RHC 'should be considered' in reduced RV function, PH or severe TR: Hausleiter
- Heart Team composition; HF specialist for SMR; STS PROM and EuroSCORE II; palliative care if life expectancy <1 year; LVAD or HTx in advanced HF: Hausleiter
- Only a minority with relevant SMR respond to GDMT/CRT/rhythm restoration, with smaller MR reduction than M-TEER; inotropes and/or IABP proposed in severe LV dysfunction or large gaps but 'not part of daily clinical practice': Hausleiter (Treatment optimisation)
- Guideline classes as summarised by Hausleiter: PMR high/prohibitive risk IIb B (ESC), AHA/ACC IIa for selected NYHA III–IV; SMR meeting COAPT criteria IIa B (2021); beyond COAPT IIb C. 2025: PMR TEER IIa B; ventricular SMR meeting RCT criteria Class I, LOE A; IIb C maintained outside the criteria; atrial SMR surgery with AF ablation and LAAO (IIa B) preferred, TEER for high-risk: ref adamo-vhd-update-2026 (summarising esc-vhd-2025)
- COAPT figure footnotes: BNP ≥300 pg/mL or NT-proBNP ≥1,500 pg/mL; anatomy including MVA >4.0 cm²; exclusion SBP ≤90 mmHg, shock, inotropic and/or mechanical support: Hausleiter (Figure 5 legend; figure body is an image and not used). Full criteria: Academy sh-coapt-mitrafr (cross-reference)
- Predictors in SMR: NYHA III/IV; 28% higher risk of death/HF hospitalisation per NYHA class increase in COAPT at 2 years; LVEF; chronic AF; RV function and TR; RV–PA coupling index (EuroSMR, COAPT); COPD (benefit attenuated, interaction p = 0.04), CKD, PH: Hausleiter
- Risk scores: multimodality MitraScore associated with MR reduction at discharge; MITRALITY and a second MitraScore predict 1-year mortality; limited overlap: Hausleiter (named scores not repeated in the lesson)
- 2025 anatomical scale for PMR (ideal, suitable, challenging, impossible); impossible examples MVA <3.0 cm², relevant MS (mean gradient >5 mmHg), posterior leaflet <5 mm, calcification in grasping zone, leaflet perforation, rheumatic MS; ideal examples central pathology, no calcification, MVA >4.0 cm², posterior leaflet >10 mm: ref adamo-vhd-update-2026 (Table 2)
- GDMT and CRT optimisation; 42% improved; persistence of 2+/3+ almost 2-fold risk; goal MR 1+; sinus rhythm restoration may decrease SMR; euvolaemia; neurohormonal drugs maintained; GA vasodilatation and hypotension underestimate SMR: Hausleiter
- Severe MR table qualitative and semi-quantitative rows (colour jet >50% of LA or eccentric wall-impinging; flow convergence large throughout systole; CW holosystolic/dense/triangular; pulmonary vein systolic flow reversal; E wave dominant >1.2 m/s; TVI mitral/aortic >1.4): Hausleiter Table 1. 2025 row: SMR EROA ≥30 mm² and/or RVol ≥45 mL associated with adverse outcome; assess on optimised GDMT, euvolaemic and normotensive: ref adamo-vhd-update-2026
- Severe MR table (VC ≥7 mm, ≥8 biplane; EROA ≥40 mm², ≥30 mm² if elliptical in SMR; RVol ≥60 mL, ≥45 mL low flow in SMR; RF ≥50%; flail/ruptured papillary muscle; ESD ≥40 mm; LA ≥55 mm or ≥60 mL/m²; severe tenting, poor coaptation): Hausleiter Table 1 and text
- Worked example (LVEF 35%, LVEDV 200 mL, HR 75, BSA 2 m², SV 70 mL; RVol 45 → CI 0.9; RVol 60 → forward 10 mL, CO 0.8, CI 0.4); thresholds barely compatible with survival; multiparametric; 3D VC planimetry linked to prognosis; PISA underestimates semilunar orifice: Hausleiter Table 2 and text
- Large v waves in PCWP in MR, 'transmitted left ventricular pressure waves' that 'occur during the down-slope of the left ventricular pressure trace'; not specific (VSD, infiltrative disease): oxford p.135. Ventriculographic grades, catheter-induced MR and ectopy: oxford p.135, cross-referenced to hb-specific-conditions-mitral
- Guide reads LA pressure (clip system 4th generation, continuous LA pressure through guiding catheter): Hausleiter
- EVEREST criteria high likelihood of optimal result; outside them lower likelihood of durable repair: Hausleiter
