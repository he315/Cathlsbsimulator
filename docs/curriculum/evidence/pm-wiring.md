## Lesson pm-wiring-guide-choice

- Guide selection one of the most critical decisions for success, efficiency and safety: brilakis p.97 (5.1.1)
- Poor support makes simple PCI difficult; good back-up makes complex procedures straightforward; select on support, which depends on shape and calibre: oxford p.214
- Choice based on access site, target vessel and size of aorta: brilakis p.97 (5.1.2)
- Anticipate a larger guide (rotational atherectomy, complex two-stent) to avoid switching mid-PCI: oxford p.214
- 6 Fr diagnostic vs guide: ID 0.057 in (1.45 mm) vs 0.069–0.072 in in table 30.3, while table 30.4 and the text of 30.2.2 give 0.070–0.072 in (1.78–1.83 mm) by maker; the lesson now gives 0.070–0.072 in and notes the table's 0.069; OD 0.079 in (2 mm) both; layers 2 vs 3: brilakis p.518–519 (30.2.2, tables 30.3, 30.4). Thinner walls via metal mesh; diagnostic more torquable, less likely to kink; guide softer tip for longer engagement and deep seating with less dissection risk: brilakis p.518 (30.2.1)
- Guide layers: outer polyurethane (shape), middle woven mesh (stiffness), inner PTFE lining (Oxford's word is the trade name; the module uses PTFE only), atraumatic soft tip; 6 F may accommodate two balloon or stent catheters: oxford p.213
- Guides do not behave like diagnostic counterparts (thinner walls): oxford p.229
- Diagnostic catheter to engage and wire, exchange to guide over 300 cm supportive 0.014 in wire: brilakis p.518 (30.2.1), p.117–118 (5.7.3.3)
- Definition of support; friction-based retrograde force disengages guide; loss of visualisation and expulsion of equipment; tortuous/calcified anatomy needs more support: oxford p.214
- Support factors: 7–8 Fr > 5–6 Fr; EBU > JL4 (left); AL1 > JR4 (RCA); femoral > radial; long sheaths; left radial stronger for RCA, right radial stronger for left; forward push (prolapse risk); deep intubation increases support but may dissect, usually RCA or SVG, rarely left: brilakis p.175 (9.7.8.3.2.1.1–1.5). 8 F > 7 F > 6 F: oxford p.214. Extra-support shapes buttress against contralateral aortic wall: oxford p.213 (Fig 8.3 caption), p.214
- Damping common with ostial lesions and large guides such as 8 Fr: brilakis p.485 (28.1.1.1)
- Radial as the guideline default: ref esc-acs-2023 (radial standard approach in ACS unless procedural considerations, class I) and ref esc-ccs-2024 (radial preferred for invasive angiography in CCS, class I); wording matches module 05-access
- 7–8 F guide manipulation among causes of LMS dissection: oxford p.323
- Careful manipulation of aggressive shapes such as AL (dissection prevention): brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3)
- Guide extensions, anchor techniques, forward push, deep intubation as support measures: brilakis p.175 (taught in module "Lesion preparation and stenting")
- Check 1 (radial RCA): AL guides preferred for radial RCA PCI as JR guides often poor support: brilakis p.117 (5.7.2.2); radial PCI options AL1, Ikari Right, 3D Right, avoid JR: brilakis p.522 (30.2.6.1); AL strongest support, preferred for complex RCA (CTO): brilakis p.116
- Shape table: LM femoral PCI EBU/XB/Voda, EBU 3.75 and XB 3.5 most cases, EBU/XB/Voda 4.0 for Cx, EBU 3.5 for LAD, JL generally avoided (poor support); radial PCI EBU, XB, Ikari left: brilakis p.523 (30.2.6.2). EBU 3.0 or 3.5 for LAD, EBU 4.0 or 4.5 for Cx: brilakis p.115. Oxford support grading LAD JL3.5/4 +, AL2 ++, XB/EBU3.5 +++; Cx JL4/5 +, AL2 ++, Voda/XB/EBU3.5/4 +++; RCA JR4 + (more if deep-throated without trauma), AR1/2 ++, hockey stick/AL1/2 +++: oxford p.214 (table 8.4)
- RCA femoral PCI JR4, AL1 for complex, 3D Right: brilakis p.522; Williams right preshaped, little manipulation, aorto-ostial RCA: brilakis p.116
- IM grafts: JR4 engages most, IM larger distal bend; VB1 (modified IM) and Bartorelli-Cozzi larger bend, only BC described as designed for LIMA via left radial; no division by route: brilakis p.523 (30.2.6.3); most IM guides 90 cm: brilakis p.519 (30.2.3)
- Left-sided SVG: JR4 diagnostic; AL for PCI or radial, stronger support; MP and LCB less support: brilakis p.523 (30.2.6.4). Right-sided SVG: inferiorly directed take-off, JR4 or MP, RCB: brilakis p.524 (30.2.6.5)
- JR size = length of secondary curve 3.5/4/5 cm; JL size = length between primary and secondary curves 3.5/4/5/6 cm; size with aortic root; ascending aorta diameter + 1 = JL size (4 cm → JL5); dilated root rotates counterclockwise, RCA anterior, AR-1/2 or AL 1/2: brilakis p.522 (30.2.6)
- Engagement planning: prior CABG anatomy (not used), prior catheters, switch access site if difficult before, aortic CT for dilation and anomalies, AS/AR with dilated ascending aorta needing larger catheters: brilakis p.34 (1.5)
- Check 2: 5 cm aorta → JL6 by the +1 rule; larger roots need larger catheters (applies to guides): brilakis p.522
- Most PCI through 6 Fr; 7–8 Fr better support and visualisation; 2.0 mm burrs need 7 Fr; V-stenting and SKS need 7 Fr: brilakis p.518 (30.2.2)
- Outer diameters 5/6/7/8 Fr: 0.066 in (1.67 mm), 0.079 in (2.0 mm), 0.092 in (2.33 mm), 0.105 in (2.67 mm); inner diameters 6 Fr 0.070–0.072 in (1.78–1.83 mm), 7 Fr 0.081 in (2.06 mm), 8 Fr 0.090–0.091 in (2.29–2.31 mm): brilakis p.519 (table 30.4), p.518; compatibility app: brilakis p.519
- 6 F routine, 5 F radial not uncommon; improved handling and fewer access complications: oxford p.213
- Length: most 100 cm; 90 cm for retrograde CTO or distal targets via bypass grafts; 125 cm for very tall patients or very tortuous aortas: brilakis p.519 (30.2.3); shortening (other module): p.519–520
- Side holes: ostial RCA or ostial graft lesions; prevent damping, antegrade flow, lower hydraulic dissection risk; false security; hydraulic dissection still possible; damping desired in antegrade dissection-reentry; thoughtful active guide manipulation often chosen instead (operator preference); avoid in unprotected LM except ostial LM CTO because suboptimal position may go unrecognised, decreased LM flow, global ischaemia, haemodynamic collapse; higher contrast use, image degradation; hand-made holes (18–23 gauge needle or scalpel) may prevent guide extension advancement, weaken guide and cause kinking: brilakis p.520–522 (30.2.5); trapping balloon may rupture next to hand-made side holes: brilakis p.162
- Pitfalls: JL avoided (p.523), JR radial (p.522), curve too big or small causes failure to engage (p.118, 5.7.3.4), equipment-driven size (p.518), inner diameters vary (p.518–519), side holes in LM (p.522)
- Pearl: plan from the end of the case: oxford p.214 (anticipate larger calibre)

## Lesson pm-wiring-guide-seat

- Stable coaxial position particularly important for PCI (wording now 'particularly important', not 'most of all'): oxford p.229
- Optimal engagement: no damping; coaxial (orthogonal projections); 2–3 mm depth; deep engagement more support but dissection, damping, ischaemia: brilakis p.114 (5.7.1)
- Why coaxial matters (table row): non-coaxial catheter position causes coronary dissection (brilakis p.113, 5.6.4.3) and aorto-coronary dissection (p.114, 5.6.4.4); coaxial engagement avoids tenting that could lead to dissection (p.123, 6.2.1); wire difficulty leaving the guide tip from suboptimal engagement (p.155, 8.6.3.1)
- Preparation (aspirate 2–3 mL, connect while flushing, trace): brilakis p.108, p.110, p.111 (taught in pm-engaging)
- Femoral LM technique: J-wire looped over valve, tip 2–3 cm above bottom of right cusp at upward reflection of wire, wire withdrawn, tip falls into left cusp; counterclockwise rotation may be needed as LM lies posteriorly: brilakis p.114–115 (5.7.2.1)
- JR4: to bottom of right cusp, wire out, retract a few cm, clockwise rotation with back-and-forth movement: brilakis p.116; AL1: advanced until tip faces up, rotated to engage: brilakis p.116; radial universal/standard catheters: brilakis p.115, p.116
- Graft engagement: left-sided grafts moved up and down several cm (aortic cusp to brachiocephalic trunk origin) with gentle rotation after each movement; right-sided grafts in LAO, MP or RCB rotated to face the right side of the aorta then moved up and down; IM grafts: catheter over 0.035 in wire past the origin, withdrawn with counterclockwise rotation, preferably RAO; long sheaths for proximal tortuosity (kinking): brilakis p.117 (5.7.2.3–5.7.2.5)
- Step 8 pressure check after engagement: brilakis p.120 (5.8)
- Re-check guide engagement after balloon removal (aspirate, flush, may have disengaged or deeply engaged): brilakis p.183 (9.7.13.2); after stent balloon removal: brilakis p.191 (10.2.13.2)
- Check 1: three marks; forceful injection causes dissection: brilakis p.114, p.123 (6.2.3.1)
- Additional causes of damping after engagement: ostial lesions, small target vessel: brilakis p.120 (5.8.1, 5.8.2); general causes p.111–112 (5.6.3)
- Damping common with ostial lesions and large guides such as 8 Fr: brilakis p.485
- Solutions: keep catheter disengaged, 0.014 in wire into vessel, intermittent engagement to advance equipment or inject; smaller French or different shape less likely to deeply engage; manually reshape tip (ostial); smaller French (small vessel): brilakis p.120
- Damping algorithm when the trace does not recover (withdraw or reposition, aspirate, check transducer, check for kink, and only if all these fail remove the catheter while maintaining suction with a syringe, without injecting and without advancing a guidewire): brilakis p.112–113 (5.6.3.5, Fig 5.16); the guide-seat step and pci-guide-damped-work-outside now make failure of the steps (not failure to aspirate) the trigger and include removal under suction; do not insert a guidewire or flush if you cannot aspirate: brilakis p.109
- Disengage by torquing to a non-coaxial position rather than pulling back (loss of distal wire position): brilakis p.273 (15.1.5)
- Careful handling of aggressive shapes such as AL: brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3). The Amplatz lifting manoeuvre itself is not described in either manual; the module only cross-refers to the existing Academy diagnostic lesson 'Catheter anatomy and the main shapes' for it and makes no claim of its own about the mechanism
- Side holes limits: brilakis p.520–522; never inject with damping: brilakis p.113
- LMS dissection causes: guide manipulation (especially 7 F/8 F), contrast injection with guide pointing to LMS roof, wiring (polymer-covered and hydrophilic-coated wires), balloon, stent edges; incidence 0.02%; large myocardium at risk: oxford p.323
- LMS dissection management: conservative possible in low-degree dissection, stable, unimpaired flow; bifurcation involvement and stability decide (Table 10.1: spared → PCI or conservative; involved and stable → cardiac surgery or PCI two-stent; unstable → PCI, discuss surgery); femoral access ready for IABP/Impella; IVUS better than OCT to confirm true-lumen wire (contrast may expand false lumen): oxford p.323–324. Gentle catheter retraction, workhorse wire into true lumen, stent, intravascular imaging to ensure complete coverage of ostial dissection: brilakis p.124 (6.2.3.1)
- Iatrogenic type A aortic dissection; guide can tear intima near coronary ostia; incidence 0.01%; more benign than spontaneous (healthy wall), conservative first; CT after procedure and at 24 h (keypoint reads 'CT after the procedure and again at 24 hours', not 'at once'); stent the ostium to seal entry when it began at an ostium; surgery for propagation to neck vessels with neurological symptoms, new or worsening pericardial effusion, severe AR, haemodynamic instability: oxford p.325
- False hypotension: open haemostatic valve, open connection or air in line, damping, catheter obstruction by air/thrombus/contrast, bulky equipment: brilakis p.485 (28.1.1.1)
- Wire cannot be advanced through guide tip: cause suboptimal engagement; prevention coaxial, not too deep, no damping; solutions reposition guide, change wire tip shape, leave wire and use a second: brilakis p.155 (8.6.3.1)
- Wire into conus: withdraw guide and obtain coaxial engagement; wire into Cx instead of LAD: smaller guide; or leave wire in Cx stabilising guide and second wire to target: brilakis p.155 (8.6.3.2)
- Check 2: as above; deep intubation rarely in left coronary: brilakis p.175; polymer wires increase dissection/perforation risk: brilakis p.149–150
- Failed engagement five-step approach (Fig 5.20): brilakis p.118–119; diagnostic catheter + 300 cm supportive wire, 0.014 in wire to facilitate engagement (IMA grafts), larger or smaller curve or a guide extension when the curve size is suboptimal (not limited to a curve that is too small, p.118–119 5.7.3.4 and step 3), 6 Fr AL3 with 4 Fr diagnostic telescoped in very dilated root: brilakis p.117–118 (5.7.3.3–5.7.3.5)

## Lesson pm-wiring-wire-choice

- Goal: wire able to reach desired location while minimising complications: brilakis p.149 (8.2.1)
- Seven categories: workhorse, polymer-jacketed, stiff, support, atherectomy, externalization, pressure: brilakis p.560 (30.7)
- Workhorse: soft tip usually 1 g or less, no polymer jacket; reduce injury risk; non-workhorse exchanged before equipment delivery: brilakis p.561 (30.7.1)
- Hydrophilic coating (not a full jacket) increases deliverability and injury risk; most stainless steel; nitinol better tip shape retention, harder to shape; core-to-tip vs shaping ribbon (common in nitinol); multiple cores or dual coils: brilakis p.561
- Core-to-tip stiffer, enhanced push, torque, support; shaping ribbon floppier, less push and support, more flexible, less traumatic: oxford p.215
- Marker bands on some workhorse wires (lesion measurement): brilakis p.563–564 (table 30.7)
- Short wire 180–190 cm, long 300 cm: brilakis p.160 (8.9.2)
- Polymer-jacketed: tip fully covered by slippery polymer; tortuosity, CTO collaterals; distal perforation, subintimal tracking and dissection; only when necessary, exchange before balloons/stents: brilakis p.561 (30.7.2)
- Stiff tip for resistant CTO caps, moderate and strong penetration: brilakis p.561 (30.7.3)
- Support wires: strong body, soft tip, equipment delivery or guide exchange: brilakis p.561 (30.7.4); 300 cm versions: brilakis p.566 (table 30.9)
- Atherectomy wires dedicated: brilakis p.565 (30.7.5); externalization 330 and 350 cm: brilakis p.565 (30.7.6); pressure wires: brilakis p.566 (30.7.7)
- Ladder: noncomplex workhorse; tortuous option 1 advanced workhorse (composite core, dual core, nitinol), option 2 soft tip hydrophilic tip coating, option 3 soft nontapered polymer-jacketed (listed as options, not as an ordered sequence; the module now says so and q5 asks which is among the listed options); higher tip load + hydrophilic/polymer only if workhorse fails, then exchange for workhorse; monitor tip if used for delivery: brilakis p.149–150 (8.2.2 items 1–2)
- CTO: polymer-jacketed tapered tip initially, stiff penetrating often: brilakis p.150 (item 3)
- >1 wire may be needed (workhorse to reach, polymer to cross); if >1 anticipated, microcatheter recommended: brilakis p.150 (item 4, confirmed on PDF image)
- After crossing with polymer or stiff: change via microcatheter or OTW balloon to workhorse, highly supportive wire, or atherectomy wire; exchanges best with trapping: brilakis p.150 (item 5)
- Escalate–de-escalate, standard CTO technique also for non-CTO: brilakis p.154 (8.6.2.9)
- Oxford workhorse default (balance of stiffness/support and flexible tip with shaping ribbon): oxford p.215
- Workhorse wires preferred for both MV and SB: brilakis p.296 (16.2.8)
- Check 1: exchange via microcatheter before balloons/stents: brilakis p.150, p.160 (8.8.3 prevention: exchange immediately after crossing); polymer-jacketed, stiff or tapered tips increase perforation risk: brilakis p.164 (8.10.3.1)
- Three polymer-jacketed groups; soft nontapered for non-CTO, soft tapered and stiff for CTO: brilakis p.561; table: soft nontapered 0.8–2.1 g (Fielder FC 0.8, Sion Black 0.8, Whisper 0.8/1.0/1.2, Pilot 50 1.5, Choice PT 2.1) non-CTO wiring through tortuosity; stiff 1.7–4.1 g (PT Graphix 1.7, PT2 2.9, Pilot 150 2.7, Pilot 200 4.1, Gladius 3, Raider 4.0) non-CTO difficult-to-penetrate lesions: brilakis p.565 (table 30.8)
- Oxford categories (workhorse, stiff for tortuous/calcified support, floppy for re-crossing dissected lesion, hydrophilic for occluded vessels): oxford p.215
- Polymer/hydrophilic wiring among LMS dissection causes: oxford p.323; stiff wires straightening anatomy provoke spasm: oxford p.174
- Waved support wire: three deflections starting 6 cm from tip over next 6 cm, amplitude 3 mm; reduce side-wall bias, centre the wire; delivery through stents, tortuosity, calcification; not for primary wiring; exchange over microcatheter or OTW balloon; manual bends on a workhorse can mimic it: brilakis p.561 (30.7.4)
- Rotational atherectomy wire: direct wiring possible but practically challenging; microcatheter to exchange: oxford p.270
- Check 2: hydrophilic coating (brilakis p.561), nitinol (p.561), core-to-tip vs ribbon (oxford p.215), distal position monitoring (brilakis p.164, 8.10)

## Lesson pm-wiring-shape-load

- Steps 3–5 of wiring: brilakis p.150–153
- Preshaped wires save shaping but limit customisation: brilakis p.150; preformed J: oxford p.216
- Shape depends on vessel size and lesion/vessel angulation; big bends big vessels, small bends small vessels; distal bend smaller than target vessel diameter; different bends to reach vs cross (septal: large to enter, small to cross, Fig 8.1); two bends (proximal larger, distal smaller) improve reach in larger vessels and angulated ostia: brilakis p.150 (8.3.2)
- Technique A through introducer ('best performed' this way), small bends with accuracy, may fracture core especially nitinol; some nitinol wires to be shaped only with packaged needle: brilakis p.150. Technique B compress and stretch against introducer for large bends, may deform wire: brilakis p.150, p.151 (Fig 8.2 caption)
- Wire shaped with or without introducer needle: oxford p.216 (Fig 8.4 caption)
- Excessive bending causes (forceful manipulation; pulling from hoop by the tip); prevention (introducer; do not hold the tip when removing from hoop; small bend first, easy to add, hard to remove); treatment discard: brilakis p.150–151 (8.3.3)
- Check 1: distal bend smaller than vessel diameter, a general rule ('In general'), so the explanation and q6 say 'should be smaller', not 'must' (the rule only; no 'catches the wall' rationale is given), start small: brilakis p.150–151 (2.5 mm vessel is illustrative)
- Y-connector with haemostatic valve; automated valves minimise blood loss from back-bleeding, important with larger guides such as 8 Fr, easier to use than standard rotating valves: brilakis p.539–540 (30.5)
- Standard Y-connector opened then closed; automated valves no need to open: brilakis p.171 (9.7.6.2)
- Balloon deformation from forceful advancement through too tightly closed valve; cutting balloons higher risk; open valve or use automated: brilakis p.171 (9.7.6.3.1)
- Open haemostatic valve first cause of false hypotension: brilakis p.485
- Forceful aspiration may suck air through the Y-connector valve with wedged/blocked guide; remove all air before next injection: brilakis p.108 (5.4.3.2)
- Back-bleed the Y-connector before injection: brilakis p.168 (9.7.2.2); after trapping: brilakis p.162 (8.9.2.1 step 7)
- Nitroglycerin through manifold or directly through Y-connector using the introducer needle: brilakis p.124 (6.3)
- Manifold 3–5 ports: proximal to catheter, pressure via flush, contrast, syringe distal; zero at mid-chest open to air; flush carefully to avoid air: oxford p.97 (ch 4 Equipment)
- Loading: tip withdrawn into introducer; introducer inserted into valve; large bend may need back-loading; or preload in microcatheter/OTW balloon: brilakis p.151 (8.4.2); microcatheter bypasses introducer: brilakis p.149 (8.1.2)
- Tip deformation causes (not fully withdrawn, side arm, contact with guide extension, balloons, stents); prevention (no protrusion, no force, introducer all the way through, remove devices, fluoroscopy through extension collar); treatment exchange: brilakis p.151 (8.4.3)
- Advance to guide tip under fluoroscopy; markers at 90 and 100 cm; know guide length (90 vs 100 cm); side-hole guides, wire exiting side hole (Fig 8.3): brilakis p.152 (8.5.2)
- Inadvertent advancement into coronary, including during contrast or saline injections without fluoroscopy → dissection, perforation, loss of guide position; prevention: monitoring, proximal marker, torquer at ~90 cm; treatment: do not remove (sometimes in intended branch), contrast and redirect: brilakis p.152 (8.5.3.1)
- Tip deformation via guide extension, past balloons/stents, side holes; prevention: remove devices; else fluoroscopy without forcing; advance balloon into artery during wire advancement then retract; dual-lumen microcatheter (monorail lumen over first wire, new wire through OTW lumen) or aspiration catheter, removed by trapping; treatment reshape or discard: brilakis p.152–153 (8.5.3.2)
- Check 2: brilakis p.152

## Lesson pm-wiring-navigate

- Goal of wiring (efficient, no complication, platform for equipment): brilakis p.149
- Planning: prior challenges wiring target lesion: brilakis p.35 (1.8)
- Fluoroscopy with intermittent contrast; still frame reference; dynamic roadmap: brilakis p.153 (8.6.2); small amount of contrast usually suffices; orthogonal projections esp CTO, tortuous, overlapping side branches: brilakis p.154 (8.6.2.6); reference image or dynamic roadmap distal advancement: brilakis p.159 (8.8.2)
- Continuous fluoroscopy entering the vessel to see side branches and buckling: oxford p.216
- Views: caudal to enter LAD or Cx from LM; LAD RAO cranial or AP cranial; Cx/OM RAO caudal or LAO caudal; RCA LAO to distal, LAO or AP cranial into PDA or posterolateral: brilakis p.153 (8.6.2.1)
- Torquer optional, preference and experience: brilakis p.153 (8.6.2.2); push/withdraw and rotation: p.153 (8.6.2.3); underhand preferred by authors; complex: left hand push/withdraw, right hand rotate: p.153 (8.6.2.4)
- Continuous tip movement with repeated short rotations so tip does not lodge in lesion or wall: oxford p.216
- Keep tip free; under plaque → dissection and tip entrapment; withdraw and readvance or parallel wiring: brilakis p.153–154 (8.6.2.5)
- CTO: contralateral (or bypass graft) injection often required to see the wire position: brilakis p.154 (8.6.2.6)
- Knuckle: effective and safe, especially after crossing the area of stenosis; travels through tortuosity without entering side branches; less likely to perforate but larger perforation; keep small and within radiopaque portion; avoid with highly supportive wires: brilakis p.154 (8.6.2.7)
- Change damaged tip, ideally via microcatheter and trapping: brilakis p.154 (8.6.2.8)
- Escalate–de-escalate: brilakis p.154 (8.6.2.9)
- Multiple wires organised: towel (superior branch on top), colours, bend back end, torquer on one: brilakis p.154–155 (8.6.2.10, Fig 8.4)
- Check 1: brilakis p.153–154; pull back and rotate slightly when tip deflection observed: brilakis p.159 (8.7.4); forceful injection and dissection: brilakis p.123. The unsourced 'spinning traps the tip' rationale was removed.
- Failure to cross: treatment list is unordered (change tip shape; different wire; microcatheter or OTW balloon): brilakis p.158 (8.7.3)
- Crossing under fluoroscopy and intermittent injections: brilakis p.158 (8.7.2). Causes, prevention, treatment lists for failure to cross: brilakis p.158 (8.7.3)
- Dissection while wiring: causes (severe/ulcerated lesions, stiff or polymer wires, aggressive manipulation), prevention, solutions (another wire into distal true lumen then stent; CTO techniques such as re-entry balloon need expertise): brilakis p.158 (8.6.3.4)
- Acute closure: more likely in very stenotic lesions, most commonly dissection; pseudolesion in tortuous vessels may mimic it; prevention: avoid forceful advancement (pull back and rotate slightly on deflection), cautious use of polymer-jacketed wires, partial withdrawal so the soft part is just distal to the lesion to clarify pseudolesions; confirm distal true lumen before balloon/stent; IVUS; avoid distal microcatheter injections if subintimal; same or different wire; re-entry system or penetrating wires: brilakis p.159 (8.7.4)

## Lesson pm-wiring-microcatheters

- Microcatheters usually 1.8 to 3.2 Fr: brilakis p.540 (30.6); all coronary microcatheters 0.014 in: brilakis p.542
- Step 1 of wiring: determine whether a microcatheter is needed; facilitates advancement and exchanges; consider in complex lesions (highly tortuous, near-occlusive, CTO); insert wire with microcatheter, bypassing introducer; five categories: brilakis p.149 (8.1)
- Routine in CTO; useful in tortuosity, angulation, severe calcification; bifurcation rewiring through stent struts: brilakis p.541–542
- Microcatheter for exchanging to rotational atherectomy wire: oxford p.270; >1 wire anticipated: brilakis p.150; damaged tip change via microcatheter and trapping: brilakis p.154; SB wiring: brilakis p.296
- OTW balloon alternative: stiffer, more likely to kink, marker in middle of small balloons, cheaper: brilakis p.149 (8.1.2); microcatheter vs OTW: tip marker vs mid marker in balloons ≤1.5 mm, more flexible, track better, less kinking (kink prohibits exchanges, replacement of gear), OTW balloons better support than many and cheaper: brilakis p.540–541 (30.6)
- OTW balloons add support to prevent wire tip deflection in CTO: oxford p.263
- Roles: support and tip stiffness; reshaping; exchanges; protect proximal vessel and collateral; inject medications or contrast; deliver coils, fat, thrombin; 0.014 in microcatheters cannot deliver 0.018 in coils except one; larger 2.4–2.5 Fr microcatheters; stock 0.014-compatible coils: brilakis p.541–542
- Flush after contrast to avoid stickiness; rarely wire stuck, remove both: brilakis p.558 (30.6.6)
- Large: CTO support, can be spun, avoid overspinning: brilakis p.542 (30.6.1)
- Small: small tortuous vessels, retrograde epicardial collaterals; coil versions can be spun; non-coil versions not designed to be spun: brilakis p.548 (30.6.2); one not designed for aggressive rotation and advancement → tip fracture: brilakis p.550
- Angulated: deflectable tip up to 90°, 2.5 mm bend radius, 8 mm tip, 6 Fr compatible, rapid-exchange and OTW versions with OTW for CTO PCI (wire exchanges): brilakis p.551; care in <2.5 mm arteries; straighten during advancement or removal; stiff, extra support but may predispose to vessel injury; trapping and parallel wiring require 8 Fr; prevents prolapse into side branch; difficult side branches: brilakis p.551–552; fixed angles 45, 90, 120 degrees: brilakis p.553; straight-tip design articulating up to 180° in opposing directions: brilakis p.553
- Dual lumen uses (second wire, SB including jailed, reversed wire; wire distal vessel when crossing into a side branch distal to the lesion, CTO or non-occlusive); tips (difficult delivery, stiffening mandrel, side port direction, trapping balloon for removal): brilakis p.558 (30.6.4)
- Plaque modification: modify lesion, usually balloon uncrossable (Fig 30.45 caption p.545); stranded steel type advanced counterclockwise, withdrawn clockwise, ≤20 rotations per direction, no contrast injection; others advanced clockwise: brilakis p.558 (30.6.5)
- Complications: overrotation → deformation, entrapment, fracture proximal to tip, wire binding (fatigue); fatigue → exchange; interlocking in retrograde CTO: brilakis p.558 (30.6.6)
- Check 1: dual-lumen use for distal vessel after crossing into side branch: brilakis p.558; deflection balloon definition: brilakis p.158
- Four removal techniques with short (180–190 cm) wire; trapping preferred, also with 300 cm wires; minimises wire movement and radiation: brilakis p.160 (8.9.2)
- 6 Fr cannot trap OTW balloons, CrossBoss, Stingray; 6 Fr 0.071 in ID allows low-profile microcatheters: brilakis p.160
- Guide extension with built-in trapping balloon 6–8 Fr, less fluoroscopy and equipment, best with 135 cm microcatheters, 150 cm often too long: brilakis p.160
- Trapping is now given in outline with a cross-reference to module 07-engaging, which teaches the full sequence. Dedicated trapping balloons have smaller profiles and can trap in smaller guides: brilakis p.160. Multiple devices more likely to fail, resulting in loss of wire position: brilakis p.161
- Trapping steps: withdraw microcatheter into guide just proximal; balloon next to (not over) wire; stylet in balloon tip assists through valve, then removed; monorail preferred to OTW; dedicated trapping balloons; 2.5 mm for 6 or 7 Fr, 3.0 mm for 7 and 8 Fr; ≥20 mm, especially polymer wires; deformed reused balloon → new or stylet; multiple devices more likely to fail: brilakis p.160–161; advance beyond distal marker of microcatheter, at or near primary curve; short guides → markers unreliable: brilakis p.161; 15–20 atm, guide pressure damped; rupture next to handmade side holes; withdraw (fluoroscopy initially only; polymer wires slip with aggressive withdrawal); deflate, remove; back-bleed (air entrained, embolisation), alternative continuous saline flushing; anchor balloon ≥2.5 mm in 6 or 7 Fr and ≥3.0 mm in 8 Fr can be withdrawn for trapping: brilakis p.162
