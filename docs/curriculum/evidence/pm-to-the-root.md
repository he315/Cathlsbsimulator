## Lesson pm-to-the-root-table

- Everything passes the table; embolism prevention at each connection: module framing from brilakis p.110 (5.5), p.444 (25.2.3.3)
- Coronary air embolism ~0.4% of coronary angiography procedures; training dependent: oxford p.327
- Systemic embolisation, especially stroke, one of the most feared complications: brilakis p.110 (5.5.3.1)
- Manifold: 3–5 selectable ports; proximal to catheter; one to pressure via flush infusion; one to contrast; syringe on distal end; zero by opening distal port to air at mid-chest; flush carefully to avoid air that may embolise; assistant may set up, check carefully before injection: oxford p.97
- Catheters have a Luer-lock connector for attaching to manifold (generic spelling; Oxford prints the trade name): oxford p.103
- Before the first puncture: venous access; good-quality continuous ECG; pulse oximetry, SaO2 >96% on room air; supplemental oxygen for high-risk or heavily sedated patients; lead protection and sterile scrub suit; aseptic preparation and draping per local nursing procedure; sterile trolley holds all necessary equipment: oxford p.43
- Flush: per hospital protocol; heparinised saline, e.g. heparin 1000 IU into 500 mL 0.9% saline: oxford p.97
- V4 height for transducer (physiologist section): oxford p.60; monitoring module covers choice
- Checklist: manifold adequately flushed, contrast run through without air bubbles; transducer calibrated; check sterile trolley: oxford p.43
- Poor manifold (or automated injector) preparation and poor connections as causes of air embolism; prevention list (meticulous manifold/injector preparation, clearing manifold after advancement through aorta, back-bleed after trapping, prepare balloons and large devices free of air): brilakis p.444
- Check 1 reasoning (contrast from port goes through syringe into catheter): oxford p.97 port layout
- Wire table: standard J 0.035/0.038 used in most procedures, for access and advancing catheters to ostium: brilakis p.566; J tip used typically for advancing catheters: brilakis p.97; usual diagnostic wires 0.03–0.038 inch J-tipped: oxford p.97; J less likely to enter side branches, straight tips not used for advancing catheters: brilakis p.100
- Short wires 30–45 cm for access; long exchange wires 250–300 cm: oxford p.97; short J for sheath insertion when single operator: brilakis p.97
- Keep exchange wire tip in aortic root: brilakis p.100
- Baby-J 1.5 mm vs 3 mm J radius, designed for radial: brilakis p.566; smaller calibres (0.025 inch) for arm access: oxford p.97
- Hydrophilic: useful in tortuous or narrow vessels, can be exchanged for conventional wire, slightly more fragile, not manipulated through introducer needle: oxford p.97; polymer coating may be sheared off when retracting through needle: brilakis p.98; polymer-jacketed may be more likely to create subintimal dissection: brilakis p.566
- Soft-tip 0.035 (Bentson, Wholey) for diseased tortuous peripheral vessels; stiff-body for sheath insertion and equipment advancement through calcification/tortuosity, soft tips, inserted over a diagnostic catheter: brilakis p.566
- Sheath: aspirate 3–4 mL from side arm and discard, flush with different syringe of heparinised saline: brilakis p.79 (4.2.14), p.91 (4.3.9); thrombus forms in sheath and is scooped up by catheter: brilakis p.110 (5.4.3.4 step 4); aspirate sheath before inserting guide, then flush with clear heparinised saline: brilakis p.108 (5.4.3.2); sidearm flushed with heparinised saline to prevent thrombus: oxford p.75; poor sheath management as cause of limb ischaemia: brilakis p.497
- Radial sheath dipped in saline to activate hydrophilic coating: brilakis p.90; radial step 4.3.9 is titled 'sheath aspiration and flushing' but its listed actions are aspiration of 3–4 mL and discard (p.91), then vasodilators (p.92); the separate-syringe flush is from the femoral section 4.2.14 (p.79) and before a guide (5.4.3.2, p.108), as the lesson now says; IV heparin: brilakis p.92; intra-arterial cocktail with heparin: oxford p.71
- Diagnostic catheters flushed and loaded over 0.035/0.038 wire before insertion; guides connected to Y-connector outside body and flushed; haemostatic valve prevents bleeding, severe with 7 Fr and even more 8 Fr: brilakis p.103 (5.3.2.1)
- Catheters 4–8 F, typically 6 F, most 100 cm: oxford p.103
- Pressure injector: expel air from reservoir, check catheter tightly secured (disarticulation), injector operator aspirates for bubbles, watch image and stop if displacement/staining: oxford p.97
- Organised table, wipe gloves and equipment, flush catheters with heparinised saline; dried blood and contrast sticky; debris into manifold can embolise: brilakis p.39 (2.11)
- No air in syringe or tubing before injecting: oxford p.159
- Remaining air embolism causes: pressure damping from ostial disease or tip against aortic wall; large devices entraining air into the guide; trapping without back-bleeding; rupture of a poorly prepared balloon containing air; large air embolisation can lead to cardiac arrest or death: brilakis p.444 (25.2.3.3)
- Keypoint 6 reworded (round 1) to avoid a 7-word run from brilakis p.103
- Check 3: prevention item "meticulous clearing of the manifold after advancement through the aorta"; oxygen as treatment: brilakis p.444
- Check 2: brilakis p.98, oxford p.97

## Lesson pm-to-the-root-choice

- Catheter selection is step 1; goal: engage target most easily and safely, coaxially, optimal support; guide selection one of the most critical decisions for success, efficiency, safety; based on access site, target vessel, size of aorta: brilakis p.97 (5.1)
- Aortic stenosis or regurgitation associated with dilated ascending aorta that may need larger catheters (step 'Know the aortic valve' and dilated-root bullet): brilakis p.34 (1.5)
- Thoughtful selection by aortic size and ostial size/location as prevention of failed aspiration and damping: brilakis p.108 (5.4.3.1), p.111 (5.6.3.1)
- Check catheters used before and problems; document what you use: oxford p.103 (box), p.153 (box); significant difficulty from one access site → switch access site; aortic CT for dilation and anomalies; prior CABG anatomy from surgical report and angiograms: brilakis p.34 (1.5)
- Table 30.3: 6 Fr diagnostic ID 0.057 in (1.45 mm), guide 0.069–0.072 in (1.78 mm), OD 0.079 in (2 mm) both, 2 vs 3 layers: brilakis p.519; guide thinner walls with metal mesh preventing collapse, diagnostic thicker walls more torquable and less likely to kink, guide softer tips allow longer engagement and deep seating while limiting dissection: brilakis p.518 (30.2.1; 'Handling' row and q10 reworded in round 1 to avoid a verbatim 7-word run); roles of catheters (engage, monitor pressure, deliver equipment): brilakis p.518
- 1 Fr = 0.33 mm; 6 Fr = 2.0 mm; most PCIs through 6 Fr; 7–8 Fr better support and visualisation; 2.0 mm rotablator burr requires 7 Fr: brilakis p.518 (30.2.2)
- Diagnostic 4–6 F; smaller need more force, autoinjectors help, smaller sheaths/punctures, earlier mobilisation, fewer vascular complications; floppier, reduced torque: oxford p.153
- 4 Fr sheath often needs automated injector: brilakis p.515
- Sheath French size refers to the catheter it accepts; outer diameter about that of a guide two sizes bigger (6 Fr sheath 2.62 mm vs 8 Fr guide 2.67 mm), the advantage of sheathless guides (round 2 wording: a sheathless guide lets a larger guide pass through the same size of puncture, the module's reading of 'potential advantages of sheathless guide catheter insertion'); larger sheaths occupy more of the artery, higher likelihood of limb ischaemia and arterial thrombosis; 4–6 Fr suffice for diagnostics, 5–8 Fr for PCI, 7–8 Fr preferred for complex procedures: brilakis p.515 (30.1.1.1). 'Use the smallest sheath the procedure allows' is the module's inference from the complication statement
- 6 F sheaths typical; 4 or 5 F for diagnostic angiography; 7 or 8 F for complex intervention: oxford p.75
- Diagnostic engages and wires, then exchange for guide over 300 cm 0.014 wire: brilakis p.518
- JR size = secondary curve length 3.5/4/5 cm; JL size = primary-to-secondary distance 3.5/4/5/6 cm: brilakis p.522; JL 90° primary, 180° secondary, various arm lengths: oxford p.153
- Larger root → larger catheter; dilated ascending aorta diameter + 1 = JL size (4 cm → JL5); root dilates and rotates counterclockwise, RCA anterior, AR1/2 or AL1/2: brilakis p.522
- Check 1 (tall patient, revised in round 1: root size unknown, no CT; best answer JL4 first with JL5/JL6 to hand): JL4 routine femoral or left radial; JL3.5 right radial, shorter patients, small roots, superior LMS, separate LAD; longer arm JL5 or JL6 'can be helpful' for dilated root or very tall: oxford p.153; Brilakis sizes JL to the aortic root (larger root, larger catheter; diameter + 1): brilakis p.522
- Route table: brilakis p.522–523 (30.2.6.1, 30.2.6.2: femoral LM diag JL4; PCI EBU, XB, Voda; EBU 3.75 and XB 3.5 most cases; 4.0 for Cx; EBU 3.5 for LAD; JL guides avoided for PCI; radial LM diag universal TIG, Kimny, TRA; PCI EBU, XB, Ikari left; RCA femoral diag JR4, Williams, Hockeystick or IM for shepherd's crook (Fig 30.12 p.524); PCI JR4, AL1 complex, 3D Right; radial RCA diag universal, JR4; PCI AL1, Ikari Right, 3D Right; JR guides avoided, poor support); oxford p.153 (JR4 standard femoral or radial; Williams 3DRC and AR MOD alternatives; AL for dilated sinuses or unusual ostia); oxford p.156 (shepherd's crook: AR MOD, 3DRC or IMA)
- Universal radial catheters: engage both, avoid exchange, may reduce spasm and fluoroscopy time; TIG side hole just distal to tip to minimise damping; if TIG fails, standard Judkins: oxford p.157 (generic curve name TIG used, not the trade name); side holes can mask damping or ventricularisation at the tip (side-hole catheters in general): brilakis p.111 (5.6.2)
- Check 2: brilakis p.522
- Right radial enters from right brachiocephalic; vein grafts challenging; LIMA from right radial technically challenging, may injure ostium: oxford p.67
- Left radial: less subclavian tortuosity, slightly shorter fluoroscopy times, can be more challenging and less comfortable; should be used with patent LIMA grafts: brilakis p.82 (4.3.2.2); femoral usually preferred after CABG with patent grafts, left radial if radial chosen: brilakis p.61 (4.1 step 2)
- Keypoint 5 (JL guides) now attributed: 'generally avoided for PCI, as they provide poor support': brilakis p.523 (30.2.6.2)
- Complex PCI: at least one femoral, 7 or 8 Fr, ideally 45 cm: brilakis p.61 (4.1 step 4)
- Most catheter shapes designed for femoral route: oxford p.76
- Grafts: JR4 engages most IM grafts; IM catheter bigger distal bend; modified IM (VB1) and BC larger: brilakis p.523; left-sided grafts JR4 diag, AL for PCI or radial, MP and LCB less support: brilakis p.523; right-sided grafts inferior take-off, JR4 or MP, RCB: brilakis p.524; femoral usually preferred with patent grafts; left radial for LIMA; bilateral IM → femoral strongly favoured: brilakis p.61 (4.1 step 2); Oxford femoral or left radial with LIMA; RIMA selective engagement may only be possible from right radial: oxford p.67; JR4 for SVGs, often JL4 and JR4 suffice, MPA1/MPA2 for acute-angle RCA grafts, LCB and RCB, AL for left-sided SVGs: oxford p.189
- LV and aorta: pigtail from femoral; multipurpose occasionally from arm: oxford p.107
- Check 3: brilakis p.61, p.523; oxford p.67
- Tips: diag-dilated-root-sizing (existing, pm-engaging); new tips cite oxford p.153, brilakis p.522, p.34

## Lesson pm-to-the-root-advance

- Steps 2–5 of the engagement chapter: brilakis p.97–110
- Catheter advancement always over 0.035/0.038 wire for efficiency and safety (reduces arterial injury): brilakis p.97 (5.2.1)
- J-tip wire; femoral: wire through needle (or micropuncture microcatheter) inserts sheath and delivers catheter; radial: narrow J (Baby-J): brilakis p.97 (5.2.3)
- Wire advanced together with catheter, tip always ahead, under fluoroscopy, to aortic cusps; fixed usually by assistant; catheter advanced over it: brilakis p.97
- Radial: guide wire always into aortic root before catheters through brachial and subclavian: oxford p.71
- Resistance: do not force: brilakis p.103 (5.3.2.2)
- Wire in LV causes ventricular arrhythmias; monitor and adjust tip; remove from LV: brilakis p.107 (5.3.5.3)
- Tip in root; withdraw wire gently under screening; wire withdrawal may immediately engage LMS; watch trace for damping: oxford p.155
- Check 1: brilakis p.107 (cause: wire in LV; prevention: monitor and adjust tip; solution: remove wire from LV; no drug treatment mentioned, so the explanation now says only that a drug does not treat the cause); catheter without leading wire causes dissection: brilakis p.101 (5.2.5.1)
- Keeley: 1,000 consecutive PCIs, visible debris >50%; JL 65%, Multi 60%; JR least (24%); large debris Multi, JL, VL; meticulous attention to allow debris to exit back of catheter; debris not associated with in-hospital ischaemic complications; aortic atherosclerosis a potential source of systemic embolism: ref keeley-aortic-debris-1998 (PubMed abstract, PMID 9857864); same caveat in q3 explanation
- Aspirate 2–3 mL, discard over white gauze; thrombus usually from sheath; plaque iridescent from cholesterol crystals: brilakis p.108
- Large guides: allow back-bleeding, aortic debris scraped in; improperly cleared guide → catastrophic injection into coronary or aorta: brilakis p.103
- Vacuum sucks catheter onto aortic wall or into ostial stenosis; back-bleed instead: brilakis p.108
- Cannot aspirate: tip against aortic wall or engaged; gently pull back until blood flows; contrast without this risks air embolism: oxford p.155, p.156; kink screen, removal under suction without wire, aspirate sheath: brilakis p.109–110 (5.4.3.4)
- Other causes of failed aspiration: tip next to a significant ostial lesion; catheter too large for the artery; solutions: reposition, usually by slight withdrawal; different catheter or manual reshaping of the tip: brilakis p.108 (5.4.3.1)
- Returning pressure waveform shows aspiration is feasible; do not inject or flush with dampened or missing waveform (embolisation and/or dissection): brilakis p.109 (5.4.3.4 step 1)
- Forceful aspiration may suck air through the Y-connector valve when the guide is wedged or blocked; remove ALL air before next injection (new step and pitfall reason): brilakis p.108 (5.4.3.2)
- Prevention of plaque/thrombus in catheter: aspirate sheath and flush with another syringe before a guide; long femoral sheaths in severe iliac and aortic disease; flush catheters with normal saline before insertion: brilakis p.108 (5.4.3.2)
- Connect while manifold flushing; stop flush; aspirate into manifold syringe; discard air/debris; contrast until it exits tip: brilakis p.110 (5.5.2); pressure waveform is step 6: brilakis p.111
- Check 2: iridescent plaque p.108; kink signs (tip not moving with hub, waveform lost): brilakis p.109
- Systemic embolism causes (failure to aspirate or observe back-bleeding; plaque dislodgement in aortic atherosclerosis; air, plaque, thrombus); diagnosis (focal weakness, vision loss; leg pulses decrease, discomfort); treatment (emergency head CT, neurology, many may benefit from emergent neurointervention; other sites usually conservative, sometimes endovascular): brilakis p.110
- Coronary embolism: ST elevation usually first sign; shock or arrest depending on size and vessel: brilakis p.110–111
- Complications table header now 'Brilakis 5.5.3; dissection 5.2.5.1'
- Stroke row: Brilakis's emergency head CT and neurology evaluation kept and attributed (p.110); vascular imaging (CT angiography) with non-contrast CT to select for mechanical thrombectomy, immediate stroke team, heparin as a thrombolysis consideration: ref powers-acute-stroke-2019
- Peripheral dissection causes: brilakis p.101
- Prevention list incl. sheathless guide with inner dilator preventing razor effect: brilakis p.110
- AAA: exchange over a guide wire to prevent dislodging thrombus or intimal disruption: oxford p.68

## Lesson pm-to-the-root-radial

- Sites: radial loop (Fig 5.3 p.99), brachial loop (Fig 5.4 p.100), subclavian tortuosity (Fig 5.5 p.100), small and high-origin radial (p.105), stenosis (p.103), spasm (p.105–106), arteria lusoria (p.109; p.34)
- Pushing can cause spasm, dissection or perforation (brilakis p.88, 4.3.6.3 'NEVER push hard'), avulsion of side branches by catheter advancement and forearm bleeding (brilakis p.506, 29.2.1.2); round 1 wording: side branches, not the radial artery, are avulsed
- Wire algorithm steps 1–4 incl. - Needle-level fixes (backflow, wrist extension, needle angle, bevel rotation); higher forearm: fluoroscopy, diagnostic catheter angiography: brilakis p.88, p.90 (4.3.6.3)
- Catheter algorithm step 1 femoral half: lack of waveform may suggest subintimal sheath, may be best to change access site: brilakis p.106 (5.3.3.5); Fig 5.12 p.107 labels step 1 'Transduce pressure from the sheath' (noted in the lesson)
- Stiff-body wires (check explanation, femoral check, radial case s3 feedback): stiff body 0.035 wires for sheath insertion and equipment advancement, soft tips, inserted over a diagnostic catheter: brilakis p.566 (30.7.8); Glidewire Advantage (polymer-jacketed tip, stiff nonjacketed body) among femoral tortuosity wires: brilakis p.99, p.566; stiff wire straightens iliac (parallel sheath) and aorta (failure to reach root): brilakis p.104, p.106
- Catheter algorithm (transduce from tip; spasm → vasodilators; angiography: lesion, spasm, tortuosity, complication perforation/dissection; treatment by cause; change access): brilakis p.106 (5.3.3.5), Fig 5.12 p.107; lesion solutions incl. low-profile sheath or sheathless guide gently dilating: brilakis p.103 (5.3.3.2); tortuosity solutions incl. less distal angulation, long sheath, BAT particularly for radial loops: brilakis p.104 (5.3.3.3)
- Razor effect: brilakis p.104 (Fig 5.9), p.110
- Radial loops crossed with 0.035 Glidewire (Fig 5.3 p.99) and with coronary wire then sheath over coronary wire; pseudolesions from straightening; 5F Judkins after vasodilators; PCI not possible, femoral crossover (Fig 4.40): brilakis p.90
- BAT: small compliant balloon 1:1 with guide, half in/half out, 3–6 atm, lower more flexible, higher more pushable, prevents digging in: brilakis p.104 (Fig 5.9, confirmed on image). Wire-across step is the module's description of the figure (a coronary balloon runs on a coronary wire)
- Patel: 63 of 8,245 (0.76%) with complex radial, brachial or subclavian anatomy; technical failure 3 (4.8%), two very small RA, one 360° loop: ref patel-balloon-assisted-tracking-2014 (PubMed abstract)
- BAT across loops to prevent bleeding; never force; confirm wire position on X-ray before sheath: brilakis p.506 (29.2.1.3)
- Check (BAT set-up); trapping at 15–20 atm is a different technique: brilakis p.162 (trapping, pm-engaging evidence)
- Deep breath as wire advanced out of subclavian if persistently in descending aorta: oxford p.71
- Advance catheter close to wire tip; 300 cm 0.035/0.038 for exchanges with tip in root: brilakis p.100. Long sheath can greatly ease manipulation (taught in the arm only with the radial sizing caveat; the ≥1 Fr-larger long sheath is not taught for the arm since round 1): brilakis p.100, p.515; avoid large sheaths relative to radial artery size: brilakis p.506 (29.2.1.3)
- Leaving a 0.035 wire inside facilitates manipulation (kink prevention): brilakis p.112 (5.6.3.3)
- Extreme subclavian tortuosity or arteria lusoria: change from right radial to left radial or femoral (kink prevention): brilakis p.109 (5.4.3.3); CT arteria lusoria favours left radial or femoral: brilakis p.34 (1.4)
- STEMI, delay via radial e.g. arteria lusoria → prompt conversion to femoral, time = muscle: brilakis p.378 (20.4)
- Long radial sheaths usually avoided (occlusion, spasm); sheath size by radial artery size (ultrasound) and planned procedure; 4 Fr sheaths for diagnostic angiography or thin-walled sheaths preferred: brilakis p.90 (4.3.8.2); long 16–25 cm radial sheath partly inserted for left radial: brilakis p.82 (4.3.2.2), p.91 (4.3.8.2 item 5); 16 or 25 cm sheaths especially for left radial: brilakis p.518 (30.1.3). Paragraph renamed 'read the whole book'
- Spasm causes and solutions list: brilakis p.105–106 (5.3.3.4); prevention (avoid small radials, use ulnar or femoral, hence ultrasound guidance; vasodilator after sheath insertion; avoid large catheters especially in small radials; adequate conscious sedation before access): brilakis p.106; spasm usually prevented by mild sedation: oxford p.73
- IV heparin with the goal of preventing radial artery occlusion; heparin may be delayed until catheter reaches root in some patients with extreme radial or subclavian tortuosity: brilakis p.92 (round 1: taught as an option, with heparin then given once the root is reached; pitfall and keypoint reworded); round 2: the tip diag-delay-heparin-tortuous-arm (defined in 03-antithrombotics.ts) was removed from the radial lesson and from the lab request because its title and text still state the delay as a rule and never say to give the heparin at the root; a replacement has been returned as an academyFix and the tip can be re-embedded once it lands; radial-to-femoral crossover after anticoagulation bleeds more: brilakis p.61 (4.1 step 5)

- Check STEMI: brilakis p.378, p.61
- Perforation causes (forceful advancement into side branch, small caliber radial, sheath past wire tip); angiography when unexpected course or resistance; radial: catheter across perforated segment usually suffices: brilakis p.102 (5.2.5.2); maintain wire position in rupture: brilakis p.506 (29.2.1.4)
- Forearm bleeding causes: brilakis p.506 (29.2.1.2)
- Change access site when wire or catheter fails to reach root: brilakis p.61 (4.1 step 5)

## Lesson pm-to-the-root-femoral

- Opening (round 1): 'Spasm is a radial problem in Brilakis's algorithms' (spasm appears only for radial access in brilakis p.98 step 1, p.105–106, p.106 step 1); no claim that femoral arteries never spasm
- Spasm specific to radial access: brilakis p.105; femoral causes list: brilakis p.98–101, p.103–104
- Iliac perforation → retroperitoneal haematoma: brilakis p.102
- Weaker pulse suggests occlusion: brilakis p.99 (5.2.4.2.2)
- Planning checklist (peripheral arterial disease, access issues, groin scar, kissing iliac stents or stenosis, need for long sheath; review prior films for aorto-iliac disease/tortuosity): brilakis p.32 (1.1), p.34 (1.4)
- Avoid access through severely diseased or occluded iliofemoral or subclavian arteries (history of PAD); chest CT: anomalous arch, size and disease of iliac/subclavian vessels; abdomen/pelvis CT: common femoral bifurcation and iliofemoral disease; peripheral ultrasound; desired outcome: decide access site and sheath size/length: brilakis p.34 (1.4)
- PVD not a contraindication; if neither femoral pulse palpable, radial; axillofemoral graft → aorta may be occluded; puncturing Dacron (written generically as polyester (prosthetic)) grafts may cause bleeding, generally not recommended: oxford p.67
