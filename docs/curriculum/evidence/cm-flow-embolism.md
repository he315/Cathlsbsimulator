# Evidence: cm-flow-embolism (No-reflow, embolism and stroke)

Module file: `src/content/manual/complication-management/02-flow-embolism.ts`. Page numbers are PDF pages (Brilakis PDF page as in the extracts; Oxford PDF page = printed page + 21).

New references (each checked on PubMed: authors, title, journal, year, volume, issue, pages; figures used are all in the PubMed abstract):
- fukumoto-ces-2003: PMID 12875753, J Am Coll Cardiol 2003;42(2):211–216. 1,786 consecutive patients aged 40 or over after left heart catheterisation at 11 hospitals; 25 (1.4%) cholesterol embolisation syndrome; 12 (48%) cutaneous signs; 16 (64%) renal insufficiency; in-hospital mortality 16.0% (4 patients); femoral approach did not increase incidence, "suggesting a possibility that the ascending aorta may be a potential embolic source".
- brar-poseidon-2014: PMID 24856027, Lancet 2014;383(9931):1814–1823. LVEDP-guided fluid vs standard protocol: contrast-induced AKI 6.7% (12/178) vs 16.3% (28/172); hydration stopped prematurely for shortness of breath in three patients in each group.
- khan-air-embolism-1995: PMID 8719380, Cathet Cardiovasc Diagn 1995;36(4):313–318. 3,715 angiogram and PTCA reports over 32 months; total air emboli 0.27% (10/3,715); PTCA training 0.84% vs angiography training 0.24%; no coronary air emboli in the 740 cines from the last 2 months of training.
- stone-amihot2-2009: PMID 20031745, Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2009;2(5):366–375. AMIHOT II: 301 patients, anterior STEMI, PCI within 6 h; randomised 222 SSO2 vs 79 control (3:1); 90-minute intracoronary SSO2 infusion into the LAD infarct territory vs control; among 281 with SPECT, median infarct size 20% vs 26.5%; Bayesian hierarchical pooling with AMIHOT I gave pooled 18.5% vs 25% (P=0.02, posterior probability of superiority 96.9%); 30-day MACE non-inferior.
- khan-coar-2022: PMID 35000456, Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2022;15(2):e011408. COAR: 201 normotensive ACS patients with no-reflow randomised 1:1 to intracoronary epinephrine (101) or adenosine (100); final TIMI 3 flow 90.1% vs 78% (P=0.019); no significant difference in final myocardial blush grade 3 (55.4% vs 45%), in-hospital and short-term mortality or MACE; follow-up 1 month.
- oliveri-noreflow-nma-2026: PMID 41740240, JACC Adv 2026;5(3):102599. Network meta-analysis of RCTs of intracoronary adenosine, epinephrine, nitroprusside and verapamil for no-reflow during primary PCI; 13 studies, 1,674 patients; epinephrine (OR 2.81) and verapamil (OR 2.84) associated with higher odds of final TIMI 3 vs control; none reduced MACE or mortality (underpowered). Nicardipine not among the agents (abstract lists only the four).
- prabhakaran-ais-2026: PMID 41582814, Stroke 2026;57(8):e316–e436 (correction PMID 42507797, e461–e467). Abstract: replaces the 2018 AIS guideline and the 2019 update; key updates include thrombolytic choice and eligibility, eligibility for endovascular thrombectomy, and a modified approach to thrombolysis contraindications. Used only for these abstract statements (full text not checked; no specific recommendation is quoted from it).

## Lesson cm-flow-embolism-noreflow

- No-reflow: inadequate myocardial perfusion without mechanical obstruction of the epicardial vessel; blood passes the lesion but cannot reach the myocardium: oxford p.267
- Mechanisms: microvascular dysfunction from ischaemia/reperfusion injury; distal embolisation of thrombus/atheromatous debris: oxford p.267
- Vasodilator pretreatment may reduce vasospasm contributing to microvascular dysfunction: oxford p.267
- May occur after pre-dilation; more common after stent deployment or post-dilation: oxford p.267
- Associated with increased short- and long-term post-PCI mortality: oxford p.267
- Once established difficult to treat; prevention is key: oxford p.267
- Acute vessel closure definition (partial or complete decrease in antegrade flow during or immediately after PCI); eight causes per Fig. 25.1 / Table 25.1: brilakis p.435–437
- Diagnosis: TIMI 0 or 1 after PCI; lack of myocardial blush (unobstructed vessel should blush); chest pain and ST elevation, sometimes hypotension: oxford p.267
- ST elevation usually first sign of coronary embolisation: brilakis p.111 (5.5)
- Dissection appears as linear or spiral filling defect: brilakis p.435
- New filling defect in the stent, OCT showed thrombus a few minutes after stenting: brilakis p.443 (Fig. 25.7)
- Occlusion of distal branches suggestive of prior coronary embolism, as a clue to the ACS culprit on diagnostic angiography: brilakis p.378 (20.7); extension to PCI labelled as inference
- Brilakis's Fig. 25.1 has an embolisation branch (thrombus, plaque, air) and no separate no-reflow branch: brilakis p.436
- Maintain guidewire position; stress may lead to inadvertent removal; catastrophic if dissection; guide manipulation can cause wire loss: brilakis p.435 (25.1)
- Key differentiation dissection (stenting) vs distal embolisation (may worsen with stenting; physical removal and/or vasodilators): brilakis p.435 (25.2)
- Distal contrast injection through a dual-lumen catheter, or IVUS, helps decide whether dissection has occurred (atherectomy slow flow): brilakis p.364 (19.9.5.7.1). Spasm infrequently causes complete flow cessation but can contribute in dissection and distal embolisation: brilakis p.444 (25.2.5)
- Thrombosis causes: inadequate anticoagulant/antiplatelet, suboptimal lesion treatment (stent underexpansion), hypercoagulable incl. HIT, venous access failure: brilakis p.441
- Do not stent with poor antegrade flow unless due to dissection; stenting in no-reflow worsens it: brilakis p.185 (10.1); avoiding further aggressive balloon work attributed to Oxford p.267 (no-reflow more common after post-dilation)
- 9.8: poor flow → vasodilators and thrombectomy unless dissection: brilakis p.184
- Avoid repeated contrast injections into the affected vessel (worsen ischaemia); flow often improves with time and vasodilators: oxford p.267
- Prevention list (bulky/thrombus-filled lesions, SVGs, ectatic native vessels, primary PCI; thrombectomy where thrombus; distal protection in SVG and visible thrombus; GPI pretreatment may reduce thromboembolisation; vasodilator pretreatment): oxford p.267
- Routine thrombus aspiration not recommended (TASTE, TOTAL); targeted use in large thrombus still advised: oxford p.273; also p.250
- Large thrombus with flow restored: defer with 24–48 h intense antithrombotic therapy (aspirin, oral P2Y12 inhibitor, IV platelet inhibitor and/or IV antithrombotic therapy); repeat angiography often shows less thrombus, lower risk of embolisation and no-reflow: brilakis p.381 (20.9.5)
- Rationale for deferral (stenting and post-dilation are high-risk steps for distal embolisation of atherothrombotic debris; theoretical benefits: less distal embolisation/no-reflow, better stent sizing with less undersizing/malapposition); PAMI: stenting in primary PCI not associated with a mortality benefit, only lower reinfarction or urgent revascularisation: oxford p.249–250 (book p.229). Fixer round 2: the module keeps only Oxford's no-mortality-benefit statement and corrects the rest from the trial: ref grines-stent-pami-1999 (PMID 10607811, checked on PubMed; abstract: the lower combined end point was due entirely to fewer ischaemia-driven target-vessel revascularisations, 7.7% vs 17.0%; 6-month mortality 4.2% vs 2.7%, P=0.27), so Oxford's 'lower reinfarction' is not taught
- DANAMI-DEFER (deferred stenting 48 h) failed to improve outcome, higher urgent TVR; current guidelines do not recommend a deferring stenting strategy in STEMI: oxford p.250
- Large thrombus, proceeding with PCI because of ongoing symptoms or high-risk plaque: consider thrombectomy before further balloon/stent; routine use not supported by RCT data; a filter may capture thrombus; laser may "burn" through thrombus and inhibits platelet aggregation: brilakis p.381 (20.9.6)
- Thrombectomy devices do not appear protective against distal embolisation in SVG PCI; distal protection devices reduce no-reflow and periprocedural events: oxford p.265
- Embolic protection devices can also be used for heavy thrombus burden or native lesions with large lipid core plaque: brilakis p.567 (30.8)
- SVG lesions more friable with higher thrombus burden; two protection types (distal occlusion balloon with aspiration of the static column after PCI; filter retrieved through a sheath): oxford p.265. In-stent restenotic SVG lesions low embolisation risk, no protection device needed: brilakis p.341 (18.8.2.1.1.1; same statement for the Spider, 18.8.2.1.2.1, p.344). Atherectomy generally avoided in SVG lesions (distal embolisation) but may be needed in old highly calcified grafts: brilakis p.349 (18.9.2)
- SVG: distal protection mandatory (oxford p.265); EPD in SVG PCI if technically feasible (brilakis p.341); primary stenting advocated (brilakis p.349); nicardipine 100–300 mcg intragraft before and after PCI (brilakis p.337, p.349); GPI can cause harm, not routine (brilakis p.337); routine GPI no demonstrated benefit (oxford p.265)
- Atherectomy prevention: vasodilator before in most patients unless hypotensive, nicardipine most commonly used: brilakis p.361 (19.9.5.4); contrast after each run; burr:artery <0.5; avoid >180,000 rpm; avoid damping; runs <15 s with >30 s between; avoid atherectomy with existing dissection: brilakis p.363–364; decelerations >5,000 rpm avoided: brilakis p.362; damping increases no-reflow: brilakis p.361; lower speeds 140,000–150,000 rpm rotational and 80,000 rpm orbital: brilakis p.443 ("may be associated with lower risk of distal embolization and no reflow"). Oxford differs: burr-size:artery ratio 0.5–0.6 (oxford p.270), burr not to exceed 0.6 × reference diameter (Fig. 8.29 caption, p.270), ideal speed 135,000–180,000 rpm (p.271), avoid decelerations >5000 rpm (p.271), orbital 80,000–120,000 rpm (p.271). The row now gives both books' numbers. Vasodilator flush: Brilakis cocktail of verapamil, nitroglycerin and heparin (p.361, 19.9.5.4); Oxford continuous flushing solution with vasodilators to cool the burr and prevent vasospasm (p.269). Bradycardia: Brilakis temporary pacemaker, aminophylline or atropine for RCA or dominant circumflex atherectomy (p.365, 19.9.5.7.4); Oxford severe bradycardia/heart block a complication (p.269), check heart rate and ECG, pretreat with atropine, some operators place a prophylactic temporary wire, especially for a large RCA (p.270–271)
- Rota no-reflow from overheating and distal embolisation of 5–10 µm debris: oxford p.269
- Large lipid core plaque on NIRS associated with distal embolisation and no-reflow: brilakis p.588 (30.17); lipid-rich plaque a cause of plaque embolisation: brilakis p.442
- Established no-reflow: agents via OTW balloon or multifunctional probing catheter as close to distal bed as possible; prevention doses via guide: oxford p.268
- Microcatheter or dual-lumen catheter (TwinPass or aspiration catheter) to deliver drugs distally: brilakis p.364 (19.9.5.7.1)
- Nicardipine preferred: less hypotensive than nitroprusside and verapamil: brilakis p.43
- Verapamil beware heart block; adenosine avoid in asthma/dipyridamole; GTN/nitrates not useful in no-reflow: oxford p.268
- Adenosine heart block most likely with RCA injection; avoid high doses in RCA; slow administration; aminophylline 250–300 mg IV over 10 minutes may prevent bradycardia during RCA atherectomy: brilakis p.44 (extract drops the en dash: "250300"); AF and VF with adenosine; VF treatment ask patient to cough, then defibrillation: brilakis p.44–45; Fig. 3.3 (caption): IC adenosine for no-reflow in the circumflex caused complete heart block then torsades, defibrillated: brilakis p.46
- Plaque/thrombus embolism: anticoagulation + IV antiplatelet (GPI or cangrelor); thrombectomy if large visible thrombus; IC vasodilators (nicardipine, nitroprusside, verapamil, adenosine); haemodynamic support if shock or arrest: brilakis p.111 (5.5)
- IV antiplatelet goal: prevent thrombus propagation in large thrombus or no-reflow: brilakis p.54
- Oxford doses repeatable: oxford p.268
- IABP if hypotension persists to maximise coronary perfusion pressure: oxford p.267; Oxford primary PCI section: routine IABP in MI with cardiogenic shock not routinely recommended after CRISP-AMI and IABP SHOCK: oxford p.252; no randomised trial showed lower in-hospital mortality with support devices: brilakis p.249 (14.1); IABP-SHOCK II 30-day mortality 39.7% vs 41.3%: ref iabp-shock-ii-2012; DanGer Shock: ref danger-shock-2024; 2025 ACC/AHA guideline as summarised: refs rao-acs-guideline-2025, restrepo-acs-guideline-review-2026; IABP-SHOCK II no 30-day mortality reduction in AMI cardiogenic shock: ref iabp-shock-ii-2012; routine IABP not recommended in AMI shock: ref esc-acs-2023
- IC epinephrine in refractory no-reflow: brilakis p.364
- No balloon or stent until TIMI 3 restored (no-reflow after Filterwire delivery in degenerated SVG; aspiration through guide and vasodilators): brilakis p.343. Once established, no reflow difficult to treat; prevention key (pearl attributed to Oxford only): oxford p.267
- Adrenaline: Brilakis keeps it for refractory no-reflow (p.364); Oxford does not list adrenaline or epinephrine for no-reflow (its agents p.268 are verapamil, adenosine, nitroprusside; its only epinephrine is for anaphylaxis, ch 10). The earlier "Both manuals place adrenaline late" has been corrected.
- Check 2 stem now "fits the manuals' advice best"; the explanation says Oxford does not list nicardipine (oxford p.268)
- Adrenaline beyond the manuals (COAR; network meta-analysis): refs khan-coar-2022, oliveri-noreflow-nma-2026; Academy SVG no-reflow case uses adrenaline first: src/content/tracks/complications.ts comp-case-svg-noreflow s4

## Lesson cm-flow-embolism-plaque-thrombus

- Severe embolisation can cause profound haemodynamic compromise and require prompt treatment: brilakis p.442
- Thrombus embolisation causes as thrombosis plus thrombus in sheath picked up by catheters: brilakis p.442; suboptimal catheter preparation, suboptimal balloon/stent/indeflator preparation, thrombus formation in sheath: brilakis p.437 (Table 25.1)
- Plaque embolisation causes: atherectomy, lipid-rich plaque, SVG lesions (brilakis p.442); inadequate cleaning of the catheter after aortic advancement causing aortic/iliac plaque embolisation; aspiration thrombectomy or initial balloon predilation of a STEMI culprit (brilakis p.443)
- Balloon withdrawal can suck thrombus/plaque into the guide, more likely with bulky large balloons and plaque modification balloons; air also: brilakis p.169 (9.7.2.3)
- Keeley series cited in Fig. 5.13: brilakis p.108
- Sheath: aspirate and discard, flush with another syringe of heparinised saline; thrombus may form in sheath: brilakis p.108; routine aspiration then flushing with a different syringe: brilakis p.442; sheath side arm aspirated 3–4 mL and discarded, flushed with a different syringe: brilakis p.79 (4.2.14); separate syringes so debris not reinjected: brilakis p.81 (4.2.15.3)
- Long femoral sheaths in severe iliac and aortic disease: brilakis p.108; flush catheters with normal saline before inserting into the body: brilakis p.108 (5.4.3.2), p.112 (5.6.3.2); exchange over a guide wire through an abdominal aortic aneurysm to avoid dislodging thrombus: oxford p.68
- Guide: aspirate 2–3 mL, discard over white gauze; thrombus usually from sheath; plaque often iridescent from cholesterol crystals: brilakis p.108
- Back-bleed rather than aspirate if vacuum sucks catheter onto wall or into ostial stenosis: brilakis p.108
- Failure-to-aspirate algorithm: withdraw a few mm (pressure waveform reappears), fluoroscopy for kink, presume plugged, no wire, no flush, remove with constant suction, aspirate sheath (thrombus from sheath scooped up): brilakis p.109–110
- After balloon removal: back-bleed Y-connector, aspirate guide, fill with contrast: brilakis p.168 (9.7.2.2); guide aspirated and flushed after balloon removal: brilakis p.183
- ACT >250 HemoTec or >300 Hemochron depending on complexity and IV antiplatelet use; Hemochron higher; P2Y12 (pretreatment ideal); lesion preparation; imaging: brilakis p.441. Heparin ACT goals: without GPI or cangrelor, Hemochron 300–350 s and HemoTec 250–300 s; with GPI or cangrelor 200–250 s; ACT every 20–30 minutes: brilakis p.48 (3.4.2.1.2; extract drops en dashes). Analyser names kept in step 5 body text because the targets are device-specific (MANUAL_AUTHORING rule 7); the keypoint now uses no brand names
- Avoid atherectomy or lower speeds; EPD for SVG; clear catheter after aorta: brilakis p.443
- Sheathless guide with inner dilator avoids razor effect: brilakis p.110 (5.5)
- Check (cannot aspirate): brilakis p.109 (warning: no wire/no flush; embolisation in aorta, stroke or acute coronary occlusion)
- Recognition: ST elevation first sign; RCA vs LM shock or arrest; angiography decrease/cessation of flow: brilakis p.111; IVUS/OCT diagnosis: brilakis p.437 (the earlier "branch suddenly cut off" clause has been removed)
