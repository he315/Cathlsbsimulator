## Lesson cp-ostial-kinds-plan

- Ostial lesion within 3–5 mm of the vessel origin; two types; lists of aorto-ostial (RCA, LM, aorto-coronary bypass grafts, both SVG and arterial grafts such as LIMA) and branch ostial (LAD, ramus, Cx from LM; diagonals from LAD; OMs from Cx; PDA and PL from RCA in right dominance and Cx in left dominance; Y-graft ostia, e.g. free RIMA on LIMA): brilakis p.271; Fig. 15.1 (LIMA marked yellow at its origin): brilakis p.272; definition repeated: brilakis p.132 (6.8.2.8)
- Aorto-ostial lesions fibrotic, calcified, rigid, prone to recoil, challenging to dilate, sometimes atherectomy: brilakis p.271 (15.1.1); greater thickness of muscular and elastic tissue in the aortic wall, 'often heavily calcified and balloon undilatable' (table cell now says this): brilakis p.276 (15.1.9)
- Branch ostial = essentially Medina 0.0.1 or 0.1.0 bifurcation lesions; chapter 16 applies: brilakis p.287 (15.2)
- Hazards column: damping, ischaemia (p.272), aorto-coronary dissection (p.273), missing the ostium and recoil (p.280, p.282); branch: wiring difficulty with angulation and severity, MV injury, ostial coverage (p.288; p.305)
- ACC/AHA type B includes ostial location: oxford p.212 (Table 8.2)
- SYNTAX: aorto-ostial lesion adds one point: brilakis p.141 (Step 6)
- Ostial lesions in the lesion-complexity list that bears on likelihood of technical success and risk: brilakis p.142 (figure "Should PCI be done?")
- Perforation more common in complex cases (CTOs, ostial lesions, tortuous vessels): oxford p.329
- Planning: hard to engage, severe ischaemia on engagement; guidewire preloaded, wire immediately, then disengage ("hit and run"); alternatively wire with non-selective engagement, minimising ischaemia and ostial trauma: brilakis p.271 (15.1.1)
- Monitoring: damping common, constant attention: brilakis p.271 (15.1.2)
- Medications: no special medications: brilakis p.271 (15.1.3)
- Access: radial or femoral acceptable; highly complex cases femoral and 7 or 8 Fr guides may give better support but more likely to damp: brilakis p.271 (15.1.4); 2018 ESC quoted by Brilakis: radial preferred for any PCI unless overriding procedural considerations: brilakis p.61 (4.1), ref esc-revasc-2018; keypoint 'radial by default' rests on the same; cp-left-main teaches the same point
- Haemodynamic support: standard; ostial LM rarely requires it, consider if high likelihood of compromise: brilakis p.287 (15.1.14); branch ostial: rare unless large territory (e.g. LAD), atherectomy required, and significant LV dysfunction: brilakis p.292 (15.2.14)
- Oxford: IABP used electively for LMS PCI with impaired LV function, normal LV tolerates LMS inflations well; haemodynamic compromise: short inflations and prophylactic IABP advisable; section cites BMS-era registry data and calls elective LMS PCI controversial: oxford p.261 (ch 8, Left main stem PCI); BCIS-1 (EF ≤30%, extensive disease; MACCE at discharge 15.2% vs 16.0%; authors do not support routine IABP): ref perera-bcis1-2010 (PMID 20736470); fewer deaths at median 51 months (HR 0.66): ref perera-bcis1-longterm-2013 (PMID 23224207); decision by haemodynamics and procedural risk: pm-support-choice. Brilakis: LM inflations as brief as possible especially without haemodynamic support: brilakis p.332 (17.9). Keypoint now points to pm-support-choice.
- Closure as standard: brilakis p.286 (15.1.11)
- Check 1: circumflex is a branch of LM (p.271), 2 mm within 3–5 mm; LIMA grouped with aorto-ostial (p.271)
- Check 2: hit and run (p.271, p.273); deep engagement and large guides damp (p.272–273); side holes false security (p.272); avoid automated injectors (p.273)
- Pearl: synthesis of the above (origin decides technique, p.271/p.288; guide behaviour decides engagement plan, p.272–273)

## Lesson cp-ostial-engage

- Damping on engagement may lead to ischaemia and haemodynamic compromise; exclude intracatheter thrombus or debris, kinking, non-coaxial alignment, transducer malfunction: brilakis p.272 (15.1.5), which points to section 5.6; the damping algorithm itself: brilakis p.112–113 (5.6.3.5, Fig. 5.16). Module now says 'the damping algorithm in Brilakis 5.6 (Fig. 5.16)'
- Oxford causes of damping: LMS intubation (LMS stenosis, tip angled into the wall, spasm, selective LAD or Cx intubation with a short LMS); RCA (conus cannulation, small non-dominant vessel): oxford p.155 (ch 6, Catheter manipulation)
- Catheter-tip spasm: mostly after ostial engagement; trace ventricularises then falls, recovering only on withdrawal or disengagement; commonest in non-dominant RCAs and vessels of calibre similar to the catheter, commoner in shorter, low-weight patients; IC nitrate after careful re-engagement discriminates: oxford p.173 (ch 6, Use of nitrates)
- Graft ostia: SVG PCI generally JR4 or bypass guides; El Gamal, Amplatz or multipurpose depending on the ostium: oxford p.265 (ch 8, Vein graft PCI); LIMA fragile, prone to dissection especially in the elderly, slow selective injections; RIMA slow injections to avoid ostial dissection: oxford p.201 (ch 7, Arterial grafts); side holes acceptable in bypass grafts: brilakis p.272; cross-link cp-prior-cabg
- Oxford: damping may mean the tip faces plaque at the ostium of the LMS or RCA; contrast should not be injected or only with very high caution, risk of aortic or coronary wall dissection: oxford p.61 (ch 2, The cardiac physiologist)
- Prevention list (6 Fr; avoid deep engagement; side-hole guides with caveats; Ostial Pro keeps guide outside ostium; floating wire into aortic root; small guide extension to engage and back out the guide): brilakis p.272
- Solutions list (wire advanced to guide tip before engagement; extra-support wires facilitate engagement, disengagement and coaxial alignment; smaller 6 Fr guides as larger damp more; JR4, sometimes IM for shepherd's crook, for isolated aorto-ostial disease, easier to intermittently engage/disengage, less support than AL in diffuse or tortuous vessels; avoid injection while damped, dissection down the artery or into ascending aorta; intermittent disengagement by torquing to non-coaxial rather than pulling back, which may lose distal wire; floating wire): brilakis p.273
- Check (torque off): brilakis p.273; larger guides damp more: p.271, p.273
- Side holes: may be used in RCA, bypass grafts, protected LM; not in unprotected LM (large area at risk); not in an RCA that is donor to a totally occluded left-system vessel; false sense of security; contrast exits through side holes, hinders visualisation, more contrast: brilakis p.272; uses for ostial RCA or ostial bypass graft lesions, prevent damping, may allow antegrade flow, may decrease hydraulic dissection risk; hydraulic dissections can still occur; active guide manipulation often chosen over side holes depending on operator; unprotected LM avoided except ostial LM CTOs; higher contrast and image degradation: brilakis p.520–522 (30.2.5); side-hole guides mask damping but still predispose to ostial dissection, never in unprotected LM: brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3); short LM: deep tip can completely obstruct the Cx ostium with seemingly normal waveform: brilakis p.330 (17.5)
- Check (side holes): brilakis p.272
- Guide choice: JR4 / IM preferred for isolated aorto-ostial disease, AL more support for diffuse/tortuous: brilakis p.273; Williams right "particularly useful for treating aorto-ostial RCA lesions": brilakis p.116 (5.7.2.2); radial RCA PCI: JR "should generally be avoided due to poor support": brilakis p.522 (30.2.6.1); AL "aggressive" shapes need careful manipulation: brilakis p.113
- Aorto-coronary dissection prevention (small guides; avoid deep engagement; gentle manual injection, avoid automated injectors or use gentle preset; avoid injection while damped; wire before contrast if possible; small guide extension keeping guide outside ostium): brilakis p.273; never inject with damping and/or no back bleeding: brilakis p.114 (5.6.4.4)
- Guide extension: deep advancement may cause dissection especially with injection while damped; injection through a guide extension with damped waveform may cause dissection propagating antegrade or retrograde; avoid injections through deeply engaged extensions: brilakis p.536–537 (30.3.4)
- Recognise: contrast "staining" within the aortic wall: brilakis p.114 (5.6.4.4)
- Treatment: maintain guidewire position, stop injecting: brilakis p.273; if vessel needs wiring confirm intraluminal position with IVUS (without contrast); stent vessel ostium to seal entry into the dissection flap: brilakis p.274; high-pressure flaring often required; covered stent may be needed, increased restenosis and thrombosis risk: brilakis p.114; stent protruding 1–2 mm into the aorta; IVUS to guide placement and ensure ostial coverage: brilakis p.440 (25.2.1.1, imaged)
- Oxford: iatrogenic type A dissection incidence 0.01%, more benign prognosis than spontaneous; stenting the ostium can seal the entry when it started at a coronary ostium: oxford p.325
- Aftercare now taught in place (cm-principles-closure does not exist yet): large dissection → serial CT or TOE until stabilised or resolved, particularly if the ascending aorta is involved; careful BP control after the procedure; emergency surgery rarely needed except aortic regurgitation, tamponade from rupture into the pericardium, or extension (>40 mm from the coronary ostia proposed as a cut-off): brilakis p.440 (25.2.1.1, imaged: '>40 mm')
- Oxford: iatrogenic type A dissection 'rare but potentially life-threatening'; causes: stent related (flap lifted at the proximal stent edge propagating towards the aorta) and guiding catheter related (intimal tear of the aortic wall usually near the coronary ostia): oxford p.325
- Round 2 fix (gap): Oxford management: conservative approach first because the non-diseased aortic wall resists propagation and the dissection tends to contain itself and seal; CT after the procedure and repeated at 24 hours for extension or healing; surgery considered for propagation towards the neck vessels with neurological symptoms, development or worsening of pericardial effusion (sign of retrograde propagation to the root), severe aortic regurgitation, haemodynamic instability: oxford p.325. The comparison sentence (shared triggers aortic regurgitation and pericardial involvement; neurological and haemodynamic from Oxford; 40 mm cut-off from Brilakis): oxford p.325 and brilakis p.440 (25.2.1.1)
- Treatment step reworded ('place a stent across the ostium so that it closes the point where the dissection began') to avoid the source phrasing of p.274
- Protruding stents: engaging vessels with prior aorto-ostial stenting difficult due to protruding struts, may deform the stent, may be hard to engage coaxially; wire with guide disengaged, ideally knuckling the wire to avoid passing through struts, then engage over the wire; RCA: JR4 easier for wiring through previous aorto-ostial stents; guide exchange using microcatheter and 300 cm supportive wire (Section 5.7 step 7): brilakis p.274
- Check (old stent): brilakis p.274
- Pitfalls: each restates p.272–274
- Pearl: synthesis of p.272–273 (floating wire; small guide extension; intermittent engagement p.120, 5.8.1 "may be intermittently engaged when necessary to advance equipment or inject contrast")
- Sim task: JR4 engagement and rotation off the ostium: brilakis p.273; no injection when damped: brilakis p.113, oxford p.61
- Conus branch: first RCA branch supplying the RV outflow tract, easily cannulated, pressure damps rapidly, injection can cause myocardial blush and VF; withdraw quickly and gently, gentle rotation and angulation, occasionally a different catheter: oxford p.157 (ch 6); contrast into conus can induce arrhythmia: oxford p.156
- VF often a result of catheter manipulation near the ostium of a stenosed coronary artery, plugging, conus injection; immediate defibrillation: oxford p.309 (ch 10, Arrhythmia)
- LIMA ostium more prone to dissection, care with catheter manipulation; once engaged avoid moving the catheter, particularly advancing it against a plaque: oxford p.321 (ch 10, Coronary dissection, Avoiding dissection)
- Rotablator burr hard to advance through guides with significant bends such as AL: brilakis p.361 (19.9.5.2)
- 3D right guide: ostial and more distal RCA disease treated by torque (counterclockwise for ostium, clockwise for distal) while keeping guide stability: brilakis p.525 (Fig. 30.14); radial RCA PCI options AL1, Ikari Right, 3D Right, JR generally avoided: brilakis p.522 (30.2.6.1)
- Shepherd's crook RCA: change to AR MOD, 3DRC or IMA catheter: oxford p.156; IM for shepherd's crook: brilakis p.273, p.522
- Ostial left main engagement: severe damping and extensive ischaemia, careful monitoring, prompt disengagement before haemodynamic deterioration or vessel injury; no side-hole guides except LM CTO; wire (and often a balloon on it) preloaded to the guide tip for immediate wiring and ballooning; intermittent disengagement; independent-hand or floating wire; avoid injection when damped (catastrophic LM ostial dissection); if dissection, disengage, only cautious injections from a distance, prepare to stent the entry; avoid prolonged injections with left dominance or occluded RCA (hypotension): brilakis p.330 (17.5)
- Aorto-coronary dissection: rare, any PCI, more common with CTO PCI especially retrograde, 0.8–1.8% in two contemporary series, most commonly RCA, may extend from sinus into ascending aorta and beyond; cause list (deep engagement and aggressive guides such as 8 Fr AL; damping; forceful injection especially through wedged guides; predilation of the coronary ostium; balloon rupture; retrograde wiring into the subaortic space during reverse CART; antegrade attempts at a true aorto-ostial CTO; aortic dissection extending into the ostia); aortic dissection as cause of an ostial lesion → emergency surgery with aortic root replacement: brilakis p.439 (25.2.1, 25.2.1.1)
- Prevention additions (anchor techniques instead of aggressive intubation; guide extension delivered over a balloon/inchworming rather than directly over the wire; extreme caution injecting through a guide extension, hydraulic dissection; side holes in occluded RCAs may reduce barotrauma but false security, ischaemia and dissection still occur) and treatment additions (stop injecting as injections expand the plane; stent that can expand to a diameter that seals the dissection; if contrast essential, through a dual-lumen microcatheter or aspiration catheter advanced distally, the latter better filling but bulkier): brilakis p.440 (25.2.1.1)
- Side-hole "never" in unprotected LM: brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3); exceptions for LM CTOs: p.330 (17.5), p.522 (30.2.5)

## Lesson cp-ostial-prep

- Resistant to dilatation, prone to recoil (aortic wall muscular/elastic tissue), often heavily calcified and balloon undilatable, excellent preparation before stenting: brilakis p.276; primary stenting avoided in most cases: brilakis p.282 (Challenge 3)
- Wiring tips 1–3 (wire to guide tip before engagement; hydrophilic or polymer-jacketed wire through a microcatheter to distal vessel then exchange for safer wire; great caution re subintimal tracking and acute closure; exchange for supportive wire to facilitate engagement and disengagement): brilakis p.275 (15.1.8)
- Support wires: strong supportive bodies, soft tips: brilakis p.561 (30.7.4)
- Coaxial alignment helped by extra-support wires: brilakis p.273
- No polymer-jacketed wires for stenting; exchange for soft, non-coated tip wires (workhorse or stiff body): brilakis p.286 (15.1.10.6)
- Balloons partially in vessel and partially in aorta; non-compliant and longer (such as 30 mm); plaque modification balloons facilitate expansion and prevent excessive movement: brilakis p.276
- Cutting balloon inflation very slowly, 1 atm every 5 s: brilakis p.571 (30.9.3.3); inflate slowly and avoid >14 atm (blade fracture, strut avulsion, balloon entrapment): brilakis p.417 (23.2.3; extract '.14 atm', same garble as '. 40' on p.440)
- Balloon sized 1:1; ensure full expansion before stent; intravascular imaging to ensure complete expansion: brilakis p.276
- Forceful back-and-forth balloon manipulation can cause ostial dissection, especially with preexisting ostial lesion: brilakis p.408 (23.1)
- Watermelon seeding definition (excess movement distally into the vessel or proximally into the aorta); prevention = treatment list (slow inflation 1 atm at a time while observing with gentle retraction; buddy wire; plaque modification balloon; longer balloons such as 30 mm; winged balloon; IVL or very-high-pressure OPN balloon): brilakis p.276; plaque modification balloons designed to score/modify the lesion and prevent watermelon seeding: brilakis p.570 (30.9.3); OPN is a specialised non-compliant balloon: brilakis p.573 (30.9.7)
- Check (watermelon): brilakis p.276 (longer balloons such as 30 mm recommended; the explanation no longer claims that shorter balloons slip more; p.417 item 5 uses shorter balloons to concentrate force in undilatable lesions, a different problem); underexpansion with primary stenting p.282
- Atherectomy can be very useful in severely calcified balloon-undilatable aorto-ostial lesions; rotational and orbital both usable; IVL 'another emerging option' (module now says 'emerging'): brilakis p.276
- Rotational: Rotawire extra support; coaxial guide decreases perforation/aortic dissection risk and allows maximal debulking; burr activated at end of guide with tension off to prevent jumping and entrapment; larger burrs may be required: brilakis p.276; Rotawire extra support used for aorto-ostial lesions: brilakis p.361 (19.9.5.3); may help align the guide with the vessel in ostial lesions: brilakis p.576 (30.10.1.1.3); avoid damping (no-reflow/slow flow): brilakis p.361 (19.9.5.2); coaxial guide at aorto-ostial atherectomy to minimise aortic dissection: brilakis p.364
- Predilation of the coronary ostium and balloon rupture are causes of aorto-coronary dissection: brilakis p.439 (25.2.1.1); familiarity with rated burst pressure of NC balloons: brilakis p.417 (23.2.2). 'Watch for staining after each inflation' is the module's practical inference from these.
