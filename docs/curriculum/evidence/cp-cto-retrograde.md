## Notes for checkers

- Device names: none. Generic classes replace products: "soft non-tapered polymer-jacketed wire" (Table 30.8 class listed for collateral crossing), "wire with an ultra-low tip load (0.3 g) and a long hydrophilic coating" (Table 30.7 entry "best wire for tortuous epicardial collaterals"), "small-outer-diameter microcatheter", "deflectable-tip microcatheter" (30.6.3, p.552), "angled-tip microcatheter with a 120° bend" (p.553), "dedicated externalisation wire (330 cm and 350 cm lengths)" (p.565), "vascular plug, multilayer (type II)" (p.592), "three-loop snare" (p.592). Al-Ogaili's product names (SUOH 03, Caravel) are given as classes. Hemochron appears only where the ACT target is analyser-specific.
- Synthesis flagged as such: (a) The 'retrograde wire as a marker' row now rests on Ungureanu 2025 §3.2.4 (antegrade rewiring with a microcatheter and workhorse wire using the retrograde wire as a marker, after tip-in the balloon); the Allana table label is now "'Just marker' antegrade crossing (term not defined in the abstract)". Fig 27.5 (p.469) is described accurately (retrograde true-lumen puncture confirmed by IVUS, retrograde balloon, then antegrade wiring), not as a marker technique. (b) Applying Brilakis's contralateral-injection rule (p.462, written for a branch filling through contralateral collaterals) to a perforated collateral channel is stated as an application ("a collateral channel is fed from both ends"); the septal haematoma case (Doshi) coiled both inflow and outflow but gives no reason for doing so; the two-sided filling rationale is attributed in the text to Brilakis 26.4 (Fig. 26.10 D6, p.453, contralateral injection to rule out retrograde filling), kept separate from the case fact. (c) Pearls are teaching synthesis drawn from the cited steps. (d) "Ipsilateral collateral connects two parts of the same coronary artery" is the definition implied by Azzalini and Al-Ogaili (donor and target the same artery; ping-pong second guide in the same artery).
- Disagreements taught: ipsilateral registries report different end points and populations (Azzalini 2017: perforation needing treatment 5.6%, septal channels 24% included; Al-Ogaili 2024: all perforations 17.3%, epicardial only), stated as not directly comparable; Oxford vs Brilakis on heparin reversal timing (Oxford p.263 lists protamine then covered stent after balloon, pericardiocentesis and fluids, p.329 'consider reversing heparinization' after balloon occlusion; Brilakis p.449 defers reversal until equipment is out and the pericardium drained); Brilakis vs Oxford on aorto-coronary/iatrogenic aortic dissection (Brilakis: stop injections, stent ostium 1–2 mm into aorta, IVUS, serial CT/TOE, surgery for AR, tamponade or >40 mm extension; Oxford: conservative first, CT after and at 24 h, ostial stent can seal entry, surgery for neck-vessel propagation with neurology, worsening effusion, severe AR, instability).
- Possible issues in existing Academy lessons (pci-complex `cx-cto`, for the fixer): (1) `cx-cto-hybrid` pitfall "High tension on an externalised wire, which pulls the donor guide deep into the donor vessel": tension and donor-ostial injury are supported (Ungureanu 2025), but the guide being pulled deep is not stated in either manual; unverified. (2) `cx-cto-hybrid` pitfall "Dragging a retrograde microcatheter or wire through a collateral without a microcatheter covering it" is self-contradictory as worded (a microcatheter cannot cover itself); suggest "advancing a wire through a collateral without microcatheter support". (3) `cx-cto-safety` danger table: "Loculated haematoma after prior CABG — epicardial collateral perforation with adherent pericardium" is narrower than Brilakis 26.6, where any perforation after CABG can loculate. (4) `cx-cto-safety` "Collateral perforation ... never advance without a microcatheter" is not stated in the manual as an absolute rule. (5) The stop-rule issues already noted in docs/curriculum/evidence/cp-cto-strategy.md (3-hour threshold, eGFR wording) still apply. (6) `pub-retrograde-cto-septal-haematoma` s2 feedback "Most septal perforations are benign" agrees with Doshi and Brilakis 26.5; no issue.

## Lesson cp-cto-retrograde-septal

- Retrograde definition: occlusion approached from distal vessel against original flow; wire via collateral or bypass graft into the artery beyond the occlusion; microcatheter to distal cap; then retrograde wiring or retrograde dissection/re-entry: brilakis p.393 (21.8.3)
- Antegrade generally preferred first; retrograde more complications, time, radiation; retrograde critical for high success: brilakis p.394 (taught in cp-cto-strategy; referenced only)
- Karmpaliotis: 1,301 procedures, retrograde 41%, initial 46%, after failed antegrade 54%; technical 85% vs 94%; MACE 4.3% vs 1.1%; more complex; critical for success: ref karmpaliotis-retrograde-2016
- Allana: 4,058 procedures, 44 centres; retrograde crossing 60.5%; lesion crossing 81.6%; technical 78.7%; MACE 3.5%; clinical perforation 5.8%: ref allana-retrograde-2023
- Oxford: retrograde approach among novel/evolving approaches; example proximal RCA CTO crossed from LAD via septals: oxford p.263
- APCTO three problems (tough proximal cap; crossing collaterals safely and efficiently; crossing the CTO, particularly reverse CART): ref wu-apcto-retrograde-2018
- Channel table: Rathore septal 67.5%, epicardial 24.8%, SVG 7.6% (157 patients, single centre): ref rathore-retrograde-2009; Huang septal 50.4%, epicardial 34.7%, AV groove 14.9% of 242 attempted channels: ref huang-collateral-tortuosity-2018; Allana septal 62.0%, SVG 17.4%, epicardial 19.1% of successful pathways: ref allana-retrograde-2023.
- Retrograde equipment delivered through a guide in the donor: cross-reference to cp-cto-strategy-setup (no manual statement that a diagnostic catheter is insufficient is claimed); ACT >350 s (Hemochron) for retrograde, some operators >300 s with very frequent checks in the low 300s: brilakis p.390 (21.3); analyser-dependence note follows the Hemochron qualifier on p.390
- Antegrade preferred first partly because of the need for antegrade lesion preparation even when retrograde is used; retrograde associated with favourable long-term outcomes: brilakis p.394 (21.8.4)
- Review previous angiograms: predominant collateral may shift before or during PCI; disappeared collaterals may still be crossable: brilakis p.392 (21.6.4)
- Treat donor lesions on retrograde path to avoid donor thrombosis: brilakis p.395 (21.10)
- Microcatheter lengths 135 and 150 cm: brilakis p.545 (Corsair Pro 135/150), p.547 (Mamba Flex 135 and 150)
- 90 cm guides may be needed for retrograde CTO PCI: brilakis p.519 (30.2.3); guide shortening still needed for some retrograde cases via bypass grafts or apical collaterals when retrograde microcatheters not long enough: brilakis p.519 (30.2.4)
- Channel assessment list: size, tortuosity, bifurcations, angle of entry and exit, jailing of entry or exit by stent, distance between collateral exit and distal cap: brilakis p.391 (21.6.4)
- Lack of tortuosity strongest predictor, then size: brilakis p.392
- Huang: only large size and lack of tortuosity independent predictors; score large size 1 point, no tortuosity 2 points: ref huang-collateral-tortuosity-2018
- Rathore success predictors CC1, tortuosity <90°, recipient angle <90°; failure predictors epicardial, CC0, corkscrew, recipient angle >90°, non-visible connection: ref rathore-retrograde-2009
- Donor proximal to collateral and collateral dominance determine ischaemia risk: brilakis p.392
- Check 1 (channel choice): brilakis p.391 list (jailing 'may hinder guidewire crossing'); ref huang-collateral-tortuosity-2018 (only size and tortuosity independent predictors); ref rathore-retrograde-2009 (recipient angle >90° predicts failure); septals first: brilakis p.392
- Microcatheter functions (support, reshaping, exchanges, protection of proximal vessel and collateral): brilakis p.542 (30.6)
- Soft non-tapered polymer-jacketed wires listed for "CTO PCI: collateral vessel crossing": brilakis p.565 (Table 30.8); polymer-jacketed wires facilitate wiring through tortuosity and collaterals, carry perforation/dissection risk, exchanged before balloons and stents: brilakis p.561 (30.7.2)
- Large bend to enter septal, small bend to cross: brilakis p.150 (Fig 8.1)
- Surfing: invisible septals often crossable, wire finds path of least resistance: brilakis p.392; surfing credited to Sianos; the 'deep wiring' technique (polymer-jacketed wire advanced through distal branches into a ventricle or the aorta for support; tactile, no contrast; not attributed to Brilakis himself) likened to it; for deep wiring: pull back and redirect to another channel if the wire will not advance, perforation likelier when manipulating against resistance: brilakis p.177 (9.7.8.3.2.2.1). Applying the pull-back rule to septal surfing is stated in the text as an application.
- Dautov: surfing 81% irrespective of CC size; success/time influenced by tortuosity not Werner class; a quarter septal perforations, all minor and asymptomatic; surfing vs tip injections as the two septal crossing techniques: ref dautov-septal-surfing-2017
- Tip injection after backflow check with 2–3 cc Luer-lock syringe: brilakis p.392 (reused tip pci-cto-strategy-microcatheter-injection)
- Dual-lumen microcatheter to wire various septal branches during retrograde crossing: brilakis p.558 (30.6.4 CTO use 4)
- Deflectable-tip microcatheter for collateral branches with difficult, acutely angulated takeoffs; stiff (support but vessel injury); bend radius 2.5 mm, special care deflecting in <2.5 mm arteries; release bend and straighten tip during advancement or removal; trapping removal needs 8 Fr guide: brilakis p.552 (30.6.3.1 items 5–8)
- Flush microcatheter after contrast injection to avoid wire stickiness; exchange a microcatheter showing fatigue after prolonged use: brilakis p.558 (30.6.6 items 2–3)
- Intermittent injections and orthogonal views confirm wire position: brilakis p.154 (8.6.2.6)
- Coil-wall microcatheters can be spun; non-coiled small ones have smaller OD and are not designed to be spun: brilakis p.548 (30.6.2); soft low-profile microcatheter tip fracture with aggressive rotation: brilakis p.550; avoid over-rotation, always keep a guidewire inside during manipulations: brilakis p.483 (27.4.2)
- Then cross by retrograde wiring or retrograde dissection/re-entry: brilakis p.393
- Piedimonte: small size and continuous bends predicted microcatheter tracking failure in septals; MTF procedural success 40.0% vs 93.9%; collateral perforations 20.0% vs 3.0%: ref piedimonte-microcatheter-tracking-2024
- Septal dilation ≤1.5 mm at 2–4 atm to facilitate microcatheter or device crossing; not epicardials: brilakis p.392
- Guide extension from the retrograde side increases support for retrograde equipment delivery: brilakis p.528 (30.3.2 item 5)
- Antegrade and retrograde anchoring in challenging retrograde cases: brilakis p.409 (23.1.3.1.2)
- Small OD microcatheters easier through small tortuous vessels: brilakis p.548
- Tip-in when retrograde wire in antegrade guide but MC fails: ref ungureanu-portal-techniques-2025 (taught in lesson 5)
- Check 2: brilakis p.392 (≤1.5 mm, 2–4 atm; not epicardials), p.550 (tip fracture)
- Tips: pci-cto-retro-septal-surf (p.150, p.392, p.565; ref Dautov); pci-cto-retro-microcatheter-stalls (p.392, p.528, p.548, p.409; ref Piedimonte)
- Keypoints restate the above.

## Lesson cp-cto-retrograde-epicardial

- Septals usually safer and easier than epicardials: brilakis p.392; epicardials cannot be dilated: brilakis p.392
- Opening paragraph: epicardials more tortuous than non-epicardial channels: ref benincasa-epicardial-2017; tear can rapidly cause tamponade: brilakis p.462 (26.5). Earlier unsupported wording ('often larger', 'visible when no septal is', 'thin-walled', 'lie in the pericardial space') removed.
- Step 5: no balloon dilation of the channel being crossed (p.392); gentle 2–4 minute balloon occlusion of a dominant non-interventional epicardial channel to recruit interventional collaterals: brilakis p.392, taught in cp-cto-strategy-angiogram
- Ipsilateral intro: end points and populations differ (Azzalini: perforation needing intervention, septal 24% of channels; Al-Ogaili: all perforations, epicardial only); only Al-Ogaili compares ipsilateral with other retrograde cases (more complications, 25.8% vs 16.4%); Azzalini is single-arm and concludes success is high and complications reasonable with experienced operators: refs azzalini-ipsilateral-2017, al-ogaili-ipsilateral-2024
- Epicardial perforation serious, rapid tamponade, may be difficult to control; septal perforation unlikely to have adverse consequences, but septal haematomas, RV wall haematomas and dry tamponade reported: brilakis p.462 (26.5)
- Epicardial use predicted failure: ref rathore-retrograde-2009
- Benincasa: 81 retrograde procedures; NEC 31 septal, 7 grafts; technical 35% vs 76%; perforation needing intervention 5% vs 0; MACE similar: ref benincasa-epicardial-2017
- Wu K: 155 patients, perforation 15.5%, tamponade 4.5%, managed with coil embolisation and pericardiocentesis; predictors renal dysfunction, RCA CTO, Epi-CTO ≥2; not associated with worse long-term outcomes: ref wu-epicardial-perforation-2019
- Epicardials 19.1% of successful pathways: ref allana-retrograde-2023
- Ipsilateral table: Azzalini 126 patients, 17%; epicardial 76%, septal 24%; Cx 42%, LAD 39%, RCA 19%; one guide 80%, ping-pong 20%; wire to distal cap 81%; technical 87%; ILC perforation needing intervention 5.6%; tamponade 2.4%; death 0.8%; used mostly when no other option: ref azzalini-ipsilateral-2017. Al-Ogaili 191/4,466 (4.3%), wiring success 50%, Cx 50%, dual injection 66% vs 89%, technical 76% vs 79%, complications 25.8% vs 16.4%, perforation 17.3% vs 9.0%, pericardiocentesis 3.1% vs 1.2%, donor dissection/thrombus 3.7% vs 1.2%: ref al-ogaili-ipsilateral-2024
- Check 1: brilakis p.392 (no dilation of epicardials), p.548 (small OD microcatheters choice for epicardials), p.462
- Map with complete opacification, optimal projections, tip injections after backflow: brilakis p.391–392
- Ultra-low tip load (0.3 g) hydrophilic-coated workhorse "best wire for tortuous epicardial collaterals": brilakis p.562 (Table 30.7); soft polymer-jacketed collateral wires: brilakis p.565
- Small OD microcatheters choice for epicardial collaterals: brilakis p.548
- PROGRESS IEC most used wire 39%, microcatheter 43%: ref al-ogaili-ipsilateral-2024
- Support from guide side: guide extension p.528, anchoring p.409
- Single guide limits options; AMP avoids second microcatheter in the same guide; portal techniques suggested when externalisation not desired, e.g., epicardial crossing: ref ungureanu-portal-techniques-2025; ping-pong (second guide): brilakis p.447 (26.2 item 2 definition) and ref azzalini-ipsilateral-2017
- Loculated bleeding after CABG: brilakis p.462 (26.6), p.335
- Check 2: ref ungureanu-portal-techniques-2025 (portal indications, snaring only with externalisation wires after MC crossing)
- Tip pci-cto-retro-epicardial-rules: p.392, p.462, p.548, p.562; ref Ungureanu. Tip pci-cto-retro-donor-ischaemia shown here (defined under complications lesson).

## Lesson cp-cto-retrograde-grafts

- Patent grafts ideal conduit (no side branches, predictable course, large calibre); distal anastomosis angle may be unfavourable; occluded grafts usable: brilakis p.392
- Occluded SVGs usable for retrograde crossing if occlusion morphology favourable; SVG CTOs generally not recanalised (class III, LOE C): brilakis p.341 (18.7.2.2); reused tip pci-svg-chronic-occlusion
- Nguyen-Trong: SVG in 21 of 144 retrograde (14.6%); technical 86%; procedural 81%; retrograde via SVG successful 67%; MACE 2 (one MI, one fatal tamponade); reverse CART most common re-entry; two SVGs coiled for competitive flow: ref nguyen-trong-svg-retrograde-2016
- Tajti: LIMA 20/990 (2.02%); technical 70% vs 81.05%; major complications 5% vs 6%; none significant (stated in the table); RCA target 55%: ref tajti-lima-retrograde-2018
- Acutely occluded vein graft row: ad hoc native CTO PCI preferred in ACS from failure of a highly diseased SVG: brilakis p.389 (21.1), p.341 (18.7.2.1); retrograde recanalisation via acutely occluded vein grafts is the title of ch 18 ref 27 (p.352 reference list), cited on p.341; large thrombus burden in acute SVG occlusion: brilakis p.341 (18.7.2.1)
- Step 4: two-catheter technique, one for equipment (usually native) and one for visualisation (usually a graft): brilakis p.349 (18.10.1)
- Plugs occlude a vessel emergently for perforation or electively: brilakis p.592 (30.18.3)
- Coil caveats (pushable irretrievable; most stock coils 0.018 in.; avoid coil prolapse into main vessel): brilakis p.590–591 (30.18.2), p.456 (26.4.1)
- Avoid access through IMA graft when collateral donor LAD supplied by it (global ischaemia): brilakis p.392
- Straightening tortuous LIMA causes pseudolesions; vasodilators ineffective; correct with wire withdrawal: brilakis p.348 (18.8.3); reused tip pci-lima-pseudolesion
- Deep intubation and guide extensions may dissect/perforate IMA: brilakis p.339 (18.5.3); PCI through IMA grafts avoided (ischaemia, pseudolesion, dissection): brilakis p.341 (18.7.3)
- Check 1: brilakis p.392
- Reach: short guides, deep intubation, 300 cm wires, long-shaft balloons/stents: brilakis p.341 (18.8.1); guide shortening for graft routes: brilakis p.519; reused tip pci-graft-route-reach
- Supportive guide for graft intervention: brilakis p.339 (18.5.2 last bullet)
- 120° angled microcatheter for SVGs where retrograde wire is biased toward distal vessel, and reverse-angled collaterals: brilakis p.553
- Distal caps more calcified/resistant in vessels bypassed distal to the CTO: brilakis p.391 (21.6.3)
- Loculated haematoma after CABG, may need CT-guided drainage or surgery; treat perforations immediately: brilakis p.335, p.462 (26.6); reused tip pci-cabg-perforation-act-now
- Graft closure: plug use for SVG with multiple failures after native recanalisation: brilakis p.592 (30.18.3); Wilson: 33 patients, native CTO 93.9%, success 97.0%, plugs in all, single plug 72.7%, mean 20.1 min, TLF 9.1% over mean 602 days, one transient slow flow; closure after successful native PCI; competitive flow rationale: ref wilson-svg-sacrifice-2019; Xenogiannis case (native CTO recanalised, SVG then occluded with plug): ref xenogiannis-svg-plug-2020
- Landing zone length, plug 30–50% larger than vessel at occlusion site, braided nitinol mesh disc on delivery wire, retrievable, released by counterclockwise rotation with torque device; AVP II used in most SVG cases: brilakis p.592; AVP II diameters 3–22 mm and minimum delivery IDs: brilakis p.593 (Table 30.12)
- Coils can occlude a vessel; detachable preferred (retrievable, predictable); 0.014-compatible coils through standard CTO microcatheters, 0.018 coils need larger microcatheter: brilakis p.590–591 (30.18.2); SVGs coiled: ref nguyen-trong-svg-retrograde-2016
- Check 2 (plug size for 4.5 mm graft): arithmetic from brilakis p.592
- Tips: pci-cto-retro-lima-donor (p.392, p.341; ref Tajti); pci-cto-retro-svg-angled-microcatheter (p.553); pci-cto-retro-svg-plug (p.592; ref Wilson)

## Lesson cp-cto-retrograde-reverse-cart

- Reverse CART now the dominant retrograde crossing technique: ref matsuno-reverse-cart-2018; most commonly used: brilakis p.394
- Retrograde wiring usually for short occlusions, especially tapered distal cap: brilakis p.393
- Fig 27.5: retrograde true lumen puncture confirmed by IVUS, retrograde balloon dilatation, then successful antegrade wiring: brilakis p.469
- Reverse CART mechanism (balloon on antegrade wire usually subintimal, retrograde wire into space created by deflated antegrade balloon): brilakis p.394; CART defined from ref surmely-cart-2006 (subintimal dissection created antegradely and retrogradely, limited to the CTO segment); 'converse by name' is stated as such and no manual support is implied; CART listed separately: ref allana-retrograde-2023
- Marker row: ref ungureanu-portal-techniques-2025 §3.2.4 (rewiring antegradely with a conventional MC and workhorse wire using the retrograde guidewire as a marker); Fig 27.5: brilakis p.469
- Matsuno three subtypes (conventional, directed, extended) as defined: ref matsuno-reverse-cart-2018; APCTO 'contemporary reverse CART' philosophy named but not defined in the abstract, and Allana's 'contemporary' category not defined in its abstract; the lesson says so: refs wu-apcto-retrograde-2018, allana-retrograde-2023
- Retrograde knuckle through the distal cap, guide-extension-assisted reverse CART, externalisation in Fig 26.14 case: brilakis p.461
