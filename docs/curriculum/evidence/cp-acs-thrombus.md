## Lesson cp-acs-thrombus-ecg

- Oxford STEMI diagnosis: symptoms (typical chest pain ± breathlessness, nausea, fatigue, palpitation, syncope) + ST elevation in two contiguous leads or new-onset LBBB: oxford p.248
- J-point thresholds for new ST elevation (≥2 contiguous leads, V3R and V4R included; V2–V3 ≥2.5 mm men <40, ≥2 mm men ≥40, ≥1.5 mm women; ≥1 mm other leads; absent LVH or LBBB): ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.9, Figure S2, "New ST-elevation at the J-point")
- Patterns "may prompt triage for immediate reperfusion"; LBBB/paced/RBBB only with high clinical suspicion of ongoing ischaemia; it is a figure (S2), not a table: ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.9)
- Pattern table (posterior: V1–V3 depression esp. positive terminal T + ≥0.5 mm in V7–V9; LCx/RV: V7–V9 and V3R/V4R; ≥1 mm depression in ≥6 leads + aVR and/or V1 elevation → multivessel/LM esp. haemodynamic compromise; LBBB/paced criteria QRS >120 ms, no Q in I, V5, V6, monomorphic R in I, V5, V6, ST-T opposite QRS; RBBB QRS >120 ms, rsR' V1–V3, slurred S I, aVL, often V5–V6; manage like STEMI with high clinical suspicion): ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.9)
- Up to a quarter of NSTE-ACS totally occluded vessel, higher mortality; Wellens pattern described in the early 1980s (ESC ref 56 = de-zwaan-wellens-1982); later series (ESC ref 57 = de-zwaan-unstable-angina-1989): 1260 UA patients July 1980 to December 1985, 204 (16%) had the pattern, 180 analysed, all ≥50% proximal LAD, 18% total occlusion (PubMed abstract: 33 of 180 occluded); de Winter 2008 proximal LAD occlusion; patterns from old small single-centre series, yield uncertain: ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.10); refs de-zwaan-wellens-1982, de-zwaan-unstable-angina-1989, de-winter-2008
- Check 1 (posterior, V7–V9 ≥0.5 mm): ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.9)
- Culprit criterion 1 = artery supplying myocardium of ST elevation: brilakis p.143
- Anatomy table: LAD anterior wall; diagonals anterolateral; septals anterior two-thirds of septum and left bundle; terminal LAD apex, often wraps to part of inferior wall; Cx posterior wall; OMs lateral wall; RCA acute marginal branches supply RV; conus supplies RVOT; AV node branch; sinus node branch in 60%; PDA supplies inferior wall and inferior third of septum; dominance RCA 85%, Cx 8%, co-dominant 7%: oxford p.151; RV marginal branches supply predominantly RV myocardium: oxford p.152; conus cannulation/injection → ischaemia of AV node and RVOT → VF: oxford p.61; ischaemic heart block typically RCA: oxford p.312
- Culprit clues lists: brilakis p.143 (7.2.1a: ST elevation territory, hypokinesis on LV gram/echo, thrombus, staining, poor/no collaterals, ease of wiring); brilakis p.378–379 (20.7: ECG, slow flow, haziness, collaterals, occluded distal branches suggesting embolism, LV gram, OCT rupture/erosion/thrombus, TTE hypokinesis and mechanical complications)
- Mismatch: CTO not culprit, multiple culprits, review angiograms and prior angiograms, correlate ECG; OCT best for thrombus; thrombus or ulceration strongly suggests culprit: brilakis p.380 (20.8.4)
- No culprit: takotsubo (not ischaemic), OCT, MRI: brilakis p.379
- Check 2 (V4R + AV block → proximal RCA): oxford p.151–152, p.312; ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.9)
- Baseline ECG morphology and rate at start; changes of concern: new ST depression, new ST elevation, bradycardia, tachycardia, QRS widening, VPBs during wire manipulation, VF: brilakis p.37–38
- Oxford physiologist: three leads with pressure, 12-lead on request, 25 mm/s and 10 mm/mV; reports ST change, ectopy (bigeminy, couplets, triplets), bradyarrhythmias and AV block, new BBB, AF/flutter, VT, VF: oxford p.60; when to call: SBP <90 mmHg as general rule, damping/ventricularisation, ST change, significant bradycardia, new BBB especially left, heart block, AF/flutter, VT, AIVR "useful in primary PCI", VF, asystole: oxford p.61
- Embolism: ST elevation usually first sign; air, plaque, thrombus; failure to aspirate guide; shock or arrest depending on size and vessel (RCA vs LM); angiography shows reduced/absent flow: brilakis p.110–111
- Large dissections compromise flow, cause ST elevation, shock or arrest (especially LM): brilakis p.113
- No-reflow: chest pain, ST elevation, sometimes hypotension; more common after stent/post-dilation; thrombus-filled lesions, primary PCI: oxford p.267
- Fig 2.2: changes during CTO PCI; ST elevation and 35 mmHg pulsus paradoxus after distal vessel perforation in prior CABG patient: brilakis p.38
- AIVR and ST normalisation often indicate reperfusion: brilakis p.377 (20.2); AIVR reported by the physiologist as useful in primary PCI: oxford p.61. (Round 1: "do not treat the rhythm" removed; no source says it.)
- Lysis may normalise ECG but IRA flow restored in only about half; salvage if ST resolution <50% at 90 min: oxford p.248
- Takotsubo gross ST elevation, unobstructed arteries, classical LV gram: oxford p.148
- Paradoxical embolus: young, no RF, ECG suggesting RA/RV pathology: oxford p.147
- Spontaneous aortic dissection involving coronary ostia → emergency aortic root replacement, in contrast to PCI-induced aorto-coronary dissections that rarely need surgery; treatment of the latter: stop injecting, stent the ostium of the dissected artery: brilakis p.440
- Pitfalls and keypoints restate the above.

## Lesson cp-acs-thrombus-reperfusion

- Aim restore antegrade flow in IRA, reduce ischaemic time → less necrosis, less permanent damage and LV dysfunction; patent IRA halves mortality regardless of mode; most STEMIs thrombus after plaque rupture; lytics reduce MACE and improve survival, benefit greatest early and declines rapidly; by 12 h bleeding risks outweigh gains: oxford p.247
- STEMI 10% 1-year mortality, burden of heart failure; 'time is muscle'; hub-and-spoke network with prioritised ambulance: oxford p.247; STEMI requires immediate reperfusion to minimise infarct size and mortality: brilakis p.377; "time is muscle": brilakis p.143, p.378
- Network requisites (shared protocols, pre-hospital triage bypassing non-PCI hospitals or the ED, ambulance waits if diagnosis unclear at non-PCI centre): oxford p.247; Table S6 (geographic areas, shared protocols, bypass non-PCI or non-24/7 hospitals, straight to cath lab bypassing ED, monitored staffed area for waiting patients, ambulance waits and continues): ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.14); discourage daytime-only services; designate centres for acute cardiac surgery: ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.14)
- Initial care: O2 if sat <90%, opioids or benzodiazepine, aspirin + clopidogrel/ticagrelor loading: oxford p.248
- Opioids delay gastric emptying and P2Y12 absorption → thrombotic complications; avoid opioids in STEMI if not necessary; treat with IV antiplatelet (cangrelor or GPI): brilakis p.42 (3.1.3.2)
- Lytic co-therapy (aspirin 150–300 mg oral or 75–250 mg IV, then 75–100 mg/day; clopidogrel 300 mg load then 75 mg, >75 y 75 mg load; enoxaparin <75 y 30 mg IV then 15 min later 1 mg/kg s.c. q12h to revascularisation or discharge, max 8 days, first two doses ≤100 mg; >75 y no IV bolus, 0.75 mg/kg, first two doses ≤75 mg; eGFR <30 once every 24 h at any age; UFH 60 U/kg max 4000 U then 12 U/kg/h max 1000 U/h for 24–48 h, aPTT 50–70 s or 1.5–2.0× control at 3, 6, 12, 24 h; fondaparinux only with streptokinase, 2.5 mg IV then 2.5 mg s.c. daily up to 8 days or discharge); clopidogrel the only P2Y12 inhibitor listed; other lytics (streptokinase 1.5 million U over 30–60 min, contraindicated after previous streptokinase or anistreplase; alteplase 15 mg bolus, 0.75 mg/kg over 30 min up to 50 mg, 0.5 mg/kg over 60 min up to 35 mg; reteplase 10 + 10 U 30 min apart): ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.19, Table S10)
- Network row "trained physician, nurse or paramedic staff in appropriately equipped ambulances or helicopters": ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.14, Table S6)
- Short successful resuscitation not a contraindication; prolonged/traumatic successful resuscitation relative contraindication; refractory arrest: lysis ineffective, more bleeding, not recommended; streptokinase may cause hypotension, severe allergy rare, do not re-administer (antibodies, allergy): ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.20, 6.3.2)
- 12–48 h stable step: Oxford p.248–249 gives no rule for this window; OAT enrolled from day 3 (ref oat-2006); pointer to mcs-stemi-reperfusion for the 2023 ESC row
- Preloaded P2Y12 increases bleeding if emergency CABG: brilakis p.143 (7.2.5)
- Decision steps: reperfusion for pain <12 h; PPCI strategy of choice; fibrinolysis particularly if delay from STEMI diagnosis (not FMC) to PCI >120 min; lysis within 10 min of diagnosis; refer to PCI centre: oxford p.248; PPCI if diagnosis-to-PCI <120 min; >12 h with ongoing pain or haemodynamic/electrical instability; no benefit >48 h stable (OAT); avoid secondary transfers; deliver direct to PCI centre, preferably cath lab: oxford p.249; OAT details: ref oat-2006
- Check 1 (150 min > 120 from diagnosis): oxford p.248–249
- Fibrin-specific preferred; tenecteplase equivalent to accelerated tPA for 30-day mortality, fewer non-cerebral bleeds and transfusions, easier pre-hospital: ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.20, 6.3.1)
- Tenecteplase table (30/35/40/45/50 mg; 6000–10 000 U; half dose ≥75): ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.19, Table S10)
- Contraindications table: ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.20, Table S11)
- Hazards: small significant excess stroke mostly cerebral haemorrhage, first day; ICH 0.9–1.0% in latest trials; predictors (advanced age, lower weight, female, previous cerebrovascular disease, Black population, prior stroke, systolic and diastolic hypertension); advanced age associated with LV free-wall rupture; major non-cerebral bleeds 4–13%; GI commonest; risk greater in women; STREAM ICH excess ≥75 reduced after 50% dose reduction: ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.20, 6.3.2)
- Rescue criteria (ST resolution <50% at 90 min, electric/haemodynamic instability, persisting pain); flow restored in about half: oxford p.248
- Pharmaco-invasive: transfer after lysis, angiogram as rescue or non-urgent; 2–24 h; superior to lysis alone (CARESS-AMI, TRANSFER AMI); non-inferior to PPCI within 3 h of symptoms and PCI delay >1 h: oxford p.251
- STREAM details: ref stream-2013 (PubMed abstract)
- Facilitated PCI definition (upfront lysis and/or GPIs, PCI in every case regardless), meant to buy time; ASSENT-4 and FINESSE disproved; ASSENT-4 stopped early for death, HF, ICH, ischaemic events; pharmaco-invasive recommended: oxford p.251. (Round 1: the failed-lysis GPI option in check 2 and in the transfer case is no longer called facilitated PCI; it is taught as ignoring failed lysis and adding bleeding risk, with IV antiplatelet agents kept for large thrombus or bail-out: brilakis p.377 (20.3); GPI bleeding common: oxford p.245; no GPI in Table S10.)
- Check 2 (30% resolution = failed lysis; facilitated PCI): oxford p.248, p.251
- Ad hoc PCI needed in most ACS with active ischaemia because of CABG delays; delayed PCI list (complex disease, contrast/radiation, site of service, consent, fatigue, pretreatment, preference); scheduling example of new STEMI while ad hoc PCI considered: brilakis p.145–146
- Consent problems (moribund, opiates; best interests or proxy): oxford p.249
- Radial conversion for arteria lusoria: brilakis p.377–378

## Lesson cp-acs-thrombus-ppci

- ACS more likely haemodynamic instability and arrhythmias: brilakis p.377 (20.2); more likely to need haemodynamic support: brilakis p.386 (20.14)
- Planning: prior stenting (stent thrombosis), CABG anatomy, EF (shock risk), comorbidities (renal failure, diabetes, future surgery), medications; postpartum → SCAD; stressful event in women → takotsubo, angiography often still needed: brilakis p.377 (20.1)
- Consent: oxford p.249
- Drugs: potent anticoagulation and antiplatelet; prasugrel/ticagrelor; ± GP IIb/IIIa or cangrelor for large thrombus or bailout; UFH most common: brilakis p.377 (20.3); UFH standard intra- and post-procedurally (MATRIX, HEAT-PPCI), bivalirudin alternative; anticoagulant/antiplatelet as early as practical per local protocol: oxford p.249; MATRIX anticoagulant arm: ref matrix-antithrombin-2015 (matrix-2015 is the access trial)
- Access: radial default; MATRIX, RIFLE, RIVAL prognostic benefit in PPCI: oxford p.249; radial preferred (lower bleeding and mortality), SAFARI-STEMI no difference, femoral in prior CABG, convert for delays (arteria lusoria): brilakis p.377–378; ref matrix-2015
- Engagement: 8–13 min saving; RCA culprit without left anatomy risks (support, CABG, balloon only): brilakis p.378
- Dottering not reliable, may restore flow; aspiration clarifies anatomy distal and side branch vs main vessel: brilakis p.380 (20.8.3, 20.8.4)
- Restore flow: wire alone sometimes; small balloon usually 2 mm at 6–8 atm: brilakis p.381; balloon angioplasty restores flow and helps assess lesion length and vessel diameter: brilakis p.381 (20.9.2)
- DES standard (EXAMINATION, NORSTENT, COMFORTABLE AMI) mainly repeat revascularisation; PAMI no mortality benefit, lower reinfarction/urgent revasc: oxford p.249; Stent-PAMI 900 patients, 6-month mortality 4.2% vs 2.7% (NS), composite reduction entirely from ischaemia-driven TVR 7.7% vs 17.0%: ref stent-pami-1999; thrombus → undersizing and malapposition: brilakis p.385; trapped thrombus dissolving later → malapposition: oxford p.273
- Imaging: OCT gold standard for culprit (ulceration, thrombus, some erosion only); after stenting expansion, apposition, geographic miss, residual thrombus, dissection: brilakis p.386 (20.13)
- Physiology: not in culprit (microcirculation injury); non-culprit measurements do not change significantly: brilakis p.386 (20.12); 2023 ESC: non-IRA PCI in stable STEMI based on angiographic severity (class I, LoE B); FLOWER-MI 5.5% FFR vs 4.2% angiography, no significant difference: ref licordari-esc-acs-2024
- Complete revascularisation (COMPLETE); culprit-only in shock (CULPRIT-SHOCK): brilakis p.379; refs complete-2019, culprit-shock-2017; 2023 ESC complete revascularisation during index PCI or within 45 days (class I, LoE A): ref licordari-esc-acs-2024
- Closure: radial advantageous with potent regimens; femoral sheath left a few hours in borderline haemodynamics, increased bleeding: brilakis p.385 (20.11)
- ACT table: brilakis p.377, p.39 (2.8: 300–350 s most procedures, >350 s retrograde CTO, 200–250 s with GPI/cangrelor); oxford p.245 (70–100 IU/kg intra-arterial, operator discretion, ACT >250–300 s; GPIs used with heparin); Hemotec vs Hemochron (>250 s Hemotec, >300 s Hemochron; Hemochron reads higher): brilakis p.441; Oxford names no analyser ("bedside assay"): oxford p.245. (Round 1: the paragraph no longer claims the device explains the gap between the books. Analyser names kept in body text per MANUAL_AUTHORING rule 7.)
- Check 1: brilakis p.377, p.39; oxford p.245
- Flow-failure checklist (large residual thrombus → aspiration or laser; proximal inflation in TIMI 0; severe distal disease; wire not distal; small branch → leave wire and rewire; perforation if difficult wiring; drugs crucial): brilakis p.381 (20.9.1)
- Check 2 (leave wire, second wire; no injection through OTW/microcatheter): brilakis p.381, p.380
- Aspiration catheter in 6 Fr guide "may lead to pressure dampening"; thrombus within the catheter, injection → coronary or systemic embolism: brilakis p.38 (2.3)
- Pearl: wire crossing "sometimes" restores flow, small balloon "may restore some flow" (now "often", no frequency): brilakis p.381

## Lesson cp-acs-thrombus-thrombus

- Potent anticoagulation/antiplatelet prevents thrombus expansion and new thrombus: brilakis p.377; causes of thrombosis (inadequate anticoagulant/antiplatelet, underexpansion, hypercoagulable incl. HIT, venous access failure): brilakis p.441 (25.2.2)
- GPIs established in ACS PCI particularly with thrombus; used with heparin; abciximab irreversible; tirofiban, eptifibatide reversible, half-life <1 h; bleeding common, platelet infusions especially abciximab: oxford p.245; plasma half-life of tirofiban and eptifibatide about 2 h, paralleling antiplatelet effect: ref schror-gpi-pharmacology-2003; IV agent for large thrombus or bail-out: brilakis p.377 (20.3); abciximab statements labelled as Oxford's 2020 text, abciximab recently withdrawn: ref unzue-post-abciximab-2021; withdrawn in many countries: Academy pci-core (tracks/pci-core.ts, GP IIb/IIIa paragraph) (round 2)
- Algorithm branches: brilakis p.380 (Fig. 20.2, imaged), p.381 (20.9.1–20.9.5: large thrombus balloon/stent → severe distal embolisation; ongoing ischaemia critical; 24–48 h intense antithrombotic therapy incl. aspirin, oral P2Y12, IV platelet inhibitor and/or IV antithrombotic; repeat angiography often less thrombus, lower embolisation and no-reflow); thrombectomy options: brilakis p.381–382
- Check 1: brilakis p.380–381
- Reconciling paragraph (round 1): Oxford unqualified "current guidelines do not recommend a deferring stenting strategy": oxford p.250; ESC allows deferral in selected cases where no-reflow likely and clinically significant (e.g. heavy thrombus): ESC supp p.24; overlap with, not identity to, Brilakis's subgroup: brilakis p.381
- Deferral table: Brilakis p.381; Oxford p.249–250 (rationale, theoretical benefits, DANAMI-DEFER 48 h no improvement, more urgent revascularisation, guidelines do not recommend); ESC supp p.23–24 (DEFER-STEMI 12–48 h reduced angiographic no-reflow and intraprocedural thrombotic events; DANAMI-3-DEFER 1215, 48 h, no effect on outcome, microvascular function or infarct size at 3 months, more acute TVR for reocclusion; routine deferral not recommended; selected cases with heavy thrombus)
- DANAMI-3-DEFER 18% vs 17%, median 42 months: ref danami3-defer-2016
- Proceed with large thrombus due to ongoing symptoms or high-risk plaque → consider thrombectomy; reasonable in selected cases, routine use not supported by RCTs (reworded, round 1): brilakis p.381; TOTAL figures: ref total-2015; Oxford reserves aspiration for large thrombotic burden: oxford p.249, p.273
- Filters capture thrombus; approved only for SVGs in US: brilakis p.379, p.381, p.567; also native lesions at high risk of distal embolisation such as large lipid-core plaque: brilakis p.567 (30.8)
- Laser: burns through thrombus, inhibits platelet aggregation; 0.9 mm most common in native, larger for SVG/very large native; 5-min warm-up: brilakis p.381; 0.9 mm compatible with 6 Fr guide; coronary laser catheters lase 10 s then off 5 s; lasing usually with saline infusion: brilakis p.582 (30.11)
- Rheolytic thrombectomy higher mortality (AiMI), rarely used: brilakis p.582
- Direct stenting: primary stenting may be preferred for lesion with thrombus when no thrombectomy planned or thrombectomy fails: brilakis p.168 (9.6); favouring direct stenting is an aim of thrombectomy: oxford p.273
- Stenting over thrombus (usually delayed until after thrombectomy; trap; cheese grater; undersizing and malapposition): brilakis p.385 (20.10)
- No stent: Escaned 28 STEMI patients, four criteria, patent at 6 ± 2 days; especially small vessel: brilakis p.385 (20.10.1, imaged); primary paper: single-centre retrospective, scheduled control angiography in 11 of 28 (all patent, TIMI 3), minor distal embolisation in 10, one sudden cardiac death on follow-up; authors call for larger studies: ref escaned-lone-aspiration-2013. "Not a guideline recommendation" and "plan a repeat angiogram, as the series did" follow from these facts.
- Check 2: brilakis p.385
- Embolisation: ST elevation first sign, shock/arrest, angiography: brilakis p.111; no-reflow TIMI 0–1 and no blush, chest pain, ST elevation, hypotension: oxford p.267; aspiration or initial balloon predilation of STEMI culprit causes plaque embolisation; avoid stenting plaque embolisation, aspirate, laser if aspiration fails, vasodilators: brilakis p.443–444; thrombus forming in sheath; aspirate and flush sheath with different syringe: brilakis p.442; treatment of thrombus/plaque emboli (anticoagulation + IV antiplatelet, thrombectomy if large visible thrombus, nicardipine, nitroprusside, verapamil, adenosine; support): brilakis p.111; embolised thrombus often easier to aspirate: brilakis p.382; once established difficult to treat, prevention is the key, drugs used for prevention may be useful; avoid repeated contrast injections, flow improves with time and vasodilators, IABP if hypotension persists: oxford p.267
- Oxford prevention list; more common after stent/post-dilation; associated with short- and long-term mortality: oxford p.267

## Lesson cp-acs-thrombus-rv-posterior

- Anatomy table: oxford p.151–152; ESC patterns: ref esc-acs-2023 (ESC supp p.9); ischaemic block typically RCA: oxford p.312
- Check 1 and nitrate rules (do not give with hypotension or RV infarction, no route exception; causes venous and arterial dilatation; acute inferior MI with RV infarction as cause of hypotension; stop nitrate, give normal saline; half-life 1.5–7.5 min; waiting, hypotension often resolves after a few minutes; vasopressors (norepinephrine or phenylephrine) if extreme or persistent; if persistent assess other causes such as bleeding; prevention: no nitrate after recent PDE-5 inhibitor or in HOCM): brilakis p.42 (3.2.1.3), p.124 (6.3.3)

- Avoid preload-lowering drugs (nitrates, opiates) in RV infarction with HF; hypotension + raised JVP + clear lungs = hallmark of RV failure; loop diuretics considered only with raised CVP and congestion: ref nagele-rv-infarction-hf-2022
- Atropine 0.5 mg IV repeat to total 3 mg; lowest dose to avoid rebound tachycardia; dry mouth, blurred vision, flushing: brilakis p.58; Oxford atropine 0.5–1 mg for transient bradycardia; atropine 1 mg IV may improve AV conduction; isoprenaline rarely improves conduction but increases sinus automaticity; IV isoprenaline bolus 25–50, infusion 5–10 micrograms/min: oxford p.312
- Bradycardia steps (transient during injection; vasovagal precedes collapse; advanced block with conduction delay and mechanical or ischaemic cause; recovery within minutes unless ongoing ischaemia; first-degree and Wenckebach no urgent intervention; Mobitz II and CHB may need urgent temporary transvenous pacing): oxford p.312
- External pacing (temporising; pads apex and right parasternal; rate 70 demand; capture confirmed with pulse; uncomfortable, sedation): oxford p.277–278; transvenous (femoral vein usually, sheath larger than wire, RV apex, threshold ≤1 V desirable, output ≥3× threshold): oxford p.277–278
- Hypotension algorithm (false hypotension causes; hypovolaemia, cardiac failure incl. RV failure, vasodilation; LV filling pressure can be low in RV failure; RHC when cause unclear): brilakis p.485–486 (28.1, Fig. 28.1)
- PAPi formula and thresholds (RA >12 mmHg, PAPI <0.9): brilakis p.251 (14.1.8); low PaPi suggests RV failure: brilakis p.249 (14.1.1)
- "Before any device" step: reperfusion; 250 mL saline challenges if hypotensive with low or normal CVP and no pulmonary oedema, stop when CVP >12 mmHg without BP rise (more volume dilates RV, may worsen ischaemia); noradrenaline first line after preload optimisation; dobutamine for persisting hypoperfusion; AV-synchronous pacing preferred to ventricular pacing alone with ongoing AV dissociation (RV output dependent on rate and AV synchrony); MCS for shock persisting despite preload/afterload optimisation and vasopressors/inotropes; evidence mainly small studies, extrapolation and expert opinion: ref nagele-rv-infarction-hf-2022
