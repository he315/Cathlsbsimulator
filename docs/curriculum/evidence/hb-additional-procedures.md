## Lesson hb-additional-procedures-pacing

- Academy lessons bed-pacing-indications, bed-pacing-insertion, bed-pacing-settings exist and cover ward indications, lead types, routes, troubleshooting (src/content/tracks/procedures-career.ts).
- Transient bradycardia/junctional rhythm common during injection, recovers spontaneously or with atropine 0.5–1 mg IV; may be a sign of vasovagal reaction and precede collapse: oxford p.312
- Advanced block usually with known conduction delay, mechanical damage to (usually right) bundle branch or ischaemia (typically RCA); recovery usually within minutes unless ongoing ischaemia; first-degree and Wenckebach no urgent intervention; Mobitz II and complete block may need urgent temporary transvenous pacing; atropine 1 mg IV may improve AV conduction; IV isoprenaline (bolus 25–50, infusion 5–10 micrograms/min) or other sympathomimetics rarely improve conduction but can increase sinus node automaticity: oxford p.312 (bolus unit taken as micrograms from the same line)
- AV block definitions table (first degree PR >200 ms, QRS after every P; Mobitz I progressive PR lengthening to a non-conducted P; Mobitz II fixed-ratio non-conduction without preceding PR lengthening; complete block: no P conducts, slow dissociated escape, narrow or wide by origin): oxford pp.312–313 (Figs 10.8–10.11 legends); urgency column: oxford p.312
- Adenosine AV block: short half-life, resolves in seconds; cough; if block without escape persists, transient CPR and temporary pacing; avoid >100 mcg IC in RCA: brilakis p.224 (12.2.6.4.1)
- Amiodarone: AV nodal effect, bradycardia/AV block; do not give in 2nd/3rd degree block without pacemaker; discontinue; temporary pacing may be needed: brilakis p.57 (3.7.1.3.2)
- Atherectomy: pacemaker not routinely needed for LAD and non-dominant Cx; RCA or dominant Cx: temporary pacemaker, aminophylline (250–300 mg IV over 10 min or 20–40 mg IC bolus), or brief test runs; test runs allow tailoring and avoid RV perforation risk from the lead; orbital may be less likely than rotational to cause bradycardia; bigger burrs and longer runs more likely: brilakis p.360 (19.9.4). Atropine (0.5–1.0 mg IV) before atherectomy as an alternative: brilakis p.355 (19.3); temporary pacemaker, aminophylline or atropine to prevent bradycardia: brilakis p.365 (19.9.5.7.4)
- Stable VT only: 'Stable monomorphic VT may respond to overdrive pacing'; persisting VT → synchronised DC shock under sedation/GA (200–360 J or biphasic equivalent): oxford p.310. Poorly tolerated VT → immediate synchronised DC cardioversion under sedation/GA; reasonably tolerated VT → consider beta-blocker or amiodarone, prepare for DC cardioversion: oxford p.309; lidocaine if BP stable: oxford p.310
- Physiologist delivers the shock when instructed; role typically assigned at team briefing: oxford p.59
- Overdrive pacing (80–90%; details below): oxford p.310
- Rapid pacing during stent deployment can minimise movement: brilakis p.191 (10.2), p.281 (15.1.10.4, ostial)
- BAV rapid pacing: ref keeble-bav-2016; ASA wire because heart block most common complication: ref rigopoulos-asa-2016
- External pacing as temporising measure: oxford p.277
- Check 1: oxford p.312; brilakis p.224
- Femoral vein usual; sheath larger diameter than the wire: oxford p.277. Femoral vein may be used for temporary pacing wires in anticoagulated or clotting abnormalities (low complication risk): oxford p.80
- "So the wire turns and slides freely": author's gloss on the larger sheath (Oxford gives no reason).
- Under X-ray into RA; J-shaped distal contour, tip directed by rotating the proximal end: oxford p.277
- RV apex just medial to the apex of the cardiac silhouette on AP: oxford p.277
- Loop in the atrium, tip on right lateral atrial border, rotation and advancement → prolapse through TV: oxford p.277. Same loop for right heart catheters ("similar to the technique used for pacing leads"): oxford p.111
- Ectopics on entering the ventricle confirm position: oxford p.277
- Tip curves down to apex; final shape heel of a sock in RA, toe in RV apex (Fig 9.2): oxford p.277 (image read)
- Coronary sinus orifice above TV; more cranial on AP; posterior on lateral vs anterior RV lead: oxford p.277
- RBBB paced pattern suggests LV capture (coronary sinus etc.): Academy lesson bed-pacing-insertion (keypoints)
- Check 2: oxford p.277; "pushing harder risks perforating the thin RV wall": oxford p.305 (RV relatively thin walled; pacing wire manipulation as tamponade cause)
- Threshold: pace above intrinsic rate, start 3 V, turn down slowly to loss of capture, increase slowly to recapture = threshold; ≤1 V desirable; output ≥3× threshold: oxford pp.277–278
- Demand at e.g. 70; paces when no ventricular activity sensed above rate, red pace light; inhibits when spontaneous rate higher, sense light; output ≥3× threshold; lower setting more sensitive; sensitivity so each intrinsic beat detected but skeletal muscle interference does not inhibit; ensure demand, asynchronous risks ventricular arrhythmias: oxford p.278
- Suture lead and sheath, transparent occlusive dressings, tape/fixatives, skin loop so tugs tighten the loop: oxford p.278
- Fig 9.3 box: sensitivity in mV, output in V: oxford p.278 (image read). Academy bed-pacing-settings uses mA; CathMaster pacing lab output knob in mA (src/ui/bedside/PacingLab.tsx, "Output" unit "mA").
- External pacing: pads apex and right parasternal, rate 70 demand, output up to electrical capture, confirm with pulse, uncomfortable skeletal muscle contraction, ECG artefact, sedation usually required: oxford p.278. Femoral pulse/arterial trace better than carotid: Academy bed-pacing-settings
- Overdrive: 80–90%; 15–20 bpm faster than VT; ×3 setting; 5–10 V; capture = QRS morphology change and rate rise to pacing rate; abrupt stop after 5–10 s capture; acceleration/degeneration to pulseless VT/VF, be prepared to defibrillate; background pacing 90–110 bpm, useful with pauses/bradycardia: oxford p.310
- Physiologist: checks temporary pacing box working and stocked; cooperates in setting pacing rate, threshold and amplitude: oxford p.59; records venous access (for temporary pacing) and settings of temporary pacing in the log: oxford p.62
- Cannon waves in complete heart block (large a waves, RA contracting against closed TV): oxford p.114 (Fig 4.18 legend)
- Tamponade causes include manipulation of catheters and pacing wires in the thin-walled RV; rapid deterioration with tachycardia, hypotension; echo confirms, high suspicion → immediate pericardiocentesis: oxford p.305
- Brilakis test runs avoid RV perforation risk: brilakis p.360
- Pitfall 'Leaving the box asynchronous': oxford p.278 (risk of inducing ventricular arrhythmias); the R-on-T mechanism was removed in round 1.
- Pearl (physiologist reads back four numbers): teaching gloss built on oxford pp.59, 62, 278.
- Sim: PacingLab scenario short label "Inferior STEMI · CHB", femoral route, lateral view, output in mA, VVI (src/simulation/pacing.ts, src/ui/bedside/PacingLab.tsx).

- ESC 2025 and 2021 statements: refs above.

JR4 or AL1; AL2 for horizontal root/vertical valve; AR1; gentle rotation; hydrophilic wire if needed; AP or 10–20° LAO; exchange via J-tipped long wire to pigtail; simultaneous pigtail and femoral sheath side port, dual-lumen pigtail more accurate;
