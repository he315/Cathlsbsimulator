The figure is implausible for a guidewire and could not be verified, so it was left out rather than repeated. Oxford p.61 says the conus branch supplies the AV node; not repeated (anatomically doubtful and not needed).

## Lesson pm-engaging-trace

- Pressure check is a formal step before engagement (5.6), after engagement (5.8) and around angiography (6.1, 6.5, 6.7): brilakis p.111, p.120, p.123, p.126, p.128
- Trace confirms tip free and lumen clear of thrombus, plaque, air: brilakis p.111 (5.6.1), p.123 (6.1.1)
- Aspirate 2–3 mL, discard ideally onto white gauze; thrombus usually from sheath; aortic plaque often iridescent from cholesterol crystals: brilakis p.108
- Causes of failed aspiration: against aortic/coronary wall, next to ostial lesion, too large for artery; reposition by slight withdrawal; different catheter or withdraw and manually reshape the tip; prevention by catheter selection for aortic size and ostial size and location; vacuum may suck tip onto wall or into stenosis so back-bleed instead: brilakis p.108
- Connect hub while manifold flushing; stop flush, aspirate into manifold syringe, discard air/debris; after ensuring no air or debris, inject contrast until it exits the tip: brilakis p.110 (5.5.2)
- Baseline ECG morphology and rate evaluated at start so later changes are identified: brilakis p.37 (2.2)
- Damping and ventricularisation can be mild or severe; example Fig 5.15: brilakis p.111. Oxford Fig 6.2: oxford p.156. The table's descriptions of the free and damped trace are labelled as the module's own wording (compare with baseline; "dampening or disappearance" brilakis p.38)
- Damped or lost waveform may reflect true aortic pressure: brilakis p.38 (2.3). Causes of hypotension at the nitrate step: nitroglycerin, radial verapamil, RV infarction, hypovolaemia (fasted), PDE5 inhibitors, vasovagal, transducer or connection issues (pseudohypotension): brilakis p.124 (6.3.3.1)
- Causes of damping at the tip (wall, deep or subselective, ostial lesion, too large): brilakis p.111 (5.6.3.1)
- Causes in the system: plaque/thrombus/air in catheter p.112 (5.6.3.2); kink p.112 (5.6.3.3); transducer bubble, switch off p.112 (5.6.3.4); thrombus in catheter, kinking, aspiration catheter in 6 Fr guide, transducer disconnection: brilakis p.38 (2.3)
- Ventricularised description ("systolic pressure dips", not "a little"): dip in systolic, ventricularisation of diastolic, presystolic deflection, slower upstroke, steeper downstroke, a waves: oxford p.155 (box), p.156 (Fig 6.2 caption)
- Causes of damping in left main (LMS stenosis, tip angled into wall, spasm, selective LAD/Cx with short LMS) and RCA (conus, small non-dominant): oxford p.155
- Catheter-tip spasm: ventricularises then falls, rises only after withdrawal; commonest in non-dominant RCA and vessels of similar calibre to catheter, shorter low-weight patients: oxford p.173; pressure declines a few seconds after engagement: oxford p.180
- AS trace may look dampened but is parvus and tardus: brilakis p.111
- Side holes can mask dampening: brilakis p.111; Tiger has a side hole just distal to the tip to minimise damping on intubation: oxford p.157; side-hole guides should never be used in unprotected left main: brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3); suboptimal position unrecognised → global ischaemia and collapse: brilakis p.522 (30.2.5)
- Fluid-filled line between tip and transducer can cause a timing delay and some damping (taught as 'can', not 'always'); catheter impact, whip and end-pressure artefacts: oxford p.95
- Check stem numbers (135/75, 125, 10 mmHg) are illustrative; the pattern is from oxford p.155
- Test injection 1–2 mL; confirms coaxial engagement and avoids tenting that could dissect; may be skipped when the operator is certain from catheter movement and "jump" (experience not mentioned): brilakis p.123 (6.2)
- Before cine: no dampening, ventricularisation, new hypotension; no new ECG changes; contrast may cause ST elevation or depression or T-wave inversion: brilakis p.126 (6.5.2)
- Contrast: transient bradycardia that usually resolves within a few seconds; transient ST changes and QRS widening are commonly seen: oxford p.101
- After each run: dampening, new hypotension, ST or rhythm changes such as VF: brilakis p.128 (6.7)
- Catheter occluding flow → hypotension, arrhythmia, VF; withdraw and re-engage more carefully or smaller French: brilakis p.126 (6.5.4)
- Poor pressure signal: flush transducer and check connections; poor ECG: reposition electrodes: brilakis p.126 (6.5.3)
- Cardiac physiologist notifies team of significant fall in BP from baseline (general rule systolic <90 mmHg), damping/ventricularisation, ST change, bradycardia, block, BBB, VT, VF: oxford p.61
- Never inject with damping; gentle manual injection rather than automated systems in this setting: brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3), p.127 (6.6.3.1, manual preferred with slight dampening); the lesson states the never-inject rule alongside it. Fluid bolus sentence in check 2 explanation is the module's reasoning tied to the hypotension causes on brilakis p.124
- Dissection causes (non-coaxial, injection despite dampening, ostial lesion): brilakis p.113; full list (deep, subintimal or noncoaxial intubation, tip at edge of or under eccentric plaque especially LM, ostial lesion, forceful injection, dampened waveform): brilakis p.123 (6.2.3.1); avoid moving engaged catheter, especially advancing against plaque; LIMA ostium prone to dissection: oxford p.321; aorto-coronary dissection, contrast staining: brilakis p.114; embolism (ST elevation first sign) p.111; systemic embolism incl stroke most feared p.110
- VF causes: manipulation near stenosed ostium, plugging (intermediate vessel), conus injection: oxford p.309
- Ventricularised trace in check 1 is the reason not to inject: oxford p.321 ("Do not inject with a damped or ventricularized trace")

## Lesson pm-engaging-damping

- Intro wording: "simplest checks come first" is the module's description of the order; no frequency ranking claimed
- Six-step damping algorithm (withdraw/reposition; aspirate; check transducer; check kink; remove without injecting or advancing wire while maintaining suction; aspirate sheath): brilakis p.112 (5.6.3.5), p.113 (Fig 5.16)
- Failed aspiration algorithm (partial withdrawal few mm, waveform reappears; fluoroscopy whole length for kink; presumed plugged, warning not to wire or flush, stroke or acute coronary occlusion; remove with constant suction; aspirate sheath because thrombus forms in sheath and is scooped up): brilakis p.109–110 (5.4.3.4, Fig 5.14)
- Forceful aspiration may suck air through Y-connector valve; all air removed before next injection: brilakis p.108
- Prevention: aspirate sheath and discard, flush with heparinised saline; long femoral sheaths with severe iliac/aortic disease; flush catheters before insertion: brilakis p.108, p.112
- Ostial lesions after engagement: keep disengaged, 0.014 in wire, intermittent engagement, smaller French or different shape, manual reshaping: brilakis p.120 (5.8.1)
- Small target vessel: keep disengaged with 0.014 wire; smaller French: brilakis p.120 (5.8.2)
- Catheter-tip spasm: nitrate after careful re-engagement discriminates spasm vs ostial disease; withdrawal may be needed; acquire image documenting relief: oxford p.173–174; catheter-tip spasm most commonly occurs after ostial engagement: oxford p.173; over-engagement may result in spasm especially RCA (taught as a possible trigger, not the usual one); pressure declines a few seconds after engagement; withdraw and nitrates if required: oxford p.180. "With a free trace" is the module's application of oxford p.321 (do not inject with a damped or ventricularised trace)
- Aorto-ostial extras: wire to guide tip before engagement, extra-support wires help engagement/disengagement/coaxial alignment; floating wire; dedicated device keeping guide outside the ostium (Ostial Pro, named generically); small guide extension to back out the guide; less aggressive guides (JR4, sometimes IM for shepherd's crook) for isolated aorto-ostial disease, less support than AL: brilakis p.272–273; nonselective angiography with severe dampening reduces dissection risk but poorer images: brilakis p.274 (15.1.6). Side-hole costs: contrast exit hinders visualisation and raises contrast volume: brilakis p.272, p.522; exception for ostial LM CTOs; hand-made side holes block guide extension and weaken guide (kinking): brilakis p.522
- Aorto-ostial guides: 6 Fr, avoid deep engagement, larger guides more likely to damp; disengage by torquing to non-coaxial position rather than pulling back (loss of wire): brilakis p.272–273 (15.1.5)
- Side-hole guides: can be used in RCA, grafts, protected LM; not unprotected LM; not RCA donor to occluded left vessel: brilakis p.272
- Coronary dissection presentation (large: flow compromise, ST elevation, shock/arrest esp LM; small: filling defects, assess ostium at end, IVUS/OCT): brilakis p.113
- Treatment: do not lose wire position; stenting for larger; small non-flow-limiting often no treatment; ping-pong via diagnostic catheter and second access: brilakis p.113–114; stop injecting, gentle retraction, workhorse wire into true lumen or distal re-entry with CTO techniques, possibly a guide with a slightly different distal curve, intravascular imaging strongly recommended to ensure ostial coverage: brilakis p.124 (6.2.3.1)
- Aorto-coronary dissection: staining; do not inject; stent ostium with high-pressure flaring; covered stent with restenosis/thrombosis risk: brilakis p.114
- Coronary embolism: ST elevation first sign; flow reduction; shock/arrest: brilakis p.111; air: 100% oxygen, aspiration through guide or thrombectomy catheter, repetitive forceful saline, intracoronary epinephrine (0.05 mg) has also been used; plaque/thrombus: anticoagulation and IV antiplatelet (GP IIb/IIIa or cangrelor), thrombectomy for large thrombus, IC vasodilators; haemodynamic support: brilakis p.111
- Systemic embolism: stroke signs focal weakness or vision loss; leg: pulses decrease or disappear, lower extremity discomfort; stroke → emergency head CT, neurology, neurointervention; other sites usually conservative, sometimes endovascular: brilakis p.110
- Check (JR4 damps in small RCA with ostial narrowing): manage per 5.8.1/5.8.2 (p.120); automated injector warning p.113
- Pearl: the algorithm's steps only withdraw, aspirate or check (structure of p.112)

## Lesson pm-engaging-left

- Planning (prior catheters; change access site if previous difficulty, e.g. femoral; prior CABG anatomy; CT aortic dilation and anomalies; AS or AR with dilated ascending aorta needing larger catheters): brilakis p.34 (1.5)
- Document catheters used / check prior study: oxford p.153, p.103
- Optimal engagement: no dampening; coaxial, orthogonal projections to verify; 2–3 mm depth; deep engagement adds support but dissection, dampening, ischaemia: brilakis p.114 (5.7.1)
- Femoral PCI guides EBU, XB, Voda; EBU 3.75 and XB 3.5 in most cases; EBU 4.0, XB 4.0, Voda 4.0 for Cx; EBU 3.5 for LAD; JL guides poor support: brilakis p.523 (30.2.6.2)
- EBU 3.0 or 3.5 for LAD, EBU 4.0 or 4.5 for Cx: brilakis p.115
- Radial diagnostic universal (TIG, Kimny, TRA); radial PCI EBU, XB, Ikari left: brilakis p.523
- JL4 femoral or left radial; JL3.5 right radial; JL3.5 for short patients, small roots, superior LMS origin, separate LAD origin; JL5/JL6 for dilated root or very tall; AL for dilated sinuses or unusual ostia; JL 90° primary and 180° secondary curve: oxford p.153
- JL size = distance between primary and secondary curves, 3.5, 4, 5, 6 cm: brilakis p.522 (30.2.6)
- Dilated ascending aorta: diameter + 1 = JL size (4 cm → JL5): brilakis p.522
- Femoral technique: 0.035 in wire looped over valve; tip advanced 2–3 cm higher than bottom of right cusp at upward reflection of the J; wire withdrawn, catheter folds into left cusp; too far into right cusp drives it deeper there: brilakis p.114, p.115 (Figs 5.17, 5.18)
- Rotation: some rotation sometimes needed; counterclockwise may be required as LM usually posterior: brilakis p.115. Oxford: occasionally gentle clockwise (posterior) or anticlockwise (anterior): oxford p.155. Tiger: anticlockwise in left sinus to engage left coronary: oxford p.157. (Labels differ; the lesson says so and tells the learner to watch the tip.)
- If LMS not visualised easily, change catheter size or type: oxford p.155
- Test injections may identify anomalous origins and unusual take-off: oxford p.155
- Cannot aspirate: tip against aortic wall or tightly engaged in LMS; pull back until blood flows; injection without doing so risks air embolism: oxford p.155
- Deep breaths can make the catheter disengage during injection, especially radial: brilakis p.127 (6.6.3.2)
- Tiger side hole just distal to the tip minimises damping on intubation: oxford p.157; side holes mask damping: brilakis p.111
- Guide support for left PCI: LAD JL3.5/4 (+), AL2 (++), XB/EBU3.5 (+++); Cx JL4/5 (+), AL2 (++), Voda/XB/EBU3.5/4 (+++); larger calibre more support (8>7>6 F): oxford p.214 (Table 8.4), rewritten as prose
- Wire withdrawal can engage LMS immediately; watch trace; cannot aspirate → pull back gently until blood flows; little manipulation, slow withdrawal; PA or LAO imaging: oxford p.155
- AP usual initial projection for left main ostium: brilakis p.125 (6.4.2)
- Radial universal: wire looped over valve, advance while withdrawing wire until tip enters LM, then usually withdraw for coaxial alignment; standard JL/XB/EBU also usable especially left radial: brilakis p.115
- TIG: tip in left sinus, anticlockwise rotation, slight withdrawal; then gentle clockwise to fully engage; PA for left; disengage by gentle withdrawal; use standard Judkins if TIG fails: oxford p.157
- Deep inspiration helps especially right radial: oxford p.155
- Short LMS: JL selectively engages LAD → one size larger normally engages Cx (paraphrased): oxford p.180
- Separate LAD/Cx ostia and anterior RCA are normal variants: oxford p.181
- Anomalous Cx commonest anomaly 0.2–0.7%, from proximal RCA or right sinus; retro-aortic course to posterolateral LV; dot sign at ventriculography or absence of vessel to lateral wall; if overlooked, incomplete revascularisation or ligation during valve replacement surgery; retro-aortic segment often very small, difficult to graft: oxford p.181
- Sudden death reported with anomalous LCA vessels passing between aorta and pulmonary artery: oxford p.181
- Left sinus anomalies: JL of unusual size or AL; MP useful but aggressive angle, dissection: oxford p.182

## Lesson pm-engaging-rca

- JR4 femoral: advance to bottom of right cusp (contrast with 2–3 cm above for LM); withdraw wire; retract a few centimetres; rotate clockwise with right hand while left hand performs back-and-forth to transmit rotation to tip: brilakis p.116 (Fig 5.19)
- JR catheters require rotation and withdrawal: oxford p.156. The JR4 'Watch in LAO' step no longer claims the tip moves anteriorly
- Intro and pitfall: conus easily cannulated during manipulation at the ostium: oxford p.157; back-and-forth transmits rotation from hub to tip: brilakis p.116 (no stored-torque claim)
- LAO imaging; clockwise torque with gentle withdrawal; tip may dive particularly in large dominant vessels; careful withdrawal or anticlockwise rotation; confirm position before acquisition; too much torque can pull catheter out: oxford p.156
- "Jump" of catheter on engagement: brilakis p.123
- Williams right: preshaped, limited manipulation, occasional clockwise rotation, useful for aorto-ostial RCA lesions: brilakis p.116
- AL1: advance until tip faces up, rotate to engage; strongest support, complex RCA PCI, CTO: brilakis p.116; AL preferred for radial RCA PCI, JR poor support: brilakis p.117
- Alternatives to JR4 (two of them): Williams (3DRC) and AR MOD: oxford p.153; shepherd's crook (steep vertical take-off): AR MOD, 3DRC, IMA: oxford p.156. AR1 or 2 (own table row) for anterior RCA in dilated root: brilakis p.522. 3D Right listed separately from Williams by Brilakis (femoral PCI and radial PCI): brilakis p.522; kept as a separate row with no claim that it equals the 3DRC
- Femoral diagnostic RCA: JR4, Williams, Hockey stick or IM for shepherd's crook; femoral PCI JR4, AL1, 3D Right; radial diagnostic universal or JR4; radial PCI AL1, Ikari Right, 3D Right; JR avoided: brilakis p.522 (30.2.6.1)
- Universal radial for RCA: clockwise torque with gentle withdrawal like JR4; tends to dive, potential for dissection; gentle anticlockwise with withdrawal: oxford p.157; universal catheters for RCA via radial: brilakis p.116
- Conus: first branch, supplies RVOT; easily cannulated; pressure often damps rapidly; injection → blush → VF; quickly and gently withdraw; gentle rotation and angulation; different catheter occasionally: oxford p.157 (box), p.151 (anatomy)
- Prolonged cannulation or contrast injection into the conus can induce ischaemia and VF: oxford p.61
- Do not inject with a damped or ventricularised trace: oxford p.321 (basis for the re-sequenced conus check, q7 and case step: damping first, withdraw before any puff)
- Large volume into conus → VF: oxford p.180; VF needs immediate defibrillation per ALS: oxford p.309
- Ostial lesions missed if tip too far in; injection into right coronary sinus: oxford p.156
- Catheter-tip spasm most often after ostial engagement, commonest in non-dominant RCA and vessels similar to catheter calibre: oxford p.173; over-engagement may result in spasm especially RCA; decline a few seconds after engagement: oxford p.180; spasm can mimic severe stenosis, trial of IC nitrate if in doubt: oxford p.174
- Guide support for RCA PCI: JR4 (+; more if deep-throated without trauma), AR1/2 (++), Hockey Stick/AL1/2 (+++); larger calibre more support: oxford p.214; larger guides more likely to damp at aorto-ostial lesions, 6 Fr preferred: brilakis p.273
- Universal radial row: Tiger technique and dive: oxford p.157; Tiger side hole: oxford p.157
- Ventricularised RCA: conus or small non-dominant vessel: oxford p.155
- Anterior RCA origin normal variant: oxford p.181
- Root dilates and rotates counterclockwise, RCA anterior → AR-1 or 2, AL 1 or 2: brilakis p.522
- Right-sinus anomalies: JR4 possible but Amplatz (AL or AR) often needed: oxford p.182
- Ostial lesion and sinus injection (paraphrased): oxford p.156
- Check (dilated root, AR): brilakis p.522

## Lesson pm-engaging-fail

- Five-step failure algorithm (cusp angiography or aortography with pigtail usually 60 mL over 3 seconds, may show ostial occlusion; manoeuvrability: 0.035/0.038 wire inside, long sheath, diagnostic instead of guide, 1 Fr smaller than sheath, change access; curve size or guide extension; other shapes; stop if large contrast and no emergency (no STEMI or cardiogenic shock), CT): brilakis p.118–119 (5.7.3.6, Fig 5.20)
- Anomalous origin: cusp angiography/aortography; RAO instead of LAO; different catheters, e.g. AL; CT: brilakis p.117 (5.7.3.1); right-sinus anomalies AL or AR often needed: oxford p.182
- Flush ostial occlusion; collaterals confirm: brilakis p.117 (5.7.3.2)
- Tortuosity: wire increases stiffness, reduces kinking; back end of 0.035 in wire without exiting straightens tip, especially radial; long (e.g. 45 cm) sheath; catheter slightly smaller than sheath (7 Fr guide in 8 Fr sheath); diagnostic catheter easier to manipulate (thicker walls) then 300 cm 0.014 in supportive wire to exchange for guide; spasm → vasodilators and smaller catheters; change access: brilakis p.117–118 (5.7.3.3)
- Size: smaller/larger curve; guide extension; very dilated root, 6 Fr AL3 guide with 4 Fr diagnostic telescoped: brilakis p.118 (5.7.3.4)
- Shape: other shapes; 0.014 in wire into the vessel to facilitate engagement, often for IMA grafts: brilakis p.118 (5.7.3.5)
- Anomalies 1–2% of unselected patients; separate ostia and anterior RCA normal variants; anomalous types (RCA from left sinus, LMS from right sinus, ostia in ascending aorta; rarely LMS/LAD from pulmonary artery); anomalous Cx; no hard and fast rules, aortography decides: oxford p.181–182
- Check stem numbers (220 mL) are illustrative; stem states steps 1–4 done (cusp injection and aortogram located the ostium; wire inside, long sheath; a larger curve (JR5) with a guide extension for step 3; three further shapes); decision from brilakis p.118–119 step 5
- Check (tortuosity, wire inside): brilakis p.117, p.119

## Lesson pm-engaging-torque

- Rotation transmitted from hub to tip using right-hand rotation and left-hand back-and-forth: brilakis p.116
- Avoid excessive torquing especially when tip not moving proportionally: brilakis p.109 (5.4.3.3)
- Wire inside the catheter facilitates manipulation and decreases kinking: brilakis p.112 (5.6.3.3), p.117 (5.7.3.3), p.119 (5.7.4.1)
- Long femoral sheaths 25–55 cm: brilakis p.109; 45–55 cm: brilakis p.112
- Catheter 1 Fr smaller than sheath: brilakis p.117, p.119
- Diagnostic catheters thicker walls, more torquable, less likely to kink: brilakis p.518 (30.2.1)
- Smaller thin-walled catheters floppier, reduced torque: oxford p.153
- Shortened guides poor torque transmission: brilakis p.520 (30.2.4); hand-made side holes weaken guide and lead to kinking: brilakis p.522 (30.2.5)
- Kink diagnosis: tip not moving when rotating hub; waveform dampened or lost; fluoroscopy confirms location: brilakis p.119; suspect when engagement challenging or waveform lost: p.109; unable to aspirate → fluoroscopy whole length: p.109
- Causes: severe iliac/aortic/subclavian tortuosity; extensive manipulation: brilakis p.119
- Do not inject or flush with dampened or missing waveform: brilakis p.109
- Five recovery steps (untwist opposite direction + 0.035 in wire; back end risk of perforating catheter; second access and snare tip; BP cuff at brachial artery for radial kink below brachial; indeflator 3–4 atm; cut at hub, sheath same size or 1–2 Fr larger to encase, risk of vessel trauma without dilator; pull kinked segment through arteriotomy if enough length for guidewire); fractured catheter fragments snared without surgery: brilakis p.119–120 (Fig 5.21)
- Knotting (context stated: Oxford's right heart catheter section): vigorous prolonged manipulation; expert help; do not pull tight; guide wire may undo; else pull tight, back to sheath, remove; bleeding, surgical repair; snares and forceps; surgical removal reported: oxford p.112
- Prevention: long sheaths, avoid excessive rotation, wire inside: brilakis p.119; change access (right radial to left radial or femoral) for extreme subclavian tortuosity or arteria lusoria: brilakis p.109, p.112
- Check (kink below brachial, BP cuff): brilakis p.119

## Lesson pm-engaging-exchange

- Catheters advanced over 0.035 or 0.038 in J wire; wire tip ahead of catheter; fixed by assistant: brilakis p.97 (5.2)
- Advancing catheter without leading wire causes dissection: brilakis p.101 (5.2.5.1)
- Wire entering LV causes arrhythmias; keep tip out: brilakis p.107 (5.3.5.3)
- Exchange wires 250–300 cm; hydrophilic wires not through introducer needle: oxford p.97; polymer-jacketed wire never through needle (coating sheared): brilakis p.98
- AAA: exchanging over a guide wire recommended to prevent thrombus dislodgement: oxford p.68
- Guide wires to exchange catheters reduces plaque disruption (cholesterol embolisation): oxford p.335
- Multiple exchanges cause radial spasm: brilakis p.105; universal radial catheters avoid exchange and may reduce spasm: oxford p.157
- Radial: 300 cm 0.035/0.038 in wire for all exchanges keeping tip in aortic root; long (e.g. 45 cm) sheath ≥1 Fr larger (step retitled 'Consider a long sheath'); femoral long sheath after crossing: brilakis p.100 (5.2.4.2.3), p.101; radial long sheaths 24 cm reaching the brachial artery: brilakis p.509
- Preparation: diagnostic catheters flushed and loaded over wire; guides connected to Y-connector and flushed outside; haemostatic valve prevents bleeding; back-bleed large guides (aortic debris): brilakis p.103 (5.3.2.1)
- Aspirate sheath before guide, flush with heparinised saline: brilakis p.108
- Diagnostic catheter engages, 300 cm supportive 0.014 in wire, exchange for guide: brilakis p.117–118 (5.7.3.3), p.518 (30.2.1)
- Support wires designed for equipment delivery or even guide exchanges: brilakis p.561 (30.7.4)
- Protruding aorto-ostial stent: wire with JR4 guide; guide exchange with microcatheter and 300 cm supportive wire: brilakis p.274 (15.1.5)
- Trapping preferred technique with short (180–190 cm) or long (300 cm) wire; minimises wire movement (position loss, distal injury, perforation) and radiation; four techniques (trapping, hydraulic exchange, guidewire extension, circumcision): brilakis p.160 (8.9.2)
- Trapping steps: withdraw device into guide proximal to balloon position; insert balloon next to, not over, wire; monorail preferred over OTW; dedicated trapping balloons smaller profile; stylet helps through valve then removed; 2.5 mm for 6 or 7 Fr, 3.0 mm for 7 and 8 Fr; length ideally ≥20 mm, polymer-jacketed wires slippery: brilakis p.160
- Used balloon deformed → new one or stylet; multiple devices more likely to fail; advance beyond distal marker near primary curve; short or shortened guides markers unreliable: brilakis p.161
- Inflate 15–20 atm; guide pressure should damp; rupture near hand-made side holes; fluoroscopy only initially; polymer-jacketed wires slip; deflate; back-bleed Y-connector because air entrained, embolism likely if not; continuous saline flushing alternative; anchor balloon ≥2.5 mm (6 or 7 Fr) or ≥3.0 mm (8 Fr) reused; air embolism prevention and treatment; loss of position → rewire: brilakis p.162
- 6 Fr guide cannot trap OTW balloons etc.; 0.071 in ID 6 Fr guides trap low-profile microcatheters: brilakis p.160 (also in q9 stem and explanation)
- Guide extension with built-in trapping balloon (TrapLiner, named generically), 6–8 Fr; less fluoroscopy to position a trapping balloon; trapping balloons hard to reinsert after multiple inflations; best with short (135 cm) microcatheters, long (150 cm) often too long: brilakis p.160
- Air entrainment with trapping as a cause of dampening: brilakis p.38 (2.3)
- Air embolism: 100% oxygen: brilakis p.111; oxford p.327
- Check (trapping in 8 Fr with 3.0 mm at 18 atm then back-bleed): brilakis p.160, p.162

## Lesson pm-engaging-spasm

- Causes: small radial, high-origin radial, tortuosity, multiple needle passes, extensive manipulation, multiple exchanges, women and smokers, inadequate sedation: brilakis p.105–106 (5.3.3.4); large sheaths relative to artery, anxiety, no vasodilators, IV vasopressors: brilakis p.508 (29.2.3.2)
- Intro: 'muscular' and 'cold' removed; triggers listed are from brilakis p.105–106, p.508
