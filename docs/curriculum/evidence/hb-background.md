## Lesson hb-background-depth

- Spine on the image left = RAO, right = LAO; in all views of the LCA the Cx is the vessel closest to the spine; rule offered because the inexperienced find it hard to tell LAD from Cx: oxford p.179
- Cx in left AV groove supplying posterior wall; LAD in interventricular groove supplying anterior wall; RCA in right AV groove towards inferior aspect; conus to RVOT: oxford p.151
- Branch naming: LAD branches towards Cx territory (lateral wall) are diagonals, towards RV and septum are septals; Cx branches towards the lateral wall are obtuse marginals, last one may be the terminal marginal, AV Cx continues in the groove towards the base; RCA RV marginal branches pass anteriorly towards the septum and supply mainly RV: oxford p.179
- Left main ostium usually lies posteriorly; anticlockwise rotation may be required: brilakis p.115
- Oxford JL: occasionally gentle clockwise (posterior) or anticlockwise (anterior) rotation: oxford p.155. Disagreement with Brilakis stated as such
- LIMA: anticlockwise rotation brings the tip anteriorly; arises anteroinferiorly from left subclavian a few cm beyond the vertebral artery; catheter seen entering LIMA just after the vertebral artery (Fig. 7.10): oxford p.199, p.200
- RIMA: clockwise rotation brings the tip anteriorly: oxford p.201 (RIMA paragraph, ch 7 Arterial grafts)
- Advice to turn a little and watch the tip because the manuals disagree: this module's reasoning; same advice taught in pm-engaging
- SVGs: proximal anastomosis on the anterior wall of the ascending aorta just above the sinuses; Cx grafts most superior, RCA grafts most inferior: oxford p.189; LAD and diagonal grafts just above the LCA ostium, cannulated in LAO with gentle clockwise rotation, RAO useful to see whether the catheter is directed anteriorly: oxford p.191; Cx grafts most cranial, RAO tip away from the spine: oxford p.192; RCA graft ostium just above and anterior to the native RCA: oxford p.193
- Aortogram as road map in the same projection; sternal sutures and rib spaces as markers: oxford p.187; surgical clips may give clues: oxford p.189
- Look at arteries in more than one plane; oval lesion severe in one view and normal in another: oxford p.179
- Lesion in at least two projections, preferably orthogonal (90°), avoiding foreshortening and overlap: oxford p.175
- Oxford imaging sequences include right lateral for both LCA and RCA: oxford p.160
- Coronary sinus posterior and RV lead anterior on lateral: oxford p.277
- Before graft angiography: check previous report and films, operation records, and that invasive assessment is justified; engagement signalled by resistance or a slight jump; check pressure not damped (slow withdrawal and rotation); slow test injection; occluded graft stump recorded; increased VF risk with vein graft cannulation: oxford p.189. LIMA fragile and prone to dissection, particularly in the elderly, inject slowly: oxford p.201

## Lesson hb-background-plaque

- Angiography 'lumenography'/'lumenology'; information on extent of atheroma, thrombus, spasm, myocardial bridging, dissection: oxford p.27
- Limitations: inter- and intra-observer variability; may underestimate severity by up to 50% in extreme cases (incomplete views, eccentricity); appearance may not match physiology; diffuse disease makes the vessel appear smaller, remodelling may make it appear larger; spatial resolution about 0.2 mm: oxford p.175
- Compensatory (outward) enlargement of atherosclerotic coronary arteries: ref glagov-remodelling-1987 (title)
- Not all plaques that rupture are obstructive, because of positive remodelling; intravascular imaging can confirm rupture or laminated thrombus: oxford p.147
- Check 1: oxford p.147, p.175; OCT for culprit: brilakis p.145, p.231
- OCT appearances: normal three layers; fibrous homogeneous signal-rich; calcific signal-poor with sharp borders, depth and arc measurable in most; lipid-rich signal-poor with poorly defined borders, media not visible behind; white thrombus platelet and WBC rich, minimal attenuation; red thrombus RBC rich, high attenuation, shadow: brilakis p.239 (Fig. 13.5)
- Superficial calcium more likely to need ablative techniques, deep calcium may respond to balloon angioplasty: brilakis p.241
- OCT features associated with stent underexpansion (max calcium angle > 180°, thickness > 0.5 mm, length > 5.0 mm); favour plaque modification such as atherectomy or lithotripsy: brilakis p.240, p.241
- OCT may not show the EEM under heavy plaque, especially lipid; stent sized to the distal reference, never the proximal (vessels taper): brilakis p.241
- SCAD: OCT helps diagnosis, but wiring and contrast injection may extend the flap (used in the MI lesson SCAD check): brilakis p.240
- Fibrous plaque row now says only that it is the bright reference for the dark plaques (signal-rich): brilakis p.239
- Jia caption: 22 lesions (17.5%) classified as others (tight stenosis, dissection, spasm, fissure, takotsubo, none); erosion and CN diagnosed by new OCT criteria not validated by pathology: ref jia-oct-erosion-2013 (full text, results and limitations)
- Check 1: Oxford names intravascular imaging (oxford p.147); Brilakis names OCT (brilakis p.145, p.231)
- White thrombus predominantly with erosion and calcified nodule, red thrombus most often with rupture: ref jia-oct-erosion-2013 (full text, results)
- OCT high-risk plaque: fibrous cap < 65 μm, lipidic core with posterior attenuation, lipidic arc > 90°, macrophage infiltration; neoatherosclerosis as a mechanism of stent failure: oxford p.238
- TCFA OCT definition (lipid in ≥2 quadrants, thinnest cap < 65 μm): ref jia-oct-erosion-2013 (full text, methods)
- Landing zones < 50% plaque burden, no lipid-rich plaque, especially TCFA, prone to edge dissection: brilakis p.241
- Jia series: 126 ACS patients with pre-intervention OCT; rupture 43.7%, erosion 31.0%, calcified nodule 7.9%; ages 60.6 / 53.8 / 65.1; NSTE-ACS 29.1% (rupture), 61.5% (erosion), 100% (nodule); lipid plaque 100% vs 43.6%; cap 60.4 vs 169.3 μm; lipid arc 275.8° vs 202.8°; diameter stenosis 68.8% / 55.4% / 66.1%: ref jia-oct-erosion-2013 (abstract)
- Calcified nodule: cap disruption over calcified plaque with protruding, superficial calcium; nodule patients had the most hypertension and chronic kidney disease; erosion not more frequent in women than men in this series: ref jia-oct-erosion-2013 (full text)
- Erosion more common in women and smokers: oxford p.147 (difference from Jia stated)
- Majority of MIs due to plaque rupture: oxford p.147; majority of STEMI caused by thrombotic occlusion after plaque rupture: oxford p.247
- Atherothrombosis (thrombus with rupture or erosion) is the commonest acute coronary pathology in primary MI; thrombus can embolise distally; non-flow-limiting stenoses are common causes of MI: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (section 7.2.1)
- No culprit: repeat careful angiographic review for subtle staining or thrombus; OCT the modality of choice for its high resolution; culprit findings thrombus and plaque rupture: brilakis p.145. OCT preferred over IVUS for culprit (resolution); ulceration, erosion, thrombus or calcified nodule locate the culprit: brilakis p.231, p.239. Ulceration and thrombus the hallmarks; some lesions only erosion: brilakis p.386
- Predict distal embolisation: large lipid core plaque or large thrombus: brilakis p.231. Plaque embolisation causes: atherectomy, lipid-rich plaque, SVG treatment: brilakis p.442; Table 25.1 plaque embolisation treated by aspiration, vasodilators, balloon angioplasty if epicardial embolisation: brilakis p.437
- Restenosis: elastic recoil (immediate), inflammation and neointimal proliferation (weeks/months), negative remodelling (months/years); stents largely eliminated recoil and negative remodelling: oxford p.221
- SVG failure: excessive intimal hyperplasia, atherosclerosis within the graft, graft thrombosis due to plaque rupture: oxford p.194 (ch 7 Saphenous vein grafts, long-term patency)
- Tips shown: pci-bg-lipid-embolisation-forecast (new; brilakis p.231, p.437, p.442); pci-img-landing-zone (pm-imaging; brilakis p.241)

## Lesson hb-background-mi

- Fourth UDMI numbered types as taught in fnd-phys-mi: ref thygesen-4udmi-2018
- Fifth UDMI published 28 August 2026 (Eur Heart J), numerical classification replaced by primary, secondary and procedure-related MI: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (summary section; PubMed PMID 42661425)
- Acute myocardial injury: rise and/or fall of cTnI or cTnT with at least one value above the sex-specific 99th percentile URL; sex-specific to avoid under-recognition in women: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (section 6.1, Box 1)
- Troponin cannot distinguish ischaemic from non-ischaemic causes; non-ischaemic mechanisms: inflammation, physiological stress response, catecholamine stress, cardiotoxic agents, trauma: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (sections 6.1, 7.2.2)
- Chest discomfort preferred; 'typical' and 'atypical' discouraged, atypical labels commoner in women and may delay diagnosis: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (section 12)
- Primary MI: spontaneous, acute coronary pathology; secondary MI: another acute condition causing supply–demand imbalance (increased demand e.g. tachycardia, severe hypertension; reduced supply e.g. hypotension, hypoxia, anaemia; tachyarrhythmia most common); procedure-related within 30 days: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (summary, sections 7.2.1–7.2.3)
- Confirmation: primary if atherothrombosis or other acute coronary pathology identified; angiography may miss it, OCT/IVUS may help, CMR may be needed; secondary confirmed if ischaemia is a consequence of obstructive CAD without acute coronary pathology, or new RWMA or loss of viability: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (summary, sections 7.2.1, 7.2.2)
- Mapping table: SCAD, vasospasm, embolism from a non-coronary source as acute coronary pathologies of primary MI; previously grouped with supply–demand as type 2; restenosis, stent thrombosis or graft failure > 30 days = primary MI, within 30 days procedure-related; MINOCA renamed myocardial injury with non-obstructive coronary arteries, a working diagnosis: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (sections 7.1, 7.2.1, 11); fourth-definition labels (types 1, 2, 4a, 4b, 4c, 5): ref thygesen-4udmi-2018
- SCAD considered particularly in women under 50 and in pregnancy or post partum: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (section 7.2.1)
- Secondary MI pathway: patients less likely to report chest pain, more breathlessness; ST/T changes often global; invasive angiography if persistent or recurrent ischaemia with primary MI as differential; otherwise imaging often deferred, e.g. CCTA; sometimes not appropriate in frailty or limited life expectancy; sepsis or bleeding can trigger atherothrombosis; most have no obstructive CAD or new loss of viability and secondary MI is excluded; aetiology of injury should then be determined: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (sections 7.2.2, 12)
- Aim to restore antegrade flow; less ischaemic time, less necrosis; patent IRA regardless of mode halves mortality; majority of STEMI from thrombus after plaque rupture; thrombolysis benefit greatest early, by 12 h bleeding risk outweighs gains; PCI the default strategy; STEMI 10% 1-year mortality: oxford p.247
- No culprit: repeat review for staining or thrombus; LV gram (or TTE) for hypokinetic area or alternative diagnoses such as takotsubo; OCT; cardiac MRI: brilakis p.145 (7.2.10–7.2.12), p.379 (20.7)
- STEMI during catheterisation 0.1%, from dissection, air, thrombus or atherosclerotic debris; small enzymatic periprocedural events usually silent but associated with increased future risk of death or MI: oxford p.301
- ISCHEMIA: 5179 patients with moderate or severe ischaemia; primary outcome sensitive to MI definition; secondary analysis more procedural MIs of uncertain clinical importance: ref ischemia-2020 (abstract)
- MINOCA under the fifth UDMI: most have non-coronary cardiac (myocarditis, takotsubo, cardiomyopathy) or non-cardiac (e.g. PE) causes; intravascular imaging may identify erosion/rupture, mural thrombus without rupture, or SCAD; functional testing may identify microvascular dysfunction or spasm; CMR ideally within 2 weeks; MI the final diagnosis in 22–27% when CMR done: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (section 11)
- MINOCA estimated at around 5–10% of MI presentations: oxford p.147
- Check 3 (stent thrombosis at 8 months = primary MI): ref mills-5udmi-2026 (section 7.2.1)
- Sex-specific 99th percentile URLs already recommended by the fourth UDMI in 2018 for high-sensitivity assays: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (section 14). 'Atypical' labels commoner in women and may contribute to delayed diagnosis: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (section 12)
- Obstructive CAD for secondary MI = ≥70% stenosis by angiography or ≥50% flow-limiting on physiology: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (Box 4). MINOCA cut-off no stenosis ≥50%: section 11
- Primary MI also confirmed by new RWMA or absence of viable myocardium in an ischaemic pattern: Box 3; without angiography or imaging, primary MI on biomarkers, ECG and clinical criteria, unknown aetiology (BA41.09/BA41.19): section 7.2.1 and Figure 2 legend
- Secondary MI when imaging not feasible or appropriate: clinical judgement; known CAD, significant injury affecting function, ischaemic ECG changes support a clinical diagnosis: section 7.2.2; Box 4 lists known CAD and strong clinical suspicion from the extent of ECG ischaemia or troponin elevation as additional features; round 2 text names all of these (re-read in the full text, scratchpad u5flat.txt)
- Marked ECG changes not resolving rapidly with treatment of the trigger (e.g. after cardioversion) should prompt reconsideration of primary MI: section 13
- Fourth UDMI types: 4b stent thrombosis and 4c restenosis at any time; type 5 CABG-related with graft occlusion within 48 h: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (summary of the fourth definition); ref thygesen-4udmi-2018. Procedure-related after an initially uncomplicated procedure only in the same vessel or territory treated: section 7.2.3
- STEMI/NSTEMI ECG classification retained, ICD-11 BA41.0/BA41.1: section 7.1.2; MI confirmed after MINOCA reclassified as primary, secondary or procedure-related; CMR yield higher within 2 weeks as reversible changes of takotsubo and myocarditis resolve: section 11
- Fibrinolysis within 12 h when primary PCI cannot be delivered within 120 min of diagnosis: ref esc-acs-2023 (as taught in tracks/acute-mcs.ts)
- Oxford MINOCA causes table (plaque rupture and intravascular imaging with risk factors and localising ECG changes; spasm after cocaine, epinephrine, chemotherapy, no traditional risk factors, ergonovine or acetylcholine provocation, calcium-channel blockers first line; paradoxical embolus with abrupt terminal-branch occlusion or none, young without risk factors, right atrial or ventricular ECG changes, TTE/TOE; myocarditis, MRI, biopsy rarely, supportive; structural: takotsubo, HCM, DCM, echo or MRI cornerstone, some argue routine MRI; takotsubo recovers, recurrence possible): oxford p.147, p.148
- Academy note sentence (fnd-phys-mi, phy-mv-minoca use 2018 and 2023 ESC wording): tracks/foundations.ts, tracks/physiology-imaging.ts
- Pearl: when angiography identifies the acute coronary pathology the final diagnosis should reflect it, giving insight into future risk and treatment: ref mills-5udmi-2026 (section 7.2.1). Example wording is this module's
- Tips shown: diag-bg-secondary-mi-question (new; mills section 7.2.2; oxford p.43 review that the investigation is still indicated); diag-bg-minoca-working (new; mills section 11; oxford p.147; brilakis p.145)

## Lesson hb-background-general

- Table, heart failure: catheterisation mortality rises with age, LMS stenosis, three-vessel disease, impaired LV, class IV HF, aortic valve disease: oxford p.300; pulmonary oedema with large contrast volumes in LV impairment, raised LVEDP, severe AS and MS, dialysis-dependent renal failure: oxford p.302; LV gram relative contraindications include LVEDP > 35 mmHg and pulmonary oedema; good echo provides all the information: oxford p.117; patients unable to lie on their back (severe back pain, CHF) favour radial: brilakis p.61
- Table, AF: radial preferred with oral anticoagulants or high bleeding risk: brilakis p.61, p.34 (1.4), p.33 (1.3); warfarin stopped 3–5 days before elective procedures in low-risk AF (normal LV, no previous stroke); continued or switched to IV UFH in moderate/high-risk AF (poor LV, previous stroke, diabetes), prosthetic (particularly metal) valves, rheumatic mitral disease, known non-laminar thrombus: oxford p.85, also p.42; Brilakis: warfarin discontinued 5 days before elective procedures, INR checked on the day: brilakis p.33; DOACs withheld 24 h, dabigatran with renal impairment 72 h: oxford p.86; Brilakis Table 1.2: apixaban 2 days, edoxaban and rivaroxaban 2–3 days, dabigatran 2–5 days by creatinine clearance: brilakis p.34; femoral generally safer if INR < 2.0 on warfarin: oxford p.85; AF in the lab and cardioversion: oxford p.310, p.311
- Table, valve disease: coronary angiography considered in symptomatic patients assessed for valve surgery (useful guide: over 40 with risk factors, suspected ischaemia, LV systolic dysfunction; endocarditis only with multiple risk factors or coronary embolisation): oxford p.27, p.28; pulmonary oedema in severe AS or MS: oxford p.302; aortic valve disease in mortality list: oxford p.300 and stroke risk list: oxford p.303
- Table, kidneys: stop before contrast volume ≥ 3.7 × GFR, lower threshold in CKD or single kidney, count recent CT contrast: brilakis p.39; stage non-emergent PCI in renal failure or anticoagulated: brilakis p.29; dialysis-dependent pulmonary oedema: oxford p.302; dialysis shunts in the ipsilateral arm make access unsuitable: brilakis p.61
- Table, diabetes: early on list, morning insulin and short-acting oral hypoglycaemics withheld, metformin withheld the day before and for 2 days after: oxford p.42; Brilakis: metformin held on the day and not restarted for at least 48 h only in CKD, otherwise renal function checked after; insulin reduced for fasting: brilakis p.33 (1.3); ESUR: stop from the time of contrast only with eGFR < 30 (IV or second-pass intra-arterial contrast), AKI, or first-pass intra-arterial contrast (left heart injection is first pass; coronary injection is second pass): ref vandermolen-esur-pcaki-2018, PMID 29417249 (PMC5986837), full text re-read in round 2 (definitions in Materials and methods, rule in Conclusion and Key points); CABG generally preferred for diabetic multivessel patients who are good surgical candidates: brilakis p.139
- Table, surgery or non-adherence within 6–12 months: PCI may be best avoided to minimise stent thrombosis due to the surgery and early DAPT discontinuation; medical therapy or CABG may be preferred: brilakis p.29; ESC 2022 deferral of elective surgery (6 months after elective PCI, 12 months after ACS): ref esc-ncs-2022 as taught in pm-planning (pci-manual/01-planning.ts) and hb-after-the-procedure; avoid stent implantation if possible if non-cardiac surgery needed within 6–12 months: brilakis p.185 (10.2). Current guideline home: ref esc-ncs-2022 (not quoted)
- Table, mimics: myocarditis mimics MI; catheterisation generally normal, raised LV diastolic pressure worse prognosis; MRI confirms: oxford p.147, p.148; takotsubo: stress trigger (not always), postmenopausal women, ST elevation, unobstructed arteries, apical ballooning: oxford p.148; spasm after cocaine, epinephrine, chemotherapeutic agents: oxford p.147
- Five-question screen: structure is this module's. ## Errors or out-of-date points noticed in existing Academy lessons (for the checkers and fixer)

- fnd-phys-mi (foundations.ts): teaches only the fourth universal definition (types 1–5) and defines MINOCA as myocardial infarction with non-obstructive coronary arteries. The fifth universal definition (Mills et al., 2026; ref mills-5udmi-2026) replaces the numbered types with primary, secondary and procedure-related MI, moves SCAD, vasospasm and coronary embolism from type 2 to primary MI, and renames MINOCA myocardial injury with non-obstructive coronary arteries. The lesson's table row for type 2 and its MINOCA definition should say they are the 2018 definitions, with a pointer to the 2026 change (taught in hb-background-mi).
- fnd-phys-mi table row 4a ("within 48 h") and the fnd-q-phys-06 item ("Type 2 MI") are correct for 2018 but are now superseded terminology; they should name the definition year.
- fnd-phys-athero: states erosion is more frequent in women; Oxford (p.147) agrees, but the OCT series of Jia et al. (2013) found erosion patients younger yet not more often female. A one-line qualifier would keep it accurate.
