## Lesson pm-physiology-when (Deciding to measure: which lesions need physiology)

- Inter- and intra-observer variability; angiography may underestimate severity by up to 50% in extreme cases (incomplete views, eccentricity); luminology may not match physiological effect; diffuse atherosclerosis makes reference look smaller, remodelling larger; spatial resolution about 0.2 mm: oxford p.175 (Limitations of coronary angiography).
- Inter-observer variability about ±20% in moderate lesions (mild and severe most accurate); haemodynamically important >70% diameter (~90% area) or >50% LMS; grading mild ≤30%, moderate 30–70%, severe >70%: oxford p.175 (Quantifying lesion severity); Fig 6.18 caption 70% diameter / 90% area: oxford p.176.
- ≥70–90% non-LM and ≥50% LM stenosis considered significant; %DS has limitations (Fig 6.4); physiology preferred, not in ACS culprits: brilakis p.130 (6.8.2.2). FAME angiographic analysis 35%/80%/96%: brilakis p.132 (Fig 6.4); ref tonino-fame-angio-2010 (already verified in pm-interpreting).
- Diameter stenosis ≥90% almost always haemodynamically significant; otherwise physiology determines severity more accurately: brilakis p.139 (7.1.2).
- Pressure as surrogate for flow, valid only when microvascular resistance low/trivial (lesson now says 'low (in its words, low or trivial)'; 'steady' removed); FFR achieves maximal vasodilation with a vasodilator (usually adenosine), hyperaemic index; FFR = maximum flow with stenosis as proportion of maximum flow without; iFR uses wave-intensity analysis to find a wave-free diastolic window, resting index; both are Pd/Pa (wire/guide): oxford p.233.
- Symptoms equivocal and no preprocedural non-invasive test showing ischaemia → physiology useful (rephrased in round 1 to avoid copied wording); ask about prior adverse reaction or contraindication to intracoronary vasodilators such as adenosine: brilakis p.35 (1.12).
- Adenosine contraindicated in asthma or with dipyridamole: oxford p.234.
- Caffeine prevents adenosine binding; ensure patient has not received caffeine: brilakis p.224 (12.2.6.3.2).
- No physiology in ACS culprit or suspected culprit; use intracoronary imaging: brilakis p.217 (12.1.1). SVG and CTO vessel exclusions: brilakis p.227 (12.3.3), p.395 (21.12.1).
- Deferral safe for FFR >0.80 (>0.75 in earlier studies) or non-ischaemic resting index such as iFR >0.89; also applies to left main: brilakis p.217 (12.1.1). Oxford FFR ≤0.80 / >0.80, iFR ≤0.89 / >0.89: oxford p.235.
- Multivessel: adenosine FFR, usually in lesions <90% DS, → fewer lesions treated and better outcomes vs angiography-guided PCI: brilakis p.217 (12.1.1). Oxford indications: serial stenoses in one vessel; relative lesion importance in multivessel disease: oxford p.233.
- Calcified lesions: physiology encouraged because of poor correlation of angiography with physiology: brilakis p.356 (19.6), p.374 (19.12).
- Small vessels very helpful (stenosis severity and territory size); large vessels also challenging: brilakis p.404 (22.3.12). Long lesions: step-up suggests more focal: brilakis p.406 (22.4.12).
- DEFER 15-year: 325 patients; FFR ≥0.75 randomised Defer 91 / Perform 90; death 33.0% vs 31.1%; MI 2.2% vs 10.0%; no late catch-up: ref zimmermann-defer-2015 (PMID 26400825, abstract). Cited by Brilakis p.217 [1], p.228.
- FAME threshold 0.80: ref fame-2009. DEFINE-FLAIR and iFR-SWEDEHEART non-inferiority of iFR; FFR the older index with much more evidence than iFR: oxford p.234 ('FFR or iFR?' box); refs define-flair-2017, ifr-swedeheart-2017.
- Pooled 5-year iFR-SWEDEHEART and DEFINE-FLAIR: 4,511 patients (2254 iFR, 2257 FFR); deferral 50.0% vs 45.2%; all-cause death HR 1.34; MACE HR 1.18; MI and unplanned revascularisation no difference: ref eftekhari-ifr-5y-2023 (PMID 37634144, abstract).
- FAME 5-year: MACE 31% angiography vs 28% FFR (RR 0.91, p=0.31); benefit up to 2 years, then parallel; stents per patient 2.7 vs 1.9: ref vannunen-fame-5y-2015 (PMID 26333474, abstract). FAME 1-year benefit: ref fame-2009.
- Dipyridamole potentiates the effects of adenosine (check 1 explanation): ref malcolm-adenosine-ep-1993 (PMID 8485069, abstract). The reuptake mechanism is not stated in the sources used and is not taught.
- Oxford's "derived FFR of less than 0.75 indicates the lesion is flow-limiting": oxford p.233 (Indications); vs ≤0.80 at oxford p.235. Oxford multivessel PCI: FFR guidance "shows promise, but randomized data are required": oxford p.255. Brilakis FAME 2- and 5-year follow-up refs [6,7]: brilakis p.228; the lesson now gives the actual FAME course (benefit to 2 years, similar at 5 years) from vannunen-fame-5y-2015.
- Large ischaemia (>10% LV) or abnormal invasive FFR among subgroups with possible prognostic benefit: brilakis p.140 (7.1.7).
- Lack of evidence of ischaemia and unavailability of FFR or IVUS as a reason against ad hoc PCI: brilakis p.146 (7.3.1 list).
- Cardiac physiologist cooperates in pressure wire studies; report includes FFR/iFR value: oxford p.59, p.62.
- 0.80 vs 0.81 no major difference; benefit higher with lower FFR: brilakis p.223 (12.2.6.2 point 4).
- Check 1 (dipyridamole → resting index): from the above (oxford p.234; brilakis p.35, p.221–222).
- Check 2 (92% RCA): brilakis p.139; FAME 96% in 91–99%: brilakis p.132.
- Tip pci-phys-decide-before-measuring: brilakis p.217, p.227–228, p.427, p.431, p.139. Wording follows Brilakis: caution in severe AS (p.427) and cardiogenic shock (p.431); best avoided in CHF with high RA pressure and LVEDP (p.228, 12.3.6).
- Pearl: FFR ≤0.80 ischaemic (stent), >0.80 defer: brilakis p.224 (Table 12.2); oxford p.235.
- Tip pci-planning-adenosine-history belongs to pm-planning (brilakis 1.12).

## Lesson pm-physiology-setup (Setting up the study)

- Sensor about 3 cm from the tip at junction of radiolucent and radiopaque segments, 0.014-inch wire: oxford p.234; Brilakis sensor usually 3 cm from the tip: brilakis p.218 (12.2.1.2).
- Pressure guidewire makes and rapid-exchange FFR microcatheter used as in chapter 12: brilakis p.566 (30.7.7).
- Console needs aortic pressure source; ECG source for iFR: oxford p.234.
- Nitrate before FFR: not in Brilakis 12.2 or Oxford pressure wire section; intra-arterial GTN typically given as for standard FFR assessment (≤200 µg suggested for the CMD protocol): ref ford-cormica-review-2019 (PMC full text). Lesson and sim say 'follow local protocol'.
- Starting point: guide engaged in target vessel; waveform not damped; damping underestimates severity (raises FFR and non-hyperaemic indices): brilakis p.218 (12.2).
- Step 1: flush container with saline, connect to PIM, zero; shape tip without damaging sensor; unable to zero (poor connection, defective wire) → check connections, replace; inadequate calibration discovered after insertion delays procedure (removal and recalibration); wire damage may require new wire: brilakis p.218 (12.2.1). Oxford flush to eliminate air and zero: oxford p.235.
- Step 2: introducer through haemostatic valve; advance to guide tip; proximal end of radiopaque portion at guide tip (Fig 12.2): brilakis p.219 (12.2.2.2). Challenges: suboptimal engagement, guide choice (too large EBU → wire into circumflex instead of LAD and vice versa), tip shape; prevention/treatment (better-fitting guide, reshape): brilakis p.219. Wire deformation causes (shaping, protruding through introducer, other equipment in guide), prevention (retracted inside introducer, do not push hard, remove equipment or advance under fluoroscopy), treatment (reshape or new wire): brilakis p.219–220.
- Step 3: remove introducer, flush guide with saline, equalise; introducer not removed and guide not flushed are the first two listed causes of failed equalisation (lesson no longer says which trace each distorts); means may equalise while waveforms do not overlap → error in hyperaemic FFR; causes (introducer, no flush, wire failure, tip too deep, air in aortic line); prevention; change wire if still fails: brilakis p.220 (12.2.3). Oxford: waveforms superimposed, Pd/Pa = 1: oxford p.235.
- Step 4: introducer reinsertion optional; advance distal to lesion(s), ideally as distal as possible: brilakis p.221 (12.2.4.2). Cannot advance → sections 8.6–8.8; target vessel injury (12.2.4.4.1, see 8.7): brilakis p.221. Wire may rarely cause dissection: oxford p.234. Drift causes, prevention (equalise before crossing; avoid disconnecting during manipulation), treatment (retract to guide tip, re-equalise, readvance, repeat): brilakis p.221 (12.2.4.4.2).
- Tip pci-mon-aortic-line-physiology belongs to pm-monitoring.
- Checks: brilakis p.220 (mean vs waveform; the sentence extending the error to resting indices was removed as unsourced), p.219 (EBU example).

## Lesson pm-physiology-indices (Resting ratios and hyperaemic FFR)

- Step 5: Pd and Pa recorded simultaneously; several strongly correlated indices; only iFR has clinical trial validation [2,3]; advantages (shorter, cheaper, no hyperaemic-agent complications): brilakis p.222 (12.2.5.2). Oxford: only FFR and iFR largely tested/validated: oxford p.233; iFR console identifies free-wave period using ECG, check pressure and ECG tracings: oxford p.235; iFR benefits less time, cheaper, better tolerated (adenosine can induce chest pain, bronchospasm, bradycardia, AV block): oxford p.234.
- Table 12.1: Pd/Pa ≤0.91; iFR, dPR, dFR, RFR ≤0.89: brilakis p.222.
- Resting indices much more influenced by drift: brilakis p.222 (12.2.5.3.1).
- Discordance about 14%, especially large arteries (LM, proximal LAD) and diabetes; decide on clinical presentation: brilakis p.228 (12.3.7).
- Step 6 optional when resting indices suffice: brilakis p.222 (heading 12.2.6).
- Papaverine 'not currently available in the US': brilakis p.223 (12.2.6.2); lesson attributes it to the time of writing.
- IV adenosine via central or large peripheral vein (check on hand vein, q4, case s3/c-false-deferral): brilakis p.44 (3.2.5.2).
- VF usually after a ventricular pause via R on T, may also occur without a pause: brilakis p.45 (3.2.5.3.3); R on T: brilakis p.224 (12.2.6.4.2).
- Agents: adenosine most common; regadenoson costly; papaverine outside US, avoided (ventricular arrhythmias), RCA 8 mg, LM 12 mg; contrast usual 8 mL, contrast-FFR threshold 0.83; adenosine IC RCA 50–100 mcg, LM 200 mcg (up to 600 mcg in studies), IV 140–180 mcg/kg/min: brilakis p.223 (12.2.6.2). Chapter 3: IC LM 100–200 mcg; IV 140 mcg/kg/min through central or large peripheral vein: brilakis p.44 (3.2.5.2).
- After IC adenosine flush the guide; record Pd and Pa 1–2 s after IC adenosine, lowest value = FFR; IV: measure 1–3 minutes after start when stable; SBP drop of 10 mmHg and HR increase of 5–10 beats/min surrogates: brilakis p.223. Artefact of measuring before guide waveform returns to baseline: brilakis p.223 (12.2.6.3.1).
- Oxford: IV more stable hyperaemia than IC, 60–90 seconds to hyperaemia, slightly more side effects; Pd/Pa drops to minimum steady value = FFR; drift/jump check; adenosine can then be stopped: oxford p.235.
- FFR continuous; 0.80 vs 0.81; benefit greater with lower FFR: brilakis p.223. Table 12.2 (≤0.80, ≤0.83): brilakis p.224.
- Pullback during IV adenosine (sentence in IC step): brilakis p.225 (12.2.7.2 point 1).
- Failure of vasodilation causes, prevention, treatment: brilakis p.224 (12.2.6.3.2).
- Artefacts table: brilakis p.223 (12.2.6.3.1).
- AV block (no >100 mcg IC in RCA; resolves in seconds; cough; persistent block without escape → transient CPR and temporary pacing); VF (R on T; no >100 mcg RCA or dominant circumflex; defibrillation); AF (adenosine-induced AV block or AF; cardioversion if no spontaneous conversion): brilakis p.224–225 (12.2.6.4).
- Adenosine flushing/chest constriction; wire may rarely cause dissection: oxford p.234.
- Tips pci-adenosine-rca-dose and pci-adenosine-block-cough-wait belong to pm-lab-drugs.
- Checks: brilakis p.224 (failed vasodilation); p.223 (continuum).

## Lesson pm-physiology-pullback (Pullback, drift check and reading the trace)

- Step 7: withdraw under fluoroscopy with simultaneous pressures; resting or during IV adenosine; stop when radiopaque portion reaches guide tip; serial lesions: FFR reflects summed result, does not apply to individual lesions, treat biggest gradient first, repeat FFR, stent remaining if abnormal; iFR pre-PCI pullback virtual intervention for serial or diffuse lesions; co-registration map: brilakis p.225 (12.2.7).
- Oxford: withdraw slowly along coronary to assess each lesion; retrieve into guide to check drift (Pd/Pa at guide ≠ 1) or jump in FFR/iFR in tandem lesions: oxford p.235.
- Fig 12.7: focal step-ups vs diffuse without step-ups; PCI challenging, long stent length; CABG preferable if feasible: brilakis p.225.
- Fig 12.8: iFR distal 0.41; drop in selection 0.51 over 48 mm; estimated 0.92; after 48-mm stent 0.93; panel C, no step → stenting not practical: brilakis p.226 (lesson says 'a third panel', not 'another vessel').
- Lesion order in the same artery (stable patients): more distal lesions generally first unless severe pressure damping or ischaemia from a proximal or ostial lesion, or devices cannot pass a tight proximal lesion; all significant lesions in the target vessel in the same procedure: brilakis p.146 (7.4). Physiology picks the lesion (biggest gradient first, re-measure): brilakis p.225 (12.2.7.2).
- UK CMD protocol drift ≥0.03 → re-equalise and repeat: ref perera-bhf-cmd-2023; Brilakis 0.98–1.02: brilakis p.226.
- Long lesions: step-up suggests more focal: brilakis p.406 (22.4.12). Table rows reworded: 'Treat that segment' (no prediction of result); long-lesion row action 'locate the step on the angiogram before planning stent length' (no shorter-treatment claim).
- Step 8: Pd/Pa >1.02 or <0.98 → re-equalise, repeat steps 4–7: brilakis p.226 (12.2.8.2). Fig 12.9: drifted FFR 0.78 → after re-equalisation 0.85, not significant: brilakis p.227.
- Damping underestimates severity: brilakis p.218. Extreme tortuosity → wire bias and accordion effect (cause of suboptimal post-PCI FFR): brilakis p.218 (12.1.2 list); no interpretive instruction is given. Reading before guide trace recovers: brilakis p.223.
- Resting indices much more influenced by drift (drift check explanation): brilakis p.222 (12.2.5.3.1).
- Pitfall 'removing the wire or closing the case before the drift check': brilakis p.226 (12.2.8); oxford p.235 (adenosine may be stopped at that point).
- Tip pci-phys-damped-guide: brilakis p.218, p.227.
- Checks: serial (brilakis p.225); drift with resting index (brilakis p.226, p.222).

## Lesson pm-physiology-subsets (Lesion and patient subsets)

- Aorto-ostial: damping increases indices; disengage guide; IC adenosine then disengage, or IV: brilakis p.227 (12.3.1). Equalise with pressure wire and guide in ascending aorta, guide disengaged from ostium; disengage for FFR and non-hyperaemic measurements; final assessment same: brilakis p.286–287 (15.1.12). Nitroglycerin to exclude spasm when aorto-ostial severity questionable: brilakis p.275 (15.1.7).
- Left main: hyperaemic and resting indices both usable; ostial LM disengage; both LAD and circumflex with pullback; FFR ≥0.80 (Brilakis's symbol, read on the PDF) in one vessel but not the other suggests LM not significant: brilakis p.227 (12.3.2) and p.332 (17.12.1). Note: Brilakis writes "≥0.80" for the non-ischaemic branch although his ischaemic cut-off is ≤0.80; the lesson gives his wording. Downstream stenosis decreases maximum flow, higher distal pressure, underestimates LM: brilakis p.332 (17.12.1). LM deferral (Hamilos): brilakis p.217 [4]. IVUS <6.0 mm², OCT <5.4 mm²; cut-off may differ by population, 4.5 mm² by IVUS proposed for Korean patients with smaller arteries: brilakis p.333 (17.13.1).
- LM check: stem now gives LAD FFR 0.93 (no unexplained loss); explanation: downstream disease can hide LM severity but a significant LM lesion still gives a step on both pullbacks: brilakis p.227, p.332.
- SVG: FFR reflects graft, native (unless occluded) and collateral flow; normal FFR despite severe stenosis with competitive flow; intermediate SVG lesions progress fast, new lesions develop; recommend against: brilakis p.227 (12.3.3); p.351 (18.12). FFR-CT not validated with stents and/or grafts: brilakis p.351 (18.12).
- ACS culprit: no physiology (microcirculatory dysfunction); non-culprit can be assessed: brilakis p.228 (12.3.4); non-culprit measurements do not change significantly over time: brilakis p.386 (20.12.2). Oxford avoid FFR in STEMI culprit: oxford p.234. DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI and COMPARE-ACUTE FFR ≤0.80: oxford p.253. 2023 ESC ACS guideline bases STEMI non-culprit PCI on angiographic severity: ref esc-acs-2023, as taught and verified in pm-target (10-target.ts).
- CTO vessel: always ischaemic despite collaterals; post-stenting step-up → additional treatment: brilakis p.395 (21.12.1). Donor: Sachdeva 6 of 9 FFR ≤0.80 reverted to >0.80, mean increase 0.098 ± 0.04; another study 0.03; ideally after recanalisation: brilakis p.228 (12.3.5); 0.03–0.10: brilakis p.395 (21.12.2).
- CHF: high RA and LVEDP affect FFR; FFR = (Pd − Pv)/(Pa − Pv) with RA pressure; best avoid: brilakis p.228 (12.3.6).
- Cardiogenic shock: FFR affected by afterload, caution: brilakis p.431 (24.2.12).
- Severe AS: FFR decreases after TAVR (increase in hyperaemic flow); iFR unchanged; FFR may underestimate: brilakis p.427 (24.1.12). Ahmad: 28 patients, 30 lesions; wave-free diastolic flow unchanged; whole-cycle hyperaemic flow increased (systolic hyperaemic flow); iFR 0.88 → 0.88; FFR 0.87 → 0.85; hyperaemic indices including systole underestimate severity: ref ahmad-tavr-physiology-2018 (PMID 30154062, abstract). Lesson says 'in one study' and 'iFR did not change in that study'; TAVI used in teaching text.
- NOTION-3: 455 patients undergoing TAVI; lesions FFR ≤0.80 or diameter stenosis ≥90%; MACE 26% PCI vs 36% conservative at median 2 years, HR 0.71; bleeding 28% vs 20%: ref lonborg-notion3-2024 (PMID 39216095, abstract).
- Oxford limited validation: CTO (collateral donor artery), aortic stenosis, HCM, diabetes, chronic kidney failure: oxford p.234.
- SCAD limited role: brilakis p.401 (22.1.12). Stent failure: physiology can help, imaging preferred: brilakis p.403 (22.2.12).
- Discordance tip: brilakis p.228 (12.3.7).
- Tips pci-no-physiology-in-culprit (pm-target) and pci-interp-donor-ffr (pm-interpreting) belong to other modules.
- Checks: LM pattern (brilakis p.227, p.332); AS (brilakis p.427; ahmad ref).

## Lesson pm-physiology-after-pci (Physiology after stenting and in jailed side branches)

- Post-PCI FFR emerging tool; >0.91 associated with optimal outcomes: brilakis p.217 (12.1.2).
- TARGET-FFR: 260 randomised; 68.1% initial post-PCI FFR <0.90; FFR-guided optimisation did not significantly increase final FFR ≥0.90 (P=0.099) but reduced final FFR ≤0.80: ref collison-target-ffr-2021 (PMID 34279606, abstract).
- Fig 12.1 algorithm: post-PCI FFR ≤0.80 or iFR ≤0.89 → pullback through stent → in-stent: post-dilation or additional stenting; proximal or distal disease: focal → stent, diffuse → no further treatment: brilakis p.217.
- Causes of suboptimal post-PCI FFR (11 items): brilakis p.218.
- Physiology/imaging to assess result and minimise contrast: brilakis p.191 (10.2.13.2 point 6). Co-registration for stent positioning: brilakis p.187 (10.2.8.2).
- Aorto-ostial final assessment: damping should be gone unless vessel similar to or smaller than guide; FFR or non-hyperaemic index: brilakis p.286 (15.1.10.7); disengage: p.287 (15.1.12).
- CTO post-stenting step-up: brilakis p.395 (21.12.1).
- Koo: 73 jailed side branches with ≥75% stenosis, only 20 with FFR <0.75 (used only for the overestimation sentence): ref koo-jailed-sb-ffr-2005 (PMID 16098427, abstract).
- Jailed side branch: angiographic assessment challenging; IC nitroglycerin may reverse spasm (lesson, keypoint and tip now say 'may reverse spasm', not 'first'); FFR ≤0.80 → further treatment (vessel ≥2 mm), increased risk of adverse events; >0.80 no further treatment: brilakis p.218 (12.1.2 point 2); refs [11–13] incl. koo-jailed-sb-ffr-2005. Visual interpretation frequently overestimates SB stenosis; physiology usable (in non-culprit ACS lesions) to determine SB ischaemia: brilakis p.300 (16.2.10.1.1 step 4).
- LM provisional to LAD with jailed circumflex concern: imaging and physiology; FFR >0.80 or NHPR > cutoff → defer; ≤0.80 → T-stenting, TAP, reverse crush or culotte: brilakis p.333 (17.12.2). Fig 16.79: FFR 0.52, step-up at LM on pullback, significant focal ostial circumflex stenosis: brilakis p.326.
- Bifurcation physiology roles (baseline severity, final result, SB compromise): brilakis p.325 (16.2.12).
- Checks: brilakis p.217 (algorithm), p.218 (jailed SB rule).

## Lesson pm-physiology-derived (Angiography-derived and CT-derived FFR)

- Invasive angiography: good spatial and time resolution; CT up to 320 slices per rotation, whole heart in one short breath-hold: oxford p.29.
- At time of writing guidelines recommended functional non-invasive tests first; CT-FFR and perfusion CT likely to evolve this: oxford p.29.
- High sensitivity and NPV (up to 100%); specificity and PPV lower (<50% in some studies); accuracy influenced by scanner (64-row minimum), pretest probability, high calcium score, high heart rate, high BMI; CT mainly to rule out: oxford p.29.
- CT not usually in high pretest probability, calcium score >1000, large BMI, previous stenting (<3 mm, thick-strut first generation): oxford p.30.
- FFR-CT feasible for native vessels, not validated with prior stents and/or bypass grafts: brilakis p.351 (18.12).
- NXT: 254 patients; ischaemia FFR-CT or FFR ≤0.80; AUC 0.90 vs 0.81; per-patient sensitivity/specificity FFR-CT 86%/79%, CTA 94%/34%, ICA 64%/83%; increase in specificity: ref norgaard-nxt-2014 (PMID 24486266, abstract).
- PLATFORM: 584 patients, usual testing vs CTA/FFR-CT (prospectively assigned); planned ICA with no obstructive CAD 12% vs 73%; ICA cancelled in 61%; similar radiation: ref douglas-platform-2015 (PMID 26330417, abstract).
- QFR: 3D-QCA from two projections plus contrast flow velocity (frame count); no hyperaemia; ≤0.80 threshold: ref westra-favor2-2018 (full text) and favor3-china-2021 (QFR ≤0.80).
- FAVOR II E-J acquisition: nitroglycerin 100–200 µg IC after first projection; two projections per lesion; ≥12.5 frames/s; minimal foreshortening and overlap; brisk continuous fast injections; no zooming or table movement; whole vessel to the transducer location; end-diastolic frame; reference fitted to healthy segments; frame count in a run with clear contrast movement; user interaction at frame selection, contouring and flow evaluation: ref westra-favor2-2018 (PMC full text).
- FAVOR II E-J population: stable angina or secondary evaluation after MI; AMI within 72 h, severe asthma or COPD, contrast or adenosine allergy, AF excluded: ref westra-favor2-2018 (PMC full text, Patient Population). Hybrid limits are a theoretical analysis assuming FFR 100% accurate; defer limit 0.87 in Results and figure, 0.86 in Discussion (both stated in the lesson). Paper could not determine whether standard projections added procedure time (Study Limitations): pearl now says 'may add a little time'.
- FAVOR II E-J results: 317 vessels; sensitivity 86.5% and specificity 86.9% vs 2D-QCA 44.2% and 76.5%; AUC 0.92 vs 0.64; accuracy 71.3% for FFR 0.75–0.84; median time 5.0 vs 7.0 min; hybrid limits 0.77 (treat) and 0.87 (defer) for ≥95% sensitivity and specificity, wire avoided in 64%; Medina 1.1.1 and 1.0.1 excluded; atrial fibrillation excluded: ref westra-favor2-2018.
- FAVOR III China: 3825 patients; 1-year MACE 5.8% vs 8.8%; HR 0.65; driven by fewer MI and ischaemia-driven revascularisation: ref favor3-china-2021 (PMID 34742368, abstract).
- FAVOR III Europe: 2000 patients, 40–90% stenoses; 6.7% vs 4.2%; HR 1.63; non-inferiority not met; results do not support QFR if FFR available: ref favor3-europe-2024 (PMID 39488224, abstract).
- Tip diag-phys-qfr-acquisition: ref westra-favor2-2018; two projections avoiding foreshortening and overlap also in oxford p.175 (Quantification of stenoses: at least two projections, preferably orthogonal, avoid foreshortening and overlap).
- Video 6DArgI6Kilc: title only (product name replaced by the generic class).
- B('12.1') removed from the lesson's sources (12.1 does not discuss derived FFR).

## Lesson pm-physiology-microvascular (CFR, IMR and acetylcholine testing)

- Nearly half of angina patients ANOCA; about two-thirds of these have CMD: ref perera-bhf-cmd-2023 (abstract).
- Pressure ≈ flow only with low microvascular resistance: oxford p.233. Microcirculatory dysfunction in MI culprits: brilakis p.228 (12.3.4).
- Spasm confirmed by IC ergonovine or acetylcholine provocation; CCB first-line; severe vasoconstriction can nearly occlude the vessel causing MI or sudden death; often in mild plaque but also in normal arteries after vasoactive substances (cocaine, epinephrine, chemotherapy agents); traditional risk factors not always present: oxford p.147. Ergonovine provocation rarely performed (intractable spasm → MI): oxford p.180 (Common pitfalls, Catheter-tip spasm).
