## Lesson cm-patient-complications-hypotension

- Oxford: hypotension common pre-, peri- and post-catheterisation; fasting dehydration and hypotensive drugs; slow IV fluid often all that is needed; reversible causes: tamponade, contrast reaction, vasovagal reaction, bleeding, ischaemia — oxford p.304
- Fixed differential; false vs true hypotension; five checks (Y-connector valve, transducer connection or air, damping, obstruction by air/thrombus/contrast, bulky equipment) — brilakis p.485–486 (Fig. 28.1); taught in pm-monitoring-pressure
- Three groups of true hypotension: hypovolaemia, cardiac failure, peripheral vasodilation; stroke volume (myocardial: ischaemia, LV failure, RV failure; valvular: regurgitation, stenosis; pericardial: tamponade, myocardial rupture) or heart rate (brady/tachy) — brilakis p.485–486 (Fig. 28.1)
- Vessel occlusion: hypotension, bradycardia and ventricular tachyarrhythmias common; IABP if cardiogenic shock ensues, if available — oxford p.301
- Hypovolaemia most commonly from bleeding; haematoma at access site; dented bladder sign — brilakis p.487
- Retroperitoneal haematoma: hypotension, diaphoresis, lower abdominal or back pain; often bradycardia (table and step 2 now say 'often') — brilakis p.499
- Tamponade usually with tachycardia — oxford p.308; anaphylaxis: tachycardia and hypotension can occur — oxford p.307
- Vasovagal: heart rate slows before blood pressure — oxford p.308
- Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate; decreased SV from LV/RV dysfunction (usually ischaemia), valve (acute regurgitation), tamponade — brilakis p.487
- Amplatz catheters and deeply curved EBU can push an aortic cusp open causing acute AR; repositioning corrects at once; Fig. 28.2 pressure recovers after withdrawal — brilakis p.487
- Tachy- and bradyarrhythmias reduce output — brilakis p.487
- Peripheral vasodilation: vasovagal, anaphylaxis, nitroglycerin (especially after PDE-5 inhibitor), verapamil intracoronary or intra-arterial for radial spasm — brilakis p.487
- Immediate complete treatment of cause best but not always feasible (acute closure that cannot be reopened); concomitant support — brilakis p.488
- Vasopressors and inotropes, MCS in some; cardiac arrest: CPR ideally automated (Lucas), then VA-ECMO if no prompt recovery — brilakis p.488
- Vasopressors may be required if MAP < 60 mmHg; central line recommended if possible — brilakis p.56
- Right heart catheterisation when cause not apparent; patterns: hypovolaemia low filling/low output; cardiac failure high filling (low in RV failure)/low output; vasodilation low filling/high output — brilakis p.485–486 (Fig. 28.1 and footnote)
- SVR normal 800–1400 dynes·s·cm⁻⁵, low in sepsis, high in cardiogenic shock — oxford p.115
- Table per cause, first moves: saline and blood; arterial access bleed: balloon to stop, then endovascular or surgical repair — brilakis p.488; slow infusion often all needed — oxford p.304; myocardial dysfunction: revascularisation, MCS — brilakis p.488; acute MR/AR: vasodilators may help, often poorly tolerated, urgent/emergent surgery, IABP for acute MR — brilakis p.488; tamponade: pericardiocentesis; myocardial rupture (usually AMI) emergency surgery — brilakis p.488; bradycardia/AV block often from medication such as adenosine, very short duration — brilakis p.488; vasovagal: legs up, atropine, saline — oxford p.308; anaphylaxis: epinephrine and fluid — brilakis p.488; anaphylaxis within 15 minutes of first injection, itching/flushing/rash/wheeze/angio-oedema — oxford p.307
- Pulsus paradoxus after perforation (Fig. 2.2) — brilakis p.38; contrast collecting around the heart, tachycardia, hypotension, syncope within minutes; perform pericardiocentesis if high clinical suspicion — oxford p.305
- Check 1 (AL1 deeper, pressure falls): Amplatz or deeply curved EBU pushes cusp open, repositioning corrects — brilakis p.487; Fig. 28.2 caption: deep advancement of an Amplatz on the aortic valve, pressure rises after withdrawal — brilakis p.487; option now names the deep Amplatz (Brilakis does not name the secondary curve); the five false-reading checks (Y-connector valve among them) come first — brilakis p.485–486. The earlier claim that an open valve gives a flattened trace was removed (Brilakis does not describe its waveform).
- Nitroglycerin: hypotension from venodilation (preload) and arterial dilation (afterload); PDE-5 inhibitors (avanafil, sildenafil, vardenafil within 24 h; tadalafil within 48 h); HOCM worsens LVOT obstruction; do not give in hypotension or RV infarction — brilakis p.42; hypovolaemia when NPO without fluids; vasovagal reaction to nitrates; verapamil before radial angiography — brilakis p.124
- Nicardipine, nitroprusside, verapamil hypotension treated as for nitroglycerin — brilakis p.43
- Treatment: no further nitroglycerin, half-life 1.5–7.5 minutes; normal saline; waiting (often resolves after a few minutes); vasopressors (norepinephrine or phenylephrine) if extreme or persistent; if persists assess other causes such as bleeding — brilakis p.42; saline often given prophylactically after nitroglycerin; phenylephrine in severe cases — brilakis p.125
- Phenylephrine pure vasopressor, choice in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy; norepinephrine mainly vasopressor — brilakis p.56
- Intracoronary nitroglycerin dose by systolic pressure: >120 mmHg 200 mcg; 100–120 mmHg 100 mcg; <100 mmHg 50 mcg or none; wait 1–2 min before angiography; drop in systemic pressure common — brilakis p.124
- Check 2 (inferior STEMI after 200 µg nitro at 112 mmHg; Brilakis's dose would have been 100 µg): brilakis p.42, p.124–125
- Arrhythmias in lab usually due to ischaemia; post-reperfusion arrhythmias — brilakis p.488
- Left main: early vasopressors (norepinephrine) to prevent rapid deterioration; defibrillation pads ideally placed — brilakis p.329
- Prevention list (connections, clearing catheter, avoiding deep engagement; access, radial, avoid excessive anticoagulation; hydration; prophylactic support; avoid prolonged occlusion; avoid perforation; premedication for contrast allergy; early correction of arrhythmias; sedation and hydration for vasovagal; avoid excessive nitroglycerin) — brilakis p.487–488
- Catheter occluding flow → ischaemia, hypotension, arrhythmias, VF; withdraw and re-engage carefully or smaller catheter — brilakis p.126
- Side-hole guides mask damping; avoid in left main except ostial LM CTO — brilakis p.485, p.522
- Pearl (assign support to a team member): this module's reasoning, consistent with brilakis p.488 (support concomitant with treatment of cause)

## Lesson cm-patient-complications-vasovagal

- Tamponade causes include left ventriculography with intramyocardial injection (Fig. 10.2); leave the drain in until bleeding stops (may be a few days) — oxford p.305
- Pitfall note that sitting up belongs to pulmonary oedema — oxford p.302 (sit the patient up if possible)
- Faints in 2–3% of catheterisation patients; neurocardiogenic syncope combined cardioinhibitory and vasodepressor; triggers: high emotion, dehydration, mild starvation, anticipated pain; young anxious males; often on the ward at venesection and cannulation; at arterial puncture and compression haemostasis — oxford p.308; 2–3% also oxford p.299
- Vagal reaction to nitrates — brilakis p.124
- Painful haematoma can precipitate collapse via vagal reaction — oxford p.315
- Local anaesthetic before sheath insertion to minimise pain-induced vasovagal reactions; saline before sheath removal — brilakis p.197
- Presentation: non-specifically unwell, fatigued, nauseated, sweaty; HR slows before BP; pale, syncopal, apnoeic, appear dead; early recognition may abort syncope — oxford p.308
- Management: lie down, legs up; ECG and haemodynamic monitoring; atropine 0.5–1.0 mg IV; rapid 0.9% saline; improve within a few minutes, else reconsider and exclude MI, tamponade, retroperitoneal bleed — oxford p.308
- Atropine 0.5 mg IV push, repeat to total 3 mg; rebound tachycardia, dry mouth, blurred vision, flushing — brilakis p.58
- Vasovagal reaction treated with normal saline, atropine for bradycardia — brilakis p.197; atropine for vasovagal bradycardia during perforation — brilakis p.449
- Keep compressing during vagal reaction at sheath removal: this module's reasoning (supported by the access-compression-vasovagal tip in pm-closure); personnel available to give atropine or fluids during manual compression; telemetry and non-invasive BP — brilakis p.196
- Mimics: tamponade can present similarly and as quickly but usually with tachycardia; immediate echo if uncertain — oxford p.308
- Retroperitoneal: hypotension, diaphoresis, lower abdominal or back pain, bradycardia often, tenderness on abdominal/suprapubic palpation, usually within first 3 hours; closure devices do not exclude; suspect in every patient with hypotension after femoral access — brilakis p.499, p.501
- Anaphylaxis: within 15 min of first injection; pruritus, flushing, rash, wheeze, angio-oedema; tachycardia — oxford p.307
- MI: chest pain, pallor, sweating; hypotension, bradycardia common; ST elevation with occlusion — oxford p.301
- Check 2 (79-year-old after closure device): brilakis p.499, p.501 (general measures; reverse anticoagulation after diagnosis established — p.500 Fig. 29.4)
- Prevention: adequate sedation and hydration — brilakis p.488; local anaesthetic, saline before sheath removal — brilakis p.197; personnel ready, telemetry — brilakis p.196
- Pearl (write the time; pre-agreed look for blood and pericardial fluid): this module's reasoning from oxford p.308
- Pitfall "sitting a fainting patient up": reasoning from oxford p.308 (lie down)

## Lesson cm-patient-complications-arrhythmia

- Arrhythmia 0.4%, with VF, VT, AF, SVT and heart block listed under it (no proportions given; 'most of it' removed) — oxford p.299; transient benign arrhythmias frequently seen (supraventricular and ventricular ectopics, sinus bradycardia; 'far commoner' removed) — oxford p.309
- Usually ischaemic; often after reperfusion — brilakis p.488
- Baseline ECG morphology and heart rate; changes of concern: new ST depression, ST elevation, bradycardia, tachycardia, QRS widening, VPBs during wire manipulation, VF — brilakis p.37–38
- 12-lead ECG before catheterisation as baseline — oxford p.41
- Physiologist calls: SBP <90, damping/ventricularisation, ST changes, significant bradycardia, new BBB (especially left), heart block, flutter/AF, VT, AIVR (useful in primary PCI), VF, asystole, 15–20 min ACT warning — oxford p.61
- Team knows who defibrillates — oxford p.43
- Defibrillation pads should ideally be placed in left main PCI — brilakis p.329 (step retitled 'Pads on in left main PCI'; the tip no longer mentions other high-risk work or draping)
- Trigger table: VF from catheter near stenosed ostium, plugging (intermediate), conus injection, ischaemic complications (dissection, air) — oxford p.309; conus damping, injection → myocardial blush, VF; withdraw quickly and gently; rotation and angulation or different catheter — oxford p.157; selective RCA engagement involves conus and may cause VF with large volume — oxford p.180; conus branch is the first RCA branch and supplies the RV outflow tract; injection gives a myocardial blush and VF — oxford p.157 (section 'Catheter manipulation'). The module no longer repeats oxford p.61's statement that the conus also supplies the AV node, which is anatomically wrong (the AV nodal artery arises at the crux); catheter occluding flow → hypotension, arrhythmias, VF; withdraw, re-engage carefully or smaller catheter — brilakis p.126; contrast not clearing → VF/VT; disengage — brilakis p.275; contrast injection: transient bradycardia (resolves in seconds), ST changes, QRS widening; ventricular arrhythmias uncommon — oxford p.101; transient bradycardia/junctional rhythm on injection, recovers spontaneously or with atropine 0.5–1 mg IV — oxford p.312
- Adenosine: heart block most likely in RCA; avoid high doses in RCA; slow administration; aminophylline 250–300 mg IV over 10 min for RCA atherectomy; watchful waiting — brilakis p.44; AF most commonly documented (2.7% after IV), well tolerated except accessory pathways; treatment DC cardioversion, or amiodarone and AV nodal blockers (beta blockers or calcium channel blockers) — brilakis p.44–45; torsades/VF after pause (R on T) or without pause; cough; defibrillation — brilakis p.45; Fig. 3.2 (RCA 40 mcg adenosine → CHB → AF, stented, sinus rhythm restored with cardioversion at end of procedure) — brilakis p.45; physiology: do not give >100 mcg IC in RCA; VF prevention RCA or dominant circumflex; AV block without escape: transient CPR and temporary pacing; cough (self-CPR) — brilakis p.224
- Papaverine may cause VF — brilakis p.44
- Atherectomy bradycardia: aminophylline 250–300 mg IV over 10 min or 20–40 mg IC; atropine 0.5–1.0 mg IV — brilakis p.355; temporary pacemaker, aminophylline or brief test runs for RCA/dominant LCx; test runs allow tailored runs and avoid RV perforation from the pacing lead; orbital atherectomy may be less likely to cause bradycardia than rotational; bigger burrs and longer runs more likely — brilakis p.360; aggressive runs (prolonged, forceful advancement) as a cause, avoid them; bradycardia usually resolves after atherectomy stops — brilakis p.365
- VT during RV outflow tract manipulation and LV catheterisation — oxford p.309; AF from atrial irritation during RHC, ischaemia, pericarditis; most self-terminate — oxford p.310; SVT from catheter manipulation in RA and RV with predisposition such as accessory pathway — oxford p.311
- "Taking the catheter out of the ventricle is the obvious first move": labelled as this module's reasoning in the table
- Mechanical injury to right bundle or RCA ischaemia → advanced block; recovery within minutes unless ongoing ischaemia; first-degree and Mobitz I no urgent intervention; Mobitz II and complete block may need urgent temporary transvenous pacing; atropine 1 mg may improve AV conduction; isoprenaline rarely improves conduction, increases sinus automaticity — oxford p.312
- Amiodarone hypotension: allergic reaction, or vasodilation and depressed contractility partly from the solvent; caution with low baseline pressure; stop amiodarone, treat anaphylaxis, otherwise vasopressors and inotropes — brilakis p.57
- Amiodarone: bradycardia/AV block (do not give in second/third-degree block without pacemaker; temporary pacing may be needed); torsades/VF with prolonged QT or WPW with AF; defibrillate and stop — brilakis p.57–58
- Inotrope arrhythmias: excess adrenergic stimulation, ischaemia; lowest dose, MCS; lidocaine, amiodarone; VA-ECMO refractory — brilakis p.56–57
- Check 1 (adenosine CHB then AF): brilakis p.44–45 (Fig. 3.2); flecainide 2 mg/kg (max 150 mg) IV over 30 min only with good haemodynamics, no pulmonary oedema and no known structural heart disease (no previous ischaemic heart disease or valve disease, normal echo) — oxford p.311
- Cardioversion: under IV benzodiazepine sedation or GA; gel pads over sternum and apex; electrodes connected firmly, ECG leads of defibrillator connected, lead with tallest QRS; sync with dot on R wave not T; change leads if not optimal; stable starting energies 200 J monophasic, 150 J biphasic; firm pressure on the defibrillator electrodes 8–12 kg reduces impedance and improves success (Oxford describes hand-held electrodes over gel contact pads) — oxford p.281; the 2021 ERC guideline refers only to defibrillation pads (not paddles), and hands-on defibrillation during manual compressions is a risk to the rescuer even with gloves — ref soar-erc-als-2021 (read in the authorised German translation, PMC8185697)
- Poorly tolerated VT: 200–360 J or equivalent biphasic — oxford p.309; AF with severe compromise: 360 J monophasic or 200 J biphasic — oxford p.311
- VF: unsynchronised high initial energies 200–360 J monophasic, 150–200 J biphasic; if repeatedly unsuccessful check settings and consider different defibrillator; internal cardioversion via balloon flotation catheter for resistant arrhythmias — oxford p.281; amiodarone 300 mg IV and different defibrillator, correct K and Mg — oxford p.309
- VA-ECMO for refractory arrhythmias — brilakis p.57
- Up to three successive shocks may be considered for initial VF/pVT in a witnessed arrest with a defibrillator immediately available, e.g. in the cath lab — ref soar-erc-als-2021 (German translation PMC8185697)
- Tachycardias that persist: poorly tolerated VT, synchronised DC 200–360 J under sedation/GA; tolerated VT, metoprolol 5 mg IV bolus or amiodarone 150 mg IV over 10 min via a large vein, both repeatable; monitor for hypotension, inform an anaesthetist, prepare for DC — oxford p.309; lidocaine 50 mg over 2 min, every 5 min, to a maximum of 200 mg; overdrive pacing for sustained monomorphic VT terminates 80–90%, 15–20 bpm faster than the VT, a high output (5–10 V) may be required (module now says 'may be needed', not 'often'), capture signs, stop after 5–10 s of capture, risk of acceleration to VF, background pacing 90–110 bpm afterwards; AF precipitants, majority self-terminate; severe compromise (rate >150, hypotension and hypoperfusion, reduced conscious level, pulmonary oedema, ischaemia): high-flow O2, heparin 5000–10,000 IU IV — oxford p.310; immediate synchronised DC 360 J monophasic or 200 J biphasic; mild–moderate compromise: heparin then weight-adjusted LMWH if AF persists; flecainide conditions; electrical cardioversion 2–3 h later if the drug fails; amiodarone 300 mg over 1 h, poor efficacy — oxford p.311; details also in pm-lab-drugs-antiarrhythmics
- Tolerated VT, current guidance: the 2022 ESC ventricular arrhythmia guideline makes synchronised cardioversion first-line for haemodynamically tolerated sustained monomorphic VT when the sedation or anaesthetic risk is low, so Oxford's drug-first sequence becomes the alternative; same statement as pm-lab-drugs-antiarrhythmics — ref esc-va-2022 (PMID 36017572). Checked: the summary by Könemann and Eckardt (PMC9691474, full text read) reports the new class I recommendation for electrical cardioversion as first-line even for haemodynamically tolerated VT; the guideline's own section 6.1.2 (read on the publisher's page) recommends prompt termination even of tolerated SMVT and says the individual risk of the anaesthesia or sedation needed for cardioversion must be considered
- 'Remove the trigger' item: treating ischaemia usually treats the rhythm — brilakis p.488; removing the catheter from the ventricle is labelled as this module's reasoning
- "A defibrillator in sync mode waits for an R wave that VF does not have": reasoning from oxford p.281 (sync times the shock to the R wave), as in cp-complex-patients-lab-arrest
- Cough to maintain consciousness until defibrillation — brilakis p.45
- Check 2 (150 µg adenosine, no escape): brilakis p.224; amiodarone slows AV conduction — brilakis p.57; aminophylline preventive — brilakis p.44, p.355

## Lesson cm-patient-complications-radiation

- Deterministic (skin injury, cataract) vs stochastic (cancer, birth defects); deterministic correlate with air kerma to a skin area — brilakis p.488
- Oxford lists impaired fetal development among deterministic effects (books classify fetal harm differently) — oxford p.35
- Coronary angiogram: typical effective dose 6 mSv, equivalent background 2.7 years, additional lifetime fatal cancer risk 1 in 3500; normal lifetime cancer risk about one in three — oxford p.37 (Table 1.2)
- Exposed worker limit <20 mSv a year averaged over 5 years; typical interventional cardiologist up to 5 mSv a year — oxford p.35
- Thresholds: <5 Gy unlikely; 5–10 possible; 10–15 likely, physicist assessment; >15 Gy sentinel event, US Joint Commission, reporting — brilakis p.488 (checked against PDF image; extract garbles the symbols). Brilakis lists these as air kerma here and at p.39, but p.493 defines the Joint Commission sentinel event as peak skin dose >15 Gy; the table row, caption and keypoint now say so
- Fig. 28.3: erythema and epilation on back 1 month after CTO PCI, 11.8 Gy air kerma — brilakis p.489
- Monitor cumulative air kerma and DAP continuously; usually stop if air kerma exceeds 5–7 Gy; >15 Gy sentinel event; dose rate trackable; operator dosimeters — brilakis p.39 (checked on PDF image)
- Protocol announcing dose every 1 Gy and/or every 30 min; consider stopping at 7–8 Gy and staging — brilakis p.492 (checked on PDF image)
- 'Many operators do not typically look at' the dose rate; feedback, change if >20 mGy/min — brilakis p.492, p.490 (Fig. 28.4)
- Real-time operator dosimeters reduce operator dose by approximately 30% — brilakis p.492
- Radiographer must warn operator at 1 Gy; operator records exposure in report and communicates it to patient — oxford p.39
- Levers: heavy foot, pedal lag especially early in training — brilakis p.490; Oxford: think before screening; let intensifier finish moving — oxford p.36; markers, guide length, 90 cm guide proximal marker — brilakis p.490; trapping technique; fluoro store; cine ≈10× fluoroscopy dose, not in fluoroscopy time — brilakis p.490 (checked on PDF image); IVUS reduces contrast and radiation — brilakis p.491
- 6–7.5 fps (3.75 fps in thin patients), halves dose vs 15 fps, routine — brilakis p.491 (checked on PDF image); Abdelaal trial: 7.5 vs 15 fps, operator dose −30%, patient DAP −19%, fluoroscopy times similar, 363 transradial patients — ref abdelaal-low-frame-rate-2014
- Low magnification — brilakis p.491; magnification increases skin dose rate — oxford p.37
- Collimation reduces skin area and DAP, total air kerma unchanged, less overlap; caveat: wire tips/guide out of view, distal wire migration, deep guide engagement — brilakis p.491
- Steep angles >30° from AP higher exposure, automatic dose increase not recognised; AP includes spine so slightly angulated views preferred; RAO lower operator dose, challenging for RCA wiring, excellent for mid-LAD — brilakis p.491
- Table high, image intensifier close; Fig. 28.6 (1.0, 1.4, 2.6 dose units; 3 Gy → 4.2 Gy → 7.8 Gy; panel C 260%) — brilakis p.491–492 (figure reproduced from the 2018 consensus, ref radiation-consensus-2018)
- Reposition image intensifier often; dose spread over skin areas — brilakis p.492
- Newer X-ray machines; very obese patients with newer systems; ROI add-on (Control Rad) reduces dose rate to the rest of the screen, resulting in about 75% radiation dose reduction overall; Zero Gravity, robotic PCI, EggNest operator only — brilakis p.489–490
- Check 1 (geometry), rewritten in the figure's own terms: first half 3 Gy skin dose with table high and receptor close; second half low table and raised receptor gives about 7.8 Gy; 4.2 Gy for low table with receptor close; the figure is about skin dose at the beam entrance, not displayed air kerma — brilakis p.492 (Fig. 28.6 caption)
- Follow-up: >5 Gy follow-up within a month, document skin exam, inspect back, refer dermatologist/plastic surgeon; biopsy risk → non-healing ulcer; >10 Gy physicist peak skin dose promptly, skin exam at 2–4 weeks; PSD >15 Gy sentinel event, risk management and regulators within 24 h — brilakis p.493 (checked on PDF image)
- Prior radiation injury on the back: may postpone or modify procedure — brilakis p.33; limit cine runs and avoid including affected area in beam — brilakis p.35
- IR(ME)R 2017: register of referrers, practitioners, operators; cardiologist often all three; clinical evaluation recorded for all exposures including fluoroscopy; doses audited; greater-than-expected dose: contact radiation protection adviser — oxford p.36. (Oxford's sentence on an action for overexposure under criminal, not civil, law was deleted from the module as legally confused.)
- IR(ME)R incident definition; near miss; incident not requiring notification; requiring notification; overexposure typically ≥10× intended dose — oxford p.39
- Team protection: inverse square law (1 m to 2 m reduces four-fold) — oxford p.36; distance, appendages out of field — brilakis p.493; lead gowns 0.25–0.35 mm LE, thyroid shield 0.35 mm, lead glasses — oxford p.36; fixed shields close to patient and not too high; disposable shields over abdomen, not in field of view (raises tube output); manifold extension (Fig. 28.7) — brilakis p.493–494
- Staff limits: employee 20 mSv/yr effective, 500 skin and extremities, 20 eye; trainee <18: 6/150/15; women of reproductive capacity 13 mSv to abdomen per 3 months — oxford p.39
- Radiographer advice when high doses approached (projections, fluoro save, collimation, low acquisition rate, avoid zoom, filtration) — oxford p.63 (used in existing tips shown)

## Lesson cm-patient-complications-kidney

- No specific treatment once established, prevention the goal; most return to baseline; some dialysis — brilakis p.496
- Oxford: transient renal dysfunction up to 15% of coronary interventions; about 7 in 1000 AKI requiring renal support; other causes cholesterol embolisation and nephrotoxic drugs (aminoglycosides) — oxford p.331
- <1% irreversible dialysis-dependent; significance of minor rises in creatinine yet to be proven; dialysis-requiring AKI 35% mortality at 1 year; creatinine increases ≥25% excess mortality, sepsis, bleeding; measures not shown to ameliorate — oxford p.333
- Risk factors (Brilakis): CKD eGFR <30 incidence up to 27%; CV/CrCl >2; hypotension (cardiogenic shock, acute HF, ACS); age >75; diabetes; anaemia; low EF; volume depletion — brilakis p.494
- Risk factors (Oxford): pre-existing renal dysfunction, diabetes; age, hypertension, hypovolaemia, CCF, PVD, high contrast volumes — oxford p.333
- Scores: Mehran, BMC2, NCDR Cath-PCI — brilakis p.494; Mehran variables — ref mehran-cin-2004 (as listed in fnd-safety-contrast)
- Hydration single most important pre- and post-procedural measure; IV normal saline because no other solutions (bicarbonate, half-normal saline) shown advantageous; high-dose statins; discontinue NSAIDs — brilakis p.494 (lesson now notes the books differ on half-normal saline: oxford p.333 allows 0.45–0.9%)
