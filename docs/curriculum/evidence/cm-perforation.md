- kinnaird-perforation-uk-2016: PMID 27486140, Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2016;9(8):e003449. Abstract figures used: 67 all-comer studies, 5,568,191 PCIs 1982–2020; pooled incidence 0.39% (about 1 in 250); tamponade 21.1%; Ellis III 43%; mortality 7.5%; risk factors female sex, hypertension, CKD, previous CABG; distal wire exit 37%, balloon catheters 28%; covered stents 25%; emergency surgery 17%.

Existing registry reference used: ellis-1994 (PMID 7994814, Circulation 1994;90(6):2725–2730, re-checked on PubMed). ## Lesson cm-perforation-classify

- Perforation among the most feared complications because it can lead to effusion and tamponade; effusions accumulate rapidly, so often hypotension, sometimes emergency pericardiocentesis, rarely surgery (round 2: reason now given as effusion and tamponade, rapid build-up as a separate sentence): brilakis p.447
- Meta-analysis figures in 'Across many series' and the location-table caption note: ref mikhail-perforation-copit-2022
- Women and older patients over-represented: ref ellis-1994, ref kinnaird-perforation-uk-2016
- Rest of Ellis's registry (0.5% of 12,900; atherectomy/laser 1.3% vs PTCA 0.1%; class distribution; surgery avoided 85/90/44%): ref ellis-1994
- Can instead produce a loculated effusion (especially prior CABG) compressing chambers, intramyocardial haematoma, or intracavitary bleeding: brilakis p.447
- 0.3% of PCI; not reported in routine diagnostic studies; more common in complex cases (CTOs, ostial lesions, tortuous vessels) and debulking (atherectomy); half wire-related: oxford p.329
- UK BCIS 2006–2013: 1,762 of 527,121 (0.33%); predictors age, previous CABG, left main, rotational atherectomy, CTO; worse adjusted outcomes; emergency surgery 3%: ref kinnaird-perforation-uk-2016 (and brilakis p.456 for the 3% of 1,762)
- CTO: perforations common, most without serious consequences; tamponade about 0.3%, retrograde about 1.3%; occlusion of perforated CTO target vessel usually causes no ischaemia, allowing sequential strategies and preparation of hardware: brilakis p.447
- Diagnostic catheterisation cardiac tamponade 0.1%: oxford p.299
- Classification by location: large vessel, distal vessel, collateral (septal or epicardial); 75% large vessel, 25% distal in one series: brilakis p.447 (Figs 26.1, 26.2 p.448)
- Definitive treatments by location (table): brilakis p.450 Fig. 26.5, p.451 (dissection techniques), p.462 (collateral: epicardial serious complication of retrograde CTO PCI, rapid tamponade; septal unlikely adverse, but septal haematomas, RV wall haematomas and dry tamponade reported; round 2 added the caveat to the location table), p.590 (coils should be available for collateral perforation)
- Ellis classes I, II, III, III cavity spilling (coronary sinus, RV, LV): brilakis p.447, p.449 (Fig. 26.3); also brilakis p.133 (6.8.2.12)
- Ellis registry outcomes per class, delayed tamponade 2/15 within 24 h, observe ≥24 h, low-risk perforations occasionally poor outcome: ref ellis-1994
- Coronary sinus perforation from wire and microcatheter, hypotension, coiling required: brilakis p.449 (Fig. 26.4)
- Classification must be adapted for epicardial and septal collateral channels: brilakis p.447
- Check 1: class II definition; classes I/II may seal with prolonged balloon inflation: brilakis p.447
- Oversized stents or high-pressure inflations, especially calcified vessels or SVGs; Fig. 26.6 NC balloon at 25 atm for an undilatable lesion, vessel rupture: brilakis p.450
- Stent diameter to match distal reference; sizing to proximal vessel can cause distal dissection or perforation: brilakis p.186 (10.2)
- Intravascular imaging to guide balloon and stent size; avoid very high-pressure inflations: brilakis p.451
- Balloon rupture (round 2 wording: "More likely in" rather than "Commonest in"; a pressure drop "suggests" rupture, fluoroscopy confirms: brilakis p.180 "should be suspected if there is sudden indeflator pressure drop. Fluoroscopy can confirm"; p.359 "Rapid loss of pressure suggests balloon rupture"): angiogram immediately after removal (p.450); causes complex (highly calcified) lesions and high pressures >20 atm; prevention: consider atherectomy or lithotripsy before balloon angioplasty in heavy calcium, avoid >20 atm; sudden pressure drop: suction immediately to prevent contrast exiting forcefully, remove balloon, aspirate guide (air), angiography; new balloon immediately to the site if perforated: brilakis p.180 (9.7.9.3.1); rupture of an inadequately prepared balloon risks air embolism, new balloon proximal to perforation: brilakis p.359 (19.9.3)
- Rotational atherectomy perforation causes (small vessel, large burr, high speed, tortuosity, Rotawire tip too close to lesion, wire fracture with uncontrolled ablation, extreme wire bias); prevention avoid burr oversizing and highly tortuous vessels: brilakis p.364 (19.9.5.7.2)
- Atherectomy risks (incl. perforation) rise with tortuosity (relative contraindication), thrombus, dissections, bypass grafts: brilakis p.418 (23.2.4)
- Severe tortuous anatomy a contraindication (increased risk of perforation); presence of coronary dissection a contraindication: oxford p.269
- Orbital: slow speed 80,000 rpm initial, less risk than high speed 120,000 rpm; high speed avoided in arteries <3.0 mm: brilakis p.369 (19.9.6.6)
- Laser: 0.9 mm catheter usually at maximum settings (80 Hz, 80 mJ/mm²); perforation especially in tortuous vessels; risk may be higher with the high settings and contrast; lower inside stents; laser with contrast generally only within previously deployed stents: brilakis p.412 (23.1.6)
- Wire exit then device advancement enlarges a small self-sealing hole; extravasation may occur only after a stent: brilakis p.450
- Confirm wire position within vessel architecture (true lumen or subintimal) before advancing other equipment: brilakis p.451
- If the wire exits the vessel structure, withdraw and redirect without advancing microcatheters, balloons or stents: brilakis p.392 (21.8.1)
- Distal migration: stiff, tapered, polymer-jacketed wires; knuckled wires not safe: brilakis p.453; trapping vs hydraulic exchange: brilakis p.453, p.160, p.163
- Hydrophilic wires positioned very distally: care to avoid perforation: oxford p.216
- Round 2 (distal-migration row and keypoint): workhorse wires have a soft tip and no polymer jacket, which reduces the risk of dissection and perforation; hydrophilic coating (different from a full polymer jacket) increases deliverability but increases the risk of vessel injury: brilakis p.561 (30.7.1); polymer-jacketed wires carry increased risk of distal perforation, use only when necessary and exchange for a workhorse before balloons and stents: brilakis p.561 (30.7.2); "Do not use polymer-jacketed guidewires for stenting": brilakis p.286 (15.1.10, aorto-ostial stenting, distal wire perforation)
- Pearl (round 2): "Many perforations ... come from one of the two" (no quantity claimed); module framing of the causes on brilakis p.450, p.453
- Deep wiring technique, obvious risk of perforation; distal perforation more likely when manipulating against resistance: brilakis p.177 (9.7.8.3.2.2.1)
- Knuckle less likely to perforate but larger if it occurs; keep the knuckle small, not beyond the radiopaque portion; avoid knuckles on highly supportive wires: brilakis p.154 (8.6.2.7)
- Clues to extraluminal wire: abnormal course or motion with the cardiac cycle, multiple ventricular extrasystoles; perforation diagnosed by dye accumulating in the pericardial space or pulsatile exit: oxford p.263; diagnosis usually immediately visible on angiography: oxford p.329; contralateral injection and orthogonal projections help determine wire position: brilakis p.392 (21.8.1)
- Acute occlusion: soft workhorse wires first; escalation to stiffer polymer-jacketed wires increases perforation risk, avoid if possible: brilakis p.379 (20.8.1)
- Collimation may hide wire tips (distal perforation): brilakis p.491 (28.2.2.4.3); also p.40 (2.12)
- Monitor the wire tip continuously or at least intermittently, especially with collimation and when equipment delivery or withdrawal is difficult (excessive wire movement); a system with high resolution in part of the screen and low resolution elsewhere (including the wire location) is another option: brilakis p.164–165 (8.10.3.1)
- Wire should not sit in a small branch before balloon inflation, especially a branch close to the lesion, as the balloon may enter it and perforate it: brilakis p.168 (9.7.1.3.1)
- Deep wiring: if the wire will not pass into a cavity, pull back carefully and redirect to another channel: brilakis p.177 (9.7.8.3.2.2.1)
- Zero-contrast PCI: TTE before and after to exclude effusion from perforation: brilakis p.495 (28.3.2)
- Check 2: acute occlusion initial wiring with soft workhorse wires to minimise perforation risk: brilakis p.379 (20.8.1)
- Tip pci-perf-wire-tip-watch: brilakis p.453–454, p.160, p.164–165 (8.10.3.1), p.491; oxford p.216
- Tip pci-perf-size-and-pressure: brilakis p.450–451, p.186, p.180

## Lesson cm-perforation-first-minutes

- Universal algorithm (Fig. 26.5): brilakis p.450
- Balloon proximal to or at the site, immediately; same size as vessel; semi-compliant; no more than 8–10 atm to occlude antegrade flow without overstretching; prolonged, at least 10–15 min; may suffice for less severe perforations (Ellis I or II): brilakis p.447
- Culprit vessel immediately occluded with a balloon; prolonged inflation may be required; perfusion balloons available to reduce downstream ischaemia: oxford p.329
- Second arterial access 'should be strongly considered' (taught as 'recommends giving strong consideration'; case 1 s2 'should be strongly considered') in large vessel perforation if guide not 8 Fr; second guide (ping pong) with specific hardware while haemostasis maintained with balloon through first guide; haemostasis confirmed by contrast injection (round 2: no longer tied to after device placement): brilakis p.447, p.449
- Step 1 note (round 2): Academy comp-perforation gives 5–10 minutes (src/content/tracks/complications.ts, lesson comp-perf-large-vessel); Brilakis at least 10–15 minutes: brilakis p.447
- Step 4 addition (round 2): vasopressors and inotropes, mechanical circulatory support in some patients; cardiac arrest: CPR ideally with an automated system, then VA-ECMO if no prompt recovery: brilakis p.488 (28.1.3.1)
- IV fluids and vasopressors; atropine if bradycardia from vasovagal reaction: brilakis p.449
- Haemodynamic instability requires immediate pericardiocentesis; smaller effusions may be best managed conservatively as elevated pericardial pressure may help tamponade the site; X-ray guidance often possible because of contrast exit; echo for size, guidance if time allows, result; echo contrast agent detects ongoing bleeding: brilakis p.449
- "? autotransfusion" in the algorithm: brilakis p.450
- Bleeding into the pericardium can rapidly cause tamponade with collapse; immediate pericardiocentesis: oxford p.329
- Cardiac surgery notified early: brilakis p.449; surgery in 3% of 1,762 UK perforations: brilakis p.456; ref kinnaird-perforation-uk-2016
- Persistent extravasation → treat cause; continued → reverse anticoagulation → failure → surgery: brilakis p.450
- Stop GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors or cangrelor: brilakis p.450
- Oxford: consider protamine and platelets if abciximab (ReoPro) given: oxford p.329; three IV antiplatelet agents available in the US (eptifibatide, tirofiban, cangrelor): brilakis p.54 (3.5.2.2); no reversal agent, infusion stopped: brilakis p.55; tamponade: consider protamine, platelets and clotting factors: oxford p.305; CTO: reversal of heparin and covered stent may be necessary: oxford p.263
- Reversal in most cases NOT until equipment removed and pericardium evacuated; risk of guide/target vessel thrombosis and of pericardial blood thrombosis making it inaccessible to drainage: brilakis p.449
- Protamine Brilakis 26.2: 1 mg per 100 units, maximum 50 mg, rate ≤5 mg/min; anaphylaxis with prior NPH insulin or fish allergy: brilakis p.449
- Protamine Brilakis 3.4: 1–1.5 mg per 100 units, not to exceed 50 mg, slowly to minimise hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis; risk factors NPH or protamine zinc insulin, fish hypersensitivity, vasectomy, severe LV dysfunction, abnormal preoperative pulmonary haemodynamics; severe systemic hypotension and relative pulmonary hypertension usually 5–10 min after; methylene blue may help recovery: brilakis p.48
- Keypoint 'All three sources cap the dose at 50 mg and give it slowly (Brilakis no faster than 5 mg/min; Oxford over 10 minutes)': brilakis p.449, p.48; oxford p.306
- Protamine Oxford: IV infusion over 10 min, max 50 mg; 1 mg neutralises 80–100 U when given within 15 min of heparin; half-life 60–90 min so lower doses if longer; high doses anticoagulant; allergic reactions particularly diabetics who received isophane insulin: oxford p.306
- CTO: UFH preferred because reversible in severe perforation; bivalirudin best avoided (cannot be reversed); GP IIb/IIIa and cangrelor not given even after successful crossing and stenting because minor wire perforations could reopen and cause delayed effusion and tamponade: brilakis p.390 (21.3)
- Check 1: semi-compliant, vessel-sized, 8–10 atm, 10–15 min: brilakis p.447
- Check 2: reversal timing and rate: brilakis p.449; oxford p.306
- Tip pci-perf-balloon-now: brilakis p.447; oxford p.329 (wire statement: module inference, see notes)
- Tip pci-perf-second-access: brilakis p.447, p.449, p.451
- Tip pci-perf-support-and-call: brilakis p.449; oxford p.329, p.305

## Lesson cm-perforation-large-vessel

- Terminology: single guide "block and deliver"; two guides dual, "ping pong" or "dueling": brilakis p.451
- Treatment order: balloon; covered stent if extravasation persists despite prolonged inflations; single or dual guide; prolonged inflations if covered stent cannot be delivered; heparin usually not reversed until haemostasis and all equipment removed; emergency surgery if bleeding continues; dissection techniques (subintimal plane proximal or distal) require extensive dissection/re-entry experience: brilakis p.451
- Coils can stop a large vessel perforation by occluding the vessel: brilakis p.590 (30.18.2); vascular plugs for emergency occlusion in perforation: brilakis p.592 (30.18.3)
- Oxford: balloon, prolonged inflation, consider protamine, covered stents to seal, coils for small vessel leaks: oxford p.329
- Covered stent availability as of 2020 (two in US, both approved through a humanitarian device exemption for large vessel perforations; one in Europe): brilakis p.589
- Diameters (discrete): sandwich 2.8, 3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 4.8 mm; polyurethane 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 5.0 mm: brilakis p.451 (Table 26.1)
- Dual (ping-pong) technique often required for the sandwich design, though balloon plus covered stent feasible through 8 Fr: brilakis p.589 (item 8)
- Sandwich design: two stainless steel stents with ePTFE middle layer; bulky, difficult to deliver, excellent guide support; 2.8–4.8 mm; lengths 16–26 mm (Table 26.1: 16, 19, 26); 6 Fr for 2.8–4.0 mm, 7 Fr for 4.5 and 4.8 mm; difficult through previously deployed stents (distal anchor, guide extension); minimum 15 atm, higher pressures up to 60 s and IVUS preferred; shortening up to 1.6 mm each side (3.2 mm at nominal 15 atm), overlap; postdilate shoulders if extravasation persists behind stent; delivery of balloon and covered stent feasible through 8 Fr: brilakis p.589; brilakis p.451 (Table 26.1, "usually 8 Fr for Graftmaster")
- Polyurethane design: single stent; 5 Fr (6 Fr for 4.5 and 5.0 mm); 2.5–5.0 mm; 15, 20, 26 mm; postdilation maxima 3.50, 4.65, 5.63 mm; easier to deliver, can be dislodged from balloon: brilakis p.451 (Table 26.1), p.589–590
- Single-layer PTFE (Europe): cobalt-chromium open-cell platform, PTFE membrane clamped at proximal and distal ends, crossing profile 1.1–1.4 mm, 5 Fr for all sizes: brilakis p.590
- Second guide ideally ≥6 Fr for the polyurethane stent and 8 Fr for the sandwich stent: brilakis p.453 (Fig. 26.8 legend)
- Single-guide steps A–I (second wire to guide tip, transient deflation, reinflation, covered stent to balloon as distal anchor and support, deflate and deliver, reinflate if fails, remove first wire and balloon, deploy and postdilate, high pressure for sandwich design, extra stent for residual dissection as re-entry point): brilakis p.452 (Fig. 26.7)
- Two-guide steps A–I (first guide pulled back into aorta, second guide to ostium with second wire and covered stent, transient deflation, delivery, reinflate if fails, remove first wire and balloon, deploy and postdilate): brilakis p.453 (Fig. 26.8)
- Covered stents bulky, excellent support, distal anchor; postdilate aggressively for expansion and to reduce long-term thrombosis (round 2: reworded to avoid the source sentence); residual dissection sealed with additional stent (re-entry point for bleeding): brilakis p.451
- 'Do not reverse heparin while equipment is in the artery': brilakis p.449 (risk of guide/target vessel thrombosis), p.451 (usually not reversed until haemostasis and all equipment removed)
- Pitfall 'nominal 15 atm ... higher pressures up to 60 seconds, ideally IVUS': brilakis p.589 (items 6, 7)
- Fig. 26.9 (taught as a separate bullet, not linked causally to heparin): calcified LAD CTO, orbital atherectomy, balloon rupture perforation, balloon inflated, second guide, 2.8 × 19 mm covered stent over 8 Fr guide extension, thrombus in LAD stents, dissection into left main, aspiration and LM stenting, small effusion: brilakis p.454
- Covered stents increased risk of restenosis and thrombosis: brilakis p.114 (5.6.4.4), p.443 (25.2.3.2)
- Equipment expiration expected for covered stents and coils: brilakis p.513; must be available in all labs: brilakis p.588 (30.18); CTO-complex PCI cart with covered stents and coils: brilakis p.597 (30.23)
- Checks 1 and 2: brilakis p.451–453, p.589
- Tips pci-perf-covered-one-guide, pci-perf-covered-two-guides, pci-perf-covered-expand: brilakis p.451–453, p.589

## Lesson cm-perforation-distal

- Distal perforations occasionally difficult to diagnose, especially with collimation; slow; tamponade may occur hours after; monitor closely; no GP IIb/IIIa or cangrelor: brilakis p.451, p.453
- Insidious presentation with progressive hypotension and tamponade back on the ward: oxford p.329
- Delayed tamponade within 24 h, observe ≥24 h: ref ellis-1994
- Causes: inadvertent advancement of wire, balloon and/or microcatheter into a distal small branch; stiff, tapered, polymer-jacketed wires more likely; knuckled wires not safe; OTW exchanges; trapping first choice, wire extensions second, avoid Nanto (hydraulic) exchange: brilakis p.453
- Prevention: attention to distal wire position (stiff and polymer-jacketed), trapping, exchange stiff/polymer-jacketed for workhorse immediately after crossing: brilakis p.454
- Step 1 balloon proximal; pericardiocentesis if hypotension; notify surgery: brilakis p.456
- Promptly used blocking balloon may leave only a small effusion not requiring pericardiocentesis: brilakis p.460
- Step 2: if sealed, observation and heparin reversal after equipment removal; suction through microcatheter may collapse the vessel; definitive treatment preferred in most cases to minimise late reopening and late tamponade: brilakis p.456
- Step 3: embolisation most common (fat or coils); covered stent across ostium if too small or angulated; prolonged inflations otherwise surgery; 3% of 1,762 (2006–2013): brilakis p.456
