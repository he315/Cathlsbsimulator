## Lesson hb-the-operator-entrustment

- Operator competence listed with impact of CAD, lesion complexity and comorbidities in the benefit–risk assessment; greater operator experience associated with higher success and fewer complications; experienced operators may be more adept at managing complications (hedged in the source, taught as 'may'); operator and staff condition (well rested, not sleep deprived) affects outcome: brilakis p.140 (7.1.8)
- Paying attention to the operators' and team's operational state helps identify conditions such as excessive fatigue: brilakis p.39 (2.9)
- EAPCI levels: I no procedural skill required, participation during training may be valuable; II some procedural skill, usually as assistant or second operator; III interprets data, recognises indications, performs the procedure and manages complications as first operator under direct supervision of a senior IC; IV independent first operator without direct supervision, post hoc review with senior colleagues possible; V as IV plus ability to teach and supervise: ref eapci-curriculum
- Levels adapted from the description of Entrustable Professional Activities: ref eapci-curriculum
- EPA eight sections (title; specification and limitations; risks in case of failure; most relevant competency domains; knowledge, skills, attitudes and experience; information sources to assess progress and support summative entrustment; entrustment or supervision level expected at which stage; time to expiration if not practised); EPAs first proposed in 2005: ref tencate-epa-guide-2020 (abstract)
- EPA examples ('radial diagnostic angiography', 'primary PCI of a culprit RCA') and "an activity you stop doing can lapse" as the practical reading of the expiry section: this module's reasoning
- Fellowship: supervised procedures with increasing complexity, minimum 200 PCIs; discuss cases with supervisor; primary PCI on-call rota; expertise in intravascular imaging and invasive physiology: oxford p.49
- No minimum or optimal numbers of procedures, because numbers are not sufficient per se to describe proficiency; focus on competencies: ref eapci-curriculum
- Expertise in both radial and femoral access essential for the contemporary interventional cardiologist; radial guide support may be suboptimal: brilakis p.61 (ch 4 introduction)
- Radial access technically more challenging for operators not specifically radially trained; steep learning curve; increased procedural duration and failure rates when crossing over from femoral: oxford p.69
- Apprenticeship pre-procedural items (admission, consent, pre-PCI non-invasive tests, pre-PCI drug therapy, heart team) and post-procedural items (report, monitoring, discussing drugs and follow-up with supervisor): oxford p.49
- Cardiologist duties across the pathway (taught in pm-monitoring): oxford pp.47–48
- Coronary air embolism in about 0.4% of coronary angiography procedures; shown to be training dependent: oxford p.327
- Inexperienced operators or outdated equipment may produce incomplete or misinterpreted studies: oxford p.180
- Assessment: documentation of attendance at accredited courses; procedures logbook; skill assessment by an independent trainer; skill assessment on a simulator; appraisal by training programme director; final examination: oxford p.50
- Regular review or appraisal of progress and formal assessment of knowledge and procedural competencies: ref eapci-curriculum
- Simulators have shown benefit in the progress of novice operators; centres encouraged to incorporate on-site simulators; exact role in most advanced skills still to be clarified: ref eapci-curriculum
- App behaviour: level II earned from passed practice cases, levels III and above only in exam mode on held-out cases (Competencies notice, src/ui/sections/Analytics.tsx line 62); PCI, haemodynamics, MCS, structural and bedside sessions are logged as practice and can reach level II (Assessment section text, src/ui/sections/Challenge.tsx line 179; Competencies notice in src/ui/sections/Analytics.tsx)
- "Entrustment is a standing judgement; whether to proceed today is a fresh one"; pearl on naming activities: this module's reasoning from brilakis p.140 and the EAPCI per-activity levels
- Check 1 (level III: first operator managing the dissection herself under direct supervision; level IV needs it unsupervised): ref eapci-curriculum. Check 2 (radial learning curve, both routes, radial support; recent concern about higher operator radiation dose): oxford p.69; brilakis p.61
- Tip pci-operator-know-your-level: brilakis p.140, p.39; "have a senior scrubbed" from the level III definition (ref eapci-curriculum)
- Tip pci-operator-help-early: level III vs level IV definitions (ref eapci-curriculum); experienced operators more adept with complications (brilakis p.140); cardiologist leads complication management (oxford p.48)
- Tip pci-operator-check-the-device: verification that equipment provided by the circulating or scrub nurse is as requested is a cardiologist's intra-procedural duty: oxford p.47; reading the label aloud: this module's reasoning

## Lesson hb-the-operator-pathway

- General cardiology training a fundamental prerequisite; standardised across Europe; 1–2 years general medicine; 3–4 years cardiology with invasive and non-invasive exposure: oxford p.48
- EAPCI core curriculum approved and followed by national societies: oxford pp.48–49
- At least two years of training in interventional cardiology in addition to four years devoted to cardiology; minimum dedicated subspecialty training two years full time; minimum four years full-time general cardiology: ref eapci-curriculum (also PubMed abstract PMID 32624457)
- Apprenticeship or fellowship ideally 2 years; minimum 200 PCIs; formal learning at least 30 full days (240 hours) over 2 years; involvement in research: oxford p.49
- Structured learning under supervision, a minimum of about two hours a day suggested; explicit learning (journal club, postgraduate teaching, EBM exercises) and implicit learning (ward rounds, case-based discussion, supervised skills); research participation recommended for trainees: ref eapci-curriculum
- Core curriculum learning objectives (indication and timing with cost-effective use of resources; communicating pros and cons of medical, percutaneous and surgical options to patients, family and MDT; skills to perform PCI as independent first operator; pre-, intra- and post-procedural management: access, adjunctive pharmacology, adjunctive techniques, complications, haemostasis, follow-up and secondary prevention): oxford p.49
- Oxford centre requirements: independent IC unit integrated with the cardiology department; more than 800 coronary interventions a year; electronic database regularly audited by the national interventional society; at least two supervisors with more than 1000 PCIs and over 5 years' experience: oxford p.50
- EAPCI centre requirements: cath lab with on-call 24-hour PCI; Heart Team meetings including clinical cardiologists; electronic database audited by the national society; periodic, at least annual, performance report; quality assessment and improvement programme including radiation safety; standard centres: 24-hour PCI on call mandatory; IVUS and/or OCT available; FFR/iFR assessment mandatory; strongly recommended to be involved in complex PCI (left main, multivessel); LV assist devices (the page names IABP/Impella; taught generically as an intra-aortic balloon pump or a microaxial flow pump), a CTO programme and rotational atherectomy desirable but not mandatory; advanced centres: similar requirements to standard centres plus a structural heart programme including at least TAVI according to national regulation, transseptal procedures and a structured CTO programme; other structural procedures (stroke, carotid, peripheral, GUCH, septal ablation, left atrial appendage occlusion, paravalvular leak closure, etc.) recommended for advanced centres; institutional coronary and structural Heart Teams encouraged for standard centres even without TAVI; Heart Team mandatory: ref eapci-curriculum (EuroIntervention article page re-read on 2026-10-08 for round 1 at eurointervention.pcronline.com/doi/10.4244/EIJ-D-18-00448; the wording 'desirable but not mandatory' for LV assist devices confirmed; re-read again on the same EuroIntervention page for round 2, which confirmed the 'other structural procedures ... are recommended' sentence for advanced centres and the encouragement of Heart Teams in standard centres; both now taught in the 'Where you train' table)
- No specific volume recommendation; follow national minimums; public reporting of training programmes, volumes and outcomes advisable: ref eapci-curriculum
- At least two trainers with more than five years' experience; trainees per institution should not exceed faculty or trainers; ideally full-time trainers match or exceed trainees: ref eapci-curriculum
- Active clinical research programme or trial involvement mandatory for centres: ref eapci-curriculum
- Logbook is one of the main documents to present for final accreditation or reaccreditation (reason given in logbook step 1): oxford p.50
- Logbook template CARDS, recommended by the ESC; fields: date, diagnostic versus intervention, first versus second operator, vascular access, clinical presentation (elective stable CAD, urgent ACS, emergent primary PCI or shock), complexity (bifurcation, LMS, multivessel, calcified, CTO, SVG), special techniques (physiology, imaging, rotational atherectomy, laser, thrombus aspiration, distal protection filters): oxford p.50; complications (perforation, dissection, kit loss); monthly review and sign-off for trainees; annual appraisal and revalidation for non-trainees: oxford p.51
- Quarterly sorting for gaps; "next year's training plan from the empty columns": this module's reasoning
- Certification launched by EAPCI in 2018; Part A written MCQ test on the core curriculum; Part B practical, under development when the book was written; access requires EAPCI membership and national board certification or fellowship confirmed by the training director; certification granted on passing Part A; validity 5 years then recertification: oxford p.51
- Two-part certification, full certification requires both parts; Part A MCQ (twice a year): ref eapci-curriculum
- Oxford final examination: theoretical section of 100 MCQs in 120 minutes; case studies of 50 questions on 10–20 cases: oxford p.50
- Cardiologist 'can also have' educational or teaching duties towards medical students, trainees and CathLab team (taught as 'may'); may be involved in research according to the individual job description: oxford p.47
- Lifelong learning; decisions on best available evidence; four principles (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice): ref eapci-curriculum
- Advice to check current certification rules (they 'may have changed') and the job description: this module's reasoning
- Check 1: oxford pp.49–50; ref eapci-curriculum. Check 2: oxford pp.50–51
- Tip pci-operator-log-the-case: oxford pp.50–51

## Lesson hb-the-operator-teaching

- Techniques largely learnt by apprenticeship, guided by the skills and beliefs of the trainer; importance of an independent reference: oxford p.23
- Reading the manual will not make you an expert; expertise requires deliberate practice (working to improve areas of deficiency with a teacher), not naive practice: brilakis p.26 (Introduction)
- Manual aims to help operators develop rich, accurate mental representations; these are key to expert performance: brilakis p.26
- Template: goal, how, challenges, what can go wrong; for each challenge and complication causes, prevention, treatment: brilakis p.26
- "Always improving" as the ultimate goal; algorithms are the authors' and not necessarily the best; feedback invited; vision of freely available, continually improved algorithms: brilakis p.26
- After catheterisation review all data; review sessions are educational and training opportunities to which all team members should be invited (paraphrased); each stored loop examined, complications such as dissection or embolism excluded; stenosis quantification reassessed; haemodynamic data examined; blood results (renal function, cholesterol, glucose); after complex PCI, ECG and troponin: oxford p.339
- Naming a target, saying the expectation aloud, asking for comment during the step: this module's reasoning applying brilakis p.26
- Roles: director of training (national or regional), cath lab director or supervisor, training (educational) mentor, clinical trainers (clinical mentors); training mentor organises the programme (rotations, courses, structured learning); cath lab director responsible for the unit and for trainees meeting objectives; trainees and training mentors subject to periodic assessment: ref eapci-curriculum
- Level V includes ability to teach and supervise: ref eapci-curriculum
- Cardiologist teaching duties ('can also have', so the roles table says 'may also have'): oxford p.47
- Radiographer teaches other professionals radiation protection, trains new and junior radiographers, audits: oxford p.63
- Structured learning about two hours a day; trainee:trainer ratio: ref eapci-curriculum
- Nurse training: departmental programme; supernumerary period usually 10 weeks depending on learning needs; workbooks; observed per procedure with an assessment grid; 100% before sign-off; educational events: oxford p.54
- Teaching steps (agree level, agree target, let trainee lead first moves of a bail-out under supervision): this module's reasoning from the level II and III definitions (ref eapci-curriculum)
- Debrief the team at the end of the day, especially after complications or learning points: oxford p.48
- Proctoring = cooperation and supervision by a more experienced operator (the earlier added clause 'usually when a technique is new' was removed in round 1 as unsupported), associated with improved CTO success; remote proctoring with HoloLens 2 head-mounted display, web-based audio and video, feasible in 10 CTO procedures; outcome impact to be assessed: ref calic-remote-proctoring-2024 (abstract)

- Mentor definition (guides development over months or years): general definition, consistent with the training mentor role in ref eapci-curriculum

## Lesson hb-the-operator-programme

- CTO definition: complete occlusion, TIMI 0 flow, estimated duration at least 3 months: brilakis p.389
- Crossing strategy selection depends on lesion characteristics and local equipment availability and expertise: brilakis p.394 (21.8.4); also p.394 (21.8.5)
- Advanced centres must have a structured CTO programme: ref eapci-curriculum
- Key indication symptom improvement; ad hoc CTO PCI discouraged in most cases (time for planning, preparation, counselling); preplanning minimises contrast and radiation, reduces fatigue, allows viability testing: brilakis p.389 (21.1)
- Exceptions: ACS from failure of a highly diseased SVG (treat native CTO if feasible); very challenging arterial access: brilakis p.389
- CCTA 'can be useful' to evaluate the CTO and plan, and to identify projections with least foreshortening (taught as helpful, not a requirement): brilakis p.389
- Monitoring: CTO PCI long, may need high radiation and contrast doses; ECG, haemodynamics, radiation and contrast watched throughout; operators ready for complications such as perforation: brilakis p.389 (21.2)
- Collateral evaluation critical for retrograde feasibility; high-quality angiography, ideally low magnification, breath hold, no panning, complete collateral opacification, in optimal projections, as part of routine diagnostic studies when a CTO is found (paraphrased in round 1): brilakis p.391 (21.6.4)
- Dual injection: needed in most CTO PCI; simplest and most effective technique to raise success, should be done in all patients with contralateral collaterals; improves safety by showing guidewire location and facilitating management of complications such as perforation: brilakis p.390 (21.6)
- Four angiographic characteristics (proximal cap; length, course and composition; distal vessel; collaterals): brilakis p.390 (21.6)
- Routine determination of case appropriateness: ref karmpaliotis-cto-programme-2013
- Dual (sometimes triple) access; femoral and wrist; long radial sheaths; 45 cm femoral sheaths: brilakis p.390 (21.4)
- 7 or 8 French guides with supportive shapes (AL for RCA, EBU or XB for left main); 6 French limits IVUS-guided cap penetration and complication management: brilakis p.390 (21.5)
- IVUS-guided re-entry requires 8 French guides and expertise in IVUS interpretation, and may be hindered by limited wire manoeuvrability with the subintimal IVUS catheter: brilakis p.393 (21.8.1)
- Dedicated re-entry systems: brilakis p.393 (21.8.2); guide catheter extensions facilitate reverse CART: brilakis p.394; multiple long stents: brilakis p.395 (21.10)
- Haemodynamic support used in about 4% of CTO PCI in the US: brilakis p.395 (21.14)
- Facility lacking necessary interventional equipment listed as a scenario favouring delayed PCI: brilakis p.146 (7.3.1)
- UFH preferred because reversible; ACT (Hemochron) > 300 s antegrade (some use > 250 s), > 350 s retrograde (some use > 300 s, check very frequently if low 300s); ACT every 20–30 min once therapeutic; bivalirudin best avoided (not reversible; unpublished guide thrombosis in long procedures), taught as 'best avoided', not forbidden; no GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors or cangrelor even after successful crossing and stenting, because minor wire perforations could reopen and cause delayed effusion and tamponade: brilakis p.390 (21.3)
- Donor artery lesions in the path of a retrograde route treated to avoid donor thrombosis: brilakis p.395 (21.10)
- Intravascular imaging critical for stent optimisation: brilakis p.395 (21.10, 21.13.2)
- Operators ready to manage complications such as perforation: brilakis p.389 (21.2)
- Reasons to stop (taught as 'reasons to stop (stopping triggers)', not a fixed rule): complication; high radiation dose (usually > 5 Gy air kerma without crossing or substantial progress); contrast > 3.7 × estimated creatinine clearance; exhaustion of options; patient or physician fatigue; individual risk versus benefit at each stage; on many occasions may be best to perform STAR or fail rather than pursue highly aggressive strategies (paraphrased in round 1; round 2 removed 'controlled' and 'prefers': STAR used uncontrollable re-entry (p.393, 21.8.2) and the source hedges with 'on many occasions', so step 6 and the c-adhoc and s4 feedback now say failure or a STAR bail-out is often better): brilakis p.394 (21.8.5)
- Contrast: stop before 3.7 × GFR generally; lower threshold preferable in CKD or single kidney; recent contrast taken into consideration: brilakis p.39 (2.6)
- STAR evolved into a bailout without stenting: brilakis p.393
- Change of strategy including antegrade to retrograde conversion; retrograde approach critical for high success rates especially in complex CTOs; antegrade generally preferred initially because retrograde has higher complication risk, longer procedure time and more radiation and needs antegrade lesion preparation anyway; some retrograde complications caused by antegrade attempts; proximal cap ambiguity and flush aorto-ostial CTOs often approached primary retrograde: brilakis p.394 (21.8.4, 21.8.5)
- Programme audit items (procedural outcomes, in-hospital events, cost): ref karmpaliotis-cto-programme-2013; contrast, fluoroscopy, bleeding: ref young-cto-learning-curve-2019; "compare with three benchmarks (learning curves, the proctored model programme, experienced centres)" and the "first page" pearl: this module's reasoning from those refs and brilakis p.146
- CTO PCI success 85–90% at experienced centres: brilakis p.146 (7.4)
- Karmpaliotis: 160 consecutive procedures in 145 patients, October 2009 to December 2010; success 85.6%; in-hospital death 0.6%, emergency CABG 0.6%, tamponade 0.6%, MI 1.9%; higher procedural and total cost, similar contribution margins; outcomes comparable with established centres: ref karmpaliotis-cto-programme-2013
- Young: CathPCI Registry; new operators < 10 CTO PCI per year; 70,916 cases, 7,251 new operators; success 61.4%; MACE 4.2%; improvements in crossing, stenting and success with accrued volume, with more contrast, fluoroscopy and bleeding; MACE stable beyond the 12th case: ref young-cto-learning-curve-2019
- Zein: 7,389 CTO PCIs; success 53%; 45% and 65% in lowest and highest operator-experience tertiles; experience related to success, not MACE; inexperienced operators at high-experience centres had higher success; authors suggested selective referral of CTO PCI to experienced operators at highly experienced centres: ref zein-cto-experience-2020. 'Work with an experienced centre and refer the most complex cases' is labelled in the table and case as this module's way of reconciling that with a new programme
- Experienced-centre figure (case step s6 and the lesson's second check): now quoted as 85–90%, brilakis p.146 (7.4). Teaching: experienced-centre results are not the right benchmark on their own; 62% after 15 cases sits within new-operator registry data (Young 61.4%) but below the proctored model programme (Karmpaliotis 85.6%), so the audit should prompt review of case selection and technique with the proctor: refs young-cto-learning-curve-2019, karmpaliotis-cto-programme-2013; the conclusion is this module's reasoning
- Check 1: brilakis pp.389, 390, 394, 146; ref karmpaliotis-cto-programme-2013. Check 2: refs young-cto-learning-curve-2019, zein-cto-experience-2020
- Tip pci-operator-cto-kit-before-start: brilakis pp.390, 393, 146
- Tip pci-operator-cto-second-operator: refs karmpaliotis-cto-programme-2013 (proctorship and two operators), zein-cto-experience-2020 (higher success of inexperienced operators at high-experience centres; authors' referral suggestion); greater operator experience associated with higher success and lower complications: brilakis p.140 (7.1.8), now the tip's `sources` entry (B('21') removed)

## Lesson hb-the-operator-evidence

- Active clinical research or trial involvement mandatory for centres; trainee participation recommended; decisions on best available evidence; journal club and EBM exercises: ref eapci-curriculum
- Algorithms are the authors', not necessarily the best; feedback invited; vision of freely available, continually improved algorithms: brilakis p.26
- Radial change driven by randomised trial and registry data showing reduced bleeding and mortality; mortality improvement thought, not proven, to derive from fewer bleeds; uptake highly variable (operator, institutional, procedural factors): oxford p.69
- DES (PES and SES) reduce TLR (< 10%) and MACE in highly selected trial populations; similar but less striking reductions in real-world complex lesions in registries and smaller RCTs (paraphrased in round 1; the selected-population point is attributed to the DES trials only): oxford p.224
- Radial: further benefits (quicker mobilisation, comfort, reduced costs); drawbacks include occlusion (relevant in future dialysis or full arterial grafting); recent concern about higher operator radiation dose: oxford p.69
- Angiographic restenosis much commoner than clinical; clinical restenosis in about 10% (given in the epidemiology paragraph on the balloon and BMS eras, so qualified in step 3 and q10); angiographic restenosis a controversial surrogate; restenosis without symptoms at planned follow-up angiography results in inflated repeat revascularisation: oxford p.221
- Restenosis 30–60% at 6 months in the balloon era; BMS generations reduced it from about 25% to 10–15% (quiz q10): oxford p.221
- Late lumen loss defined (renarrowing in mm between post-procedure and follow-up angiograms); predicted angiographic and clinical restenosis in DES versus BMS; whether SES superiority in LLL over PES translates into clinical differences remains to be seen: oxford p.224
- Stent thrombosis frequency difficult to assess due to inconsistent definitions and limited follow-up in randomised trials; ARC universal definitions after a 2006 Dublin meeting: oxford p.227 (Oxford writes 'Academic Research Council'); meetings in Washington and Dublin in 2006, published 2007: ref cutlip-arc-2007 (PMID 17470709, abstract re-read); updated as ARC-2 in 2018, revisiting the 2007 definitions for more complex lesions and populations and novel devices, strongly advocated for clinical trial purposes: ref garcia-garcia-arc2-2018 (PMID 29891620, abstract read)
- Inter-observer variability about ±20% in lesions of moderate severity: oxford p.175
- Concern over very late DES thrombosis, sometimes despite ongoing DAPT; ongoing large-scale studies with prolonged follow-up: oxford p.227; relatively short-term efficacy and safety data among considerations with DES: oxford p.224; "events that appear late are missed by short trials": this module's reasoning
- Update on DES (step 5 and DES table row): Oxford's concerns concern first-generation PES and SES (oxford pp.223–224, 227); newer-generation DES used in the vast majority of patients because of less restenosis and less stent thrombosis than BMS, except SVG where outcomes are similar: brilakis p.185 (10.2.3.2.1); network meta-analysis of 49 RCTs, 50,844 patients: definite stent thrombosis lower with CoCr-EES than BMS at 1 year (OR 0.23) and 2 years (OR 0.35): ref palmerini-des-thrombosis-2012 (PMID 22445239, new, PubMed-checked); 2018 ESC/EACTS: DES recommended over BMS for any PCI: ref esc-revasc-2018 (existing verified ref; recommendation as recorded in docs/curriculum/evidence/cp-prior-cabg.md, p.136, and taught in src/content/tracks/pci-core.ts)
- CrossBoss First: 246 patients, 11 US centres, catheter first versus wire escalation; crossing time 56 vs 66 min (p = 0.323); similar success and MACE; no differences in secondary end points, equipment use or cost: ref karacsonyi-crossboss-first-2018 (existing ref; PubMed abstract read again, PMID 29413236). Interpretation (round 1): a non-significant superiority result shows no demonstrated difference, not equivalence; sample size and confidence intervals decide what difference could have been missed: general appraisal principle, this module's reasoning
- CONSORT 2025: 30-item checklist and flow diagram; new open-science section; earlier versions 1996, 2001, 2010: ref hopewell-consort-2025
- DES history: elimination of recoil and negative remodelling by stents left neointimal proliferation as focus; similarity to malignant proliferation led to anticancer and immunomodulatory agents; drug combined with polymer coating; PES and SES reduced MACE and TLR: oxford p.223
- BRS: Absorb first largely tested after nearly 30 years of uncertain research; a number of devices approved in Europe and the USA, yet in the light of randomised data the 2018 European guidelines do not recommend BRS outside clinical studies; research continues on first-generation limitations: oxford p.225 (objective 5 now says 'restriction to clinical studies', not withdrawal, which Oxford does not mention)
- STAR: uncontrolled re-entry, long stented segments, side branch occlusion, extensive injury, high ISR and reocclusion; evolved into bailout without stenting before repeat attempt after 2–3 months; limited dissection/re-entry techniques minimise injury, limit dissection and stent length, increase side branch preservation, and are associated with favourable clinical outcomes: brilakis p.393
- Donor-lesion significance may change after CTO recanalisation, FFR increasing by 0.03 to 0.10 (two studies); take into account: brilakis p.395 (21.12.2). The check now says the FFR 'may' rise; the earlier sentence about populations and methods was removed as unsupported
- Research involvement in formal learning: oxford p.49
- Appraisal questions and the four-question pearl (labelled in the text as this module's rule of thumb, softened to 'weigh with caution, ideally within a study'): this module's reasoning from the facts above
- Check 1: oxford pp.221, 224. Check 2: brilakis p.395
- Tip diag-operator-moderate-lesion-measure and evidence drill: ±20% variability (oxford p.175); oval lesion severe in one view and normal in another (oxford pp.175, 179); at least two projections, preferably orthogonal; may underestimate severity by up to 50% in extreme cases; spatial resolution about 0.2 mm; appearance may not correlate with physiological effect (oxford p.175); QCA available in most labs (oxford p.177); pressure wire or intravascular imaging when angiography leaves uncertainty (oxford p.177)
