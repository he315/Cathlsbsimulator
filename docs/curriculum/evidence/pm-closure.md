## Lesson pm-closure-plan (Planning femoral closure: the algorithm and the femoral angiogram)

- Closure devices favoured for shorter time to ambulation and potentially fewer complications; the latter controversial: brilakis p.195 (11.1.1); devices improve comfort, speed ambulation, may reduce complications but may cause complications: brilakis p.195 (11.1.3); "may reduce ... although the latter is debated": brilakis p.585 (30.15.1).
- Oxford: devices reduce need for (often painful) digital compression; earlier mobilisation and discharge; meta-analysis concern about first-generation devices, haematoma and possibly pseudoaneurysm: oxford p.89. Further reading Koreny JAMA 2004: oxford p.90.
- Koreny meta-analysis: 30 randomised trials, many of poor methodological quality; across all trials no significant difference in groin haematoma (RR 1.14, 0.86–1.51) or pseudoaneurysm (RR 1.19, 0.75–1.88); time to haemostasis 17 min shorter (range 14–19); only in trials with explicit intention-to-treat analysis haematoma RR 1.89, pseudoaneurysm RR 5.40: ref koreny-closure-meta-2004 (PubMed abstract, PMID 14734598).
- Cochrane 2016: 52 studies, 19,192 participants; clip-based MD −14.81 min, suture-based MD −14.58 min time to haemostasis vs extrinsic compression; collagen-based lower haematoma (OR 0.46) and pseudoaneurysm (OR 0.74); no difference in vascular injury requiring repair or mortality; infection no difference for collagen and suture devices: ref robertson-closure-cochrane-2016 (PMID 26948236). Cited by Brilakis p.195 [1] and p.585 [66].
- ISAR-CLOSURE: 4524 patients, 6 Fr diagnostic angiography; primary endpoint 6.9% vs 7.9%, non-inferior; median time to haemostasis 1 min vs 10 min; device failure intravascular 5.3% vs extravascular 12.2%: ref schulz-schupke-isar-closure-2014 (PMID 25399273).
- UNIVERSAL: prespecified subgroup analysis; with a closure device, US-guided access 20/170 (11.8%) vs 37/158 (23.4%), OR 0.44; without a device 14.2% vs 8.6% (no benefit); interaction p = 0.004: ref dentremont-universal-closure-2023 (PMID 36876864, abstract checked). Overall trial neutral for the composite: taught in pm-access (ref jolly-universal-2022).
- Active infection or immunocompromised: may be best to avoid closure devices: brilakis p.35 (1.11). Skin infection at access site: brilakis p.505 (29.1.5.3).
- Angioseal within 90 days: consider contralateral femoral or radial access: brilakis p.33 (1.4).
- Prior access site complication: ask what it was and how it was managed; avoid using the same access site: brilakis p.33 (1.4).
- Angioseal components (anchor, suture, collagen) absorbed within 60–90 days: brilakis p.585 (30.15.1.1). Oxford collagen absorbed after approximately 3 months: oxford p.89; plug reabsorbed within 3 months: oxford p.90 (Fig 3.11).
- Puncture above bifurcation, away from calcification, disease, bypass grafts and previously placed closure devices: brilakis p.67 (4.2.4).
- Algorithm (Fig 11.1): can the sheath be removed (staged PCI / support device / bleeding around sheath) → able to use closure device (femoral artery >5 mm, significant disease, access at bifurcation, femoral calcification for Perclose) → high stick (above inguinal ligament) → Perclose; not high → Angioseal or Perclose; no device → ACT <180 → remove + manual pressure; ACT not <180 → suture sheath, reassess: brilakis p.195 (Fig 11.1).
- Sheath may need to stay for staged PCI within a few hours or likely support device in next few hours; remove immediately if continued bleeding around sheath; alternative larger sheath if arteriotomy larger than sheath; secured with sutures; long sheaths replaced with short (10–13 cm): brilakis p.195 (11.1.2).
- Device requirements: large femoral artery (>5 mm) without significant disease; access above CFA bifurcation; femoral angiography critical: brilakis p.195 (11.1.3).
- Avoid Perclose in heavily calcified arteries; avoid Angioseal in high puncture: brilakis p.196 (11.1.3).
- High stick = above inguinal ligament; Angioseal avoided because of difficulty advancing collagen plug to artery; Perclose preferred: brilakis p.196 (11.1.4).
- Algorithm reflects authors' practice; other devices can be incorporated: brilakis p.195 (11.1.1).
- Devices can be used with high ACT; may be best not to use if ACT >300 s: brilakis p.196 (11.1.5). Protamine for earlier sheath removal: brilakis p.196 (11.1.5).
- Protamine for earlier sheath removal only once all coronary equipment is out; reversal carries a risk of stent thrombosis if stents were implanted: brilakis p.49 (3.4.4.1, bleeding treatment). Used in the algorithm step 4, the femoral-compression table, protamine paragraph and keypoints.
- Collagen plug (or any other arterial closure device) to be avoided after a high stick (inconsistency with Fig 11.1/11.1.4, which prefers the suture device): brilakis p.207 (11.1.7.8.2.1 prevention). The lesson states this as Brilakis's advice ('on that advice no device would be used'); neither manual says what share of operators follow it, so no claim about operator practice is made (round 2).
- Strict asepsis, datasheets and indications: oxford p.89.
- Microaxial pump sheath sizes 13 Fr / 14 Fr (check-block explanation; the 4 Fr distractor is now explained only as unable to take the pump without upsizing): brilakis p.515 (30.1.1.1).
- Femoral angiogram technique (wire in aorta, undamped waveform, ipsilateral oblique 20–30°, low magnification, 5–10 mL): brilakis p.79–80 (4.2.15); its goal includes determining feasibility of a closure device: brilakis p.79 (4.2.15.1).
- Femoral angiogram interpretation: perforation; dissection or intraluminal filling defects; antegrade flow to the leg; disease, calcification, tortuosity; puncture location and artery size and quality; goal: rule out complications, assess their risk, decide closure-device feasibility (table row 'Perforation, dissection ... loss of antegrade flow'): brilakis p.79–80 (4.2.15.1–4.2.15.2).
- Oxford: femoral angiogram before removing the sheath confirms site, calibre and disease; puncture below bifurcation or small calibre → manual or mechanical compression: oxford p.317.
- Accessory profunda example, no device, manual compression: brilakis p.80 (Fig 4.29).
- Previously placed Angioseal as a filling defect: brilakis p.80 (Fig 4.28).
- Plug-based device can create a new stenosis with pre-existing atherosclerosis or small femoral arteries: oxford p.317.
- High/noncompressible puncture defined as above inferior border of inferior epigastric artery; Angioseal avoided because plug may become entangled in abdominal wall muscle layers: brilakis p.499 (29.1.2.2).
- Higher puncture (above femoral head and inferior epigastric origin) → retroperitoneal bleeding; over femoral head allows compression against bone: brilakis p.64 (4.2.3).
- Ellis 2006: 28,378 consecutive PCI patients (1992–2003), RPH 0.57%; associated with sheath above inferior epigastric artery, female sex, Angioseal, GP IIb/IIIa, AMI presentation; inversely related to weight; 10.4% died in hospital: ref ellis-retroperitoneal-2006 (PMID 16547938). Cited by Brilakis p.196 [2].
- Infection: surgery for closure-device infection, abscess, prior synthetic graft; prevention: avoid devices in high-risk patients, repeat antiseptic skin preparation before deployment especially after long procedures, sheath removal as soon as possible; haematoma as culture medium: brilakis p.505 (29.1.5). Sterile dressing immediately after deployment: brilakis p.207 (11.1.7.8.2.2).
- Infection diagnosis (pain, erythema, discharge; fever, leukocytosis; blood cultures; duplex to exclude pseudoaneurysm), causes (groin haematoma or pseudoaneurysm, overnight sheath, immunocompromise e.g. transplant, poor hygiene, closure devices), prevention adds prophylactic antibiotics in high-risk patients, treatment IV antibiotics; surgery for device infection, abscess, prior synthetic graft or persistent bacteraemia despite antibiotics: brilakis p.505 (29.1.5.1–29.1.5.4).
- Closure device does not exclude retroperitoneal haematoma: brilakis p.499 (29.1.2.1).
- Tip access-planning-closure-infection belongs to pm-planning (brilakis 1.11).

## Lesson pm-closure-femoral-compression (Removing a femoral sheath and holding pressure)

- Brilakis: delay removal and manual pressure until ACT <180 s; protamine can allow earlier removal: brilakis p.196 (11.1.5).
- Oxford: delay until ACT <150–200 s or 3–4 hours (local protocol); firm pressure with two or more fingers 1 cm above the vessel puncture before removal; sheath removed briskly; pressure continually ~8–10 minutes; 3 hours bed rest; alternatives: closure devices, external compression devices, procoagulant patches; hypertensive patients may need antihypertensive e.g. nifedipine 5–10 mg sublingually: oxford p.76.
- Brilakis hypertension: medications to lower blood pressure (bleeding prevention): brilakis p.197 (11.1.6.4.1).
- Oxford hypertension example (sublingual nifedipine 5–10 mg): oxford p.76. Taught as no longer advised: sublingual nifedipine capsules linked to severe hypotension, cerebrovascular ischaemia, stroke, acute MI, death; poor sublingual absorption; use should be abandoned: ref grossman-nifedipine-1996 (PMID 8861992). 'Titratable agent according to local protocol' is the module's own safety wording, not a source claim of a specific drug.
- Manual compression steps 1–10 (telemetry and NIBP; personnel for atropine or fluids; sterile gloves; hands above puncture; removal with gentle pressure and small back bleed; firm pressure confirming haemostasis; 2–3 min × sheath size, 12–18 min for 6 Fr, 16–24 min for 8 Fr; check haematoma; distal pulses; clear sterile dressing): brilakis p.196 (11.1.6.2).
- Challenge: morbid obesity: brilakis p.196 (11.1.6.3).
- Bleeding causes (hand placement too low or to the side, inadequate pressure, anticoagulation, hypertension, obesity, haematoma formation), prevention (hand position, firm pressure, ACT check, BP lowering), treatment (reposition, firm pressure, endovascular or surgery): brilakis p.196–197 (11.1.6.4.1).
- Lower extremity ischaemia causes (dissection, thrombosis, embolisation), prevention (meticulous access), treatment (emergency angiography then endovascular or surgical): brilakis p.197 (11.1.6.4.2).
- Further limb-ischaemia causes added to the table: occlusive sheath (diseased or small artery, large sheath), large sheath size, prolonged sheath and catheter dwell, poor sheath management (aspirate and discard before flushing), suboptimal anticoagulation: brilakis p.497 (29.1.1.2).
- Hypotension causes (bleeding; tamponade, ischaemia, arrhythmias, valvular regurgitation, LV/RV failure; vasovagal or anaphylactic), prevention (access technique, normal saline before sheath removal, local anaesthetic before sheath insertion), treatment (saline, transfusion, endovascular/surgical depending on type; pericardiocentesis, vasopressors, inotropes, support devices; saline and atropine for vasovagal bradycardia): brilakis p.197 (11.1.6.4.3).
- Protamine 1–1.5 mg per 100 units heparin, not to exceed 50 mg; slow administration; hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis; risk factors NPH or protamine zinc insulin, fish hypersensitivity, vasectomy, severe LV dysfunction, abnormal pulmonary haemodynamics; severe systemic hypotension and relative pulmonary hypertension usually 5–10 min after: brilakis p.48 (3.4.2.1.3).
- Methylene blue may help recovery from a protamine reaction: brilakis p.48 (3.4.2.1.3).
- Arterial sheath removed first, venous a few minutes later, instead of simultaneous removal (AV fistula prevention): brilakis p.505 (29.1.4.3).
- Oxford vasovagal: 2–3%; at arterial puncture or during compression haemostasis; unwell, nauseated, sweaty; heart rate usually slows before BP drops; tamponade similar but usually with tachycardia; immediate echo if uncertain; management: lie down, legs elevated, monitor, atropine 0.5–1.0 mg IV, rapid 0.9% saline; if no improvement in a few minutes reconsider (MI, tamponade, retroperitoneal bleed): oxford p.308. Vasovagal reactions at puncture or on sheath removal: oxford p.77.
- Retroperitoneal haematoma: hypotension, diaphoresis, lower abdominal or back pain; patients often develop bradycardia; usually within the first 3 hours; closure devices do not exclude it: brilakis p.499 (29.1.2.1). Suspect in every patient with hypotension after femoral access: brilakis p.501 (29.1.2.4 point 1).
- Retroperitoneal haematoma additions: tenderness on abdominal or suprapubic palpation; high suspicion in obese patients, uncontrolled hypertension before or during the procedure, high puncture, puncture not guided by ultrasound or fluoroscopy, multiple puncture attempts; dented bladder sign on fluoroscopy after contrast: brilakis p.499 (29.1.2.1).
- Tip access-closure-hypotension-retroperitoneal: suspect in every hypotensive femoral patient (brilakis p.501, 29.1.2.4 point 1); signs and closure devices not excluding it (p.499); general measures large IV access, saline, type and cross, manual pressure over the access site; in the lab bladder fluoroscopy and contralateral femoral angiography (p.500–501, Fig 29.4 and points 2–3).
- Mechanical compression: obesity hard (brilakis p.196); Oxford femoral compression devices: external frame applying pressure at or just above puncture; used if bleeding difficult to control manually, especially hypertensive and obese; belt-under-buttocks system with sterile inflatable dome, inflated above systolic initially then gradually reduced; bed-mounted clamp providing direct downward compression; both device types rely on a bony landmark: oxford p.87 (and Fig 3.9 caption).
- Procoagulant patches: aids to digital compression rather than closure devices; reduce time to haemostasis: oxford p.89.
- Suboptimal compression → pseudoaneurysm: brilakis p.503 (29.1.3.2). Prolonged or overzealous compression → limb ischaemia and possible DVT: brilakis p.497 (29.1.1.2).
- Groin haematoma treatment: manual compression proximal to skin entry, analgesics, compressive bandage, bed rest 6–8 hours, duplex ultrasound: brilakis p.500 (29.1.2.4).
- Oxford ch11: 6 F femoral puncture, lie flat 1 hour after line removal, mobilise 2½ hours after removal; ambulation can be short (1 hour) with radial access, closure devices and smaller femoral sheaths; varies with local practice: oxford p.338.
- IABP removal: balloon does not usually retract through sheath; withdraw until balloon reaches sheath, resistance, remove sheath and catheter as a unit; pressure haemostasis; IABP 7.5 F or larger, prolonged compression: oxford p.291.
- Oxford local haematoma up to 25% of procedures; vasovagal reactions at puncture or sheath removal; tear or dissection; retroperitoneal haemorrhage after high puncture above the inguinal ligament; pseudoaneurysm; distal embolisation of thrombus from sheath: oxford p.77.

## Lesson pm-closure-plug-device (Collagen-plug closure step by step)

- Mechanism: arteriotomy sandwiched between polymer anchor inside artery and collagen sponge outside, connected by suture: brilakis p.585 (30.15.1.1). Oxford: collagen plug tamped down; absorbed after ~3 months; generally the most straightforward: oxford p.89–90.
- Components: guidewire; insertion sheath; dilator/arteriotomy locator with side hole aligning with sheath hole 1.5 cm from tip; device with anchor, collagen sponge, suture: brilakis p.585; device parts (tamper tube, device sleeve, cap, reference indicator): brilakis p.586 (Fig 30.106).
- Sizes: 6 Fr device also for 7 Fr; 6 Fr compatible with 0.035 not 0.038 inch; 8 Fr compatible with 0.038 inch; packaged with wires: brilakis p.586. 6 Fr for 6 or 7 Fr sheaths, 8 Fr for 8 Fr; 8 Fr used for 9 Fr with good results [3] (ref [3] = Janssen 2019, 9 F punctures in acute stroke patients after IV rt-PA, brilakis p.215 reference list; taught as a single report outside standard sizing): brilakis p.203 (11.1.7.7.2.1). Supplied wire adequate for sheaths up to 25 cm; longer sheaths need longer wire; 0.038 inch usable with 8 Fr but not 6 Fr: brilakis p.199 (11.1.7.2).
- Step 1 preparation (dilator snaps into sheath, reference indicators aligned): brilakis p.197 (11.1.7.1).
- Step 2 0.035 wire, fluoroscopy confirming aorta, remove sheath: brilakis p.199.
- Step 3 advance until drip-hole flow, withdraw until it stops (inlet holes just exited artery), re-advance until flow begins: brilakis p.199 (11.1.7.3).
- Step 4 fix sheath with left hand, flex dilator tip upward, remove dilator and wire: brilakis p.200 (11.1.7.4).
- Step 5 hold device near tip, reference indicator up, insert through haemostatic valve, advance until snap: brilakis p.200 (11.1.7.5).
- Step 6 hold sheath cap firmly; pull back until resistance (anchor catches sheath tip); coloured band fully visible: brilakis p.200–202 (11.1.7.6).
- Step 7 pull back at approximately 45°; tamper tube appears; advance tamper while maintaining suture tension; do not advance tamper until resistance felt at back end; 8–10 seconds; black marker appears; check haemostasis: brilakis p.203 (11.1.7.7).
- Step 8 cut suture below clear stop; remove tamper; pull suture and cut below skin level below black (non-absorbable) compaction marker; push tissue down in obese patients: brilakis p.206 (11.1.7.8).
- Incomplete anchor deployment (incomplete withdrawal, anchor leg catches sheath; re-advance and pull back): brilakis p.203 (11.1.7.6.3).
- Anchor pulled through (poor selection e.g. 6 Fr for 8 Fr, forceful withdrawal; manual pressure; reverse anticoagulation and angiography if needed): brilakis p.203 (11.1.7.7.2.1).
- Angioseal deployed in artery: sheath too far, footplate caught on atherosclerosis in external iliac or proximal CFA, false feel; avoid in small/diseased arteries or visualise on ultrasound; tamping before apposition; arteriotomy in SFA or profunda with footplate entrapped at bifurcation; acute limb ischaemia, prompt treatment, often surgical removal, endovascular treatments described: brilakis p.204 (11.1.7.7.2.2).
- Retroperitoneal haematoma (diseased CFA, high stick, suboptimal technique; avoid devices in diseased vessels and high sticks; transfusion/fluids, reverse anticoagulation, surgical or endovascular; direct transfer to cath lab may be preferable to imaging that delays treatment): brilakis p.206–207 (11.1.7.8.2.1).
- Infection (systemic infection, immunocompromised, imperfect sterile technique; avoid; repeat groin preparation immediately before deployment; sterile dressing immediately after; antibiotics; debridement and reconstruction): brilakis p.207 (11.1.7.8.2.2).
- Oxford: plug-based devices can generate a new stenosis with pre-existing atherosclerosis or small femoral arteries; nursing checks; early vascular surgical involvement; Doppler ultrasound or CT: oxford p.317.
- Oxford lower-limb ischaemia typically associated with femoral closure devices, especially large sheaths; nursing checks: pale/cyanotic, cold, loss or weakness of pulses, pain, motor or sensory deficit: oxford p.317.
- Acute limb ischaemia treatment: contralateral femoral access and angiography, balloon angioplasty, endovascular thrombectomy or thrombolysis; surgical thrombectomy, endarterectomy or bypass in some cases: brilakis p.498 (29.1.1.4).
- Keypoint absorption: anchor, suture, collagen 60–90 days (brilakis p.585); black compaction marker not absorbable (brilakis p.206); collagen about 3 months (oxford p.89).
- Fig 29.1: acute limb ischaemia after Angioseal, CFA occlusion with monophasic SFA flow, emergency surgery and removal: brilakis p.498.

## Lesson pm-closure-suture-device (Suture-mediated closure step by step, and the other device classes)

- Single monofilament polypropylene suture; arterial 5–21 Fr, large-bore venous 5–24 Fr; three components (device, snared knot pusher, suture trimmer): brilakis p.586 (30.15.1.2).
- Large-bore arterial closure uses two devices placed before dilatation (pre-close at 10 and 2 o'clock): brilakis p.257 (14.4.2). Product names removed from the lesson text (generic class only).
- Other device classes table, suture row: knot formed by the device (oxford p.89 'tied by a clincher'); device removed first, then knot pushed down with the knot pusher and locked: brilakis p.211 (11.1.8.9–11.1.8.10).
- Step 1 flush marker lumen: brilakis p.207 (11.1.8.1).
- Step 2 0.035–0.038 inch wire, fluoroscopy confirms distal aorta, remove sheath: brilakis p.207 (11.1.8.2).
- Step 3 dilate tissue tract with haemostat: brilakis p.208 (11.1.8.3). Skin nick key for delivering closure device, especially Perclose with knot pusher: brilakis p.73 (4.2.9).
- Step 4 advance until wire exit port at skin; remove wire; advance until pulsatile flow from marker lumen: brilakis p.208 (11.1.8.4).
- Unable to advance (scar, poor track, wire kinking; meticulous preparation; stiff 0.035/0.038 wire such as Amplatz super-stiff; remove and re-prepare; exchange kinked wire for another, ideally stiffer): brilakis p.208.
- Step 5 hold at 45°, confirm pulsatile flow, lift lever to deploy foot: brilakis p.209 (11.1.8.5).
- Step 6 device between middle and index finger of right hand; pull back until resistance; flow stops or significantly decreases; left hand holds body, right pushes plunger; thumb as fulcrum pull plunger back; anterior suture connected to needle cut with cutter: brilakis p.209 (11.1.8.6).
- Suture cut or loose (device failure, forceful pulling, not 45°; careful selection, gentle handling, stabilise at 45°; reinsert wire through device, remove, deploy another): brilakis p.210 (11.1.8.6.2).
- Step 7 relax tension, push lever down to retract foot: brilakis p.210 (11.1.8.7).
- Step 8 retract until guidewire exit port visible above skin; wire can be reinserted to maintain access, important for large arteriotomies: brilakis p.211 (11.1.8.8).
- Unable to retract (malfunction, poor track, severe calcification; good track preparation; gentle rotation and withdrawal both directions; vascular surgery): brilakis p.211.
- Step 9 remove sutures; blue limb longer, advances knot; white tip locks; knot pusher over blue limb; wrap blue limb round left index finger; readvance wire through device if concern for failure; remove device with gentle back tension; push knot down; further pushing if bleeding; remove wire once haemostasis: brilakis p.211 (11.1.8.9).
- Step 10 inspect haemostasis; pull white-tipped limb to lock; remove knot pusher; trimmer over both sutures, cut, remove trimmer: brilakis p.211 (11.1.8.10).
- Oxford: suture-based devices can cause limb ischaemia from an overtightened knot, especially when multiple devices close larger holes; prevention by correct puncture and femoral angiogram before sheath removal: oxford p.317.
- Preclose with two Perclose at 10 and 2 o'clock, sutures wrapped in gauze or insulin syringes without tightening: brilakis p.257 (14.4.2). At Impella removal, sheath removed and Perclose sutures tightened; balloon from contralateral femoral into iliac to minimise bleeding: brilakis p.262 (14.4.12). Sutures partially tightened round repositioning sheath: brilakis p.262.
- A dedicated device 'available for large bore access closure' (named in the book; taught generically, mechanism not described in either manual): brilakis p.585 (30.15.1).
- Other devices: five named (Starclose, Mynx, Exoseal, Vascade, Celt) per local availability and expertise: brilakis p.196 (11.1.3). Taught as 'five other femoral closure devices', not named.
- Oxford: collagen plug; vascular suture devices with needles deployed by plunger and knot tied before sheath withdrawal; vascular clip devices (nitinol clip outside vessel encircling rim, apposition of native tissues promotes healing); procoagulant patches as aids: oxford p.89. Strict asepsis, datasheets and indications: oxford p.89.

## Lesson pm-closure-radial (Radial sheath removal and patent haemostasis)

- RAO most frequent post-procedural complication of radial access, restricting future use and use as CABG conduit: ref bernat-rao-consensus-2019 (PMID 31753298, abstract).
- Patent haemostasis aims to keep flow and minimise thrombus formation and occlusion: brilakis p.213 (11.2.5.2).
- Radial sheaths removed immediately even with high ACT, even with IV GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors or cangrelor; if not removed, flushed and connected to pressure sensor to prevent thrombosis and embolisation: brilakis p.212 (11.2.1). Oxford: radial sheaths almost always removed immediately; tourniquets historically, compression devices now: oxford p.71.
- Large sheaths relative to the artery cause spasm and RAO: brilakis p.507–508 (29.2.2.2, 29.2.3.2).
- Step 1 procedure completed, no further PCI or haemodynamic monitoring: brilakis p.212.
- Objective 6, keypoint 1, case learning point and tip access-radial-remove-now now carry the exception 'unless further PCI or haemodynamic monitoring through it is needed': brilakis p.212 (11.2.1); Oxford 'almost always removed immediately': oxford p.71.
- Radial bands: three named devices, many with an air chamber: brilakis p.587 (30.15.2). Oxford: work like a tourniquet against the radius; compact; allow early ambulation; splint-and-pad type and transparent band with inflatable bladder: oxford p.87–88.
- Step 2 vasodilator verapamil 2–3 mg and/or nitroglycerin 200–500 mcg through side arm, mixed 1:1 with arterial blood to minimise discomfort; goal: prevent/relieve spasm, facilitate removal: brilakis p.212 (11.2.2). Vasodilator before sheath removal may reduce RAO: brilakis p.92 (4.3.9).
- Step 3 band immediately above puncture site: brilakis p.212 (11.2.3). Arterial entry often more proximal than skin entry, especially with shallow angle; too distal → forearm bleeding; too proximal → wrist haematoma; band slightly proximal to puncture: brilakis p.506 (29.2.1.2–29.2.1.3).
- Step 4 inflate cuff with 10–15 cc as sheath removed; slowly release until bleeds, reinflate with 2–3 cc: brilakis p.213 (11.2.4.2).
- Step 5 oximeter on index finger; occlude ipsilateral ulnar; loosen until pleth returns or bleeding; manual compression if bleeding at patency pressure; band 1–2 hours; longer increases RAO; simultaneous ulnar compression further reduces RAO: brilakis p.213–214 (11.2.5.2).
- PROPHET: 436 patients; patent haemostasis (Barbeau test) vs conventional; 59% decrease at 24 h, 75% at 30 days; low body weight risk: ref pancholy-prophet-2008 (PMID 18726956).
- Pancholy 2012: 400 patients, retrospective; 6 h vs 2 h; early RAO 12% vs 5.5%; chronic 8.5% vs 3.5%; occlusive compression only independent predictor; maintaining patency eliminates adverse effect of duration: ref pancholy-compression-duration-2012 (PMID 21584923).
- PROPHET-II: 3000 patients; prophylactic ipsilateral ulnar compression; 30-day RAO 0.9% vs 3.0%: ref pancholy-prophet-ii-2016 (PMID 27712733).
- ULTRA: 119 vs 121 patients (240), consecutive months, not randomised; ulnar compression 1 hour; RAO 0 vs 5% within 1 hour after removal of closure device: ref koutouzis-ultra-2016 (PMID 27529655).
- MEMORY: 589 patients, 5 F diagnostic; manual vs mechanical patent haemostasis; early RAO 12% vs 8% (p=0.176); similar bleeding; haemostasis 22 vs 119 min: ref petroglou-memory-2018 (PMID 29880098). Brilakis: manual compression good results, mean 22 min in MEMORY; most centres use a radial compression device: brilakis p.211 (11.2). Fig 11.18 three fingers over puncture, patency checked every 1–2 min by transient ulnar compression and plethysmography: brilakis p.212.
- MEMORY paragraph rewording: manual 22 ± 34 min vs device 119 ± 72 min (ref petroglou-memory-2018 abstract); Brilakis' point that mean compression time can be long and that most centres use a device: brilakis p.211 (11.2).
- Inability to remove sheath (spasm): prevention vasodilator at beginning, avoid oversizing, minimise manipulations; treatment vasodilators (IV if unable to aspirate), subcutaneous nitroglycerin around artery, more sedation (propofol), warm blankets, BP cuff in the arm above systolic × 5 minutes, general anaesthesia or nerve block: brilakis p.213 (11.2.4.3.1). Excessive force can avulse the radial artery: brilakis p.509 (Fig 29.12).
- Spasm additions: warm blankets or gauze pads wet with warm sterile saline; lubricious solution; upper-arm cuff above systolic for 5 minutes (PDF p.213 reads '× 5 minutes'; p.509 'for 5 minutes'); causes: small artery or large sheath, excessive manipulations and multiple exchanges, women and current smokers, anxiety/inadequate sedation, no vasodilator after access, IV vasopressors; prevention: thin-walled smallest sheath, adequate moderate sedation, adequate local anaesthesia, long (24 cm) sheaths reaching the brachial artery: brilakis p.508–509 (29.2.3.2–29.2.3.4). Oxford: mild sedation (opiates, benzodiazepines) prevents; intra-arterial nitrates and verapamil treat: oxford p.73.
- Bleeding: suboptimal placement; careful placement and rapid adjustment; reposition device; manual pressure and arm elevation: brilakis p.213 (11.2.4.3.2). Second device, manual pressure with elevation, BP cuff above systolic: brilakis p.506 (29.2.1.4).

## Disagreements between the books (stated in the lessons)

- ACT threshold for femoral sheath removal: Brilakis <180 s (p.196); Oxford <150–200 s or 3–4 hours (p.76).
- Duration of femoral compression: Brilakis 2–3 min per French (12–18 min for 6 Fr, p.196); Oxford about 8–10 min (p.76).
- Bed rest after femoral compression: Oxford ch 3, 3 hours (p.76); Oxford ch 11, flat 1 hour, mobilise 2½ hours after removal of a 6 F line (p.338).
- Absorption of the collagen plug: Brilakis 60–90 days for anchor, suture and collagen (p.585), black compaction marker not absorbable (p.206); Oxford about 3 months for the collagen, 'leaving no foreign material behind' (p.89).
- Forearm cuff for a radial/forearm haematoma: Oxford up to 15 mmHg below systolic (p.319); Brilakis Fig 29.10 20 mmHg below systolic, deflated every 15 minutes (p.507).
- Suture-device knot: Oxford says the knot is tied by a clincher before sheath withdrawal (p.89); Brilakis removes the device and then pushes the knot down (p.211). The lesson follows Brilakis for the sequence.
- High stick: Brilakis Fig 11.1 and 11.1.4 prefer the suture device (p.195–196); 11.1.7.8.2.1 advises avoiding any closure device in high sticks (p.207). Both stated.
