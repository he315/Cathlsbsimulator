## New reference (module `refs` export)

- `gmc-nmc-candour-2015`: GMC/NMC joint guidance "Openness and honesty when things go wrong: The professional duty of candour". Checked on the PDF hosted by the NMC (nmc.org.uk/globalassets/sitedocuments/nmc-publications/openness-and-honesty-professional-duty-of-candour.pdf), pages 1–10 read as images: cover "In effect: 29 June 2015"; title page "This guidance came into effect on 29 June 2015 / This guidance was last updated on 13 December 2024"; GMC web page returned 403 to WebFetch. Claims used: four duties (tell, apologise, offer an appropriate remedy or support, explain fully short- and long-term effects) p.3; para 6 (someone in the team must take responsibility); para 9 (not for natural progression of illness; applies when harm or distress results or may result); para 10 (after putting matters right, you or someone from the team must speak to the patient; usually the lead or accountable clinician); para 11 (as soon as possible; someone available to support them, e.g. friend, relative, colleague; need not wait for an investigation, be clear what has and has not been established); para 12 (share what you know and believe about what went wrong and why, likely consequences; explain uncertainty; answer honestly; apologise); para 13 (option not to receive every detail; find out why; respect wishes as far as possible having explained consequences; record; can change mind); para 14 (three things: what happened, what can be done to deal with harm, what will be done to prevent someone else being harmed); para 15 (apology is not an admission of legal liability); para 16 (apology from the most appropriate team member regardless of who is responsible); para 17d (personalised 'I am sorry' more meaningful than organisational regret), 17e (who to contact; advocacy, counselling or support services), 17f (record apology in the clinical record; written apology may follow per wishes and policy); para 18 (death or severe harm with patient unlikely to regain consciousness or capacity: open and honest with those close to the patient); paras 21–22 (near misses: professional judgement; tell when patient needs or would want to know; seek advice if unsure); para 24 (organisations should have a reporting policy; you must follow it).

- `vandermolen-pcaki-definition-2018` (ESUR CMSC PC-AKI Part 1): PMID 29426991, PMC5986826; checked on PubMed (van der Molen AJ, Reimer P, Dekkers IA, et al.; Eur Radiol 2018;28(7):2845–2855); full text read in PMC (fix round 1). Claims used: PC-AKI replaces CIN; KDIGO definition, sCr rise ≥0.3 mg/dL or ≥1.5 (1.5–1.9) times baseline, with the CMSC retaining a 48–72 h window; decrease in renal function usually mild, peaking at 2–3 days, returning to baseline within 1–3 weeks; the older ESUR CIN definition was >25% or 44 µmol/L (0.5 mg/dL). 26.5 µmol/L is the arithmetic conversion of 0.3 mg/dL (× 88.4).

## Existing references used (checked in this session)

- `grossman-nifedipine-1996` (exported by pm-closure; PMID 8861992, checked there): reported harms of sublingual nifedipine capsules (abrupt hypotension, stroke, MI, death) and the call to abandon the practice. Used in the monitoring lesson's femoral sheath paragraph.
- `powers-acute-stroke-2019` (exported by pm-to-the-root; PMID 31662037, checked there): vascular imaging with non-contrast CT to select patients with large-vessel occlusion for mechanical thrombectomy. Used in the monitoring lesson's stroke row and check 2. "Call the stroke team at once and note the time of onset" is the practical consequence (thrombectomy is time-dependent); the Academy lesson pci-after-monitoring-hypotension says the same.
- `vandermolen-esur-pcaki-2018` (ESUR Part 2, exported by pm-planning; PMID 29417249, PMC5986837; full text re-read in fix round 1): metformin discontinuation now applies only to eGFR <30 with IV or second-pass intra-arterial contrast, to all first-pass intra-arterial contrast, and to AKI; Materials and methods define first-pass renal exposure as injection into the left heart, thoracic and suprarenal abdominal aorta or renal arteries, and list coronary injection as second-pass (fix round 2: the lesson now gives a left ventriculogram or suprarenal aortogram as examples and states that coronary injections count as second-pass); key point "stop metformin from the time of contrast medium administration". The ESUR restart rule sits in a table not present in the PMC text, so the lesson does not quote it.
- MASTER DAPT design (fix round 1): ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03023020 record (read with the trials tool): abbreviated arm "dual antiplatelet therapy is discontinued and a single antiplatelet agent is continued"; Valgimigli et al. Eur Heart J 2022;43:3100–3114 (PMID 35580836, abstract) reports 2,816 trial patients with complex PCI and/or ACS, so the trial included ACS patients.
- `esc-acs-2023` Table 7 re-read in fix round 1 (publisher page, WebFetch offset 200000): PPI "in patients on dual antiplatelet therapy at higher-than-average risk of gastrointestinal bleeds", with anticoagulant therapy and chronic NSAID/corticosteroid use among the listed examples. No statement on omeprazole or esomeprazole with clopidogrel was found, so none is taught.
- `gmc-nmc-candour-2015` paras 17a–c and 19–20 re-read in fix round 1 (NMC-hosted PDF, text extracted): 17a information in a way they can understand; 17b a place and time when best able to understand and retain information; 17c distressing information given considerately, respecting privacy and dignity; 19 respect and respond sensitively to the wishes and needs of bereaved people, take account of the patient's wishes including about sharing information, offer support (practical tasks after a death; chaplaincy or bereavement care staff); 20 those close to the patient offered support and a specific point of contact.

- `esc-acs-2023` (Byrne et al., Eur Heart J 2023;44:3720–3826; publisher page academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/44/38/3720/7243210 read with WebFetch at offsets 0, 100000 and 200000; recommendation tables are images, so no class or level is quoted). Text read: "Twelve-month DAPT (preferably with prasugrel or ticagrelor) remains the default strategy"; section 6.4 DAPT with a potent P2Y12 inhibitor plus aspirin recommended by default for a minimum of 12 months; abbreviated DAPT, preferably followed by P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy within 12 months, should be considered in patients event-free after 3–6 months of DAPT and not at high ischaemic risk; for HBR patients aspirin or P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy after 1 month of DAPT may be considered; "The presence of one major or two minor ARC-HBR risk factors indicates high bleeding risk (HBR)"; "patients with a PRECISE-DAPT score of ≥25 are regarded as high bleeding risk"; "De-escalation of antiplatelet therapy in the first 30 days is not recommended"; may be considered beyond 30 days to reduce bleeding; section 6.5: re-assess the OAC indication, continue only with a compelling indication; DAT with a NOAC at the stroke-prevention dose and SAPT (preferably clopidogrel) as default for up to 12 months after up to 1 week of TAT (aspirin and clopidogrel); NOACs preferred over VKA; DAT may be shortened to 6 months by withdrawing the antiplatelet in selected patients (e.g. multiple HBR factors); TAT may be prolonged up to 1 month when ischaemic risk or anatomical/procedural characteristics outweigh bleeding risk, then DAT up to 12 months; "the use of prasugrel or ticagrelor as part of TAT is not recommended"; AFIRE supports stopping antiplatelet therapy after 12 months and continuing OAC monotherapy in most patients; Table 7: proton pump inhibitors in patients on DAPT at higher-than-average GI bleeding risk.
- `urban-arc-hbr-2019` (PMID 31116395, PMCID PMC6736433; full text read in PMC): HBR = at least 1 major or 2 minor criteria; Table 3 major/minor criteria exactly as in the lesson table (age ≥75 minor; anticipated long-term OAC major; eGFR <30 major, 30–59 minor; Hb <11 g/dL major, 11–12.9 men / 11–11.9 women minor; spontaneous bleeding requiring hospitalisation or transfusion in past 6 months or any time if recurrent major, within 12 months not meeting major minor; baseline platelets <100 × 10⁹/L major; chronic bleeding diathesis major; cirrhosis with portal hypertension major; long-term oral NSAIDs or steroids minor; active malignancy (excluding non-melanoma skin cancer) within 12 months major; previous spontaneous ICH any time, traumatic ICH within 12 months, bAVM, moderate or severe ischaemic stroke within 6 months major; any other ischaemic stroke minor; non-deferrable major surgery on DAPT major; recent major surgery or major trauma within 30 days before PCI major).
- `valgimigli-master-dapt-2021` (PMID 34449185; abstract read): HBR patients one month after a biodegradable-polymer sirolimus-eluting stent; discontinuing DAPT non-inferior for net adverse clinical events (7.5% vs 7.7%) and MACCE; less major or clinically relevant non-major bleeding (6.5% vs 9.4%).
- `lopes-augustus-2019` (PMID 30883055; abstract read): 4,614 AF patients with recent ACS or PCI on a P2Y12 inhibitor; major or CRNM bleeding apixaban 10.5% vs VKA 14.7%; aspirin 16.1% vs placebo 9.0%; ischaemic events not significantly different. Also Oxford p.242 ("oral anticoagulation with apixaban and clopidogrel may be superior to additional use of aspirin") and p.243 further reading.
- `esc-ncs-2022` (publisher page via academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article-lookup/doi/10.1093/eurheartj/ehac270, WebFetch offsets 100000 and 200000): delay elective NCS until completion of the full course of DAPT (6 months after elective PCI and 12 months after ACS); delay time-sensitive NCS until a minimum of 1 month of DAPT; DAPT of at least 3 months should be considered before time-sensitive NCS in high-risk patients such as after ACS; ticagrelor withheld 3–5 days, clopidogrel 5 days, prasugrel 7 days before surgery; surgery performed while the patient is still on aspirin; low-dose aspirin continued peri-operatively after previous PCI in the absence of very high bleeding risk (section 5.3.1.1); Figure 5 footnote: resumption as soon as possible (within 48 h) after interdisciplinary risk assessment.
- `dvla-fitness-to-drive` (gov.uk/guidance/cardiovascular-disorders-assessing-fitness-to-drive, WebFetch; page last updated 7 November 2025): group 1 elective PCI: must not drive for at least 1 week, need not notify DVLA; group 1 ACS with successful PCI: may drive after 1 week if no other urgent revascularisation planned (urgent = within 4 weeks), LVEF ≥40% before discharge, no other disqualifying condition; otherwise 4 weeks; group 2 elective PCI and all ACS: must not drive, must notify DVLA; relicensing may be considered after at least 6 weeks if LVEF ≥40%, functional test requirements met, no other disqualifying condition.
- Not verifiable in this session: the 2024 ESC CCS and 2024 ESC AF guideline pages truncate before their antithrombotic sections (WebFetch at all offsets). The lesson therefore gives no 2024 CCS durations and refers to the existing lesson pci-pharm-dapt-duration for them.

## Lesson hb-after-the-procedure-report

- Three readers of the record: synthesis; Oxford p.48 (report, GP letter), p.338 (ward hand-over), Brilakis p.29/p.32 (prior reports and images reviewed at planning).
- Report contents (history, indication, anatomy and pattern of disease, procedural steps, equipment with number, type, size and length of stents, complications and management, post-procedural care plan): oxford p.48.
- Plan for vascular haemostasis, medications for primary and secondary prevention, cardiac rehabilitation when indicated, discharge plan, follow-up: oxford p.48.
- Verify kit integrity so that nothing apart from the stent is left in the circulation (procedural duty); sign the patient out; detailed GP letter (in-hospital stay, procedural outcome, future plan); inform department manager and coroner after intra-procedural death; communicate outcome and complications to patient and family under duty of candour; monitor post procedure for early signs of complications or renal failure; in charge of overall care until discharge; debrief the CathLab team at the end of the day, especially after complications or when learning points should be shared: oxford p.48.
- Radiographer warns operator at 1 Gy; operator records exposure in the report and communicates it to the patient: oxford p.39.
- Final result in at least two orthogonal views with the wire removed; angiographic success final diameter stenosis <20%: oxford p.230.
- Per-lesson parameters assessed in each run: brilakis p.128 (6.8.2). TIMI flow 0–3: p.128–129. %DS vs adjacent normal segment; worst, least foreshortened orthogonal view; ≥70–90% non-LM, ≥50% LM significant; physiology preferred, not in ACS culprits: p.129–130. Length in mm proximal to distal normal reference: p.130. Thrombus poorly detected, may be confused with calcific nodule; TIMI thrombus grades 1–5: p.130. Severe calcification = radio-opacities without cardiac motion before contrast, both sides of wall (paraphrased as calcium visible on both sides of the wall before contrast, even with the heart still); moderate = densities <50% of reference diameter, often only during cardiac movement; angiography underestimates vs intravascular imaging: p.130–131. NHLBI dissection: p.131–132. EBC bifurcation definition: p.132. Ostial = within 3–5 mm; aorto-ostial (RCA, LM, grafts) vs branch ostial: p.132–133. Tortuosity moderate ≥2 bends >70° or 1 >90°; severe 2 bends >90° or 1 >120°; within-lesion bend >45° significant for CTO: p.133. Aneurysm >1.5 × adjacent normal: p.133. Collaterals Rentrop and Werner: p.133. Perforation Ellis class and location: p.133, p.135. Fistula: p.135.
- Incomplete filling → declare non-diagnostic rather than possibly erroneous interpretation; causes (small catheter especially 4 Fr, side holes, large/ectatic arteries, poor engagement, high flow e.g. severe AS, HOCM, dilated cardiomyopathy, AV fistula, viscous contrast with iso-osmolar more viscous than low-osmolar, weak injection, high LVEDP); prevention (larger lumen: guide catheter or larger French diagnostic catheter; avoid side holes; stronger injection, possibly automated injector; manual injection preferred if slight pressure damping because force can be tailored): brilakis p.127 (6.6.3.1).
- Results review steps (indication, diagnostic info, admission info; review sessions educational, invite all; every stored loop examined to exclude dissection or embolism; stenosis quantification reassessed; haemodynamic data noted; bloods renal function, cholesterol, glucose; ECG and troponin after complex PCI): oxford p.339.
- Small enzymatic periprocedural MIs usually silent, associated with increased future risk of death or MI: oxford p.301.
- Brief names post-procedure destination: oxford p.55. Sign out: documentation, equipment left in situ, unexpected issues, need and duration of DAPT, loading dose given in lab or due on ward, key concerns; patient does not leave until completed: oxford pp.55–56.
- Hand-over four items: oxford p.338.
- Next operator's items (femoral bifurcation, PAD, calcification, prior stents, untreated severe lesions, crossover, groin scar, kissing iliac stents or stenosis, radial loops, subclavian stenosis or tortuosity, long sheath, anomalous origin, LIMA/graft engagement issues, catheters and support, guidewires, stent delivery issues, atherectomy): brilakis p.32 (Table 1.1).
- Death documentation (exact timing, personnel, equipment, perceived mechanism, next of kin contacted and what said): oxford p.300.
- Check 1 (stent details): oxford p.48, p.230. Check 2 (hand-over with venous sheath): oxford p.338.
- Pearl (dictate while runs on screen): practical framing, no factual claim beyond Brilakis p.32 items.
- Tips: diag-post-nondiagnostic (brilakis p.127), diag-post-ward-handover (oxford p.338), pci-post-report-stents (oxford p.48, p.230), diag-post-radiation-record (oxford p.39; brilakis p.493), pci-post-ecg-complex and pci-post-ecg-bifurcation (oxford pp.338–339, p.301). Oxford does not define complex PCI; the two tips are triggered only in the CTO and bifurcation scenarios (requires 'cto' / 'bifurcation') and ask the learner to judge whether the case was complex, so they no longer fire after every simple PCI.

## Lesson hb-after-the-procedure-monitoring

- Dedicated ward or day-case unit; specialist nurses check in, record medication, check observations, discuss procedure; available on return to observe for complications and help with line removal: oxford p.337.
- On arrival: pulse and BP checked and documented immediately; quick neurological assessment to exclude an event during transfer; access sites examined for bleeding or bruising; plan for sheath removal; regular pulse, BP, saturation, particularly after sedation; ECG after complex PCI for procedural MI; urine output and bloods in known or suspected renal dysfunction: oxford p.338.
- Full observation set (NIBP, three-lead ECG if stented, O2 saturation, respiratory rate); frequency 15-minutely first hour, half-hourly second hour, hourly thereafter with nursing judgement; radial monitoring (patent haemostasis; wound site every 15 min until device removed: colour, sensation, temperature, shape, size, pulse, pain; bleeding/haematoma; infection if long stay); femoral monitoring (femoral, popliteal, pedal pulses; wound site every 15 min until mobile; same features; bleeding/haematoma; infection): oxford pp.56–57.
- Pulses at access site and distally at beginning and end of case; continuous inspection and palpation for bleeding and haematoma: brilakis p.39 (2.7).
- Ambulation: most rest briefly (paraphrased 'most patients need only a brief period of rest and observation'); radial, closure devices, smaller femoral sheaths: short (1 hour); plan for sitting up, ambulation, eating and drinking; varies with local practice, access site, puncture size, venous/arterial; 6 F femoral: lie flat 1 hour after line removal, mobilise 2½ hours after removal: oxford p.338.
- Mobilised after 3 hours of bed rest: oxford p.76. Femoral sheath removal: ACT <150–200 s or 3–4 hours; firm pressure with two or more fingers 1 cm above the puncture; pressure approximately 8–10 minutes; antihypertensive for hypertensive patients to facilitate haemostasis, Oxford's example sublingual nifedipine: oxford p.76. Sublingual nifedipine no longer used (abrupt hypotension, stroke, MI, death): ref grossman-nifedipine-1996 (as in pm-closure). The 6 F mobilisation row and the monitoring keypoint no longer name the haemostasis method (oxford p.338 does not); the keypoint times both figures from line removal (fix round 2).
- Closure devices allow earlier mobilisation and discharge: oxford p.89.
- Diabetes: eat and drink once recovered and return to usual medication; very low lactic acidosis risk with normal renal function; metformin withheld the day before and for 2 days after: oxford p.42. Brilakis: in CKD hold on the day and do not restart until at least 48 h after; without CKD need not stop, check renal function and withhold if it deteriorates: brilakis p.33 (1.3). ESUR 2018: ref vandermolen-esur-pcaki-2018.
- Chest pain (particularly after PCI): 12-lead ECG first and alert operator, could be a procedural complication; haemodynamic changes alerted to the nurse in charge: oxford p.57. Examples (acute vessel closure, stent thrombosis) are given as the module's examples, not Oxford's; immediate revascularisation for vessel occlusion: oxford p.301.
- Hypotension common pre-, peri- and post-procedure; fasting, hypotensive medications; slow IV fluids often all that is needed; reversible causes tamponade, contrast reaction, vasovagal, bleeding, ischaemia, some life-threatening if untreated: oxford p.304. 'Call for senior help, fluids while looking for causes (site, haemoglobin, ECG, echo)': Academy lesson pci-after-monitoring-hypotension; framed as concurrent, matching the complication case.
- Tamponade: after perforation, intramyocardial injection at ventriculography, catheters and pacing wires in the RV; tachycardia, hypotension, syncope within minutes; echo confirms; immediate pericardiocentesis if high suspicion; drain left in until bleeding stops (possibly days); consider protamine, platelets and clotting factors; treat the cause, e.g. covered stent or coils for perforation: oxford p.305.
- Stroke: commonest symptoms visual disturbance, weakness, aphasia, altered mental state; may develop hours after due to late embolisation; management starts with airway, breathing, circulation; full neurological examination documented; think about anticoagulation status (haemorrhagic or embolic); CT to exclude haemorrhage; many resolve within 24 hours; expert neurological input e.g. stroke unit: oxford p.303. CT angiography for large-vessel occlusion and thrombectomy: ref powers-acute-stroke-2019.
- Contrast nephropathy definition (44 µmol/L (0.5 mg/dL) or >25% at 24–48 h), labelled as the older definition; creatinine peaks 4–5 days, 48 h tests may miss; usually asymptomatic; hydration 0.45–0.9% saline 1 mL/kg/h 12 h before and 12 h after in at-risk patients; withhold nephrotoxic drugs; renal function usually returns to baseline over a week; <1% irreversible dialysis-dependent failure: oxford p.333. Hydration key post-procedure; creatinine monitored at least 48 hours: brilakis p.496 (28.3). Normal saline because no other solution (bicarbonate, half-normal saline) shown advantageous; no specific treatment once established: brilakis p.494 (28.3.2), p.496 (28.3.3). PC-AKI, KDIGO definition, 48–72 h, peak 2–3 days, recovery 1–3 weeks: ref vandermolen-pcaki-definition-2018. 'Recheck at-risk patients at 48–72 hours or later' combines Brilakis's ≥48 h with the ESUR window and peak.
- Patient self-care list (report chest pain or dizziness; rehydrate; avoid activity risking access-site bleeding and how to apply pressure; bed rest with femoral access; DAPT importance and missed doses; avoid strenuous activity and alcohol for 48 hours, and signing legal documents if sedation given; driving restrictions): oxford p.57.
- Pearl (vagal vs bleeding): vasovagal reactions at puncture or sheath removal oxford p.77; bleeding and vasovagal both in the reversible causes oxford p.304.
- Check 1 (frequency): oxford pp.56–57; retroperitoneal bleed after high puncture presenting with progressive hypotension, flank bruising only occasionally: oxford p.315 (ch 10 vascular complications). Check 2 (late stroke): oxford p.303; ref powers-acute-stroke-2019.
- Tips: access-post-pulses-start-end (brilakis p.39), access-post-radial-obs (oxford pp.56, 338), access-post-femoral-mobilise (oxford pp.338, 57, 76), pci-post-chest-pain-ward (oxford p.57, p.301), pci-post-contrast-kidney (brilakis p.496; oxford pp.333, 338; ref vandermolen-pcaki-definition-2018).
- Video XPF_SXb657U: title only ("2. Manual of PCI - Monitoring").

## Lesson hb-after-the-procedure-dapt

- Premature discontinuation of DAPT or clopidogrel alone predisposes to stent thrombosis; adherence crucial; no evidence that heparin or LMWH protects when antiplatelets are stopped for surgery: oxford p.227.
- Default P2Y12: clopidogrel for stable CAD, prasugrel or ticagrelor for ACS unless contraindicated: brilakis p.50.
- PCI best avoided if non-compliance or non-cardiac surgery in the upcoming 6–12 months; medical therapy or CABG preferred: brilakis p.29 (1.1). Stent implantation avoided if possible when NCS needed within 6–12 months: brilakis p.185 (10.2.3).
- Brilakis durations: standard 6 months stable, 12 months ACS; PRECISE-DAPT ≥25 → 3 and 6 months; TWILIGHT aspirin withdrawal at 3 months with ticagrelor monotherapy reduces bleeding without increase in major adverse events (not restricted to non-HBR patients, hence the row label 'Dropping aspirin early'); >12 months considered without HBR and with high ischaemic risk, DAPT score ≥2 favours: brilakis p.51. Fig 3.4: de-escalation considered after ACS PCI when bleeding risk > thrombotic risk; escalation occasionally after elective PCI when thrombotic risk > bleeding risk; decisions guided by clinical, procedural and socioeconomic factors, PFT and genotyping may help: brilakis p.51.
- PRECISE-DAPT variables (Hb, WBC, age, CrCl, prior bleed); DAPT score items and points (age ≥75 −2, 65–74 −1, <65 0; smoking, diabetes, MI at presentation, prior PCI or MI, paclitaxel-eluting stent, stent diameter <3 mm 1 each; CHF or LVEF <30% 2; vein graft stenting 2; total −2 to 10); reassess at 12 months in uneventful patients; caption: figure for ACS patients; in the validation study short DAPT 3–6 months, standard at least 12 months: brilakis p.53 (Fig 3.6, page image).
- Oxford Table 8.8 (PCI stable low risk A+C 6 months; stable high risk A+C 3 months with "No <1 month" shown; ACS low risk A+T or A+C or A+P 12 months; ACS high risk A+C or A+T 6 months; CABG stable no indication; CABG ACS low 12 months, high 6 months); Table 8.9 (medical: stable no DAPT; ACS low risk 12 months, high risk A+C >1 month): oxford p.242 (page image).
- Clopidogrel at least 4 weeks after BMS, at least 6 months after DES; many centres 12 months or longer, particularly after complex PCI (now taught in the paragraph on older minimums; the '>12 months' row's Oxford cell is 'Not covered'): oxford p.239. Table 8.8 stable high-risk cell taught as 'A+C 3 months, with the unexplained note No <1 month'.
- MASTER DAPT: ref valgimigli-master-dapt-2021; switch to a single antiplatelet drug and inclusion of ACS patients: NCT03023020 record and PMID 35580836 (see above).
- ESC 2023 ACS statements: ref esc-acs-2023 (listed above).
- ARC-HBR table: ref urban-arc-hbr-2019.
- With OAC: aspirin usually discontinued after the procedure and clopidogrel only with the OAC; aspirin commonly stopped after 1–4 weeks to 6 months: brilakis p.50. Avoid prasugrel and ticagrelor at high bleeding risk such as patients on warfarin or DOACs: brilakis p.52.
- Oxford Table 8.10 (1st month A+C+O / A+C+O / C+O; 6th month A+C+O or (next line) ...; A+O or C+O; 12th month A+O or C+O; after 12th month O in all): oxford p.243 (page image). The layout puts "A+C+O or" in the 6th-month row of the ischaemic column with "A+O or C+O" on the 12th-month row; the lesson shows the 6th-month cell as triple or dual and marks empty cells as blank, with a caption saying so.
- Apixaban and clopidogrel may be superior to adding aspirin: oxford p.242; AUGUSTUS figures: ref lopes-augustus-2019.
- Bleeding prevention (reduce aspirin with OAC plus P2Y12; adjust P2Y12 duration to bleeding risk; avoid potent agents at high bleeding risk; routine PPI with DAPT; ESC 2023 Table 7 risk-based framing: ref esc-acs-2023) and treatment (platelet transfusion for life-threatening bleeding on irreversible agents; may not work with ticagrelor; reversal agent in development; de-escalate to clopidogrel; local measures): brilakis pp.52–53.
- Oxford Table 8.11 (prasugrel 7, clopidogrel 5, ticagrelor 3 days before; restart day 1–4; aspirin do not stop, case-by-case): oxford p.243 (page image). ESC 2022 NCS statements: ref esc-ncs-2022.
- Writing the plan: sign out records DAPT need, duration and loading dose status: oxford pp.55–56; explain why new tablets and how long, particularly clopidogrel: oxford p.340; nurse explains missed-dose consequences: oxford p.57. ## Notes for the checkers

- Oxford's two mobilisation figures differ (p.338: 2½ hours after removal of a 6 F femoral line; p.76: after 3 hours of bed rest); the lesson states both.
- Oxford Table 8.11 gives ticagrelor 3 days; the 2022 ESC guideline gives 3–5 days; both stated.
- Oxford p.57 attaches "(if sedation given)" to signing legal documents only; the lesson applies the 48-hour strenuous activity and alcohol advice generally.
- Existing lesson pci-after-discharge (pci-core.ts) states that car drivers are "usually advised not to drive for about a week after uncomplicated PCI"; the DVLA page read in this session says group 1 must not drive for at least 1 week after elective PCI, and after ACS with successful PCI for 1 week only if the stated criteria are met (otherwise 4 weeks). Not an error, but the ACS conditions are missing there.
