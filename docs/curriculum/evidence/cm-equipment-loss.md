### Possible errors in existing Academy lessons (for the checkers and fixer to route; not edited here)

1. `src/content/tracks/complications.ts`, lesson comp-equip-wire-burr-guide, Prevention keypoints: "Avoid jailing polymer-jacketed hydrophilic wires; their coating can strip and fragment." Brilakis 16.2 (p.299) says polymer-jacketed, hydrophilic and workhorse wires can all be jailed and that the question is controversial: in a microscopy study only 2 of 115 polymer-coated jailed wires showed mild damage versus 63 of 120 (55%) non-polymer wires, two with internal fracture; polymer emboli were found in 10% of autopsied stented hearts (not in jailing). The Academy states as a rule what the manual presents as a debated question.
2. Same lesson: "Remove the buddy wire before deploying the stent on the other wire." Brilakis's rule (18.8, p.343; 27.2.1.1, p.472) concerns a buddy wire used with a filter embolic protection device, where stenting over the buddy wire entraps the filter. As a general rule for all buddy wires it is not in either manual (jailing a wire is routine in bifurcations, 16.2). Suggest restricting it to filters.
3. `src/content/tracks/complications.ts`, lesson comp-equip-balloon-snare, "Snaring technique" step "Tighten by advancing the catheter: Push the snare catheter forward over the loop to close it, rather than pulling the snare back, which drags the loop off the target." Brilakis 27.1.3.5.1 (p.468) does hold the snare still and advance the catheter for coronary stents, but his general snaring technique (30.18.5.2, p.592) withdraws the snare into the catheter until the device is caught, and 30.18.5.1 (p.592) describes the loop being "pulled back, trapping the device against the catheter". The claim that pulling back drags the loop off the target contradicts the manual's own description.
4. Same module, comp-equip-wire-burr-guide burr table: "a burr-to-artery ratio of about 0.5". Brilakis gives "0.5 or lower" (p.575) and "<0.5" elsewhere (p.363–364); "about" understates the upper limit. Minor.
5. comp-equip-wire-burr-guide, "Freeing a trapped wire" step "Release the trap": the dilatation beside a trapped wire is step 4 of Brilakis's sequence (27.2.3.1.4, p.472), after a catheter to the trap and a balloon inflated on the trapped wire; the lesson presents it without that order. Same steps, "Steady traction ... while advancing the guide or a guide extension to provide counter-traction": a guide extension may be advanced over the wire (27.2.3.1.2, p.472), but advancing the guide itself is not in either manual, and Brilakis warns that pulling hard drives the guide deep and risks guide-induced dissection (16.2, p.301).
6. src/content/tracks/complications.ts, bifurcation pitfalls (about line 847): "avoid jailing hydrophilic polymer-jacketed wires" states as a rule what Brilakis 16.2 (p.299) presents as a debated question (see item 1).

## Lesson cm-equipment-loss-stent

- Equipment can be lost or entrapped inside or outside the coronary; can occlude or perforate the vessel; systemic embolisation including intracranial (stroke); entrapment more grave than loss and may need emergency surgery: brilakis p.465
- Iturbe 2012: 2,338 PCIs, loss 9 (0.38%), entrapment 4 (0.17%), all losses managed percutaneously, 3 of 4 entrapments emergency surgical removal and CABG: ref iturbe-device-loss-2012
- Brilakis 2005: 11,773 stent PCIs, 38 losses (0.32%), more with calcification and/or proximal angulation: ref brilakis-stent-loss-2005
- Causes: tortuosity and calcification; poor vessel preparation (ROTAXUS 2% vs 0.5% of lesions, as cited by Brilakis p.465; 2.5% of patients randomised to no atherectomy, brilakis p.356 (19.9.1); trial conclusion from ref abdel-wahab-rotaxus-2013); deformation during delivery then loss on withdrawal into the guide (Fig. 27.1: stripped off at the guide catheter); direct stenting; small (such as 5 Fr) guides; forceful withdrawal into guide or guide extension; guide extensions (catching the proximal collar on advance; on withdrawal, especially when deformed); advancement through a previously deployed stent; delivery via a collateral in retrograde CTO (stent loss and wire entrapment): brilakis p.465–466
- Stripping at the collar may occur around the subclavian curve with radial access; consider 40 cm rather than 25 cm cylinder: brilakis p.409 (23.1.3.1.1, item 4)
- Fig. 30.34 panel B: the extension tip prolapsed on itself during attempted retrieval of an undeployed stent, catching the stent's proximal edge and deforming it: brilakis p.537
- Resistance to stent delivery at a previously deployed stent: wire may have crossed under a strut; rewire through the stent, ideally after forming a knuckle more proximally: brilakis p.189 (10.2.8.3.2 item 2)
- Distal to proximal preferred, proximal to distal viable, facilitated by inserting the guide extension through the proximal stent: brilakis p.534 (30.3.3 item 9)
- Covered stents: one (PK Papyrus) easier to deliver than the other (Graftmaster, two stainless steel stents with a middle ePTFE layer) but can occasionally be dislodged from the balloon during delivery: brilakis p.589–590 (30.18.1.1–30.18.1.2)
- Bending the stent may help through tortuosity but may increase risk of loss: brilakis p.190 (10.2.8)
- Szabo technique may lead to stent deformation or loss: brilakis p.281 (15.1)
- Stent loss causes in 10.2.8.4.3: forceful advancement through resistance; deformation during advancement or withdrawal: brilakis p.190
- Prevention: avoid direct stenting especially tortuous/calcified; meticulous preparation, often atherectomy in calcium, imaging to confirm expansion before delivery; distal to proximal stenting, need for distal stent sometimes arises after proximal (distal edge dissection); guide extension for tortuous calcified vessels; avoid forceful advancement; push rod under a towel beside the Y-connector; no force at resistance in the extension: remove and reinsert avoiding wrap; insert stent into extension outside the guide and advance together; avoid 5 Fr guides in complex lesions (less room to withdraw deformed stents): brilakis p.465–466
- Fluoroscopy to watch stent entering proximal port of extension; inspect withdrawn stent for damage after failed delivery: brilakis p.536 (30.3.4)
- Proximal collar should not be advanced outside the guide catheter: brilakis p.533 (30.3.3)
- Monitor guide and distal wire position while advancing or withdrawing; avoid pushing proximal part of stent outside the guide if not advancing; maintain coaxial alignment when withdrawing stent into guide: brilakis p.190 (10.2.8.4)
- Check 1: deep breath can facilitate stent delivery: brilakis p.190 (10.2.8 "Other")
- Algorithm Fig. 27.2 (location; retrieval needed? wire maintained?; small balloon → snare → twirl → reassess → surgery; deploy; crush; leave in peripheral artery): brilakis p.467
- Lost stents can be hard to see (thin struts, obese, calcified or previously stented); IVUS to localise: brilakis p.466
- Intracoronary lost stent should be retrieved or deployed/crushed; noncritical peripheral (lower extremity, pelvic) often left: brilakis p.466
- Retrieval not always needed; noncritical coronary: deploy (wire maintained) or crush (wire lost) faster and safer; critical coronary (LM, major bifurcation) or peripheral (cerebral, renal): retrieval required; noncritical peripheral: retrieval risks often outweigh benefit: brilakis p.466
- Wire maintained facilitates small-balloon retrieval and deployment; wire lost: often impossible to rewire, retrieval limited to snares or twirling; deployment impossible, crushing only option if not retrieving: brilakis p.467
- Reassess after failed retrieval; in most cases deploying or crushing fastest and safest: brilakis p.470
- Surgical retrieval challenging, high risk, only under extenuating circumstances: brilakis p.470
- Leave in periphery; crush against iliac/femoral wall with peripheral stent if cannot be removed through sheath or vessel wall: brilakis p.471. Fig. 27.2 (p.467): outside coronaries, retrieval not needed → 11 leave in peripheral artery, with no wire question; the table row 'Peripheral, non-critical' therefore gives the same ending for both wire states (round 1).
- Small-balloon technique: balloon advanced through, inflated distal, withdrawn with stent; if not significantly deformed and guide large, balloon and stent retrieved inside the guide (round 1: wording changed from 'riding on the balloon' to the inflated balloon pulling the stent into the guide); often too deformed: balloon, stent and guide removed together (PCI Manual case 10); may be difficult to advance, possibly pushing stent distally; if partially advanced, inflate in proximal-mid stent then remove system: brilakis p.467
- Deployment: small balloon then increasingly larger; may cause more distal displacement; if balloon cannot pass (usually deformation) crush: brilakis p.470
- Crush: if wire lost and retrieval not feasible/desired, unless critical location such as LM (first choice); after failed retrieval the need to retrieve is reassessed, including surgery versus deploying or crushing (27.1.3.7, p.470), so crushing in the LM is taught only after failed retrieval (tip pci-stentloss-crush-check-struts, check 2, q5, pitfall 3); wire around the stent, balloon crush, new stent excludes it; avoid passing through struts especially if partially deployed; suspect if balloon delivery challenging; rewire ideally with knuckled polymer-jacketed wire (Fig. 27.8 knuckled wire through deformed stent, balloon and stent then delivered); if still challenging rewire again or Glider balloon/Wiggle wire (Wiggle wire: especially useful for equipment delivery through previously placed stents; not for primary wiring, exchanged over a microcatheter or OTW balloon: brilakis p.561, 30.7.4); complete apposition ideally with imaging: brilakis p.470–471
- Restenosis risk of crushing/deploying much lower than aggressive retrieval; intentional crushing in in-stent CTO: brilakis p.470–471
- No major cardiac complication when crushed or deployed; bleeding requiring transfusion 24% vs 7%; emergency CABG 5% vs 0.4%: ref brilakis-stent-loss-2005
- Three urgent CABG after failed retrieval, fatal: ref eggebrecht-stent-retrieval-2000
- Fig. 27.5: 2.5 × 28 mm stent lost in LM during retrograde CTO PCI; microsnare left it partially in aorta and LM (IVUS); then En Snare retrieved it: brilakis p.469
- Fig. 5.8: peripheral self-expanding stent caught at guide tip, dislodged into thoracic aorta, embolised to abdominal aorta, snared into right iliac and covered with a balloon-expandable stent: brilakis p.104
- Iliac stents avoided before completion of PCI (dislodged by guide advancement); long sheath through existing iliac stents: brilakis p.103 (5.3.3.2)
- Distal stent embolisation into a small branch may be left; none of 12 patients in three series had sequelae; extraction risk usually exceeds local risk: brilakis p.471
- Palmaz–Schatz first widely used stent, provided deployed, hand-crimped; dislodgement not uncommon: oxford p.220
- 1.04% manually crimped vs 0.27% premounted: ref eggebrecht-stent-retrieval-2000
- Many operators avoid indeflator suction (dislodgement risk); with contemporary stents risk very low and suction can usually be applied: brilakis p.187 (10.2.4.2)
- Entrapment is one of the causes of acute vessel closure (calcification, tortuosity, poor preparation, atherectomy, advancement through stents; retrieval or stenting over the entrapped equipment): brilakis p.437, p.445 (25.2.7) (cited as source of the cross-reference; taught in cm-principles-closure)
- Check 2 explanation: Fig. 27.5 details as above; crushing excluded in LM: brilakis p.469–470
- Tips pci-stentloss-prepare-first, pci-stentloss-guide-extension-collar, pci-stentloss-freeze-wire, pci-stentloss-small-balloon, pci-stentloss-crush-check-struts: as above (p.465–471, p.190, p.533, p.536, p.409)

## Lesson cm-equipment-loss-wire

- Entrapment and fracture infrequent, often preventable, potentially devastating especially if the wire unravels: brilakis p.471
- Entrapment causes: jailing during bifurcation stenting; deformation from aggressive knuckling; withdrawal of a looped tip through a proximal stent; buddy wire with filter (stenting over buddy wire entraps filter, very hard or impossible to remove); severe calcification: brilakis p.472
- Buddy wire removed immediately after filter delivery: brilakis p.343 (18.8.1.1 challenges: buddy wires removed immediately after filter delivery, because stenting over the buddy wire entraps the filter)
- Fracture causes: aggressive pulling, over-rotation, atherectomy over a kinked wire: brilakis p.472
- Prevention: some avoid jailing but SB occlusion risk usually exceeds entrapment risk; avoid aggressive manipulation especially small branches; straighten tip before removing through stent; do not pull hard; replace kinked wire before atherectomy: brilakis p.472
- Jailing debate: workhorse, hydrophilic or polymer-jacketed wires can be jailed; controversy over stripping the polymer on withdrawal with distal embolisation; microscopy study 2/115 polymer-coated mildly damaged vs 63/120 (55%) non-polymer, two internal fractures; jailed length a factor; SB wiring quicker with polymer-coated wire; polymer emboli downstream of stented coronaries in 10% of autopsied hearts (not jailing): brilakis p.299 (16.2)
- Polymer-jacketed wires used for wiring replaced for a workhorse wire before lesion preparation and stenting: brilakis p.296 (16.2.8)
- Jailing: do not jail across the radiopaque portion (fracture risk); straight tip before jailing (looped tip may entangle, PCI Manual cases 33, 88); avoid jailing long segments: brilakis p.299, p.302 (16.2)
- Rotawire fracture: atherectomy close to the 0.014 inch tip, over a kinked Rotawire, in highly angulated lesions, prolonged lesion contact in tortuous segments: brilakis p.365 (19.9.5.7.5)
- Restrained wire tip: withdraw and readvance, or parallel wire; aggressive advancement under plaque risks dissection and tip entrapment: brilakis p.153–154 (8.6.2)
- Fig. 27.12: guidewire fracture with distal spring coil unravelling; fracture suspected after aggressive torquing made the tip unresponsive on fluoroscopy; removed in its entirety with a microcatheter: brilakis p.475 (cited in 27.2.3.2.1 p.473 as an unravelled example; used in the unravelled steps as an example of successful percutaneous removal)
- Step 1 do not pull hard (fracture or unravelling, very challenging, may need cardiac surgery): brilakis p.472
- Pulling hard may cause deep guide intubation and guide-induced dissection; gentle constant traction: brilakis p.301 (16.2)
- Step 2 microcatheter or balloon as far as possible, pull gently; more focused withdrawal force; guide extension could also be used: brilakis p.472
- Rotation with a torquer may help (jailed wire): brilakis p.302
- Step 3 inflate balloon advanced as far distally as possible; in most cases releases the wire: brilakis p.472
- Repeat POT after balloon inflation to free a jailed wire: brilakis p.302
- Step 4 second wire next to the entrapped wire and balloon inflation (Fig. 27.11: knuckled wire trapped in an LAD CTO; CTO crossed with another wire advanced parallel; balloon angioplasty around the trapped wire; retrieved): brilakis p.472, p.475
- Step 5 surgery; last straw: pull hard with balloon or microcatheter in place hoping for fracture without unravelling: brilakis p.473
- Fracture: key question unravelling; coil may form a metal "bird's nest" predisposing to thrombosis; IVUS to confirm no unravelling: brilakis p.473 (the systemic thromboembolism risk is attached to fragments reaching the aorta, 27.2.3.2.2, p.473)
- Unravelled fragments into the aorta or in a critical location (LM or other major bifurcation): emergency surgery to remove and prevent thromboembolic complications (coronary and systemic): brilakis p.473
- Attempts to retrieve unravelled fragments generally avoided (more unravelling); successful percutaneous removal described; snare can break the wire as close as possible to the ostium (or within the coronary) to allow stenting over: brilakis p.476
- Stenting over trapped fragments (Fig. 27.13: stent loss complicated by wire entrapment and fracture in the proximal circumflex; another wire past the entrapped equipment, balloon angioplasty and stenting across it): brilakis p.476–477
- Fig. 27.15: calcific acute RCA occlusion; wire tip entrapment and unravelling; failed 2 mm snare; failed trapping balloon; proximal end in abdominal aorta; double gooseneck (20 mm inside 2 mm); proximal end snared with 20 mm; 2 mm advanced on the wire; snaring at the ostium and pullback with fracture and removal; stenting over the entrapped wire; final result (panel L): brilakis p.479. The caption does not mention surgery and does not grade the result; the module says only that the remaining wire was stented over, with panel L showing the final result (round 2: 'good' removed as unsupported).
- No unravelling (IVUS): small fragments in small distal branches or collaterals best left; long fragments especially in large artery retrieved: brilakis p.476
- Twirling: one or more wires advanced next to the fragment and twisted several times; may intermingle allowing retrieval: brilakis p.476
- Snaring with small coronary snares; if fails emergency surgery may be required: brilakis p.476
- Distal small branch fragment usually left; central fragment stented to prevent migration and reduce thrombus: brilakis p.476
- Oxford: acute arm ischaemia can be related to lost equipment (e.g. entrapped wire, Fig. 10.13 wire wrapping in the radial artery requiring surgery): oxford p.318
- Propofol or general anaesthesia rarely needed for equipment entrapment due to severe radial spasm: brilakis p.509 (29.2.3.4)
- Danek 2016: fracture may be more likely in complex lesions and with stent interaction; case 1 retrieval after second wire and balloon; case 2 fragment into ascending aorta, emergent surgery: ref danek-wire-entrapment-2016
- Published Academy case pub-calcified-lad-wire-entrapment-elca uses excimer laser for wire entrapment: src/content/cases/published.ts (Basile 2026, as already cited there)
- Check 1 explanation, check 2 explanation, tips pci-wire-tip-restrained, pci-wire-trapped-no-hard-pull, pci-wire-fracture-ivus-first: as above (p.153–154, p.472–476, p.301–302)

## Lesson cm-equipment-loss-balloon-microcath

- Entrapment may compromise flow and cause haemodynamic instability, potentially requiring haemodynamic support: brilakis p.478
- Balloon entrapment causes: rupture; failure to deflate; interaction with a previously placed stent, especially plaque modification balloons; previously placed stents occasionally extracted while retrieving cutting balloons: brilakis p.476
- Fracture cause: kinking of balloon or stent catheter shaft (Fig. 27.16): brilakis p.476
- Prevention: consider primary atherectomy for very heavily calcified lesions; avoid very high-pressure inflations in highly calcified lesions; discard a kinked catheter: brilakis p.477
- Ruptured balloon may be difficult to remove; in some cases entangled with the guidewire, requiring removal of both and losing wire position: brilakis p.409 (23.1.2 item 4)
- After high-pressure inflation many balloons become sticky and hard to remove, sometimes losing wire position; fluoroscopy during removal minimises the risk: brilakis p.417 (23.2.2 item 3)
- Balloon rupture causes include high inflation pressures (>20 atm); prevention atherectomy or intravascular lithotripsy in heavily calcified lesions and avoiding >20 atm: brilakis p.180 (9.7.9.3.1) (round 2: taught as a rule for heavily calcified lesions only, not a general ceiling)
- Routine high pressures elsewhere: stent deployment usually followed by ≥20 atm non-compliant post-dilatation, and up to 26–28 atm for non-compliant balloons when expansion is suboptimal: brilakis p.191 (10.2.9.2 items 3 and 5); undilatable lesions, non-compliant balloons often up to 26–28 atm: brilakis p.417 (23.2.2 item 1)
- Cutting balloon 5 s per atm, not to exceed 12 atm, to prevent the blades puncturing the balloon and reduce the risk of blade entrapment in calcified spicules; 6–8 atm: brilakis p.179 (9.7.9.2)
- Cutting balloon inflated slowly; >14 atm avoided; excessive forces or rupture may fracture the blade, avulse struts, entrap the balloon: brilakis p.417 (23.2.3)
- Failure to deflate causes: lesion or stent recoil, strangulation in the guide, hypotube damage; prevention avoid shaft kinking, replace promptly if kinked: brilakis p.181 (9.7.10.3.1)
- If the shaft of a balloon (or stent) catheter kinks, discard it and use a new one: brilakis p.477 (27.3.2.2); if shaft kinking occurs, promptly replace the balloon: brilakis p.181 (9.7.10.3.1)
- Shaft kink causes: forceful advancement, resistance; prevention avoid force, small bites; small kinks can usually continue, do not straighten (weakens shaft, may fracture); large kinks remove and discard: brilakis p.172 (9.7.7.3.2)
- Microcatheter entrapment causes: aggressive advancement through tortuous calcified lesions; two microcatheters over an externalised wire meeting and interlocking; manipulation without a wire inside: brilakis p.482
- Microcatheter fracture causes: aggressive manipulation especially of softer-tip microcatheters (such as the Caravel); over-rotation compromising integrity and hindering retrieval and advancement: brilakis p.483
- Prevention: avoid aggressive manipulation through complex calcified lesions or across struts; antegrade tip should not touch the retrograde microcatheter tip; avoid over-rotation; always a wire inside; stronger-tip microcatheters (Mamba, Teleport) may have lower tip fracture risk: brilakis p.483
- Rotation should not exceed 10 turns before allowing the catheter to unwind; wire always in the lumen to prevent kinking and entrapment; tip can break off and be entrapped: brilakis p.411 (23.1)
- Caravel not designed for aggressive torquing; generally not used in balloon-uncrossable lesions (tip fracture and separation): brilakis p.410 (23.1); designed for push, gentle rotation, tip fracture and separation with aggressive rotation: brilakis p.550 (30.6.2.5)
- Tornus no more than 20 rotations in any direction: brilakis p.558 (30.6.5)
- Microcatheter fatigue (wire binding; difficulty after prolonged use) → exchange; flush after contrast (wire stickiness), rarely remove both: brilakis p.558 (30.6.6)
- Entrapment algorithm (Fig. 27.17): failed to deflate? → rotation and back-and-forth → second balloon inside guide → guide extension → snare → surgery: brilakis p.478, p.480, p.481
- Rotation may help rewrapping; gentle back-and-forth may free: brilakis p.478
- Second balloon inside the guide as close to the tip as possible without exiting, inflated at high pressure (≥20 atm), guide with both balloons retracted as a unit: brilakis p.478
- Cut the shaft, advance guide extension as close as possible, pull; Trapliner allows simultaneous trapping inside the guide: brilakis p.478
- Snare advanced as close as possible, tightened, withdrawn; surgery if all fail: brilakis p.480
- Failure to deflate: another indeflator (ideally saline) or luer-lock syringe (saline); withdraw without deflation if smaller than the reference diameter; inflate at high pressure until rupture if smaller than the reference vessel; OTW balloon over a second wire inflated at 2–4 atm, wire removed, stiff wire or back end advanced to puncture, inflated OTW centres the wire; alternatively cut shaft, guide extension pushed gently against the balloon, stiff wire or back end through it, minimising proximal injury; surgery if all fail: brilakis p.482
- Shaft fracture: where did it break and is a portion inside the guide? compare retrieved fragment with an intact catheter; if inside, trap with another balloon and remove guide and fragment; if not, snare: brilakis p.480
- Second balloon at high pressure (≥20 atm), entire assembly withdrawn under fluoroscopy maintaining inflation: brilakis p.480
- Snaring of the fragment; then guidewire around the entrapped balloon (usually through a second guide) and balloon angioplasty around it: brilakis p.480
- Fig. 27.16: 2.75 × 28 mm balloon shaft kinked during insertion, could not be advanced, fractured in two; distal portion in RCA retrieved with a micro snare; TIMI 3: brilakis p.480
- Microcatheter retrieval important to prevent acute and chronic complications; techniques as for a fractured balloon (snares, guide extensions, trapping inside the guide, wiring through with another wire and balloon angioplasty); surgery if they fail; tip fracture: retrieval very challenging or impossible, stenting over the tip may be fastest and safest (Fig. 27.18 Corsair tip fracture after advancement attempts through a heavily calcified lesion): brilakis p.482–483
- Excessive manipulation can disrupt device and/or wire and lock them together requiring withdrawal of both; polymer-jacketed wire can sometimes be advanced through the established track: brilakis p.411 (23.1)

- Guide extension fracture (Fig. 30.37: tip entrapped advancing into a diagonal through an LAD stent; shaft fractured on retrieval; balloon inflated inside the cylinder and withdrawn retrieved it): brilakis p.537–538
- Longitudinal stent deformation on forceful guide extension advancement through a deployed stent: brilakis p.537
- Trapping with a guide extension difficult: balloon must be proximal to the proximal collar; equipment retracted there; Trapliner preferred when trapping anticipated: brilakis p.533 (30.3.3)
- Dedicated trapping balloons: no wire lumen, resistant to kinking and deformation, lower profile: brilakis p.572 (30.9.4)

## Lesson cm-equipment-loss-snares

- Covered stents, coils, Amplatzer plugs, pericardiocentesis tray and snares needed for perforations and equipment loss/entrapment; rarely needed but must be available in all labs: brilakis p.588 (30.18)
- Snares retrieve entrapped or lost equipment; also capture the retrograde wire in retrograde CTO PCI: brilakis p.592
- Snare = nitinol wire loop advanced through a microcatheter (or diagnostic or guide catheter), positioned round the device and pulled back trapping it against the catheter; assembly removed with the device; three-loop (tulip) snares preferred (three overlapping loops vs one in the Goose Neck): brilakis p.592
- Homemade loop snare: exchange-length coronary wire and multipurpose catheter, wire tip reinserted through the distal tip (Fig. 30.115, 0.014 in. wire): brilakis p.592, p.594
- KAM-snare: wire loop trapped by a balloon inflated at 8–12 atm at the distal site of a guide extension; wire inserted in the monorail lumen of a conventional balloon; loop shaped; introduced into the guide extension; balloon entraps the returning end; pulling or pushing the proximal end changes the diameter (Fig. 30.116): brilakis p.592, p.595; guide extension use 6: brilakis p.532
- Technique: snare withdrawn and collapsed into the introducer tool, introduced into a diagnostic or guide catheter, advanced out of the tip, positioned close to the device, withdrawn into the catheter until the device is captured between snare and catheter tip, then withdrawn with catheter and snare: brilakis p.592
- Coronary stents: snare wire held still, snare catheter advanced until the loop secures the stent, assembly withdrawn into the guide (Fig. 27.4): brilakis p.468
- Size depends on the vascular space: small (2–4 mm) coronary; large (27–45 mm or 18–30 mm) aorta: brilakis p.592
- Delivery sheath usually discarded; snare advanced through diagnostic or guide catheter; do not discard the collapsing tool (needed to reintroduce): brilakis p.594
- Coronary snares: Micro-Elite 0.014 in., loops 2, 4 or 7 mm, 180 cm, no delivery catheter; 2 or 4 mm En Snare: brilakis p.468
- Peripheral: 18–30 mm En Snare through JR4 or multipurpose diagnostic or guide catheter, three-loop design facilitates retrieval; caution, snare wires may cause vessel injury: brilakis p.469
- Fig. 27.5 (microsnare left the stent partially in the aorta and partially in the left main; a three-loop snare then retrieved it): brilakis p.469
- Second arterial access: described for snaring the tip of a kinked catheter (5.7.4.1, p.119) and for passing a wire round a broken balloon fragment, usually through a second guide (27.3.3.10, p.480); for aortic snaring the module says only 'if the first access is occupied' (sim tasks, round 1)
- Snare pitfall 3 (round 1): withdrawing catheter and snare before the device is gripped; the loop is closed by drawing the snare into the catheter (30.18.5.1–30.18.5.2, p.592) or, for a coronary stent, by advancing the catheter over the held snare wire (27.1.3.5.1, p.468)
- Ostial stent: long portion protruding into the aorta, snaring and removal can be performed: brilakis p.191 (10.2.9.3.1); >50% protruding, sometimes possible to snare and remove, risk of coronary injury and/or stent embolisation; other solutions: short (6–8 mm) oversized balloon to flare, Ostial Flash, prolonged DAPT: brilakis p.282 (15.1)
- Wire fragments: small coronary snares; snare to break an unravelled wire near the ostium; double gooseneck: brilakis p.476, p.479
- Balloon: snare as close as possible, tighten, withdraw; fragment snared: brilakis p.480
- Burr: snare advanced over the Rotablator shaft: brilakis p.364 (19.9.5.7.3)
- Kinked catheter: another arterial access, snare the tip to straighten and facilitate untwisting; fragments of a fractured catheter snared without surgery: brilakis p.119–120 (5.7.4.1)
- Catheter knotting: ask for expert help; do not pull tight; knot can occasionally be undone by carefully advancing a wire; otherwise pull tight to make it small, pull back to the sheath and remove the entire system, possibly significant bleeding and surgical repair; snares and forceps; surgical removal well reported: oxford p.112
- Retrograde wire capture for externalisation: brilakis p.592 (taught in cp-cto-retrograde)
- Brilakis 2005 retrieval: attempted 35/38, successful 30 (86%); methods and percentages as listed: ref brilakis-stent-loss-2005
- Eggebrecht 2000: 1.5 mm low-profile balloons 7/10; biopsy forceps or gooseneck snare 3/7; balloon readily available and technically familiar: ref eggebrecht-stent-retrieval-2000
- CTO-Complex PCI cart with all commonly used equipment including complication kit; readily available, restocked regularly, known to operators and staff: brilakis p.597 (30.23)
- Expiry of covered stents and coils expected given the low frequency; operator familiar with each item, its strengths and limitations, willing to use it: brilakis p.513 (chapter 30 introduction)
- Check explanations and tips pci-snare-size-for-space, pci-snare-close-and-withdraw, pci-snare-homemade: as above (p.468–469, p.592–595)
