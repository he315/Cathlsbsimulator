## Lesson pm-antithrombotics-heparin

- Heparin goals: prevent thrombus in coronary and on equipment; reduce radial occlusion: brilakis p.47 (3.4.1)
- Mechanism: activates antithrombin III, inactivates thrombin and procoagulant proteases incl. factor Xa: oxford p.245
- UFH 70–100 U/kg without GPI/cangrelor; 50–70 U/kg with; no renal adjustment: brilakis p.47 (3.4.2.1.1), p.377 (20.3)
- Oxford usually 70–100 IU/kg intra-arterially, dose at operator discretion: oxford p.245
- Radial diagnostic heparin usually 50 U/kg up to 5000 U, 100 U/kg may be more effective, goal to prevent radial occlusion; may delay until catheter at aortic root with extreme radial/subclavian tortuosity because crossover to femoral may be preferable: brilakis p.92 (4.3.9)
- Oxford radial cocktail example heparin 2500 IU, verapamil 2.5 mg, GTN 200–400 µg, injected into the radial artery before catheterisation; various regimens: oxford p.71
- Brilakis 4.3: 100 U/kg "may be more effective" (stated as effectiveness, not as practice; tip access-radial-heparin-timing): brilakis p.92
- Radial-to-femoral crossover in anticoagulated patients associated with increased bleeding, needs meticulous technique: brilakis p.61 (4.1 step 5)
- Half-life 1–2 h, dose dependent (30 min after 25 U/kg, 60 min after 100 U/kg, 150 min after 400 U/kg): brilakis p.47; Oxford 60–90 min: oxford p.306
- Causes of low ACT (three, unranked; the module no longer uses frequency words such as 'commonest' or 'often'): did not reach circulation (IV line malfunction), low dose, decreased antithrombin III (familial or acquired): brilakis p.48 (3.4.3.1); treatment: additional doses, potentially through arterial sheath, repeat ACT before PCI; avoid inserting guidewires, balloons, stents until ACT therapeutic: brilakis p.48–49
- Check ACT 5 minutes after anticoagulant before proceeding: brilakis p.49 (3.4.4.2)
- ACT every 20–30 min: brilakis p.48 (3.4.2.1.2), p.49 (3.4.4.1)
- Cardiologist: regular control of ACT: oxford p.47; nurse: timely ACT and heparin doses: oxford p.54; physiologist monitors interval from last heparin/ACT and informs operator: oxford p.59
- ACT goals: Hemochron 300–350, HemoTec 250–300, with GPI or cangrelor 200–250 (3.4 p.48 names no device; 2.8 p.39 and 20.3 p.377 give 200–250 as the Hemochron goal; no HemoTec figure for GPI/cangrelor in either manual), >350 retrograde CTO: brilakis p.39 (2.8), p.48; antegrade CTO >300 (some >250), retrograde >350 (some >300, check very frequently), check every 20–30 min once therapeutic: brilakis p.390 (21.3); Oxford >250–300 s, used by some operators: oxford p.245
- Hemochron ACTs higher than HemoTec: brilakis p.441 (25.2.2); therapeutic ACT >250 HemoTec / >300 Hemochron depending on complexity and IV antiplatelet use: brilakis p.441
- Thrombosis causes (low dose, did not reach circulation/IV infiltration, defective batch, HIT, resistance/AT III deficiency); prevention (platelet count before procedure, correct dose, working line, ACT at 5 min); treatment (manage thrombus as 20.9, additional anticoagulant to therapeutic ACT): brilakis p.49 (3.4.4.2)
- Acute vessel closure algorithm: check ACT, consider GP IIb/IIIa or cangrelor: brilakis p.436 (Fig 25.1)
- Guide extension decreases guide size by 1–2 Fr; special attention to damping and ACT to reduce thrombus risk: brilakis p.536 (30.3)
- Check 1 numbers (80 U/kg, 262 s) illustrative; Hemochron goal p.48. Reconciliation with the Academy foundation lesson (src/content/tracks/pci-core.ts, 'Anticoagulation in the lab': device-agnostic 250–350 s, ACT about 10 minutes after the bolus) is stated as a cross-reference, not as a source claim
- Check 2: 40 min since ACT > 20–30 min interval (p.48); guide extension (p.536); thrombus treatment (p.49)
- Protamine indications: bleeding, with site-specific treatment: brilakis p.49 (3.4.4.1); earlier femoral sheath removal in some patients: brilakis p.196 (11.1.5); tamponade: oxford p.305; perforation: oxford p.329; forearm haematoma heparin reversal: oxford p.319; retroperitoneal reversal of anticoagulation: oxford p.315
- Reversal only after all intracoronary equipment removed; stent thrombosis risk: brilakis p.49; perforation: 'in most cases' not until equipment removed and pericardial blood evacuated (Fig 26.5 shows reversal when extravasation continues, p.450), risks guide/vessel thrombosis and thrombosis of pericardial blood: brilakis p.449 (26.2)
- Protamine 1–1.5 mg per 100 U, not to exceed 50 mg, slowly to reduce hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis; risk factors NPH or protamine zinc insulin, fish hypersensitivity, vasectomy, severe LV dysfunction, abnormal preoperative pulmonary haemodynamics; severe systemic hypotension and relative pulmonary hypertension 5–10 min after; methylene blue may help: brilakis p.48 (3.4.2.1.3)
- 1 mg per 100 U, max 50 mg, ≤5 mg/min; anaphylaxis with prior NPH insulin or fish allergy: brilakis p.449 (26.2)
- Oxford: IV infusion over 10 min, max 50 mg, 1 mg neutralises 80–100 U within 15 min of heparin; half-life 60–90 min so lower doses later; anticoagulant effect in high doses; allergic reactions especially in diabetics with isophane insulin: oxford p.306
- Check 3: 8000 U → 80–120 mg, capped 50 mg (p.48)
- Femoral sheath removal after ACT <180 s; closure devices usable at high ACT, may be best not if >300 s: brilakis p.196 (11.1.5); Oxford ACT <150–200 s or 3–4 h: oxford p.76; radial sheaths removed immediately even with high ACT and GPI or cangrelor; if not removed, flushed and connected to pressure to prevent thrombosis and embolisation: brilakis p.212 (11.2.1)
- Bleeding causes excessive anticoagulation, access complication, hypertension; prevention avoid high doses, ACT every 20–30 min: brilakis p.49 (3.4.4.1); trend to lower heparin doses to reduce bleeding while keeping anticoagulation adequate: oxford p.245
- Tip pci-bleeding-reverse-correctly: no reversal for bivalirudin (p.48), eptifibatide/tirofiban/cangrelor (p.55); femoral bleeding treatment reverse anticoagulation, discontinue GPI/cangrelor: brilakis p.501 (29.1)
- Videos: titles only (videos.ts)

## Lesson pm-antithrombotics-alternatives

- UFH most commonly used: brilakis p.47; UFH standard intra- and post-procedurally in primary PCI (MATRIX, HEAT-PPCI), bivalirudin alternative: oxford p.249
- Bivalirudin: direct thrombin inhibitor, no HIT risk; bolus 0.75 mg/kg; infusion 1.75 mg/kg/h up to 4 h after procedure (stated only under item a, normal renal function; item b gives rates without duration); CrCl 30–59 1.75; <30 1 mg/kg/h; haemodialysis 0.25 mg/kg/h; half-life 25 min; ACT 300–350 / 200–250; no reversal, infusion discontinued: brilakis p.48 (3.4.2.2)
- Similar outcomes to UFH, used infrequently except HIT (agent of choice): brilakis p.47 (3.4)
- Oxford: equivalent efficacy vs heparin + GPIs in elective and acute PCI with reduced bleeding; DTI; weight-adjusted bolus and infusion; usually monotherapy, may combine with GPIs: oxford p.245
- BRIGHT-4: 6016 STEMI primary PCI, radial 93.1%; bivalirudin plus 2–4 h high-dose post-PCI infusion vs UFH; death or BARC 3–5 bleeding at 30 days 3.06% vs 4.39%; stent thrombosis 0.37% vs 1.10%: ref li-bright4-2022
- 2023 ESC primary PCI anticoagulation (UFH standard, enoxaparin or bivalirudin with full-dose infusion alternatives, no fondaparinux): ref esc-acs-2023 (see note above)
- CTO: UFH preferred because reversible in perforation; bivalirudin best avoided, cannot be reversed, unpublished guide thrombosis in long procedures; GPI/cangrelor not given even after success (minor wire perforations reopen → delayed effusion/tamponade): brilakis p.390 (21.3)
- LMWH and fondaparinux may reduce bleeding in PCI: oxford p.245; SC LMWH dose omitted morning of procedure: oxford p.42
- ATOLL: 910 STEMI patients; IV 0.5 mg/kg enoxaparin vs UFH before primary PCI; primary endpoint 28% vs 34%, RR 0.83, P = 0.06; major bleeding 5% vs 5% (no difference); main secondary endpoint reduced (7% vs 11%): ref montalescot-atoll-2011
- OASIS-5: 20,078 ACS; fondaparinux 2.5 mg daily vs enoxaparin 1 mg/kg bd; primary 5.8% vs 5.7%; major bleeding at 9 days 2.2% vs 4.1%; deaths 295 vs 352 at 30 days (P = 0.02), 574 vs 638 at 180 days (P = 0.05): ref yusuf-oasis5-2006. No catheter-thrombus statement is made for fondaparinux (not in either manual or these abstracts)
- FUTURA/OASIS-8: 2026 PCI patients in a fondaparinux-treated NSTE-ACS cohort; IV UFH low dose 50 U/kg regardless of GPI vs standard 85 U/kg (60 U/kg with GPI) adjusted by ACT; primary composite (major bleeding, minor bleeding, major access-site complications at 48 h) 4.7% vs 5.8%, P = 0.27; major bleeding not different; minor bleeding 0.7% vs 1.7%, P = 0.04; key secondary composite 5.8% vs 3.9% (P = 0.05); catheter thrombus very low (0.5% vs 0.1%, P = 0.15): ref steg-futura-oasis8-2010
- HIT type 1 (first 2 days, platelets normalise with continued heparin, direct platelet activation, no treatment) and type 2 (usually 4–10 days, immune, life-threatening venous and arterial thromboembolism, discontinue all heparin, non-heparin agents such as bivalirudin or argatroban): brilakis p.49–50 (3.4.4.3)
- ASH 2018 (abstract): strong recommendation for the 4Ts score rather than gestalt; avoid HIT laboratory testing and empirical treatment with a low-probability 4Ts score; HIT mediated by antibodies to PF4–heparin complexes; non-heparin anticoagulants for acute HIT: argatroban, bivalirudin, danaparoid, fondaparinux, DOACs: ref cuker-ash-hit-2018
- HIT treatment continues after PCI (type 2 treatment is anticoagulation with non-heparin agents, p.50; the 4-hour run-on is the PCI regimen, p.48)
- Heparinised saline flushes: brilakis p.39 (2.11, flushing catheters with heparinized saline) and 4.2 (side arm flushed with heparinized saline); oxford p.97 (flush infusion typically heparinized saline); radial cocktail with heparin: oxford p.71
- Platelet count before procedure to detect falling count (HIT): brilakis p.49; platelet count monitored with prolonged heparin infusion (IABP): oxford p.289
- HIT among causes of thrombosis: brilakis p.49; hypercoagulable state incl HIT causing acute closure thrombosis: brilakis p.441
- Check (day 6, 240→105) illustrative; timing p.49

## Lesson pm-antithrombotics-oac

- Weigh risks of stopping vs bleeding of arterial puncture, individual basis; guidelines only, local policy: oxford p.85
- Anticoagulation status in patients on warfarin: oxford p.41; INR checked day of procedure: brilakis p.33 (1.3)
- Cockcroft–Gault calculator link with Table 1.2: brilakis p.34
- High INR and low platelet count favour radial: brilakis p.34 (1.4)
- BRIDGE: 1884 AF patients, warfarin stopped 5 days before elective operation or invasive procedure; LMWH bridging vs placebo; arterial thromboembolism 0.4% vs 0.3% (noninferior); major bleeding 1.3% vs 3.2% (P = 0.005); AF only: ref douketis-bridge-2015
- Warfarin stop/continue lists, 3–5 days, INR morning; restart evening after if no bleeding; continue or admit and switch to IV UFH stopped immediately prior and restarted immediately afterwards: oxford p.85
- IV UFH (e.g. mechanical mitral) stopped 2 hours before; warfarin typically withheld 3–5 days; most operators comfortable INR <2: oxford p.42
- Brilakis: discontinue warfarin 5 days before elective procedures, INR on day; radial preferred: brilakis p.33
- Femoral generally safer if INR <2.0; fully anticoagulated → radial; femoral with fluoroscopy and ultrasound, compression or closure devices: oxford p.85
- Emergency: not always time to make arrangements: oxford p.85; OAC ideally stopped prior unless emergent: brilakis p.49 (3.4.4.1)
- Femoral reserved for emergencies such as IABP when anticoagulation not stopped: oxford p.68
- DOAC Table 1.2: brilakis p.34 (image checked); apixaban 2 days with no creatinine-clearance strata; no rows below 15 mL/min, edoxaban rows end at 95 mL/min
- DOAC 24 h, dabigatran with renal impairment 72 h; restart evening after especially no bleeding or radial: oxford p.86
- Access algorithm steps 1 (unsuitable sites), 2 (prior CABG with patent grafts: femoral usually preferred; strongly with bilateral IMA grafts), 4 (complex PCI: at least one femoral often preferred), 5 (change site after failure): brilakis p.61, Fig 4.1 p.62
- Brilakis femoral access sequence uses both fluoroscopy and ultrasound guidance: brilakis p.62 (4.2); Oxford: 'consider' fluoroscopy and ultrasound in anticoagulated patients: oxford p.85
- Access algorithm step 3: OAC or high bleeding risk (high or low BMI, renal failure, anaemia, thrombocytopenia) → radial: brilakis p.61; Fig 4.1 p.62; crossover bleeding: p.61
- After PCI with OAC: aspirin usually discontinued after procedure, clopidogrel with OAC: brilakis p.50 (3.5); aspirin commonly stopped after 1–4 weeks to 6 months: brilakis p.50; avoid prasugrel/ticagrelor with warfarin/DOAC; reduce aspirin duration; routine PPI on DAPT: brilakis p.52 (3.5.1.3.1)
- Oxford Table 8.10 regimens (read from page image): oxford p.243; apixaban + clopidogrel may be superior to adding aspirin; Lopes 2019 further reading: oxford p.242–243
- AUGUSTUS: 4614 patients with AF and recent ACS or PCI planning to take a P2Y12 inhibitor; 6 months of treatment; bleeding 10.5% apixaban vs 14.7% VKA; death or hospitalisation 23.5% vs 27.4% (HR 0.83, P = 0.002); 16.1% aspirin vs 9.0% placebo; ischaemic events similar: ref lopes-augustus-2019
- 'Oxford Table 8.10 predates current guidance' and the ESC default pathway: refs esc-acs-2023, esc-af-2024 (see note above)
- Patients restarting anticoagulation need instructions on repeat blood tests: oxford p.341
- Check (mechanical mitral): oxford p.85, p.42; check (rivaroxaban CrCl 40): brilakis p.34, oxford p.86
- Pitfall "pigtail into LV with apical thrombus": oxford p.85 (avoid passing catheters into LV)

## Lesson pm-antithrombotics-oral

- PCI thrombogenic: balloon disrupts intima, lipid core, tissue factor, vWF activate platelets; equipment; stents occlude if anticoagulation inadequate: oxford p.239
- DAPT goals prevent stent thrombosis and future ACS: brilakis p.50 (3.5.1.1)
- Aspirin COX-1, TxA2: oxford p.239; Brilakis 325/81–100: p.50; Oxford 300 load, 75 daily indefinitely; used since first balloon angioplasty, reduces procedural MI: oxford p.239
- Clopidogrel: thienopyridine, P2Y12 ADP receptor; 75 mg daily; 300 mg ≥72 h before or 600 mg if less time; inter-individual variability from cytochrome P450 genetics: oxford p.239; Brilakis 600 mg ideally ≥6 h before PCI in stable patients: p.50; very rarely TTP: brilakis p.54
- Prasugrel: third-generation thienopyridine, more effective metabolism, more potent, rapid, lower non-responsiveness; TRITON-TIMI 38 superiority offset by major and fatal bleeding; not recommended >75, <60 kg, active bleeding, stroke/TIA; TRILOGY no superiority medically managed; consider with known anatomy or not suitable for surgery; small first-line application, usually after stent thrombosis on other agents; 60/10 mg: oxford p.240. Brilakis 60/10, 5 mg if <60 kg or >75, contraindicated prior TIA/stroke: p.50
- Rank of prasugrel vs ticagrelor: Brilakis gives 'prasugrel or ticagrelor' without ranking (p.50); Oxford small first-line role for prasugrel (p.240) and PLATO superiority without significant bleeding increase, mortality benefit (p.241); ESC 2023 class IIa 'should be considered' for prasugrel in preference to ticagrelor: ref esc-acs-2023
- Ticagrelor: reversible, different site from ADP, hampers ADP-induced activation; more potent, rapid, no inter-individual variability; PLATO superiority without significant bleeding increase, mortality benefit; non-severe dyspnoea up to 15% in first week, bradycardia; adenosine hypothesis; 180/90 bd: oxford p.241. Brilakis: contraindicated prior ICH; avoid strong CYP3A inhibitors (list), inducers (list), simvastatin/lovastatin >40 mg: p.50
- Choice: clopidogrel stable CAD, prasugrel or ticagrelor ACS unless contraindicated; OAC patients clopidogrel: brilakis p.50; avoid potent agents with warfarin/DOAC: p.52
- ESC 2023 prasugrel preference; ISAR-REACT 5: refs esc-acs-2023, isar-react5-2019
- Pretreatment: stable not before diagnostic, given if planned PCI; NSTE-ACS no; STEMI yes before or at latest at PCI, ticagrelor 180 or prasugrel 60: brilakis p.50 (3.5.1.2.3); Oxford preload clopidogrel 300–600 per local policy in patients due or expected to undergo PCI, not restricted to stable patients (so the NSTE-ACS cell now reports this general rule): oxford p.41, p.239; ESC stable column: the 2024 ESC CCS guideline could not be read in this session, so the cell says only that it is outside the ACS guideline; STEMI initial care aspirin and clopidogrel/ticagrelor loading: oxford p.248; ESC 2023 pretreatment positions: ref esc-acs-2023
- STEMI preloaded P2Y12 increases bleeding if emergency CABG: brilakis p.143 (7.2.5)
- Surgery interruption: prasugrel 7, clopidogrel 5, ticagrelor 3 days; restart day 1–4; aspirin do not stop (case-by-case): oxford p.243 (Table 8.11); no evidence that heparin or LMWH protects patients whose antiplatelet therapy is stopped for surgery: oxford p.227
- Opioids delay gastric emptying and P2Y12 absorption; avoid opioids in STEMI if not necessary; use IV agents (cangrelor or GPI): brilakis p.42 (3.1.3.2); Oxford initial STEMI care includes pain relief with opioids or benzodiazepine: oxford p.248
- Escalation/de-escalation concept, majority stay on guideline DAPT, alternatives 'may occasionally be considered', PFT and genotyping, socioeconomic factors: brilakis p.51 (Fig 3.4)
- Switching rules: brilakis p.52 (Fig 3.5, image checked; caption cites Angiolillo 2017 international consensus; late-phase ticagrelor to prasugrel 'a LD should be considered'; MD of clopidogrel for 'bleeding or bleeding concerns'); Oxford Table 8.7: oxford p.241 (organised by stable vs ACS; stable C to T 90 mg and C to P 10 mg 24 h after last C, no load; ACS reloads; T to C 600 mg 24 h in both columns). Comparison paragraph (round 2): Oxford's stable-column maintenance-dose escalation from clopidogrel matches the consensus late phase (MD 24 h after last dose, p.52 panel B) and differs only from the early phase (load regardless of timing, p.52 panel A)
- DAPT duration: Brilakis 6 months stable, 12 months ACS; PRECISE-DAPT ≥25 shortens to 3 and 6 months; TWILIGHT aspirin withdrawal after 3 months with ticagrelor monotherapy, less bleeding without increase in major adverse events; >12 months considered without high bleeding risk and with high ischaemic risk, DAPT score ≥2 favours: brilakis p.51 (3.5.1.2.5), Fig 3.6 p.53. Oxford clopidogrel ≥4 weeks after BMS, ≥6 months after DES, many centres 12 months or longer especially complex PCI: oxford p.239. Oxford Table 8.8 (PCI and CABG durations by stable/ACS and bleeding risk, including 3 months and a <1 month entry for stable high bleeding risk) and Table 8.9 (medical management): oxford p.242 (page image read)
- Check (PRECISE-DAPT 31, stable): brilakis p.51; oxford p.242
- Bleeding: causes, prevention (reduce aspirin duration with OAC, adjust P2Y12 duration to predicted bleeding risk, avoid potent in high bleeding risk, routine PPI), treatment (platelet transfusion for life-threatening bleeding with irreversible agents; may not work with ticagrelor; reversal agent in development; de-escalate to clopidogrel; local measures): brilakis p.51–53
- Dyspnoea: ticagrelor more likely (adenosine-mediated); evaluate other causes; caffeine, aminophylline, theophylline being studied; if persists switch to prasugrel or clopidogrel: brilakis p.53
- Allergy: all can cause; replace P2Y12 culprit; aspirin desensitisation (nasal ketorolac and oral aspirin challenge; complete after 325 mg): brilakis p.54 (Fig 3.7)
- Premature discontinuation of DAPT or clopidogrel predisposes to stent thrombosis; adherence crucial: oxford p.227
- DAPT duration taught elsewhere; nurse records loading dose given in lab or needed, DAPT need and duration, educates on missed doses: oxford p.55–57
- Pre-procedure checklist: aspirin received? P2Y12 for planned PCI or STEMI?: brilakis p.33 (1.3)
