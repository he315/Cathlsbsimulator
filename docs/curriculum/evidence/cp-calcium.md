## Lesson cp-calcium-recognise

- Four difficulties (severity, delivery, expansion of lesion and stent, complications acute: dissection, perforation, equipment loss/entrapment; late: restenosis): brilakis p.355
- Severe: radio-opacities without cardiac motion before contrast, both sides of wall; moderate: densities <50% of reference lesion diameter, often only with cardiac movement: brilakis p.355 (19), p.130 (6.8.2.5)
- 1,155 lesions: angiography 38%, IVUS 73%: brilakis p.355; ref mintz-calcium-ivus-1995 (abstract: 440/1155 = 38%; 841/1155 = 73%; sensitivity 60% and 85% for three- and four-quadrant calcium; less sensitive for milder degrees)
- ACC/AHA: light or no calcification type A; moderate or heavy calcification type B: oxford p.212 (Table 8.2)
- Vessel calcification among lesion characteristics making PCI complex: oxford p.246
- SYNTAX: heavy calcification adds two points: brilakis p.141 (ch 7, step 9)
- Planning: prior challenges expanding target lesion; severe calcification → consider atherectomy, laser and IVL if available: brilakis p.35 (1.9)
- Check 1: moderate definition p.355; under-reporting p.355
- Imaging arc grading: >270° severe, 180–270° moderate, <180° mild: brilakis p.355
- Imaging benefit greater in complex lesions including severely calcified lesions: brilakis p.231 (13.1)
- IVUS: very high density (calcified tissue) bright white, sometimes with black acoustic shadow: oxford p.231
- IVUS images blocked by calcium, just shadowing; OCT preferred since it can determine depth: brilakis p.374 (19.13.1). IVUS locates calcium as superficial or deep from the leading edge (Mintz classified lesion calcium as superficial only 48%, deep only 28%, both 24%): ref mintz-calcium-ivus-1995 (abstract); 'depth' in the OCT cell now worded as how far the calcium extends into the wall (p.374)
- OCT: calcium low signal (dark), low attenuation (allows deep penetration), sharp borders: brilakis p.240; arc and depth measurable in most cases: brilakis p.239–240 (Fig. 13.5 legend)
- No commonly accepted IVUS criteria; circumferential and superficial calcification favours atherectomy (or IVL if available): brilakis p.246 (13.4.4.2.2.3)
- OCT three findings (>180°, >0.5 mm, >5.0 mm) associated with underexpansion; "increased risk of stent underexpansion": brilakis p.240, p.374 (19.13.1), p.417; score 2+1+1 = 4; in the validation cohort score 4 → mean stent expansion 78% vs 96% for scores 0–3 (mean expansion, not a probability): ref fujino-calcium-2018 (PMID 29400655, abstract re-read); Brilakis's own summary '22% likelihood of stent underexpansion' (p.241, 13.3.6.2 C) is quoted only as his summary and corrected, matching pm-imaging; favours plaque modification such as atherectomy or IVL: brilakis p.241
- IVUS-derived calcium score published (existence only): ref zhang-ivus-calcium-2021
- IVUS catheter "jumps" with tight lesions, tortuosity, severe calcification; only with the solid-state catheter (rotational pullback happens within the catheter); prevention lesion pretreatment, slow steady pullback; treatment rotational IVUS catheter, more pretreatment: brilakis p.244 (13.4.3.3.1)
- OCT catheter may fail to advance; predilatation allows advancement and contrast penetration: brilakis p.235
- Deep calcified lesions may respond to balloon; superficial more likely ablative techniques: brilakis p.241
- Balloon reasonable in deep calcium; superficial and nodular best treated with atherectomy: brilakis p.359 (19.9.3)
- Circumferential superficial, obstructive nodular or thick calcium suggests need for atherectomy: brilakis p.356 (19.9.1)
- Imaging may be performed after atherectomy if catheter will not cross: brilakis p.374
- Tip pci-calc-imaging-decides: p.356, p.241, p.374, p.235
- Check 2: OCT features p.240/p.417; 22% p.241; "suggests high likelihood of needing atherectomy" for circumferential superficial calcium p.356; OCT can see depth p.374; never primary stenting p.356
- Calcium vs thrombus: angiographic filling defect can be confused with calcific nodule: brilakis p.130 (6.8.2.4); imaging for angiographically ambiguous lesions including calcified nodule; OCT preferred in ACS due to higher resolution; plaque ulceration, erosion, thrombus or calcified nodule help identify culprit: brilakis p.231 (13.1.1)
- Multiple views; calcification hinders interpretation; physiology and imaging encouraged: brilakis p.356 (19.6); physiology encouraged because of poor correlation with angiography: brilakis p.374 (19.12)
- Step table: planning p.355 (19.1); medications, long duration, monitor anticoagulation, bradycardia with AV-node artery atherectomy p.355 (19.3); access p.355 (19.4); engagement 7–8 Fr, AL1/3D Right RCA, XB/EBU LM, support early, coaxial p.355–356 (19.5); wiring p.356 (19.8); preparation p.356 (19.9); stenting after balloon expansion, ≥20 atm 1:1 NC postdilation p.373 (19.10), imaging before and after p.374; physiology/imaging p.374; support: atherectomy associated with higher risk of haemodynamic compromise p.374 (19.14)
- Pitfalls and pearl: restatements of p.355–356, p.374, p.130
- Drill: grading definitions p.355

## Lesson cp-calcium-strategy

- Primary stenting should never be performed in heavily calcified lesions; underexpansion increases restenosis and stent thrombosis: brilakis p.356 (19.9)
- Four groups of options; plaque modification balloons delivery challenging; laser not very effective in severe calcification but used especially for underexpanded stents; IVL and OPN not available in US as of 2020: brilakis p.356; Fig. 19.2 p.358
- Upfront vs secondary atherectomy, advantages and disadvantages ("Initial balloon angioplasty may be faster" → table "May be quicker"): brilakis p.356 (19.9.1)
- ROTAXUS stent loss 2.5% of patients randomised to no RA: brilakis p.356; 2% of lesions vs 0.5% with RA: brilakis p.465 (27.1.1)
- ROTAXUS 240 patients; strategy success 92.5% vs 83.3%; crossover 12.5% vs 4.2%; late lumen loss 0.44 vs 0.31 mm; similar restenosis, TLR, MACE; conclusion balloon dilation with provisional RA default: ref abdel-wahab-rotaxus-2013 (abstract)
- PREPARE-CALC: crossover 16%: brilakis p.356; 200 patients; strategy success 98% RA vs 81% modified balloons; late lumen loss non-inferior; provisional MB feasible as long as bailout RA readily available: ref abdel-wahab-prepare-calc-2018 (abstract)
- ECLIPSE ongoing 2,000 patients: brilakis p.356; 2,005 patients; TVF 11.5% vs 10.0%; MSA 7.67 vs 7.42 mm²; conclusion supports balloon-first approach guided by imaging for lesions that can be crossed and dilated: ref kirtane-eclipse-2025 (abstract)
- Atherectomy when anticipated benefits exceed risks; benefit higher in severely calcified and long lesions; imaging features: brilakis p.356
- Risks (dissection, embolisation, perforation, equipment loss or entrapment) increase with tortuosity (relative contraindication), thrombus, dissections (relative contraindication), bypass grafts; generally not in grafts, rare SVG cases; usually avoided in dissected segments but used for CTO/undilatable: brilakis p.357
- Fig. 19.3 algorithm (severe calcification? → tortuosity/thrombus/dissection/graft? → atherectomy or PTCA → success → stents; uncrossable/undilatable → atherectomy, from the PTCA arm with no further condition): brilakis p.358 (image viewed); atherectomy performed in dissected segments for CTOs and undilatable lesions: p.357
- Oxford RA triggers: clear angiographic calcium; calcified arc >180° on intravascular imaging; failed balloon predilatation or failed balloon crossing: oxford p.269–270
- Oxford contraindications: severe tortuosity (perforation, burr entrapment), coronary dissection: oxford p.269
- Small vessels: atherectomy generally avoided especially if tortuous: brilakis p.404 (22.3.9)
- Tip pci-calc-no-stent-until-expanded: p.356, p.359 ("stent underexpansion, which is much harder to treat than balloon undilatable de novo lesions"), p.415
- Check 1: dissection relative contraindication p.357, p.358; atherectomy used in dissected segments for undilatable lesions p.357; Oxford p.269; balloon-first downside p.356; OA complications similar p.370
- 2023 EAPCI consensus scope: ref barbato-calcium-consensus-2023 (abstract)
- Guideline status paragraph: ACC/AHA/SCAI 2021 RA class 2a LOE B (fibrotic or heavily calcified lesions) and imaging for complex stenting 2a LOE B: ref angsubhakorn-calcium-review-2022 (PMC full text), guideline ref lawton-revasc-jacc-2021; ESC 2024 CCS first-time recommendation of IVUS/OCT guidance in anatomically complex lesions (left main disease, true bifurcations, long lesions): ref gurgoglione-esc-ccs-2024 (its "lumen lesions" read as left main) and ref xie-hybrid-imaging-2025 (quotes left main explicitly), guideline ref esc-ccs-2024; ECLIPSE imaging analysis (operator discretion, 9.3% vs 13.2%, adjusted HR 0.74, consistent OA vs BA): ref stone-eclipse-imaging-2025
- Orbital vs rotational (Fig. 19.4, 19.9.2): prior stents → rotational; OA not within recently placed stents, done in endothelialised stents; RA in old stents and in stents placed immediately prior that failed to expand; risks distal embolisation, burr entrapment, stent distortion; repeat stenting; higher speeds in recent stents; RA preferred for uncrossable (forward vs side ablation), OA also used; OA preferred in larger vessels (single crown); local availability, experience, cost; laser exception underexpanded stents: brilakis p.358–359 (image viewed)
- Check options shortened for answer-length balance (no content change).
- Check 2: p.359; in-stent atherectomy reserved for when other strategies (high-pressure balloons with/without buddy wires, laser, IVL or very-high-pressure balloon) fail: brilakis p.418 (23.2.5); Oxford entrapment statement p.271
- Undilatable definition, often severe calcification, often uncrossable: brilakis p.415 (23.2); de novo vs ISR key determinant: p.415
- Fig. 23.7 outline (de novo: 1 likely to respond → 2 high pressure (shorter balloon, two smaller balloons, ≥1 buddy wire) → 3 modified balloon; 4 suitable for atherectomy → 5 atherectomy → 6 laser → 7 subintimal → 8 CABG; future 9 IVL, 10 very-high-pressure balloon; ISR: 2 → 3 → 6 laser → 5 atherectomy → 7 → 8; 11 imaging): brilakis p.416
- Pitfalls: p.356, p.359, p.357, p.404, p.359
- Pearl: synthesis of p.356 (upfront planning), p.360 (pacing), p.361 (vasodilator)

## Lesson cp-calcium-balloons

- Oxford: important to maximally dilate before stent; resistant lesion much harder after stenting (paraphrased): oxford p.218 (Balloon sizing)
- NC preferred: higher pressures with lower risk of dissection or rupture; higher crossing profile, less flexible: brilakis p.359 (19.9.3)
- Standard vs plaque modification first: depends on severity and likelihood of delivery; most cases standard first: brilakis p.359; PMB less deliverable and more costly: brilakis p.417 (23.2.3)
- Balloon 1:1 must fully expand: brilakis p.359; undersized by 0.5 mm; shorter balloons; conservative sizing 1:1 or lower: brilakis p.417 (23.2.2)
- Scoring balloon: semicompliant with three nitinol spiral struts; lower crossing profile than cutting balloon; more scoring marks per mm: brilakis p.570 (30.9.3.1); slow inflation only for cutting balloon "in contrast to Angiosculpt and Chocolate": brilakis p.359
- Nitinol-caged: pillows and grooves; small dissections; grooves relieve stress and stop dissections propagating: brilakis p.571 (30.9.3.2)
- Cutting: atherotomes protrude 0.005 in., three or four radial incisions: brilakis p.571; high crossing profile, 1 atm every 5 s: p.571, p.359; usual 6–8 atm, not exceed 12 atm; slow inflation prevents blades puncturing balloon and blade entrapment in calcified spicules: brilakis p.179 (9.7.9); >14 atm avoided: brilakis p.417 (imaged); Oxford: atherotomes sharper than scalpels, may perforate gloves/skin, damage vessels if not fully deflated before withdrawal: oxford p.218
- Very high pressure: >40 atm: brilakis p.573, p.420; 30–35 atm: brilakis p.179; 326 lesions >90% expansion, 2–3% rupture: brilakis p.372–373; not available in US: p.573
- Tip pci-calc-cutting-slow: p.179, p.359, p.417, oxford p.218
- Rupture caveat added to pressure step: causes include highly calcified lesions and >20 atm; prevention: consider atherectomy or IVL before balloon angioplasty in heavily calcified lesions, avoid >20 atm: brilakis p.180 (9.7.9.3.1); know each balloon's rated burst pressure and percentage diameter growth (they differ between balloons): brilakis p.417 (23.2.2); "check the instructions for use before going above 20 atm" is the practical consequence of p.417 together with the >20 atm rupture warning on p.180.
