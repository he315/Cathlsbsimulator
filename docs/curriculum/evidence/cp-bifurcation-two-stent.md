### Possible errors in existing Academy lessons (for the fixer)

In `src/content/tracks/pci-complex.ts`, lesson `cx-bif-two-stent` and quiz:

1. DK crush step "Stent the SB with an MV balloon in place" says the SB stent protrudes "1–2 mm into the MV". Brilakis p.312 (text and Fig. 16.42, confirmed on the page image): **2–3 mm**. 1–2 mm is the TAP and V-stenting figure (p.306, p.322).
2. DK crush step "Remove SB balloon and wire, then crush" and quiz `cx-q-bif-crush-wire` treat removing the SB wire before the crush as required and say a jailed wire "could be impossible to remove". Brilakis p.313: the crush "could jail the SB guidewire although most operators remove the SB guidewire prior to crushing"; in reverse crush the SB wire is deliberately jailed by the crush and removed after rewiring (p.308, Figs 16.36–16.37). Suggest "most operators remove it" rather than "must".
3. DK crush step "Rewire the SB and perform the first kiss" omits that the first rewiring is through a **proximal** strut (p.313), and describes "dilate the SB ostium with an NC balloon at high pressure, then the first kissing inflation" without the pressures (20 atm, then both at 10–12 atm, p.314).
4. DK crush step "Stent the MV and POT" says "Remove the SB wire" before the MV stent; Brilakis p.315 step 10: the SB wire "may or may not" be left in (his figures leave it, jailed, and remove it after the second rewiring, p.317).
5. Culotte step "Deploy the second stent ... keep the overlap short": no overlap-length advice appears in either manual's culotte section (Brilakis p.317–319, Oxford p.260); source should be checked.
6. Quiz `cx-q-bif-pot`: correct option says the POT balloon's "distal marker just proximal to the carina"; Brilakis places the distal marker **at** the carina (p.300, p.316, p.317). Worth reconciling with the provisional module.
7. Table row "V / simultaneous kissing stents ... Large disease-free proximal MV (Medina 0,1,1), usually left main": Brilakis restricts SKS to emergencies and bail-out (p.295, p.322); V-stenting is for Medina 0,1,1 (p.319). Merging the two in one row blurs that distinction.

## Lesson cp-bifurcation-two-stent-choosing

- Two routes to a second stent: planned when an SB to be preserved is likely to occlude; bail-out when SB compromised after MV stenting: brilakis p.293 (16.1.1, Fig. 16.1), p.294 (16.1.3)
- Branches <2 mm usually need not be preserved; decision depends on size and supplied territory: brilakis p.293
- Oxford: vessels <2.0 mm rarely considered for treatment; SB occlusion rare when pretreatment ostial stenosis <50%: oxford p.257
- Planned order: Medina 0.1.1 → V-stenting; emergency → SKS; angulation 70°–90° → T-stent; <70° → DK crush or culotte: brilakis p.293 (Fig. 16.1), p.294 (16.1.4, 16.1.5), p.295 (16.1.6); in the left main two-stent techniques, especially DK crush, preferred for true (Medina 1.1.1 or 0.1.1) bifurcations: brilakis p.332 (17.10)
- DK crush preferred strategy in most cases at <70°: brilakis p.294 (16.1.4)
- SKS should not be performed in non-emergent bifurcation PCI; used when emergency stenting needed and access to MV and SB must be kept: brilakis p.295 (16.1.6)
- SB compromise definition (TIMI <3, DS >70%, NHLBI dissection ≥B); balloon angioplasty often suffices; sometimes SB stent: brilakis p.294 (16.1.3)
- Provisional sequence ending in kissing, final POT, "Good result? No → SB stenting": brilakis p.300 (Fig. 16.9), p.302
- Bail-out angle: 70°–90° → TAP; <70° → reverse crush, culotte: brilakis p.293 (Fig. 16.1)
- SB stent options after provisional: TAP, reverse crush, culotte (p.302); T-stenting, TAP, reverse crush, culotte (p.303): brilakis p.302–303
- SB occluded, rewiring not feasible: small (1.0–1.5 mm) balloon over jailed wire partially into the SB; if it will not cross, torquable microcatheter spun into the SB to create a channel; angioplasty to restore flow; option 1 rewire through struts ideally with dual lumen microcatheter (to avoid passing behind lifted struts); option 2 convert to inverted DK crush with SB acting as MV and MV as SB: brilakis p.303–304
- Table, DK crush: best supportive data; MV wire access maintained, little to no risk of SB loss, important with dissections; multiple steps, cumbersome, difficulties at each step: brilakis p.311; preferred for true left main bifurcations (Medina 1.1.1 or 0.1.1): brilakis p.332 (17.10)
- Table, culotte: best suited to similarly sized SB and distal MV: brilakis p.317; double stent thickness in proximal MV, final kiss mandatory: oxford p.260; the strength cell is marked * (module's summary of Figs 16.59, 16.65; Brilakis states no strength)
- Table, T and TAP: angulations close to 90°, especially when MV large relative to SB; easier and faster than DK crush or culotte: brilakis p.304; TAP advantage simplicity, disadvantage neocarina from SB struts may impede MV equipment: brilakis p.305
- Table, reverse crush: bail-out after provisional MV stenting: brilakis p.308; both strength and weakness cells marked * as the module's summary of the steps (starts from the provisional MV stent, p.308 background; SB stent rewired after the crush, step 8, p.308); Brilakis states no advantage or disadvantage
- Table, V-stenting: Medina 0.1.1; simplicity, no rewiring or kissing, wire access kept; neocarina hinders wiring and equipment; ≥7 Fr: brilakis p.319, p.322
- Table, SKS: emergency or bail-out only; very quick, both wires kept; long neocarina makes subsequent treatment extremely challenging; ≥7 Fr: brilakis p.322, p.324
- Check 1 (DK crush first, culotte alternative at 50°, elective, proximal MV diseased): brilakis p.293–294; T-stent unsuitable at <70° (prolapse needed to cover ostium): brilakis p.288 (Fig. 15.19)
- "Where the books differ" list: see Notes above (oxford p.257, p.259, p.260; brilakis p.294, p.304–305, p.311, p.317, p.320)
- Trial table: DKCRUSH-III MACE 6.2% vs 16.3%, TVR-driven: ref chen-dkcrush3-2013 (and brilakis p.311, p.317); DKCRUSH-V 5% vs 10% TLF at 12 months: brilakis p.311, ref dkcrush5-2017; 3-year 8.3% vs 16.9%: ref chen-dkcrush5-3y-2019; BBK II culotte superior to TAP (brilakis p.317), 6.5% vs 17%, TLF 6.7% vs 12.0% not significant: ref ferenc-bbk2-2016; EBC TWO similar outcomes (brilakis p.317), SB ≥2.5 mm and ≥5 mm ostial disease, 7.7% vs 10.3%, time/dose/cost favoured provisional: ref hildick-smith-ebc-two-2016; DEFINITION II 6.1% vs 11.4%, two-stent strategy with 77.8% DK crush (so 'mostly DK crush', not a test of DK crush itself): ref definition2-2020; DEFINITION II 3-year TLF 10.4% vs 16.0%, HR 0.63, no difference between years 1 and 3: ref kan-definition2-3y-2022 (PMID 35798473); EBC MAIN 14.7% vs 17.7%, NS, provisional remains default: ref ebc-main-2021; EBC MAIN 3-year MACE 23.5% vs 29.5% (P = 0.11), TLR 8.3% vs 15.6% (P = 0.013), side vessel <3.25 mm and lesion <10 mm favour provisional: ref arunothayaraj-ebc-main-3y-2025 (PMID 39907022); EBC MAIN protocol dual-stent options were T, TAP or culotte (per-protocol analysis): ref arunothayaraj-ebc-main-technique-2023 (PMID 36709485)
- "Both manuals keep provisional stenting as the default": brilakis p.297 (provisional preferred and easiest for most), p.332 (provisional preferred for most LM bifurcations without significant SB involvement); oxford p.257, p.260 (Fig. 8.21 legend)
- Guide: supportive (AL for RCA, EBU or XB for left main); most techniques through 6 Fr except V and SKS (≥7 Fr); larger guide eases two pieces of equipment, especially used balloons, more support, troubleshooting: brilakis p.295 (16.2.1, 16.2.5); larger guides (7 or 8 Fr) provide more support and are preferred for left main bifurcation PCI: brilakis p.329 (17.4); allow V-stenting: brilakis p.330 (17.5)
- Most 6 F guides accommodate two balloons for kissing: oxford p.258
- Access: radial or femoral acceptable; femoral may provide optimal support for highly complex cases: brilakis p.295 (16.2.4)
- Views separating MV and SB to facilitate wiring, stent placement and stent crush: brilakis p.295 (16.2.6)
- Wire both MV and SB in most cases, unless the SB is too small or has no significant disease; workhorse wires preferred; monitor distal wire positions (perforation); polymer-jacketed wires replaced for workhorse before preparation and stenting: brilakis p.296 (16.2.8); exchange after crossing (8.6.2.9) and keep multiple wires organised with a towel, wire on top of screen on top of towel: brilakis p.154 (8.6.2.9, 8.6.2.10, Fig. 8.4)
- SB preparation avoided in provisional (dissection necessitating stenting); SB and MV routinely prepared when two-stent techniques planned: brilakis p.296 (16.2.9)
- Imaging: size and length of MV and SB lesions, need for atherectomy or other preparation: brilakis p.296; benefit greater in complex lesions including complex bifurcations: brilakis p.231 (13); diameter and length, calcification, expansion, complications such as substent wiring: brilakis p.325 (16.2.13); final IVUS or OCT mandatory for LM, in both LAD and circumflex; minimum areas per segment (Fig. 17.3): brilakis p.333 (taught with numbers in cp-left-main)
- Imaging now a guideline recommendation: ESC CCS 2024 recommends intravascular imaging for anatomically complex lesions, in particular left main and true bifurcations: ref esc-ccs-2024 (as already taught in physiology-imaging.ts and pm-imaging evidence); OCTOBER 1,201 complex bifurcations, 2-year MACE 10.1% OCT vs 14.1% angiography: ref october-2023 (PMID 37634149); DKCRUSH VIII 555 patients, DEFINITION-complex, DK crush 96.8%, 1-year TVF 6.1% IVUS vs 14.7% angiography (HR 0.40): ref gao-dkcrush8-2026 (PMID 41914946, abstract read). 'Plan imaging for every two-stent case' is the module's advice derived from these.
- Physiology: baseline severity, final result, SB compromise: brilakis p.325 (16.2.12)
- Anticoagulation similar to standard PCI; prolonged DAPT considered with low bleeding risk because bifurcation stenting associated with increased stent thrombosis: brilakis p.295 (16.2.3); DAPT default about 6 months after elective PCI: ref esc-ccs-2024, as taught in pci-core.ts lesson pci-pharm-dapt-duration (line 675)
- Haemodynamic support for complex LM bifurcation stenting with depressed LV function, especially with occluded or severely diseased RCA: brilakis p.327 (16.2.14)
- Atherectomy: only atherectomy wire in treated artery; other wire traditionally removed to prevent fracture; rewiring after dissection of non-wired vessel can be challenging: brilakis p.296
- Guide extension over atherectomy wire only (lesion in proximal SB or distal MV): 7 Fr guide + 6 Fr extension for 1.25 mm burr or 1.25 mm orbital crown; 8 Fr + 7 Fr extension for 1.5 mm burr: brilakis p.296
- Small microcatheter over non-atherectomy wire, moved slowly during atherectomy runs so the same portion is not damaged, needs 8 Fr guide; Fig. 16.3 microcatheter perforated during orbital atherectomy; leave SB wire accepting low risk; laser atherectomy: brilakis p.296–297
- Check 2 (atherectomy arrangement): brilakis p.296
- Pitfalls: V-stenting only Medina 0.1.1 (p.319); SKS not elective (p.295, p.322); 7 Fr for V and SKS (p.295); SB preparation in planned two-stent (p.296); polymer-jacketed wire exchange and wire organisation (p.296, p.154)
- Pearl: bail-out by angle (TAP at 70°–90°; reverse crush or culotte <70°; inverted DK crush if occluded and not rewirable): brilakis p.293, p.303–304; naming the bail-out in advance is marked in the text as the module's advice
- Trifurcating left main: rare; single stent without significant SB disease; any two-stent technique with SB involvement, consider final three-balloon kiss ('trissing') to preserve the polygon of confluence; 7 or 8 Fr guides: brilakis p.332 (17.10); taught in full in cp-left-main
- 'Where the books differ', final kiss: Oxford final simultaneous kiss advisable regardless of technique, to ensure expansion and shape the carina, shown to improve outcomes: oxford p.258; Brilakis kissing after provisional not always necessary: brilakis p.302; no clear benefit of routine kissing after provisional LM stenting: brilakis p.332; V-stenting and SKS step 2 'simultaneous stent deployment with kissing balloon inflation': brilakis p.322, p.324
- 'Where the books differ', DES: restenosis high because of the amount of metal; DES may reduce restenosis but may increase stent thrombosis risk by more than 5% when more than one DES is deployed: oxford p.260 (flagged in the text as older data)
- 'Evidence for provisional' synthesis sentence marked as the module's synthesis; provisional preferred for most LM bifurcations without significant SB involvement, two-stent (especially DK crush) for true LM bifurcations: brilakis p.332; EBC MAIN authors: provisional should remain the default: ref ebc-main-2021
- Sim task (pci-bench distal left main case, stent sized to LAD, left main POT 1:1): POT ball sized to proximal MV after stent sized to distal MV: brilakis p.298–299, p.186 (10.2); lab capability confirmed in src/simulation/pciBench.ts
- Videos: titles only (HdtEEK33YVA "16.1 Bifurcation PCI step-by-step"; S61z5nfoLuc Hanna "Bifurcation Left main part 2: case studies (2-stent techniques)")

## Lesson cp-bifurcation-two-stent-dk-crush

- DK crush multiple steps, cumbersome, difficulties at each step; best data; superior to culotte (DKCRUSH-III) and provisional (DKCRUSH-V) in LM; MV wire access at all times, little risk of SB loss, important with dissections: brilakis p.311
- Goal SB coverage and MV expansion; preparation: both wired and prepared (balloon and/or atherectomy): brilakis p.311
- "Double kiss" = extra kiss before the MV stent, absent in classic crush: derived from comparing brilakis p.314–317 (two kisses) with oxford p.259 (classic crush: one kiss after MV stent)
- Oxford skeleton: SB stented first protruding into proximal main branch; balloon kept in proximal main branch crushes it; rewire, first kiss, MV stent; POT, rewire, second final kiss: oxford p.260
- Step 1: MV balloon 1:1 with distal MV, in MV at or distal to SB origin: brilakis p.312
- Step 2: SB stent protruding 2–3 mm; long enough to cover SB lesion; longer protrusion OK, shorter may give suboptimal coverage requiring another stent; delivery failure: additional SB or MV preparation (standard, modified balloon, atherectomy) or support (SB anchoring with anchor balloon in MV, guide extension): brilakis p.312
- Step 3: deploy at 10–12 atm (not higher, distal edge dissection), then balloon pulled slightly back protruding into MV and inflated at 20 atm: brilakis p.312
- Step 4: remove SB balloon (SB wire remains in Fig. 16.44): brilakis p.313
- Step 5: angiography of SB; additional SB PCI for underexpansion or distal dissection; failure to optimise may hinder rewiring: brilakis p.313
- Step 6: crush by inflating MV balloon; could jail SB wire, most operators remove it; MV balloon sized to proximal MV should not pass carina (distal MV dissection); alternatively sized to distal MV then POT: brilakis p.313
- Check (crush balloon position): brilakis p.313
- Step 7: rewire SB through crushed stent through proximal strut, then remove jailed SB wire: brilakis p.313 (Fig. 16.47); rationale (proximal cell so balloon pushes crushed stent to carina; distal strut risks moving it away), stated for mini-crush "similar to DK crush": brilakis p.291 (Fig. 15.30)
- Rewiring failure options: secondary bend, hydrophilic or polymer-jacketed wire, reversed (hairpin) wire; standard microcatheter (support, tip change without losing position), angulated microcatheter, dual lumen microcatheter: brilakis p.314
- Step 8: balloon into SB; if it will not advance: small balloon (1.0 mm example), anchor in MV, specialised balloon (product named), microcatheter track, rewire SB: brilakis p.314–315
- Step 9: two-step kiss: SB at 20 atm, then both at 10–12 atm; original MV balloon may be reused depending on proximal MV and SB balloon sizes (Finet's law): brilakis p.314
- Step 10: remove SB and MV balloons, may or may not leave SB wire: brilakis p.315; figures show SB wire left and jailed by MV stent, removed after second rewiring: brilakis p.315 (Fig. 16.52), p.317 (Fig. 16.55)
- Finet's relation applied to kissing balloons: effective diameter 0.678 × (d1 + d2), should be similar to proximal MV diameter: brilakis p.302 (Brilakis's application); the coefficient comes from native vessel diameters in ref finet-fractal-2008; example arithmetic (0.678 × 5.5 = 3.73) is the module's own
- Why two-step: Fig. 16.50 (double layer partially cleared by one-step, more fully by two-step; the module says 'removes some' and 'removes more'): brilakis p.315; 53% vs 33% ostial strut coverage: ref ormiston-crush-microct-2008
- Check (proximal vs distal strut in first rewiring): brilakis p.313, p.291, p.300–301 (distal strut after MV stenting), p.316 (second DK rewiring distal)
- Step 11: deliver and deploy MV stent: brilakis p.315; sized to distal MV: brilakis p.298, p.186 (10.2)
- Step 12: assess MV, additional distal PCI may be needed: brilakis p.316
- Step 13: POT if proximal MV significantly larger; balloon 1:1 to proximal MV, distal edge at carina; inflate, remove; why: expansion and apposition, and SB rewire not underneath MV stent: brilakis p.316
- Step 14: rewire SB through MV stent through distal strut (in contrast to first rewiring), remove jailed SB wire if left; failure: options as in step 7: brilakis p.316–317
