## Lesson pm-acquiring-test-nitrate

- Engagement step 9 hands over to chapter 6 — brilakis p.121 (5.9)
- Eight steps of chapter 6: waveform, test injection, IC nitroglycerin, position patient/receptor/shields/operator, pressure and ECG, cineangiography, pressure and ECG after, interpretation — brilakis p.123–128 (6.1–6.8)
- Step 1 goal: tip free and coaxial, not obstructing flow, lumen clear of thrombus, plaque, air — brilakis p.123 (6.1)
- Test injection goal: confirm ostial (or graft) engagement; coaxial to avoid tenting, which could lead to dissection (the module no longer ties this to the full run) — brilakis p.123 (6.2.1)
- Test injection may be skipped when certain from catheter movement and 'jump' — brilakis p.123 (6.2.1)
- 1–2 mL through a catheter cleared of thrombus, air, debris — brilakis p.123 (6.2.2) (PDF image checked: "1−2 mL")
- Small test injections of a few millilitres confirm radiographic positioning before acquisition — oxford p.159
- Test injection alerts to super-selective engagement (variant anatomy such as short LMS, or over-engagement) — oxford p.180
- Short/absent LMS: JL selectively engages LAD; a catheter one size larger normally engages Cx — oxford p.180
- Selective engagement in RCA usually involves conus; VF if large volume injected — oxford p.180; conus injection can cause VF — oxford p.156–157
- Test injections help identify anomalous origins and unusual take-off — oxford p.155
- Short left main: a catheter deep in the LAD can completely obstruct the Cx ostium with a pseudonormal trace when the catheter has side holes; side-hole guides not used to engage the left main except LM CTO — brilakis p.330 (17.5, Fig 17.1)
- Dissection causes: deep/subintimal/non-coaxial intubation, catheter at edge of or under eccentric plaque especially LM, ostial lesion, forceful injection (usually with suboptimal engagement), dampened waveform; prevention: non-dampened waveform, coaxial placement, gentle manual injection; treatment: stop injecting, gentle retraction, wire true lumen (or re-enter with CTO techniques), stent, imaging — brilakis p.123–124 (6.2.3.1)
- Nitroglycerin goal: prevent/correct spasm that could be read as fixed lesions; optimise balloon and stent sizing — brilakis p.124 (6.3.1)
- Oxford rationale: vasodilatation; differentiate spasm and obstructive lesions particularly ostial; true luminal diameter for stent sizing — oxford p.173
- Withhold if pressure low, suspected hypovolaemia, RV infarction, recent PDE-5 inhibitor — brilakis p.124 (6.3.3.1 prevention)
- Do not give nitroglycerin in HOCM: it worsens LV outflow obstruction by lowering preload and afterload — brilakis p.42 (3.2.1.3.1)
- Avoid arterial and venous dilators, including nitrates, in resting or provocable LVOT obstruction — ref esc-cardiomyopathy-2023 (PMID 37622657; already in refs.ts, verified). Severe aortic stenosis is not added as a nitrate caution: neither manual nor a checked reference states it.
- Units: the module writes µg (the manual's mcg) to match 04-lab-drugs and 09-interpreting.
- Dose ladder: >120 mmHg 200 mcg; 100–120 mmHg 100 mcg; <100 mmHg 50 mcg or none (concern for worsening hypotension); range 50–200 mcg IC — brilakis p.124 (6.3.2) (PDF image checked)
- Oxford: GTN or ISDN repeated IC boluses 100–250 micrograms (written 100–250 µg in the module); maximal vasodilatation may take 1–2 minutes; GTN spray 1–2 puffs sublingual — oxford p.173
- Routes: manifold, or introducer needle through the Y-connector ('nitro on a stick'); sublingual 0.4 mg — brilakis p.124 (6.3.2)
- Wait 1–2 minutes; vasodilatation can take up to 2 minutes; BP drop common — brilakis p.124 (6.3.2)
- Hypotension treatment: normal saline (often prophylactic), observe, vasopressor such as phenylephrine in severe cases — brilakis p.125 (6.3.3.1)
- Routine before angiography unless contraindication such as hypotension — brilakis p.128 (6.6.3.4 item 1)
- Nitroglycerin after engagement before angiography as a spasm prevention measure; causes of spasm: deep guide engagement, aggressive manipulation, instrumentation; treatment IC nitroglycerin 100–300 mcg repeated, IC calcium channel blockers — brilakis p.444 (25.2.5)
- Aorto-ostial lesions: nitroglycerin before angiography to exclude spasm — brilakis p.274 (15.1.6)
- Some operators routinely give nitrates to all; sublingual spray or infusion before procedure; often given to treat/exclude spasm; oral nitrates no bar; IV infusion response may be blunted — oxford p.173
- Spasm may come from disease or procedure; catheter-tip spasm after ostial engagement, ventricularises then falls, rising only after withdrawal; commonest in non-dominant RCA and vessels of calibre similar to the catheter — oxford p.173
- IC nitrate after careful re-engagement discriminates spasm vs ostial disease — oxford p.173; withdraw the catheter and give nitrates if required — oxford p.180. Sequence used everywhere in the module: withdraw, re-engage carefully with a free trace, give IC nitrate, wait 1–2 min, repeat the same view. Oxford's own wording is only nitrate after careful re-engagement; the 'only to the ostium' and 'confirm a free trace' points are the module's safety points, attributed to brilakis p.123 (6.1 step 1, tip free and coaxial) and oxford p.180 (over-engagement causes spasm), not to oxford p.173 (round 2).
- Catheter-tip spasm commoner in shorter patients with low body weight; pressure declines a few seconds after engagement; over-engagement of diagnostic catheters, especially RCA — oxford p.173, p.180
- Spasm with stiff wires during PCI; cocaine/amphetamine (may need IC verapamil); rarely contrast reaction; spasm can mimic severe stenosis; trial of IC nitrate; withdrawal may relieve; acquire image documenting relief — oxford p.174
- Fig 6.15: mid-RCA spasm after cocaine, after IC GTN non-obstructive plaque — oxford p.174
- Nitroprusside sometimes for intractable spasm with CCB such as verapamil — oxford p.173
- Spasm not receding after nitroglycerin: OCT or IVUS — brilakis p.124 (6.3.2)
- Ergonovine provocation now rarely performed (risk of intractable spasm and MI) — oxford p.180
- Current practice: invasive coronary function testing with intracoronary acetylcholine for suspected vasospastic angina in ANOCA/INOCA — ref esc-ccs-2024 (PMID 39210710) and ref kunadian-inoca-2020 (PMID 32626906), both already in refs.ts and verified; PubMed metadata rechecked 2026-10-07. Taught in the coronary physiology module.
- Check items: nitrate wait 1–2 min and 100 mcg band — brilakis p.124; JL one size larger — oxford p.180; OCT/IVUS for persisting spasm — brilakis p.124
- Videos: titles only (Y3bEFuzTpB8 "How to perform coronary angiography"; 6HmNE36hBec "Nitro on a stick")

## Lesson pm-acquiring-setup

- Step 4 goal: excellent cine images, lowest dose for patient and operator — brilakis p.125 (6.4.1)
- Table as high as possible while comfortable and ergonomic; receptor as close as possible; shields between patient and operator; operator far from patient and tube, manifold tubing extension — brilakis p.125 (6.4.2)
- Usual initial projections: LM AP; LAD RAO with some caudal and AP cranial; RCA LAO with cranial; SVG and radial grafts LAO; IM grafts AP — brilakis p.125 (6.4.2)
- Poor image quality causes (large patient, excessive angulation, receptor too far, density differences), prevention (shallower angulation, collimate, filters), treatment (shallower angulation, change frame rate and pulse rate to increase dose and penetration) — brilakis p.125 (6.4.3.1)
- Patient injury from excessive receptor movement; sensors; operator injury from not moving out of trajectory — brilakis p.125–126 (6.4.4)
- C-arm: tube under the patient, detector above — oxford p.31–32
- Tube parts: cathode (tungsten coil), anode rotating tungsten disc, kV accelerates electrons, X-rays plus heat, rotation dissipates heat and causes whirring; collimators lead panels — oxford p.31
- kV, mA, filtration (aluminium or copper); higher photon energy less absorption (dose) but lower image contrast — oxford p.31
- Table 1.1: kV + photons + energy; mA + photons = energy; filtration − photons + energy — oxford p.32. The table's 'practical effect' cells now state only these signs and Oxford's rule that higher energy lowers absorption and image contrast (no noise or low-energy-photon claims).
- Dose rate control modulates mA and kV to keep detector dose constant, image steady when angles change — oxford p.31
- Image intensifier (old: X-ray→light→electrons→light→camera) and flat panel (X-ray→light→electrons, digital image) — oxford p.32
- Optimisation definition and equipment/technique strategies (dose rate control, shallow angles, kV/mA, pulsed fluoroscopy, acquisition; patient far from generator, close to detector, collimation) — oxford p.33
- Raising the table lowers entrance/skin dose: X-ray source further from patient reduces skin dose — oxford p.37; Fig 28.6 Panel B (lower table, detector close) about 40% higher entrance dose rate; Panel C (low table, raised detector) skin dose 260%, image 40–50% larger; 3 Gy → 4.2 Gy / 7.8 Gy — brilakis p.492 (28.2.2.4.5 figure). Mechanism as taught: panels A and C have the same 110 cm tube-to-detector distance; in C the skin is closer to the tube (Oxford p.37: source further from the patient lowers skin dose) and the raised detector magnifies. Dose rate control (oxford p.31) keeps the image steady, so the penalty is not visible; the module no longer says that dose rate control 'pushes more dose' for the low-table geometry. For raising the detector alone (setup check 2), increased tube-to-detector distance with constant detector dose is the stated reasoning (oxford p.31, p.37 keep the intensifier close).
- Steep angles >30° from AP: significantly higher radiation, automatic dose increase often not recognised by the operator; AP includes spine so slightly angulated views preferable; RAO less operator exposure, challenging for RCA wiring, excellent for mid LAD — brilakis p.491 (28.2.2.4.4)
- Image intensifier finished moving before screening — oxford p.36
- Fixed shields close to the patient and not too high — brilakis p.493 (28.2.2.5.3)
- Inverse square law: 1 m to 2 m reduces exposure by factor four — oxford p.36
- Low magnification less radiation — brilakis p.491 (28.2.2.4.2); magnification increases skin dose rate — oxford p.37
- Collimation reduces skin area and DAP, not total air kerma; lessens overlapping exposures; caveat: equipment outside field (wire tip, guide tip) — brilakis p.491 (28.2.2.4.3)
- Reposition image intensifier often — brilakis p.492 (28.2.2.4.6); vary PCI view to avoid skin burn, skin dosimeter — oxford p.209
- Monitor air kerma and DAP continuously; usually stop if AK exceeds 5–7 Gy; air kerma >15 Gy sentinel event; dose rate tracked — brilakis p.39 (2.5) (PDF image checked: "5−7 Gray")
- Treatment thresholds: >5 Gy air kerma follow-up within a month with skin examination documented, back inspected; >10 Gy qualified physicist promptly calculates peak skin dose, skin examined at 2–4 weeks; Joint Commission: peak skin dose >15 Gy sentinel event, risk management and regulators contacted within 24 hours — brilakis p.493 (28.2.3)
- UK: IR(ME)R 2017; dose greater than expected → contact the local radiation protection adviser — oxford p.36
- Dose rate >20 mGy/min prompts change — brilakis p.492 (28.2.2.4.7)
- Consider stopping at 7–8 Gy and staging; announce dose every 1 Gy and/or every 30 min — brilakis p.492 (28.2.2.4.8). The 5–7 vs 7–8 Gy difference is between Brilakis's own chapters; the lesson states both and gives our synthesis ("between 5 and 8 Gy a deliberate decision"), labelled as such.
- Coronary angiogram typical effective dose 6 mSv; equivalent background 2.7 years; additional lifetime risk of fatal cancer 1 in 3500; normal lifetime cancer risk about one in three; estimates from nuclear bomb survivors and nuclear workers — oxford p.37 (Table 1.2 and text)
- Planning: renal failure — limit contrast via biplane, fewer cine projections, IVUS, contrast-saving systems; consider iso-osmolar contrast; hydration 1–3 mL/kg/h normal saline pre and post; prior radiation skin injury — limit runs, keep affected area out of beam — brilakis p.35 (1.6) (PDF image checked: "1−3 mL/kg/h")
- ESC/EACTS regimen in moderate to severe CKD: isotonic saline 1 mL/kg/h 12 h before and 24 h after, 0.5 mL/kg/h if LVEF ≤35% or overt heart failure — ref esc-revasc-2018 (PMID 30165437; already in refs.ts, verified; same regimen as foundations lesson in src/content/tracks/foundations.ts). 70 kg × 3 mL/kg/h = 210 mL/h is arithmetic.
- Setup check 2: fluoroscopy 6–7.5 fps halves the dose vs 15 fps — brilakis p.491; collimation reduces skin area and DAP but not total air kerma — brilakis p.491
- Pearl: Fig 28.6, up to 2.6 times the skin dose from geometry — brilakis p.492 (no ranking of measures claimed)
- Videos: titles only (CuPjSsGRREM "28.2 Preventing radiation-related complications"; b612bxTygFg "How to reduce radiation dose during cardiac catheterization")

## Lesson pm-acquiring-cine

- Step 6 goal: high-quality cine of each artery in orthogonal projections, minimising overlap — brilakis p.126 (6.6.1)
- Pulsed fluoroscopy: real time, lower exposure, typically 7.5 fps, lower quality, for scouting, wiring, balloon and stent delivery; modern systems store seconds of fluoroscopy — oxford p.33
- Cine: higher dose and frame rate, usually 12.5–30 fps (7.5 fps at Oxford Heart Centre); high resolution, low noise; number and length of runs usually the main source of patient exposure; use sparingly — oxford p.33
- Fluoroscopy 6–7.5 fps halves dose vs 15 fps; even 3.75 fps in thin patients — brilakis p.491 (28.2.2.4.1) (PDF image checked: "6−7.5 fps")
- Cine ≈10× fluoroscopy dose, not reflected in fluoroscopy time; fluoro store instead of cine for balloon and stent inflations — brilakis p.490 (28.2.2.3.4) (PDF image checked: "≈10×")
- Low frame-rate acquisitions reduce dose at the expense of quality; uses include long interventional procedures or pregnancy — oxford p.37
- Storing the unexpected (e.g. air bubble): ask radiographer to store the loop before pressing the screening pedal again — oxford p.159
- Catheter position on screen: RAO cranial tip upper left; LAO caudal (spider) in the middle; minimal or no panning — brilakis p.126 (6.6.2 step 1)
- Step 5 pressure and ECG before run (no damping, ventricularisation, new hypotension; ECG changes) — brilakis p.126 (6.5); step 7 after the run (damping, hypotension, ST, rhythm, VF) — brilakis p.128 (6.7)
- No air in syringe or tubing; tilting the distal end of the syringe up reduces embolisation risk — oxford p.159; syringe held vertical to prevent bubble embolisation — brilakis p.126 (6.6.2 step 3)
- Deep inspiration to pull diaphragm down and keep heart still; some views (LAO cranial for distal RCA) at end expiration — oxford p.159
- Deep breaths, especially in radial procedures, cause catheter disengagement during injection — brilakis p.127 (6.6.3.2)
- Press pedal, inject after 1–2 seconds (to see prior stent, calcium, contrast clearing); fully opacify with reflux into aorta — brilakis p.126 (6.6.2 steps 2–3) (PDF image checked: "1−2 seconds")
- Typical volumes 5–10 mL LCA, 3–5 mL RCA, grafts larger; streaming from inadequate speed/pressure; fill for 3 beats with reflux into root — oxford p.180
- Volume and rate vary with artery size, patient size, coexisting pathology (aortic valve disease); tiny amount for small non-dominant and occluded arteries — oxford p.159
- Panning if needed for branches, distal vessel, collaterals; best avoided as it degrades image, especially in dual injection for CTO — brilakis p.126 (6.6.2 step 4); radiographer pans to follow distal vessels; keep focus on point of interest — oxford p.159
- Continue until all branches (and collateral branches if occlusion) opacified; avoid long acquisitions except delayed collateral filling — brilakis p.127 (6.6.2 step 5); prolonged acquisition for collaterals and myocardial blush — oxford p.159
- Refill syringe at end of injection — brilakis p.127 (6.6.2 step 6)
- What can go wrong during cine: spasm, air embolisation, thrombus or other debris embolisation, dissection — brilakis p.128 (6.6.3.4)
- Inability to fill: causes (4 French, side holes, large/ectatic arteries, poor engagement, high flow — severe AS, HOCM, DCM, AV fistula — iso-osmolar more viscous, weak injection, high LVEDP); remedies (larger lumen: guide or larger diagnostic; avoid side holes; stronger injection, automated injector, manual preferred with slight damping); declare non-diagnostic rather than risk erroneous interpretation — brilakis p.127 (6.6.3.1)
- Smaller catheters need more force; autoinjectors help; 4–6 F diagnostic catheters — oxford p.153
- Catheter disengages during injection: causes, prevention (coaxial, start softly then increase), treatment (re-engage, other catheter, wire anchor, guide extension, ramp up slowly) — brilakis p.127 (6.6.3.2)
- Unable to visualise segments: causes and remedies (filling, larger catheter, at least two orthogonal projections) — brilakis p.127–128 (6.6.3.3)
- Avoid prolonged contrast injections with left dominance or total RCA occlusion (hypotension); stated in the left main engagement section, so taught as prolonged left main injections — brilakis p.330 (17.5)
- Contrast not clearing → VF/VT, disengage; avoid multiple acquisitions at aorto-ostial lesions; non-selective angiography for severe damping — brilakis p.274–275 (15.1.6)
- Prior CABG: long cine runs without panning for collaterals — brilakis p.339 (18.6.1); prolonged runs clarify distal coronary filling through long SVGs supplying a large territory — brilakis p.339 (18.6.2); non-selective proximal subclavian angiography before selective IMA angiography to identify subclavian stenosis (steal), or compare subclavian with aortic pressure — brilakis p.339 (18.6.3); graft angiography in full in cp-prior-cabg; CTO collaterals: low magnification, breath hold, without panning — brilakis p.391 (21.6.4); most CTO PCI requires dual injection — brilakis p.390 (21.6)
- Review each stored loop after the case for complications; reassess quantification — oxford p.339
- Check items: streaming definition and volumes, 'makes stenosis quantification difficult' (taught as difficult to grade reliably) — oxford p.180; 4 F and ectatic arteries, side holes listed as a cause without a mechanism (none is given in the module) — brilakis p.127
- Panning: the module now gives both positions (Oxford pans to follow distal vessels but keeps the focus on a point of interest — oxford p.159; Brilakis avoids panning as it degrades the image — brilakis p.126); the opening paragraph says panning degrades the image rather than calling the run non-diagnostic
- Videos: titles only (Y3bEFuzTpB8; XxUGDCim5sg "Coronary angiography tips")

## Lesson pm-acquiring-views

- AP naming — brilakis p.125, p.127 (6.4, 6.6); PA naming — oxford p.159–161. Both describe the same frontal view; the tube is under the patient and the detector above — oxford p.31–32, p.36

- Spine on left = RAO; on right = LAO — oxford p.179
- Proximal vessels caudal, distal vessels cranial — oxford p.159
- Initial projections — brilakis p.125 (6.4.2); complete orthogonal sets for LM, LAD, RCA, SVG/radial grafts, IM grafts — brilakis p.127 (6.6.2 step 7)
- Table 6.1 ostium/main vessel views (LMS; LAD/diagonal; Cx/intermediate/OM; RCA) — oxford p.160 (PDF image checked)
- Oxford University Hospitals sequence (LCA intubate in PA; six views with angles; RCA intubate in LAO; four views) — oxford p.160 (PDF image checked)
- What each illustrated view shows (figure captions 6.3–6.14) — oxford p.161–172
- LMS imaging in PA or LAO — oxford p.155; RCA imaging LAO — oxford p.156; TIG radial catheter (Oxford names it Tiger, abbreviated TIG; the module uses the curve name only): PA for left, LAO for RCA — oxford p.157
- Standard set may not suffice; avoid overlap and foreshortening; straight LAO/RAO inadequate especially for LCA — oxford p.180
- Orthogonal projections of each segment; grade where worst and least foreshortened — brilakis p.130 (6.8.2.2)
- At least two projections preferably orthogonal, especially eccentric stenoses; oval lesion severe in one view, normal in another — oxford p.175, p.179
- Not imaged at 90°: lesion appears shorter and more severe; avoid overlap; separate views for proximal and distal limits; PCI angiography defines length, reference diameter, complexity features — oxford p.209
- Table 8.1 recommended PCI views — oxford p.210
- Least foreshortened ostial views (RCA steep LAO ± caudal; LM LAO caudal and LAO cranial; right SVG LAO; left SVG RAO; LIMA/RIMA AP shallow cranial); prior CT gives optimal angle — brilakis p.275 (15.1.6)
- Limits of LM angiography: usually adequate for ≥70% diameter stenosis; significant interobserver variability for 30–70% lesions, no reference segment (short, diffusely diseased LM); functional assessment or intravascular imaging essential for intermediate LM lesions — brilakis p.330–331 (17.6)
- Complete LAD set added to the tip and keypoint: AP or RAO cranial, RAO caudal — brilakis p.127 (6.6.2 step 7b)
- LM bifurcation best in RAO caudal, AP caudal, LAO caudal; LAO cranial or AP cranial most reliable for aorto-ostial stent edge; LM ostium usually oval, orthogonal views important — brilakis p.331 (17.6)
- Working angle shallow (>30° from AP higher dose) — brilakis p.491; vary view in long PCI — oxford p.209

## Lesson pm-acquiring-anatomy

- LMS from left sinus, bifurcates into LAD and Cx, occasionally trifurcates with intermediate; LAD in interventricular groove, anterior wall, diagonals anterolateral, septals anterior two-thirds septum and left bundle, terminal LAD apex and often part of inferior wall; Cx posterior in left AV groove, posterior wall, OMs lateral wall; RCA from right sinus, right AV groove, acute marginals (RV), conus (RVOT), AV node branch, sinus node branch in 60%; terminates after the crux as posterior ventricular branch and PDA supplying inferior LV wall and inferior third of septum — oxford p.151
- Dominance by vessel supplying PDA: RCA 85%, Cx 8%, co-dominance/balanced 7% — oxford p.151
- PDA and posterolateral branches belong to the RCA in right dominance and to the Cx in left dominance — brilakis p.271 (15.1)
- Cx closest to spine; spine rule; diagonals vs septals by direction; OMs, terminal marginal, AV Cx; RV marginal branches pass anteriorly towards the septum, supply RV — oxford p.179
- Normal proximal LAD calibre 3.5–4.0 mm men, 3.0–3.5 mm women; LAD tapers; Cx tapers from mid portion usually after first OM; RCA uniform until the crux — oxford p.179
- Distal RCA/PDA seen in LAO cranial and PA cranial — oxford p.171–172
- Tiny amount of contrast for small non-dominant vessels — oxford p.159
- Collateral filling suggests occlusion or high-grade stenosis — brilakis p.133 (6.8.2.11)
- Catheter-tip spasm commonest in non-dominant RCA, similar calibre, shorter low-weight patients — oxford p.173; pressure declines a few seconds after engagement; over-engagement of diagnostic catheters especially in the RCA — oxford p.180
- Dominance check: co-dominance (7%) excluded because the RCA gives no PDA in the stem — oxford p.151 (neither manual defines co-dominance further)
- Prolonged injections hypotension in left dominance or RCA occlusion — brilakis p.330 (17.5)
- IC adenosine >100 mcg avoided in RCA or dominant Cx (VF; treatment defibrillation) — brilakis p.224 (12.2.6.4.2)
- Atherectomy of RCA or dominant Cx: temporary pacemaker, aminophylline, or brief test runs — brilakis p.360 (19.9.4), p.365 (19.9.5.7.4)
- SYNTAX: dominance step; segment weights 0.5 (posterolateral branch) to 6 (LM in left dominance); codominance not an option; segment numbers per dominance diagram (right: RCA 1, 2, 3, 4, 16, 16a–c; left: RCA 1–3, Cx adds 15; left system 5–14b in both) — brilakis p.141 (Fig 7.2, PDF image checked)
- Segment names (proximal/mid/distal RCA, PDA, posterolateral, left main, proximal/mid/apical LAD, first/second diagonal, proximal/distal Cx, intermediate, obtuse marginals, left posterolateral, left PDA) — ref sianos-syntax-score-2005 (already in refs.ts, verified; same scheme taught in the foundations lesson fnd-cor-segments)
- Missing territory: re-review the other injection (Fig 6.19 shows the anomalous Cx filling during the RCA injection — oxford p.182) and aortography to discern the anomalous origin and proximal course — oxford p.182
- Anomalous vessel (commonly Cx) overlooked when an area of myocardium is unsupplied; separate LAD/Cx ostia and anterior RCA origin are normal variants — oxford p.181; test injections help identify anomalous origins — oxford p.155
- Step 8 parameters list (TIMI flow, lesions, length, thrombus, calcification, dissection, branch involvement, ostial location within 3–5 mm, tortuosity, aneurysm, collaterals, perforation, fistulae) — brilakis p.128–135 (6.8)
- Cine runs cd-01135-LCA-5 (−34/−17) and cd-01135-RCA-7 (+26/+3), study labelled "left" dominance in datasets/library/dominance/index.json. Captions state only angles and the dataset label.
- Videos: titles only (3sGSkQ6hoQ0 "Case 56: Coronary anatomy assessment: core principles"; Rel5KYzYKew "Coronary artery anomalies: angiography, case illustrations, and management")
