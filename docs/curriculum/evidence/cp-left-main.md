## Lesson cp-left-main-assess (Assessing the left main)

- LM provides flow to most of the myocardium; flow compromise → hypotension, cardiogenic shock or arrest: brilakis p.329 (17 intro).
- Mortality risk of catheterisation rises with left main stenosis: oxford p.300 (Death).
- Severe LMS stenosis a relative contraindication to LV angiography (risk of hypotension): oxford p.117 (Ventriculography).
- Ostial, body and/or bifurcation disease; bifurcation involved in >80% of LM lesions undergoing PCI (refs 1, 2: EXCEL 5-year and EXCEL 2016); body easiest to treat: brilakis p.329.
- Aorto-ostial lesions fibrotic, calcified, rigid, prone to recoil, may need atherectomy; ischaemia on engagement: brilakis p.271 (15.1.1); damping common: p.271 (15.1.2).
- LMS occasionally trifurcates (intermediate branch): oxford p.151 (Coronary anatomy). Trifurcating LM rarely encountered; 7–8 Fr guides: brilakis p.332 (17.10).
- Circumflex is the side branch in most cases: brilakis p.332 (17.10).
- Planning questions (RCA disease/occlusion; RCA collaterals to left; LM protected by graft; severe calcification needing atherectomy); LV function, baseline haemodynamics, presentation, comorbidities; RHC for filling pressures, CO, need for support, monitoring: brilakis p.329 (17.1).
- Unprotected LM especially with occluded/diseased RCA as a high-risk lesion location: brilakis p.249 (14.1.2).
- Protected LMS as elective indication: oxford p.261. Side-hole guides allowed in protected LM, not in unprotected LM: brilakis p.272 (15.1.5).
- Atherectomy/prolonged ischaemia as support consideration in LM: brilakis p.333 (17.14).
- Check 1: avoid prolonged injections with left dominance or total RCA occlusion (hypotension): brilakis p.330 (17.5).
- Damping causes during LMS intubation (LMS stenosis, tip angulated into wall, spasm, selective LAD/Cx with short LMS): oxford p.155 (Catheter manipulation).
- Non-selective angiography for aorto-ostial lesions with severe dampening reduces dissection risk but images may be suboptimal: brilakis p.274 (15.1.6).
- Nitroglycerin before angiography to exclude spasm (aorto-ostial): brilakis p.274 (15.1.6).
- Avoid multiple acquisitions at aorto-ostial lesions; intravascular imaging can help; contrast not clearing → VF/VT, disengage: brilakis p.275 (15.1.6).
- LM ostium usually oval → orthogonal views: brilakis p.331 (17.6).
- Views: AP usual initial projection for LM ostium; full LM set AP, RAO caudal, RAO cranial, LAO cranial, LAO caudal (spider): brilakis p.125 and p.127 (6.4). Bifurcation RAO-caudal, AP-caudal, LAO-caudal; LAO-cranial or AP-cranial most reliable for proximal stent edge at the aorto-ostium: brilakis p.331 (17.6). Least foreshortening of LM ostium LAO caudal and LAO cranial; prior CT angle: brilakis p.275 (15.1.6). Confirm ostial LM stent in multiple views, especially LAO cranial: brilakis p.332 (17.10).
- Oxford Table 6.1: LMS ostium and main vessel PA, LAO, LAO caudal; intubate in PA; proximal vessels generally with caudal projection: oxford p.156, p.160 (Imaging the coronaries); LAO caudal shows LMS, proximal LAD (Fig 6.7): oxford p.165.
- Cine ac-161-3: AngioCAD index (datasets/library/angiocad/index.json) patient ac-161: LM 76–90%, Prox LCx 76–90%, STEMI, LVEF 25%; run ac-161-3 is an LCA run (frame viewed; projection not asserted in the caption).
- ≥50% diameter stenosis significant in LM, 70–90% non-LM: brilakis p.130 (6.8); >50% in LMS, >70% epicardial: oxford p.175 (Quantification of stenoses). Angiography may underestimate severity by up to 50% in extreme cases: oxford p.175.
- Hamilos (Brilakis 17.6 ref 4): 213 patients, FFR ≥0.80 non-surgical (138) vs <0.80 CABG (75); 5-year survival 89.8% vs 85.4%; 23% of <50% DS lesions significant by FFR: ref hamilos-lm-ffr-2009. LITRO (17.6 ref 5): ref litro-2011 as above.
- Angiography usually adequate for ≥70%; significant interobserver variability 30–70% (ref 3, CASS); due to lack of reference segment, short length, diffuse disease; functional or intravascular assessment essential for intermediate LM: brilakis p.330–331 (17.6).
- Oxford inter-observer variability around ±20% in moderate lesions: oxford p.175.
- Check 2 as above (p.330–331).
- IVUS and OCT useful for determining haemodynamic significance of LM (unlike non-LM): brilakis p.333 (17.13.1); MLA <6.0 mm² IVUS or <5.4 mm² OCT significant requiring revascularisation: brilakis p.333 (17.13.1) and p.231 (13.1.1). OCT ostial LM challenging, no OCT LM outcome data, cut-off differs between populations: brilakis p.231. OCT 5.4 mm² not prospectively validated: brilakis p.239, p.246. IVUS preferred to determine LM significance; OCT can be used for non-aorto-ostial: brilakis p.233 (Table 13.1).
- IVUS better for large vessels, LMS, aorto-ostial lesions (no contrast, deeper penetration): oxford p.238 (IVUS or OCT?). IVUS preferred over OCT for aorto-ostial because OCT needs blood clearance: brilakis p.287 (15.1.13).
- Korean cut-off 4.5 mm² (smaller arteries): brilakis p.333 (17.13.1). Park 2014: 112 patients, intermediate ostial and shaft LM, optimal IVUS MLA 4.5 mm² for FFR ≤0.80: ref park-lm-ivus-2014 (PMID 25147031 note).
- Slanted take-off: 2D IVUS area measurements interpreted with care in aorto-ostial lesions, especially for LM decisions: brilakis p.275 (15.1.7).
- Oxford IVUS before PCI to measure reference diameter and lesion length: oxford p.261.
- Damped trace features (round 2: reworded in the lesson's own structure) (systolic dip, ventricularised diastolic, pre-systolic deflection, slower upstroke, steeper downstroke, A waves); cannot aspirate → pull back until blood flows before injecting (air embolism): oxford p.155. Other causes of damping (intracatheter thrombus or debris, kinking, non-coaxial alignment, transducer malfunction): brilakis p.272 (15.1.5); stepwise algorithm (withdraw slightly, aspirate, check transducer, fluoroscopy for kink, remove catheter without aspirating or inserting wire, aspirate sheath): brilakis p.113 (Fig 5.16).
- Oxford views added: PA caudal shows Cx with ostium and body of LMS and proximal LAD (Fig 6.5, oxford p.163; Oxford makes no foreshortening claim, so the 'true ostium, least foreshortened' row now reads 'Not separately stated' and Fig 6.5 sits in the bifurcation row); LAO caudal LMS, proximal LAD, intermediate, proximal Cx (Fig 6.7); RAO caudal proximal Cx, LMS (Fig 6.9); Oxford LCA sequence (intubate PA; RAO shallow caudal, right lateral, RAO cranial, RAO steep caudal, LAO cranial, LAO caudal): oxford p.160. Academy cx-lm-assessment (src/content/tracks/pci-complex.ts) LAO straight first and caudal overlap with cusp: cross-reference only.
- Collaterals question: brilakis p.329 (17.1) lists it without explanation (the lesson now says so). Graft reduces myocardium at risk: definition of protected LMS, oxford p.261.
- Physiology: hyperaemic or resting indices; ostial LM disengage guide; both LAD and Cx with pullback to show where step-up occurs; normal in one vessel and not the other suggests LM not significant: brilakis p.227 (12.3.2) and p.332 (17.12.1). Adenosine IC then disengage, or IV: brilakis p.227 (12.3.1). Damping artificially raises indices: brilakis p.287 (15.1.12).
- Downstream stenosis lowers maximal flow, higher distal pressure, underestimates LM: brilakis p.332 (17.12.1).
- Resting/hyperaemic discordance ~14%, particularly in large arteries (LM, proximal LAD) and diabetes; decide on clinical presentation: brilakis p.228 (12.3.7). CHF with high RA pressure and LVEDP affects FFR; correction FFR = (Pd − Pv)/(Pa − Pv); best to avoid functional assessment: brilakis p.228 (12.3.6).
- Grey zone 4.5–6 mm² → consider invasive physiology; pullbacks from LAD and LCx, smallest MLA used (single pullback may oversize from oblique probe); IVUS or OCT both supported (OCTIVUS 264 LM patients, equivalent outcomes); IVUS favoured for ostial LM extension and advanced CKD: ref johnson-eapci-lm-imaging-2026.
- Check 3 option D no longer states a guide position for IVUS (the cited pages state disengagement only for physiology: brilakis p.227, p.287, p.332).
- Revascularisation may improve prognosis in LM stenosis >50%: brilakis p.140 (7.1.7). Fig 17.2 heart-team algorithm (prior CABG or not a good surgical candidate → PCI if benefits > risks, else no revascularisation; good candidate → CAD extent; SYNTAX <33 → PCI or CABG; ≥33 → CABG if benefits > risks, else PCI pathway; patient preference across outcomes): brilakis p.331, read from the PDF figure.
- Current European guidance (round 2: checked against the guideline's own Recommendation Table 23 and its table of changed recommendations, full text of esc-ccs-2024, Eur Heart J 2024;45:3415, DOI 10.1093/eurheartj/ehae177): in CCS patients at low surgical risk with significant LM stenosis, CABG is the overall preferred revascularisation mode over PCI, given lower spontaneous MI and repeat revascularisation (I A); low complexity (SYNTAX ≤22) with completeness equivalent to CABG: PCI recommended as an alternative to CABG, lower invasiveness and non-inferior survival (I A); intermediate complexity (SYNTAX 23–32) with the same condition: PCI should be considered (IIa A); 2018 row 'left main disease with high SYNTAX score (≥33), PCI' class III B is replaced in the changes table by 'in CCS patients at high surgical risk, PCI may be considered over medical therapy alone' (IIb B): ref esc-ccs-2024. The 2026 consensus summarises the IIb B as PCI for more complex disease when unsuitable for CABG: ref johnson-eapci-lm-imaging-2026 (introduction). Brilakis Table 7.1: left main SYNTAX 0–32 PCI or CABG, >32 CABG: brilakis p.141. Keypoint 8 updated accordingly.
- Oxford is the 2nd edition, 2020 (whole/oxford.txt front matter); its LMS section rests on BMS registry data and limited DES follow-up: oxford p.261.
- Five decision factors (presentation, anatomy and completeness, LV function, comorbidities such as diabetes, preference); SYNTAX ≥33 good surgical candidates usually benefit more from CABG; 0–22 and 23–32 similar; continued controversy, heart team with preference and comorbidities: brilakis p.331 (17.7). Table 7.1 LM 0–32 PCI or CABG, >32 CABG, refs [4,14], ref 4 = 2018 ESC/EACTS: brilakis p.141 and p.147; ref esc-revasc-2018.
- Oxford: elective LMS PCI controversial; elective indications (protected LMS, refusing CABG, unsuitable for CABG, suitable anatomy after informed discussion and consent, clinical trials); urgent (dissection/complications involving LMS during PCI; primary PCI for STEMI): oxford p.261.
- Table caption: both 5-year reports cited by Brilakis (17.7 refs 1 and 6, list p.334); EXCEL (1905) and NOBLE (1201) described as the two largest contemporary randomised LM trials.

## Lesson cp-left-main-technique (Left main PCI step by step)

- Monitoring: careful monitoring, early vasopressors (norepinephrine) for hypotension, defibrillation pads ideally placed: brilakis p.329 (17.2).
- Femoral or radial; femoral may be preferred in highly complex anatomy; 7 or 8 Fr guides more support, preferred for LM bifurcation: brilakis p.329 (17.4); allow V-stenting: brilakis p.330 (17.5).
- Highly complex aorto-ostial: femoral and 7–8 Fr better support but more damping: brilakis p.271 (15.1.4). Smaller (6 Fr) guides less likely to damp: brilakis p.272–273 (15.1.5).
- Most bifurcation techniques through 6 Fr except V-stenting and SKS (≥7 Fr); larger guide easier for two devices, more support: brilakis p.295 (16.2.5).
- Supportive guides EBU or XB for the left main: brilakis p.295 (16.2.5); 7–8 Fr XB or EBU for calcified LM: brilakis p.355 (19.5). EBU 3.0/3.5 for LAD, EBU 4.0/4.5 for circumflex: brilakis p.115 (5.7.2.1).
- Side-hole guides not for LM (except LM CTO); mask damping, profound ischaemia; short LM: guide can completely obstruct Cx ostium with seemingly normal pressure (Fig 17.1, side holes perfuse LAD): brilakis p.330 (17.5). Never in unprotected LM: brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3), p.485 (28.1.1.1), p.522 (30.2.5). Protected LM, RCA and grafts allowed; contrast exits through side holes: brilakis p.272 (15.1.5).
- Alternative: wire with non-selective guide engagement: brilakis p.271 (15.1.1). Damping prevention: small guides, avoid deep engagement, guide extension to back out the guide, floating wire, ostial positioning device; less aggressive guides (JR4 example) for isolated aorto-ostial disease, less support: brilakis p.272–273 (15.1.5). Round 2: the lesson now states that Brilakis's examples (JR4; IM for shepherd's crook RCA) are right coronary shapes in a general aorto-ostial section and that he names no left main curve for this purpose (brilakis p.273); cross-reference to cp-ostial.
- Side holes mask damping but still predispose to ostial dissection: brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3); hydraulic dissections can still occur upon injection: brilakis p.522 (30.2.5).
- Severe ostial LM: guidewire (often with balloon) advanced to tip of guide before engagement: brilakis p.330 (17.5); hit and run: brilakis p.271 (15.1.1).
- Careful pressure monitoring, prompt disengagement in case of damping before deterioration or vessel injury: brilakis p.330 (17.5).
- Intermittent disengagement; independent-hand technique; floating wire: brilakis p.330 (17.5). Disengage by torquing to non-coaxial rather than pulling (may lose wire): brilakis p.273 (15.1.5). Extra-support guidewires facilitate engagement/disengagement: brilakis p.273, p.275 (15.1.8).
- Avoid contrast injections while damped (LM ostial dissection catastrophic): brilakis p.330 (17.5); ostial dissection can propagate down the artery or into ascending aorta: brilakis p.273. Gentle manual injection, avoid automated injectors: brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3), p.273.
- Avoid prolonged injections with left dominance or occluded RCA: brilakis p.330.
- Check (side-hole, short LM): brilakis p.330, p.272.
- Wiring: caudal view to enter LAD or Cx from LM: brilakis p.153 (8). Wiring challenges ostial, bifurcation, trifurcation/quadrification: brilakis p.332 (17.8).
- Meticulous lesion preparation (balloon, plaque modification balloons, possibly atherectomy in heavily calcified): brilakis p.332 (17.9). Aorto-ostial: primary stenting avoided in most cases: brilakis p.282 (15.1.10.4 challenge 3); resistant to dilatation, recoil: p.276 (15.1.9).
- Aorto-ostial atherectomy: extra-support atherectomy wire; coaxial guide lowers perforation and aortic dissection risk and helps debulking; burr often activated at the guide tip with tension off (prevents jumping, entrapment); larger burrs may be required; orbital atherectomy unconstrained orbit if activated when not engaged: brilakis p.276 (15.1.9). Avoid damping (no-reflow/slow flow); burr advancement difficult through guides with significant bends such as AL: brilakis p.361 (19.9.5.2); extra-support wire for aorto-ostial: p.361 (19.9.5.3).
- Burrs: 1.5 mm preferred most times; ≥1.75 mm infrequently used except LM; burr:artery 0.5: brilakis p.360 (19.9.5.1); Table 19.1 1.25–1.75 mm ≥6 Fr, 2.0 mm ≥7 Fr, 2.15–2.25 mm ≥8 Fr: brilakis p.360.
- Attention to aortic pressure during LM balloon occlusions; brief inflations, especially without support; 70% saline/30% contrast speeds inflation/deflation: brilakis p.332 (17.9).
- Check (hypotension during inflation): brilakis p.332 (17.9), p.329 (17.2). Fluid sentence removed (no source); replaced with 'the cause is the occlusion itself, restore flow by deflating'.
- Tip pci-lm-hypotension-inflation: brilakis p.329, p.332; instability → device selection: brilakis p.250 (Fig 14.1), p.249 (14.1.1 hypotension or pulmonary oedema).
- Oxford multivessel PCI: order often operator preference; many tackle the most challenging lesion first, reserving CABG if unsuccessful, rather than leave partial revascularisation; operators may not tackle all lesions at one sitting: oxford p.255.
- Order: LAD/Cx lesions first if LM stenosis not critical; RCA first to improve safety: brilakis p.332 (17.10). Distal lesions before aorto-ostial: brilakis p.276 (15.1.10).
- Stent diameter to distal reference; proximal sizing risks distal dissection/perforation and carina shift; POT; LM: know stent expansion limits (Fig 10.1); peripheral stents for ≥6 mm vessels: brilakis p.186 (10.2.3.2.2).
- Stents from LM into LAD/Cx should be able to be expanded to LM diameter: brilakis p.332 (17.10).
- Oxford: IVUS before PCI for reference diameter and lesion length, after for deployment/expansion; visual underestimation → undersizing → restenosis or stent thrombosis; IVUS guidance eliminates: oxford p.261. Restenosis may occur if LMS not stented back to ostium: oxford p.261.
- Stents >8 mm at ostium (coverage, embolisation risk): brilakis p.280 (15.1.10.4).
- Short LM: stent may cross into LAD (provisional): brilakis p.332 (17.10).
- Steps: advance stent; disengage guide; counter forces, move as unit, push on wire: brilakis p.277–278 (15.1.10.1–2). Stent withdrawn to protrude 1–2 mm into aorta; guide just outside ostium; AP cranial for LM: brilakis p.278 (15.1.10.3). Breath hold, floating wire, stent edge vs markers: brilakis p.281. Indeflator connected: brilakis p.282.
- Flare: stent balloon withdrawn partially into aorta, high-pressure inflation to flare overhanging struts, re-engagement without distortion: brilakis p.282–283 (15.1.10.5).
- Overhang/recoil/miss (15.1.10.4): short (6–8 mm) oversized balloon or dedicated flaring balloon; consider prolonged DAPT; >50% protruding can sometimes be snared (risk of injury/embolisation): brilakis p.282. Recoil: stents with strong radial strength; postdilate and wait; peripheral stent inside: brilakis p.282; Fig 15.13 ostial LM recoil treated with a 5.0 × 12 mm renal stent: brilakis p.283 (caption ch15 extract). Missing-ostium prevention: breath hold, stent edge vs markers, floating wire, ostial positioning device, Szabo (deformation/loss), IVUS landmarking, rapid pacing; IVUS-guided deployment through 8 Fr: brilakis p.281.
- Re-engage over balloon; aligned; avoid forceful advancement: brilakis p.284–285 (15.1.10.6).
- Final: damping gone unless vessel ≤ guide size; reflux into aorta; leave short wire segment; IVUS for coverage and expansion: brilakis p.286 (15.1.10.7), p.287 (15.1.13). Imaging mandatory both branches: brilakis p.333 (17.13.2).
- Check (missed ostium): geographic miss underdiagnosed by angiography because balloon shoulder treats true ostium; IVUS strongly recommended; deploy another stent: brilakis p.280–281.
- LMS dissection: incidence 0.02%; large jeopardised myocardium, rapid deterioration; causes (guide manipulation especially 7/8 F; contrast with guide pointing to LMS roof; wiring with polymeric/hydrophilic wires; balloon dilation; stent edge dissection): oxford p.323. Brilakis causes (damped injection, non-coaxial guide, wiring, lesion prep, balloon rupture, calcified tortuous): brilakis p.435 (25.2.1).
- Wording of engagement step 2 and final-assessment step 7 rewritten in original words (round 1 copy finding).
- Bail-out: do not lose wire: brilakis p.113 (5.6.4.3). Disengage, cautious injections from a distance, prepare stent to seal entry: brilakis p.330 (17.5). Avoid injections, non-selective only; rapid stent delivery especially LM: brilakis p.438 (25.2.1). Emergent surgery if true lumen cannot be wired (e.g. LM): brilakis p.439. IVUS (better than OCT, no contrast) for wire position; both wires in true lumen if bifurcation involved; femoral access for IABP/Impella: oxford p.323–324. Table 10.1 and SKS (large guide, long neo-carina): oxford p.323–324. Conservative approach possible in low-degree dissection, stable, unimpaired flow; unstable → timing/logistics for surgery likely not feasible: oxford p.323.
- IVUS without contrast to confirm intraluminal position at an ostial dissection: brilakis p.274 (15.1.5). OCT preferred to evaluate suspected dissection but may extend it with contrast: brilakis p.233 (Table 13.2); OCT very sensitive vs IVUS for dissections: brilakis p.438 (25.2.1).
- Stent cannot be delivered → balloon or guide extension first; prolonged balloon alone sometimes: brilakis p.439. Lost wire: parallel wiring; IVUS for distal true lumen; avoid contrast via possibly subintimal microcatheter, advance microcatheter then workhorse wire; CTO re-entry (re-entry balloon, retrograde, STAR): brilakis p.438. Ping-pong second guide when a diagnostic catheter caused the dissection: brilakis p.114 (5.6.4.3).
- Aorto-coronary dissection: contrast staining of aortic wall; do not inject; stent ostium to seal, high-pressure flaring often required; covered stent if standard stents fail, more restenosis/thrombosis: brilakis p.114 (5.6.4.4). Ostial lesion caused by aortic dissection → emergency surgery with aortic root replacement: brilakis p.439. Iatrogenic type A: more benign, conservative first, CT after procedure and at 24 h, stent ostium to seal entry, surgery for propagation to neck vessels with neurological symptoms, new/worse effusion, severe AR, haemodynamic instability: oxford p.325.
- Tips: pci-lm-damped-torque-off (brilakis p.330, p.273; trigger pressure-damped, split from the preload tip so that the preload advice is event-free); pci-lm-no-side-hole-guide (p.330, p.272, p.485); pci-lm-preload-wire-ostial (p.330, p.271, p.273); pci-lm-injections-dominance (p.330, p.113); pci-lm-brief-inflations (p.332); pci-lm-ostial-stent-position (p.332, p.278, p.282–286); pci-lm-stent-expansion-limit (p.332, p.186); pci-lm-dissection-bailout (p.330, p.438–439, oxford p.323–324).
- Drill angio-pci: focal mid-LAD is one of the existing PCI-lab scenarios (MANUAL_AUTHORING.md); used as nearest practice.

## Lesson cp-left-main-distal (The distal left main)

- Provisional preferred for most LM bifurcations without significant SB (Cx in most cases) involvement; two-stent (especially DK crush) for true (Medina 1.1.1 or 0.1.1): brilakis p.332 (17.10).
- Bifurcation algorithm: SB <2 mm usually not preserved; size and territory; likelihood of SB occlusion from Medina, SB disease severity, morphology (imaging helps), angulation: brilakis p.293–294 (16.1.1, 16.1.2). Angle 70–90°: T or TAP; <70°: DK crush, culotte, reverse crush, DK crush preferred: brilakis p.294 (16.1.4). Medina 0.1.1: V-stenting: brilakis p.294 (16.1.5); V-stenting needs ≥7 Fr: p.295. SKS for emergency only (long neo-carina): brilakis p.295 (16.1.6).
- DKCRUSH-III: 419 patients, 1-year MACE 6.2% DK vs 16.3% culotte, TVR 4.3% vs 11.0%, SB ISR 6.8% vs 12.6%: ref chen-dkcrush3-2013; culotte inferior to DK crush in LM: brilakis p.317 (16.2.10.6), p.311 (16.2.10.5).
- DKCRUSH-V: 482 patients, true distal LM bifurcations (Medina 1,1,1 or 0,1,1); 1-year TLF 10.7% PS vs 5.0% DK (HR 0.42); TV-MI 2.9% vs 0.4%; definite/probable ST 3.3% vs 0.4%; no difference in cardiac death: ref dkcrush5-2017 (PMID 29096915). Brilakis quotes 5% vs 10%: brilakis p.311.
- EBC MAIN: 467 true LM bifurcations; stepwise layered provisional (230) vs systematic dual stent (237); death/MI/TLR 12 months 14.7% vs 17.7% (HR 0.8, 95% CI 0.5–1.3; P = 0.34); procedure time, X-ray dose and consumables favoured provisional; authors: stepwise provisional should remain the default: ref ebc-main-2021 (PMID 34002215).
- Oxford: terminal LMS bifurcation any dedicated technique: oxford p.261; recent data suggest provisional produces best results (Fig 8.21 caption): oxford p.260 (Bifurcation PCI techniques).
