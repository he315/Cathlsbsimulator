## Lesson pm-target-indications

- Catheterisation identifies degree and extent of CAD, complements non-invasive imaging; evaluates LV function, valve, pericardial, congenital disease and cardiomyopathies: oxford p.25
- Only absolute contraindication = refusal of consent; relative list (AKI, pulmonary oedema, contrast allergy, uncontrolled hypertension, active GI haemorrhage, acute stroke, untreated coagulopathy, untreated or unexplained febrile illness): oxford p.25
- Angiography determines anatomy and stenoses; information on extent of atherosclerosis, thrombus, spasm, bridging, dissection; cannot see beyond the lumen; "lumenography": oxford p.27
- Planning questions: presentation (stable, ACS, other); appropriateness via appropriate use criteria; ongoing chest pain; prior catheterisation/fluoroscopy images and reports; CABG surgical report: brilakis p.29 (1.1). Classic stable triggers (CCS 3–4 on treatment, high-risk non-invasive findings): oxford p.27 class 1 list
- Likely non-adherence or non-cardiac surgery in the next 6–12 months: PCI may be best avoided (stent thrombosis from surgery and early DAPT discontinuation); medical therapy or CABG preferred: brilakis p.29 (1.1)
- CT section: invasive angiography's spatial and time resolution; CT up to 320 slices per rotation, whole heart in one breath-hold; at the time of writing European and American guidelines recommended functional tests (CMR, stress echo, SPECT, PET) first-line; CT-FFR and perfusion CT likely to change this; high sensitivity and NPV, PPV lower; accuracy influenced by scanner (64-row minimum), pretest probability, calcium score, heart rate, BMI; mainly to rule out CAD; six main CT indications (rule out CAD in angina or non-anginal pain with abnormal ECG; rule out stable CAD at low–intermediate pretest probability or after inconclusive functional test; suspected ACS with inconclusive ECG and markers; vein-graft patency, with the reasons grafts image well; CTO planning: anatomy, distal landing zone, proximal cap; anomalous origin or anatomy including AV fistula): oxford p.29–30. CT not usually appropriate: high pretest probability, calcium score >1000, large BMI, previous stenting (in-stent restenosis accuracy falls with stents <3 mm and thick-strut first-generation stents): oxford p.30
- 2024 ESC CCS: CCTA first line for many patients with lower clinical likelihood, functional imaging when higher: ref esc-ccs-2024 (as taught in the Academy lesson phy-ad-ct)
- Symptoms can only improve if present; patients may deny symptoms by limiting activity; family information and exercise test: brilakis p.137 (7.1.1)
- Review prior angiograms and non-invasive results; after recent angiography or coronary CTA the target lesion can be determined beforehand: brilakis p.33 (1.1); prior non-invasive testing indicates potential culprit; review of prior angiograms essential to detect interval change: brilakis p.35 (1.7)
- Equivocal symptoms without non-invasive ischaemia → physiology useful; prior adverse reaction/contraindication to intracoronary vasodilators such as adenosine: brilakis p.35 (1.12)
- Catheters used before, difficulty engaging (tip on previous catheters): brilakis p.34 (1.5); Table 1.1 items (catheters used, support, wires, stent delivery): brilakis p.32
- ACC/AHA class 1 list (stable: CCS 3–4 on treatment, high-risk non-invasive, resuscitated SCD/sustained VT/non-sustained polymorphic VT; unstable syndromes four items; STEMI: prelude to primary PCI within 12 h, cardiogenic shock within 18 h of onset of shock, recurrent stuttering ischaemia, patients recovering from MI with ischaemia at a low workload and ≥1 mm ST depression): oxford p.27
- Valve assessment: non-invasive diagnosis difficult; routine to consider angiography in symptomatic patients assessed for valve surgery; risks outweighed by missing severe CAD; part of surgical risk assessment; CABG + AVR in elderly increases operative risk; MR may be due to CAD; guide: >40 with ≥1 risk factor (diabetes, hypertension, family history), asymptomatic suspected ischaemia, LV systolic dysfunction; practice varies; endocarditis only with multiple risk factors or coronary embolisation: oxford p.27–28
- Other indications (typical angina despite medication, atypical pain with recurrent hospitalisation, unsuitable for non-invasive testing, HF of unknown aetiology, pilots and bus drivers with borderline investigations, chest pain soon after revascularisation); angiography almost never justified without symptoms or ischaemia (paraphrase): oxford p.28
- Benefit–risk principle for revascularisation, cited only as an analogy for weighing relative contraindications: brilakis p.137 (chapter 7 introduction)
- Check 1 and 2 explanations: oxford p.27–28; fever as relative contraindication oxford p.25

## Lesson pm-target-ccs-why

- Three decisions after angiography (need, PCI or CABG, which lesions/sequence/technique); PCI easier and lower upfront risk but more repeat revascularisation: brilakis p.137
- Goals: improve symptoms or prognosis; exertional pain relieved by rest; rest pain = ACS; dyspnoea anginal equivalent; asymptomatic cannot feel better: brilakis p.137 (7.1.1)
- Revascularisation more effective for angina than medical therapy, reduces antianginals, improves QoL, no improved prognosis except possibly high-risk anatomy: brilakis p.137 (refs ORBITA, COURAGE, ISCHEMIA p.146–147)
- Oxford: PCI generally indicated for angina relief; no study clearly identified prognostic benefit from PCI, in contrast to CABG for multivessel or LMS: oxford p.207
- COURAGE: 2,287 patients with objective ischaemia; PCI + OMT did not reduce death or non-fatal MI; median 4.6 years: ref boden-courage-2007
- ISCHEMIA: 5,179 patients, moderate/severe ischaemia; no reduction in ischaemic events or death over median 3.2 years; sensitive to MI definition: ref ischemia-2020
- ORBITA: 200 randomised, single-vessel ≥70%, 6 weeks medication optimisation, no significant difference in exercise time vs placebo procedure; authors' conclusion that invasive procedures can be assessed with placebo control: ref al-lamee-orbita-2018
- ORBITA-2: 301 patients, antianginal drugs stopped, objective ischaemia; angina symptom score at 12 weeks 2.9 PCI vs 5.6 placebo: ref rajkumar-orbita2-2023. Keypoint wording "relieves angina better than medication ... improves quality of life": brilakis p.137
- REVIVED-BCIS2: 700 patients, LVEF ≤35%, extensive CAD, viability; no reduction in death or HF hospitalisation over median 41 months; LVEF similar at 6 and 12 months: ref revived-2022
- Prognostic subsets (LM >50%, proximal LAD >50%, 2–3VD >50% with LVEF ≤35%, ischaemia >10% LV or abnormal FFR, single remaining patent artery >50%, SCD due to VF/VT), from 2018 ESC/EACTS: brilakis p.140 (7.1.7)
- 2024 ESC CCS: revascularisation recommended in addition to GDMT for functionally significant 3VD with LVEF >35% to improve long-term survival, reduce CV mortality and spontaneous MI (class I A): ref dayan-esc-ccs-endorsement-2025, esc-ccs-2024
- REVIVED-BCIS2 described as a population similar to (not identical with) Brilakis's LVEF ≤35% subset; it tested PCI only; endorsing surgical societies' conclusion that CABG is recommended in complex 3VD on OMT to improve survival irrespective of LVEF: ref revived-2022; ref dayan-esc-ccs-endorsement-2025 (summary paragraph)
- Extent key determinant; ≥90% almost always significant; physiology more accurate; single-vessel mostly PCI; MVD/LM PCI or CABG; PCI preferred prior CABG, poor surgical candidates, less complex; CABG preferred diabetes, reduced EF, complex: brilakis p.137, p.139 (7.1.2)
- FFR-guided multivessel PCI: fewer lesions treated, better outcomes vs angiography-guided: brilakis p.217 (12.1.1); ref fame-2009
- Asymptomatic branches of the algorithm (single vessel: ischaemia >10% or proximal LAD ≥50%; multivessel: ischaemia >10% or EF <35% or LM ≥50%; otherwise no revascularisation): brilakis p.138 (Fig. 7.1)
- Benefit–risk four domains (CAD impact; operator experience and staff condition, sleep deprivation; lesion complexity; comorbidities; no prognostic revascularisation in terminal non-cardiac disease such as cancer): brilakis p.140 (7.1.8)
- Lists in Fig. 7.3 (CAD impact: symptoms, ischaemic burden, myocardial function; lesion list of 10; cardiac and non-cardiac comorbidities; acute complications 13 items; long-term ISR, ST, DAPT-related): brilakis p.142
- Patient preference final; interventionalist and heart team educate: brilakis p.143 (7.1.9)
- Oxford recommendations for PCI (symptoms, evidence of ischaemia: acute ECG changes or positive functional imaging, suitable anatomy, consent); Gruentzig criteria (stable angina, documented ischaemia, single vessel preferably proximal non-occluded non-calcified, no features precluding bail-out CABG); still suitable with low risk; expansion to unstable patients, MVD, bifurcation, LMS, vein graft, unfit for CABG: oxford p.207
- Check 1 (asymptomatic single vessel): brilakis p.138; check 2 (terminal cancer and severe angina): brilakis p.140; ORBITA wording from ref al-lamee-orbita-2018
- Tip pci-plan-name-the-goal: brilakis p.137, p.140; oxford p.207
- Drill wording (≥90% vs intermediate): brilakis p.139

## Lesson pm-target-heart-team

- PCI easier, lower upfront risk, more repeat revascularisation: brilakis p.137
- Redo CABG infrequent due to higher death and complications; considered with severely diseased or occluded grafts, natives not amenable to PCI, depressed LVEF, absence of patent arterial grafts; IMA grafts if feasible at reoperation: brilakis p.139 (7.1.3)
- Diabetes and MVD better with CABG including lower mortality in several trials, mostly first-generation DES; restenosis and progression in non-stented segments; LIMA-LAD; CABG generally preferred in good surgical candidates: brilakis p.139 (7.1.4)
- FREEDOM: 1,900 patients with diabetes and MVD; 5-year death/MI/stroke 26.6% PCI vs 18.7% CABG; stroke 2.4% vs 5.2%: ref farkouh-freedom-2012
- STS score (in-hospital or 30-day mortality and in-hospital morbidity); EuroSCORE II (in-hospital mortality); cardiac and non-cardiac factor lists: brilakis p.139–140 (7.1.5)
- Oxford on diabetes (diffuse multivessel small-calibre disease, impaired LV function, cerebrovascular disease, renal impairment; historical trials favoured CABG, mortality benefit; glycaemic control with HbA1c ≤7.0% given as the handbook's target, statins, prehydration, NAC, minimal contrast, GPI especially insulin-treated, DES): oxford p.256. PRESERVE (no benefit of oral acetylcysteine over placebo for death, dialysis, persistent kidney decline at 90 days or CA-AKI): ref preserve-2018
- GPI caveat: ISAR-SWEET (701 diabetic patients, elective PCI after 600 mg clopidogrel; death or MI 8.3% vs 8.6%; restenosis reduced with bare-metal stents): ref mehilli-isar-sweet-2004. Goals of IV antiplatelet agents (thrombus propagation with large thrombus or no reflow; stent thrombosis): brilakis p.54 (3.5.2.1)
- Modality step 4: PCI preferred for less complex CAD, CABG for complex MVD: brilakis p.139 (7.1.2); Table 7.1 allows either for 3VD 0–22: brilakis p.141; Fig. 7.1 (page image viewed): diabetes yes → good surgical candidate?; diabetes no → complex CAD? (yes → good surgical candidate?, no → PCI benefits > risks): brilakis p.138
- Oxford history: complete revascularisation and CABG as gold standard; BARI, EAST (similar death and MI, more events from repeat interventions); SoS, ARTS (less further PCI); ARTS-II (DES favourable vs historical controls): oxford p.255
- Left main significance: angiography adequate ≥70%, interobserver variability 30–70%, no reference segment, short and diffuse; caudal views incl. spider; orthogonal views for oval ostium; functional or imaging assessment essential for intermediate LM: brilakis p.330–331 (17.6). Disengage guide for ostial LM; FFR or non-hyperaemic indices in LAD and Cx with pullback; normal in one branch but not the other suggests LM not significant; downstream disease underestimates LM: brilakis p.332 (17.12.1). MLA <6.0 mm² IVUS, <5.4 mm² OCT; 4.5 mm² proposed for Korean patients: brilakis p.333 (17.13.1). Right heart catheterisation (filling pressures, cardiac output, need for support, monitoring): brilakis p.329 (17.1). Support with low EF, high LVEDP or PCWP, occluded RCA, prolonged complex procedures such as atherectomy: brilakis p.333 (17.14)
- Oxford LMS: increasingly PCI because of large calibre, lack of tortuosity, short lesion length (lower restenosis); IVUS before and after; elective IABP with impaired LV function, normal LV tolerates inflations; repeat angiography 3–6 months considered: oxford p.261
- SYNTAX: lesion = ≥50% in vessel ≥1.5 mm: ref sianos-syntax-score-2005; 11 steps with all point values, no codominance, weights 0.5 to 6 (LM 6 left dominance, LM 5 right dominance from colour key), ×2 for 50–99%, ×5 occlusion; occlusion extras; trifurcation +3 to +6; bifurcation Medina groups and angle <70°; aorto-ostial +1; tortuosity +2; length >20 mm +1; heavy calcification +2; thrombus +1; diffuse disease +1 per segment number (≥75% of the segment distal to lesion <2 mm): brilakis p.141 (Fig. 7.2); complexity assessed with SYNTAX incorporating these characteristics: brilakis p.140 (7.1.6)
- Worked example arithmetic (5 × 2 + 1 + 2 = 13) is the module's own illustration of the p.141 rules
- Table 7.1 (2 vessels PCI or CABG; 3 vessels 0–22 either, >22 CABG; LM 0–32 either, >32 CABG): brilakis p.141
- LM: high (≥33) CABG usually better in good surgical candidates; low (0–22) or intermediate (23–32) similar; controversy, some studies better with CABG; heart team, preference and comorbidities; five factors listed: brilakis p.331 (17.7)
- LM algorithm (Fig. 17.2, after Ramadan, Boden, Kinlay): brilakis p.331
- SYNTAX trial: 1,800 patients 3VD/LM; 12-month MACCE higher with PCI (17.8% vs 12.4%) driven by repeat revascularisation; stroke more with CABG: ref syntax-2009. EXCEL and NOBLE cited as Brilakis refs 1, 2, 6, 7: brilakis p.334
- LM planning questions (RCA disease/occlusion, collaterals from RCA, protection by bypass graft, calcification needing atherectomy; LV function, haemodynamics, presentation, comorbidities, support): brilakis p.329 (17.1)
- Haemodynamic support often used for LM PCI (general practice, not the author's personal habit), especially with low EF, decompensated haemodynamics (high LVEDP or PCWP), occluded RCA, planned prolonged ischaemia such as atherectomy: brilakis p.333 (17.14)
- Oxford LMS: traditionally CABG; increasingly PCI (large calibre, no tortuosity, short length); urgent: dissection/complications, primary PCI; elective controversial: protected LMS, refusal of CABG, unsuitable for CABG, suitable anatomy after informed discussion, trials: oxford p.261
- Heart team educates; final decision patient's: brilakis p.143; trainee heart-team participation and ability to explain options to patients, family and multidisciplinary team: oxford p.49; where traditional CABG indications present, advise and consent accordingly: oxford p.255
- 2024 ESC CCS, three-vessel disease (CABG + OMT over PCI and over OMT alone with diabetes, class I A; without diabetes CABG over OMT alone for survival, symptoms and MACE, class I A; PCI along with CABG for low/intermediate complexity only if similar completeness, class I A, less invasive and non-inferior for overall survival; CABG superior to PCI for spontaneous MI and CV death in that cohort; repeat revascularisation consistently higher with PCI; equal recommendations → heart team, no ad hoc PCI, class I C): ref dayan-esc-ccs-endorsement-2025, esc-ccs-2024. These are taught for three-vessel disease only; nothing from this reference is applied to the left main.
- SYNTAX table row "2024 ESC" and Q1: ref dayan-esc-ccs-endorsement-2025; "intermediate" for 27 uses Brilakis's band 23–32 (p.331)
- Check (diabetic 3VD): brilakis p.138–139; ref farkouh-freedom-2012. Check (LM SYNTAX 26): brilakis p.331, p.145 (unprotected left main first on the list of reasons to defer); no 2024 ESC claim
- Pitfalls: thresholds brilakis p.141; no codominance brilakis p.141
- Tip diag-stop-for-heart-team: brilakis p.145 (unprotected LM, MVD among reasons to defer); 3VD no ad hoc PCI with equal recommendations: ref dayan-esc-ccs-endorsement-2025, esc-ccs-2024

## Lesson pm-target-timing

- Ad hoc definition; advantage (no second procedure and its risks); disadvantages (more contrast and radiation, less planning, less time for risk–benefit discussion); ad hoc needed in most ACS with active ischaemia because of CABG delays; delayed favoured in stable or ACS without active ischaemia with complex anatomy: brilakis p.145 (7.3.1)
- Eleven reasons (complex/high-risk disease incl. unprotected LM, CTO, MVD, bifurcations; excessive contrast or radiation during or anticipated; site of service; inadequate consent; uncertain symptoms; no ischaemia evidence and no FFR or IVUS; complication e.g. stroke or access bleeding; operator or patient fatigue; scheduling e.g. STEMI; inadequate pretreatment: no aspirin, inadequate antianginal trial, inadequate hydration; patient preference): brilakis p.145–146
- SCAI consensus (no RCTs; cohort studies no consistent differences; default for ACS; planning and reassessment after diagnostic angiography in SIHD): ref blankenship-ad-hoc-2013
- Renal failure or anticoagulated: stage non-emergent PCI: brilakis p.29 (1.1)
- Ad hoc CTO PCI discouraged in most cases; reasons (planning, preparation, counselling, contrast and radiation, fatigue, viability, discussion); exceptions (ACS from failing highly diseased SVG with native CTO preferred; very challenging arterial access): brilakis p.389 (21.1)
- 2024 ESC no ad hoc PCI when equal recommendations (three-vessel disease): ref dayan-esc-ccs-endorsement-2025, esc-ccs-2024
- Table row "Where it is favoured": ad hoc needed in most ACS with active ischaemia; delayed favoured in stable or non-actively-ischaemic ACS patients in the listed scenarios: brilakis p.145 (7.3.1)
- Complete revascularisation in STEMI (immediate or staged) improves hard outcomes; observational stable data: brilakis p.146 (7.3.2); COMPLETE results (CV death or MI): ref complete-2019
- Oxford: complete revascularisation associated with improved survival; registries PCI complete in 30–70%: oxford p.255
- Factors favouring staging (complexity of remaining lesions; no active ischaemia in ACS; excessive contrast or radiation; complications; operator fatigue; patient fatigue): brilakis p.146 (7.3.2)
- Oxford: may not tackle all lesions at one sitting, especially CTOs, bifurcations → staged; hypotension/haemodynamic compromise if target vessel occludes with multivessel stenoses; more stents/length → restenosis and stent thrombosis: oxford p.255
- Sequence: ACS culprit first; same vessel distal first unless severe damping or ischaemia from proximal/ostial lesion or inability to advance devices; all significant lesions in target vessel same procedure; less complex first; high contrast/radiation → easier first; LAD CTO with RCA/LCx lesions, two approaches, CTO success 85–90%: brilakis p.146 (7.4). Oxford most challenging first with CABG reserve: oxford p.255
- Left main sequence (LAD and LCx first if LM not critical; RCA first to improve LM safety): brilakis p.332 (17.10)
- Checks: brilakis p.145–146, p.389; ostial damping exception brilakis p.146
- Tips: diag-ad-hoc-pause brilakis p.145–146; pci-stage-on-dose brilakis p.146; pci-serial-distal-first brilakis p.146; pci-lm-branches-and-rca-first brilakis p.332; pci-stage-after-complication brilakis p.146

## Lesson pm-target-culprit

- Culprit definition; priority; sometimes no culprit or multiple: brilakis p.143 (7.2.1a)
- Planning clues (prior stent → stent thrombosis; CABG anatomy expedites; cardiac function: shock risk higher with low EF; comorbidities renal failure, diabetes, need for future surgery; medications given; postpartum → SCAD; recent stress in women → takotsubo but angiography often needed): brilakis p.377 (20.1); haemodynamic support more likely in ACS than stable angina: brilakis p.386 (20.14); prior non-invasive tests and prior angiograms: brilakis p.35 (1.7)
- Radial generally preferred in ACS, especially STEMI (prior studies: lower bleeding and mortality); SAFARI-STEMI no difference in clinical outcomes; femoral preferred in prior CABG (graft engagement hard by radial); prompt femoral conversion if radial engagement delays reperfusion (arteria lusoria): brilakis p.377–378 (20.4)
- Immediate culprit engagement vs full diagnostic angiography "often debated": brilakis p.378 (20.5); immediate culprit engagement shortens door-to-balloon by 8–13 minutes; RCA PCI without left anatomy may be suboptimal (LM/MVD needing support or CABG; balloon only without stent may be preferable); Fig. 20.1 sequence (anterior: engage LM with guide → PCI → RCA angiography; non-anterior: diagnostic left → culprit in left? yes guide into LM; no → engage RCA with guide → PCI): brilakis p.378 (20.5)
- Culprit criteria: ECG territory, hypokinesis on LV gram or echo, thrombus, contrast staining, poor or no collaterals, ease of wiring: brilakis p.143 (7.2.1a); ECG, slow antegrade flow, luminal haziness, collateral flow, occlusion of distal branches (embolism), LV gram, OCT (rupture, erosion, thrombus), TTE (hypokinesis, VSD, papillary or ventricular rupture): brilakis p.378–379 (20.7); ulceration, erosion, thrombus, calcified nodule, OCT preferred over IVUS: brilakis p.231 (13.1.1); OCT resolution 10–15 μm vs 100 μm: brilakis p.232; Oxford OCT culprit identification (plaque rupture, plaque erosion): oxford p.238
- Collateral reasoning for chronic occlusions (collaterals may not have had time to form in acute occlusion): brilakis p.379 (20.8)

## Lesson pm-target-nste

- Ongoing ischaemia (STEMI, NSTE-ACS with ongoing chest pain) → immediate PCI of culprit: brilakis p.143 (7.2.1b)
- Ad hoc needed in most ACS with active ischaemia (CABG delays); delayed favoured in ACS without active ischaemia and complex anatomy: brilakis p.145 (7.3.1)
- Modality concepts same as stable (7.2.2–7.2.7); urgency higher, delayed revascularisation consequences: brilakis p.143, p.145 (7.2.8–7.2.9)
- STEMI patients preloaded with P2Y12 inhibitors → bleeding if emergency CABG: brilakis p.143 (7.2.5)
- Pretreatment: stable not before diagnostic angiography but given if PCI planned; NSTE-ACS: no; STEMI: yes: brilakis p.50 (3.5.1.2.3)
- Oxford ACC/AHA unstable list (four items): oxford p.27
- 2023 ESC: STEMI and NSTE-ACS merged; early invasive <24 h for confirmed diagnosis, dynamic ST-segment or T-wave changes, transient ST elevation or GRACE >140 is class IIa A (previously class I A); logistics; Kite meta-analysis (17 RCTs, >10,000 patients) reduced recurrent ischaemia and rehospitalisation, no hard-endpoint benefit; routine pretreatment not recommended when an early invasive strategy (<24 h) is planned, IIb if invasive management later than 24 h: ref licordari-esc-acs-2024 (Table 1 and section 4)
- Step 2 is stated as reasoning from the source: the ECG criterion is ST-elevation territory (brilakis p.143), so it is usually unavailable without ST elevation; remaining criteria from brilakis p.143, p.378–379
- Step 6 (know the left anatomy before stenting an RCA culprit; support, CABG, balloon only): brilakis p.378 (20.5)
- Table column relabelled "Our reading of the item (not stated in the handbook)": only the left column is from oxford p.27
- 2023 ESC NSTE-ACS with MVD: complete revascularisation should be considered, preferably at the index procedure (IIa C; 2020: index completion IIb B); shock: culprit-only class I, staged non-IRA IIa C; 2020 NSTE-ACS guideline: routine immediate non-culprit PCI in shock not recommended (III B): ref licordari-esc-acs-2024 Table 1 and section 5

## Lesson pm-target-multivessel-stemi

- Nearly half of STEMI patients have MVD: oxford p.253; about two thirds of MI with shock have MVD: brilakis p.431 (24.2.7)
- Four trials and definitions/timing (PRAMI >50% at primary PCI; CvLPRIT >70% at primary PCI or same admission; DANAMI PRIMULTI FFR ≤0.80 staged same admission; COMPARE ACUTE FFR ≤0.80 at primary PCI or same admission); benefit mainly fewer revascularisations; guidelines then: before discharge: oxford p.253
- COMPLETE: 4,041 patients (2,016 + 2,025); CV death or MI 7.8% vs 10.5% (HR 0.74) over median 3 years; benefit regardless of intended timing: ref complete-2019; Brilakis's statement of improved hard outcomes: brilakis p.146, p.379
- MULTISTARS AMI: 840 stable STEMI patients; immediate vs staged at 19–45 days; primary composite 8.5% vs 16.3%; non-inferior and, on testing, superior (P<0.001 for both); MI 2.0% vs 5.3%, ischaemia-driven revascularisation 4.1% vs 9.3%: ref multistars-ami-2023
- Book dates: Oxford 2nd edition © 2020 and cites 2017–2018 ESC guidelines on p.253 without COMPLETE; Brilakis © 2021 cites COMPLETE (p.379 ref 9, p.146): the module says only that Oxford's summary does not include COMPLETE
- Non-culprit physiology vs angiography: Brilakis accepts physiology (p.386, 20.12.2); 2023 ESC non-IRA PCI on angiographic severity (I B) and the appraisers' comment against FFR during the index STEMI procedure: ref licordari-esc-acs-2024 Table 1; FLOWER-MI no benefit of FFR guidance over angiographic guidance: same ref; FULL REVASC compared FFR-guided completion with culprit-only PCI (not with angiographic guidance) and is worded that way everywhere (multivessel lesson list, step 3, keypoint, quiz q8, case s3): ref bohm-full-revasc-2024.
