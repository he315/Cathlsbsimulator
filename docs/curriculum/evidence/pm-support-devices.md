## Lesson pm-support-devices-iabp-insert (Inserting a balloon pump, step by step)

- All PCI cath labs should have immediate availability of an IABP: oxford p.289. (Round 1: the module now says only that Oxford expects one to be immediately available; the earlier claims that the balloon pump is the device you are most likely to insert, and that every lab should be able to start one, were removed as unsupported.)
- Cardiac physiologist checks balloon pump working and stocked; cooperates with operator in setting up counterpulsation: oxford p.59 (ch 2, The cardiac physiologist). Settings of aortic counterpulsation recorded in the report: oxford p.62.
- Anticoagulated patients: radial; femoral route reserved for emergencies such as IABP insertion: oxford p.68. Some operators prepare the right femoral artery in radial cases in case alternative access (e.g. IABP) is needed: oxford p.71.
- IABP inserted percutaneously through a 7–8 Fr femoral sheath (14.2) / Table 14.1 7–8 French: brilakis p.252, p.254. 7–9 Fr for IABP, can be inserted without a sheath: brilakis p.515 (30.1.1.1). Femoral sheath provided with the IABP kit: brilakis p.252 (14.3.1).
- Oxford: IABP catheters inserted directly or via a sheath; devices tend to be 7.5 F or larger, prolonged compression: oxford p.291 (printed 270); Fig 9.10 caption "sheathed or sheathless insertion": oxford p.290.
- Sizing: Brilakis Table 14.2: 30 cc 147–162 cm; 40 cc 162–182 cm; 50 cc >182 cm: brilakis p.255 (PDF read). Oxford: sized by height; manufacturer (named in the book) recommends 25 mL <152 cm, 34 mL 152–163 cm, 40 mL 164–183 cm, 50 mL >183 cm: oxford p.289. Size selected usually by height: brilakis p.253 (14.3.2 step 1).
- Small balloon for the patient's aorta is a cause of poor augmentation: brilakis p.256.
- Check item (158 cm): Brilakis 147–162 → 30 cc; Oxford 152–163 → 34 mL: same pages. Distractors now name each manual's values wrongly (40 cc Brilakis starts at 162 cm; Oxford 40 mL starts at 164 cm; Oxford 25 mL below 152 cm).
- Preparation steps: one-way valve on gas lumen, not removed until IABP in position in the aorta; vacuum with provided syringe, syringe removed keeping valve; catheter removed from tray immediately before insertion; stylet removed from wire lumen; wire lumen flushed with saline; fibre-optic cable connected to console and fluid transducer zeroed: brilakis p.253 and p.255 (14.3.2 steps 2–7).
- Oxford: Seldinger then dilator over the wire; introducer wire from the kit is smaller than a standard exchange wire, which will not fit the balloon lumen; one-way valve and large syringe vacuum; advance balloon over wire until tip just distal to left subclavian artery, X-ray screening if available; advance sheath seal into hub (if used) and secure to leg; remove guide wire and aspirate 3 mL blood; flush inner lumen so no trapped blood, attach pressure circuit; remove the one-way valve yellow lumen insert; connect balloon to catheter extender, then to pump: oxford p.291.
- Oxford's insertion tips are headed "Insertion tips for" one named manufacturer's system (oxford p.291); the module labels them as one manufacturer's system and tells the learner to check the device's instructions for use for the yellow insert and extender.
- One-way valve: Brilakis gives no reason for keeping it on (brilakis p.253); the check explanation labels the vacuum-holding mechanism as presumed. Round 2: the opening sentence that stated the mechanism as fact was removed; the presumed mechanism is now stated once.
- Balloon place: proximal descending aorta: oxford p.289. The module no longer says the two tip landmarks are identical.
- Kit guidewire 0.025 inch advanced under fluoroscopy to the aortic arch; troubleshooting as in chapter 5: brilakis p.255 (14.3.3). (Module points to pm-to-the-root for the manoeuvres.)
- IABP advanced under fluoroscopy until tip at level of tracheal bifurcation: brilakis p.255 (14.3.4).
- Start: guidewire removed, wire lumen to arterial pressure monitoring, helium port to console, counterpulsation initiated: brilakis p.255 (14.3.5).
- Migration: cause catheter inadvertently pulled back; prevention suture IABP after insertion; treatment reposition and secure: brilakis p.256–257.
- Check 1:2 timing after starting: brilakis p.256 (14.3.6.2).
- Catheters for coronary angiography or PCI can be exchanged past the balloon: oxford p.292 (Fig 9.11 caption).
- Avoid drawing arterial blood samples from the IABP pressure line: narrow calibre, prone to blockage: oxford p.289.
- Transport checklist (vehicle loading/securing, trained personnel, power supply with spare battery, receiving centre pump, plans to return the machine, telephone advice): oxford p.291.
- Removal: used balloon will not usually retract through sheath; withdraw until balloon reaches sheath, resistance; sheath and balloon catheter pulled out together; pressure haemostasis; 7.5 F or larger, prolonged compression: oxford p.291.
- Helium inflation (mechanism context): brilakis p.252; oxford p.289.

## Lesson pm-support-devices-iabp-run (Running the balloon pump)

- Monitoring: 1:2 augmentation and optimal timing; monitor systemic pressure; monitor IABP position; therapeutic anticoagulation, if not possible use 1:1 to minimise thrombus on the balloon: brilakis p.256 (14.3.6.2).
- Fig 14.4: augmented peak arterial pressure should be higher than non-augmented; end-diastolic pressure after an assisted beat lower than after an unassisted beat: brilakis p.256 (PDF read).
- Row E: "reduced systolic pressure" on a normal cycle (oxford p.293, Fig 9.13); afterload reduction from deflation at the start of systole (oxford p.289).
- Running ratio: Brilakis 14.3.6.2 pairs 1:2 with the timing check and gives 1:1 when anticoagulation cannot be given (brilakis p.256); Oxford's wean from every beat implies 1:1 as the running ratio (oxford p.289); the Academy lesson mcs-iabp-physiology (src/content/tracks/acute-mcs.ts) moves to 1:1 after the timing check. The module states all three and defers to local practice.
- Weaning steps: only Oxford's ratio steps (oxford p.289); "monitor systemic pressure" is Brilakis's monitoring item 2 (brilakis p.256). The module says neither manual gives more of a weaning protocol.
- Pearl and tip mcs-iabp-read-1to2: a failed comparison points first to timing, then to Brilakis's non-timing causes (brilakis p.256).
- Retroperitoneal check stem now places the pump during high-risk PCI and states it was left at 1:2.
- Oxford Fig 9.13 labels: A unassisted aortic end-diastolic pressure; B unassisted systolic; C diastolic augmentation; D assisted aortic end-diastolic; E assisted systolic; normal cycle: inflation just after aortic valve closure, augmented diastolic pressure, reduced aortic end-diastolic and reduced systolic pressure: oxford p.293.
- Timing usually from ECG; machines allow traces to be frozen; inflation immediately after aortic valve closure (dicrotic notch), deflation just before systole (R wave): oxford p.291. Inflate immediately after aortic valve closure, deflate at end of diastole: oxford p.289.
- Fig 9.12: console shows trigger (pressure), heart rate, augmented pressure, aortic and balloon waveforms, battery, helium supply: oxford p.292.
- Dicrotic notch as timing marker for IABP inflation: oxford p.110 (ch 4, Left heart pressures and waveforms).
- Early inflation: C encroaches on B; early AV closure, AR, raised LVEDP, increased O2 requirement: oxford p.293 (Fig 9.14). Late inflation: C suboptimal: oxford p.293 (Fig 9.15). Early deflation: sharp suboptimal drop C to D; retrograde flow possible; O2 demand may increase: oxford p.294 (Fig 9.16). Late deflation: deflates as valve starts to open; C prolonged, D similar to A, prolonged assisted systole E; little afterload reduction; myocardial work may increase as LV contracts against balloon: oxford p.294 (Fig 9.17).
- Weaning: reduce inflation frequency to every second, later every third cycle: oxford p.289.
- Poor augmentation (mean pressure not increasing) causes: hypovolaemia, low balloon position, small balloon, low SVR, improper timing, kinked catheter, delivered volume too low; prevention: optimal size, placement, inflation volume and timing; treatment: treat causes: brilakis p.256.
- Poor afterload reduction causes: balloon not inflated to full volume, compliant aortic wall, improper position, partial obstruction of gas lumen, improper timing; prevention and treatment as above: brilakis p.256.
- The bedside order of checks in the module is a teaching sequence built from those lists (no order is given in the book).
- Most patients on IV heparin; platelet count with prolonged heparin; distal pulses marked and checked: oxford p.289.
- Tachycardia, especially irregular: less effective; may need triggering from aortic pressure rather than surface ECG: oxford p.289.
- IABP failure (balloon rupture, exhausted helium, ECG trigger failure): pumping resumed in 10–15 min or catheter removed; static IABP source of clot and distal embolisation: oxford p.289.
- Retroperitoneal bleeding may require cessation of anticoagulation; if discontinued, use 1:1: brilakis p.257 (14.3.6.4.1).
- What can go wrong: access site complications (limb ischaemia, retroperitoneal bleeding; causes suboptimal access technique, anticoagulation); ischaemic limb usually recovers after removing IABP and sheath with manual haemostasis, may need surgical or endovascular treatment; aortic dissection; thromboembolism (thrombus on IABP); IABP catheter entrapment: brilakis p.257.
- Cautions: may worsen renal blood flow; peripheral compromise usually leg on insertion side, contralateral possible; cold, pale, painful limb with reduced pulses demands immediate specialist attention: oxford p.290 (Box 9.1).
- Pearl (A to E routine) and pitfalls are restatements of the above.

## Lesson pm-support-devices-impella-insert (Inserting a microaxial pump)

- Non-pulsatile axial flow pump advanced through aortic valve, moves blood from LV to aorta; 2.5 and CP percutaneous, 5.0 surgical cut-down: brilakis p.252 (14.2).
- Oxford: microaxial pump inserted into LV through aortic valve; pigtail at distal end for atraumatic resting in LV; pump placed underneath the aortic plane, aspirates LV blood into ascending aorta (module now says it sits across the valve plane, inlet below and outlet above, with Brilakis's 3.5 cm inlet depth, brilakis p.259–260); femoral or axillary; three sizes; contraindications include severe aortic stenosis; requires prolonged anticoagulation: oxford p.295.
- Steps 1–4 (summary): 5–6 Fr sheath and femoral angiography; two Perclose sutures at 10 and 2 o'clock, wrapped in gauze or insulin syringes untied; stiff kit wire; 8, 10, 12 Fr dilators; peel-away sheath short 13 cm / long 25 cm; alternatively 14 Fr Cook sheath (module: "a 14 Fr sheath of another make"); femoral angiography after sheath insertion for limb ischaemia; UFH; ACT >250 s; then remove dilator, aspirate and flush: brilakis p.257–258 (14.4.1–14.4.4). Taught in full in pm-access-large-bore.
- Step 5: pigtail to aortic root; pigtail preferred over Amplatz or multipurpose because less likely to advance behind a papillary muscle; through the valve into LV; 260 cm stiff 0.014 or 0.018 inch wire through pigtail into LV; pigtail removed leaving 0.018 wire: brilakis p.258 (14.4.5).
- Difficulty crossing: cause aortic stenosis; prevention balloon dilation of stenotic valve may be needed; treatment 0.035 inch J-wire to stiffen the pigtail: brilakis p.258.
- Suction prevention includes crossing with a pigtail; cannula under papillary muscles a cause of suction: brilakis p.260.
- Step 6: catheter connected to controller and purged per IFU; usually done by the cath lab technician while the operator obtains access and pre-closes: brilakis p.258 (14.4.6).
- Step 7: wire into red loading lumen at the tip of the pigtail after straightening the tip; advanced until it exits near the label; red lumen removed: brilakis p.258 (14.4.7.2). (Brilakis names it the "red EasyGuide lumen"; the module uses "red loading lumen".)
- Unable to use red lumen (removed inadvertently, device removed and reinserted): backload on back end of 0.018 wire; wire exits the outlet area on the inner radius of the cannula aligned with the straight black line: brilakis p.258.
- Red tube not completely removed (improper preparation, fracture): device fails (Fig 14.5); prevention ensure complete removal; treatment remove device, remove tube, use new device: brilakis p.258–259.
- Step 8: wire fixed by assistant; advanced through sheath with fast small movements because of significant bleeding while inlet inside sheath and outlet outside; through iliacs and valve under fluoroscopy; inlet ~3.5 cm below aortic valve; pigtail from second access in a coronary cusp if unsure of valve location; wire removed: brilakis p.259 (14.4.8).
- Unable to pass iliacs: causes iliofemoral lesions, tortuosity and calcification; prevention least diseased femoral; treatment long peel-away sheath (sometimes short sheath better), iliac balloon angioplasty, IVL of heavily calcified iliacs (ref 8): brilakis p.259. Ref riley-ivl-impella-2019 (PMID 30811781): case of IVL to deliver 14 Fr sheath for Impella before high-risk PCI.
- Axillary/transcaval for poor femoral route: brilakis p.93–94 (4.4.3–4.4.4), p.251 (14.1, as taught in pm-support-choice).
- Step 9: confirm position (inlet ~3.5 cm below valve), wire removed, aortic waveform on controller, press start, monitor flow; "Auto" usually selected in the lab; after 3 hours defaults to P9: brilakis p.260 (PDF read). The module adds that controller behaviour varies by console generation and the IFU is the authority (general caution, no numeric claim). The sim task no longer mentions motor current or stepwise increases.
- Step 11: Impella shaft 9 Fr, sheath 14 Fr, room for up to 7 Fr sheath; avoids second arterial puncture; micropuncture needle through valve, 0.018 then 0.035 wire, 7 Fr sheath; 100 (or 125) cm guides; hold Impella and monitor on fluoroscopy, forward movement may injure LV: brilakis p.261 (14.4.11).
- Figure impella-position: app figure, caption states the 3.5 cm depth (brilakis p.259–260).
- Videos: titles only (5lDjuvQruZ0, WFn91e2qA08, BXRpJS8jtFM); notes now use the generic class name.

## Lesson pm-support-devices-impella-run (Running a microaxial pump)

- Monitor flow; monitor pressure waveform: aortic waveform should be present, ventricular waveform → pull back: brilakis p.260 (14.4.10.2). The earlier generalisations (almost every problem shows in flow or waveform; falling flow as common end point) were removed.
- Other causes of suction (tamponade, bleeding): the Academy lesson mcs-impella-management (src/content/tracks/acute-mcs.ts, suction row: "exclude tamponade and bleeding"); attributed to it in the lesson and the pearl.
- Oxford comparison with the IABP: no native LV systolic pulsation needed, more useful the more dysfunctional the LV; larger access (more access complications), prolonged anticoagulation, more expensive, longer learning curve: oxford p.295.
- Fig 24.8 (NCSI algorithm, brilakis p.430, PDF read): ICU care, daily haemodynamic assessment with a detailed vascular assessment; monitor for signs of haemolysis and adjust Impella position as indicated; device weaning once off all inotropes and vasopressors, CPO >0.6 and PAPi >0.9. Source { brilakis-pci, 24.2 } added to the lesson and the haemolysis tip.
- Round 2: Oxford's contraindication to anticoagulation (oxford p.295, listed once) is now stated once, in the Anticoagulation bullet; the duplicate in the Bleeding bullet was removed.
- Bleeding: Table 14.1 lists high bleeding risk as a contraindication for all four devices, IABP included; bleeding risk IABP +, Impella ++: brilakis p.254.
- Pulled back into aorta: causes patient movement, imperfect suturing; prevention good sedation, secure device in groin; treatment re-advance into LV ideally under fluoroscopy, else remove and repeat steps 5–10: brilakis p.260.
- Suction alarm: causes hypovolaemia, RV failure, cannula under papillary muscles; prevention optimise volume while maintaining RA pressure <10 mmHg, minimise device movement, cross with pigtail; treatment reduce P-level by 1 or 2, fluids if hypovolaemic, check and correct position, support RV if needed: brilakis p.260 (PDF read).
- Impella stops: causes malfunction, battery, thrombus; prevention console plugged into outlet, proper anticoagulation; treatment attempt restart, if not remove from ventricle to avoid aortic regurgitation: brilakis p.260–261.
- Forward movement during sheath insertion through Impella sheath may cause LV injury; cause suboptimal fixation; prevention hold device, fluoroscopy: brilakis p.261.
- Table 14.1: haemolysis risk IABP +, Impella ++; bleeding IABP +, Impella ++: brilakis p.254 (PDF read).
- Oxford complications: device migration, device thrombosis, limb ischaemia, vascular trauma, haemolysis, stroke, infection; requires prolonged anticoagulation; contraindication to anticoagulation is a contraindication: oxford p.295.
- Bleeding and haemolysis the two most frequent complications; haemolysis mostly from pump instability (patient- or device-related); upfront echo, frequent monitoring, prompt intervention: ref van-edom-impella-bleeding-haemolysis-2023 (PMID 37495347, abstract).
- Correct insertion depth and rotational angle; mal-rotation towards the mitral valve may cause suction alarms, haemolysis, AR and inadequate support; discern suction from malposition versus haemodynamic problems: ref balthazar-impella-position-2023 (PMID 37607271, abstract).
- Purge at preparation per IFU: brilakis p.258. The manuals do not describe purge solutions; stated as such.
- ACT >250 s at insertion: brilakis p.258.
- Step 12 removal: decrease slowly to P2; withdraw into aorta; turn off and disconnect; remove through peel-away sheath quickly (severe bleeding when outlet out and inlet in); remove sheath and tighten Perclose sutures; balloon in iliac from contralateral femoral to minimise bleeding: brilakis p.262.
- Continued support: remove peel-away sheath while advancing repositioning sheath; assistant manual pressure; peel-away retracted completely outside body (partially inside → arterial injury, difficult bleeding); wings bent back and peeled; side arm of repositioning sheath flushed; advanced into artery; sutures partially tightened; gauze for 45° entry angle; sheath and device sutured to skin: brilakis p.262.
- Weaning criteria in shock (off all vasopressors and inotropes, CPO >0.6 W, PAPi >0.9): brilakis p.430 (Fig 24.8, confirmed on the PDF page), ref basir-ncsi-2019; taught in pm-support-choice.
- Impella often left in place for LV venting during VA-ECMO: brilakis p.252 (14.1.9).
- Case 36 and Hanna Impella video: titles only.

## Lesson pm-support-devices-ecmo-cannulate (VA-ECMO cannulation)

- VA-ECMO: centrifugal non-pulsatile pump, membrane oxygenator, venous blood aspirated, oxygenated and returned through arterial cannula; large cannulae and a perfusionist: brilakis p.252 (14.2).
- Oxford: ECMO probably most adopted ECLS in the cath lab due to relatively simpler insertion; VA: one cannula femoral artery, one in IVC via femoral vein, oxygenation and circulatory support; VV: both venous, oxygenation only, isolated lung failure; larger access, surgical cut-down often required: oxford p.297.
- Situations: cardiac arrest, VA-ECMO essential to prevent death: brilakis p.249 (14.1.1); arrest in PCI → CPR then VA-ECMO if no prompt recovery: brilakis p.488 (28.1.3); refractory arrhythmias → VA-ECMO: brilakis p.57 (3.6.2.3.1); refractory shock → VA-ECMO or Impella 5.0: brilakis p.252 (14.1.9); shock with hypoxaemia → VA-ECMO (supports oxygenation and circulation): brilakis p.251 (14.1.7); biventricular failure → VA-ECMO usually: brilakis p.252 (14.1.8); SCAD: high risk of acute closure, access to devices such as VA-ECMO recommended: brilakis p.401 (22.1.14) (the module now names SCAD specifically).
- ECLS-SHOCK: early routine VA-ECMO in infarct-related shock did not lower 30-day mortality (47.8% vs 49.0%) and caused more moderate or severe bleeding and peripheral vascular complications: ref ecls-shock-2023 (existing in refs.ts; PubMed PMID 37634145 checked).
- Contemporary sizes: arterial usually 15–19 Fr, venous 21–25 Fr, distal perfusion catheter 5–8 Fr: ref ortoleva-vaecmo-review-2026 (abstract and Table).
- Table row renamed 'Cardiac output support': Table 14.1 'Cardiac output increase (L/min) 4–5', footnote 'Depending on arterial cannula size': brilakis p.254; Oxford 'can guarantee from 3 to 7 L/minute of cardiac output': oxford p.297.
- Sizes: venous 25 Fr most patients; arterial 15 Fr most women, 17 Fr most men, larger occasionally: brilakis p.264–265 (14.5.4, 14.5.5). Table 14.1: arterial 15–19 Fr, venous 21–25 Fr; CO increase 4–5 L/min depending on arterial cannula size: brilakis p.254. 30.1.1.1: 15–19 Fr arterial, 25 Fr venous: brilakis p.515. Oxford: venous 18–21 F, arterial 14–16 F, 3–7 L/min (vs max 5 L/min for Impella 5.0): oxford p.297.
- Step 1: access per chapter 4; venous right CFV (easier to advance to RA), arterial left CFA; not same groin (venous congestion from femoral vein collapsed by adjacent arterial cannula); angiography of femoral arterial access except emergencies: brilakis p.263 (14.5.1). Module wording now 'advises against' (Brilakis 'should not'). ELSO registry same-side vs opposite-side femoral cannulation (19,093 patients): no difference in limb ischaemia needing intervention; bleeding, vessel repair and in-hospital mortality lower with opposite sides after adjustment: ref ortoleva-vaecmo-review-2026.
- Step 2: antegrade access to preserve limb perfusion; best before arterial cannula (may interfere); urgent cases after ECMO started; ultrasound and micropuncture, CFA or SFA; 6 Fr 24 cm sheaths (named product) usually used, less likely to kink; may not be needed with 15–17 Fr cannulae; NIRS <50% or >15% difference vs contralateral → place: brilakis p.263 (14.5.2), ref 10 = patton-rivera-nirs-ecmo-2018 (PMID 29848162: 25 patients; 6 with ischaemia all <50% for >4 min; >15% differential in all with cannula obstruction and none without; one false positive from venous saturation <30%).
- NIRS caveat: 5 patients with StO2 <50% for <4 min had no limb hypoperfusion: ref patton-rivera-nirs-ecmo-2018 (abstract re-read).
- Prophylactic distal perfusion: Marbach meta-analysis, prophylactic DPC OR 0.31 (95% CI 0.21–0.47): ref marbach-ecmo-limb-ischaemia-2022 (PMID 35523371); Juo meta-analysis, 22 retrospective studies, 9.74% vs 25.42% with vs without DPC: ref juo-distal-perfusion-2017 (PMID 28762511); ELSO interim guideline recommends a DPC for arterial cannulae >17 Fr, and routine prophylactic placement 'seems reasonable': ref ortoleva-vaecmo-review-2026; Academy lesson mcs-ecmo-circuit places one at cannulation. Round 2: the 'many units place one routinely' wording and the sentence calling the NIRS rule 'not a reason to leave one out' were removed as editorial; the lesson, keypoint, tip mcs-ecmo-antegrade-sheath and case learning point now state the ELSO recommendation above 17 Fr and the Academy practice, and that with smaller cannulae Brilakis (p.263) and the ELSO guidance accept close monitoring (Ortoleva, full text re-read: DPC 'may not be needed with smaller cannulas' if the limb is closely monitored by Doppler and other modalities). Tip mcs-ecmo-leg-rescue now says a low reading 'suggests' cannula limitation and asks for examination of the leg, because of the false positive from venous saturation below 30% (patton-rivera-nirs-ecmo-2018).
- New tip mcs-ecmo-leg-rescue (manage/complication + limb-ischaemia): brilakis p.263 and p.266 (NIRS thresholds, back-bleed, side-port connection).
- Curled-wire check: option D replaced by a clearly wrong action (advance over the curled wire), because Oxford's IVC description made the old option defensible.
- Step 3: UFH 70–100 units/kg: brilakis p.263 (14.5.3).
- Step 4: stiff guidewire (named products) from right CFV into SVC; distal SVC to avoid migration into RA (cannula against intracardiac structure, perforation); skin dilated with haemostat; 25 Fr cannula to RA; optimal tip at SVC–RA junction (Fig 14.10); back end clamped: brilakis p.264–265.
- Challenges: resistance at entry → additional skin dilation; venous thrombosis → contralateral CFV or IJV; IVC filter → IJV. What can go wrong: lower extremity ischaemia; vessel laceration (advance under fluoroscopy with wire in SVC, stop with resistance); improper positioning (Fig 14.11: against the interatrial septum, likely from poor distal wire position): brilakis p.265.
- Step 5: stiff wire via CFA into thoracic aorta; haemostat skin dilation; 15 Fr women / 17 Fr men; proximal wider portion not advanced through skin; clamp. Challenge: difficult advancement → wire position, no kinking or prolapse; insertion may cause perforation. What can go wrong: displacement → massive bleeding or haematoma; bleeding around cannula; perforation (insert under fluoroscopy over a stiff wire): brilakis p.265.
- Step 6: cannulae and tubes together; assistant injects saline over the connection to fill completely; check no bubbles. Bubbles: cause imperfect connection; prevention continuous saline dribbling, avoid oversqueezing tubing, do not tighten until air removed; treatment arterial bubbles through side port, venous small bubbles removed by circuit, bigger → disconnect and reconnect. What can go wrong: thrombus embolism, air embolism, circuit clotting: brilakis p.266.
- Step 7: clamps removed, pump started, flows monitored: brilakis p.266.
- Step 8: circuit clamped; antegrade sheath back-bleeds to remove air; connected to arterial cannula side port with male-to-male connector; clamps removed and restarted: brilakis p.266.
- Videos: titles only (7vfM7zRRmTk, L6lKm8VZy4w).

## Lesson pm-support-devices-ecmo-run (Running VA-ECMO)

- VA-ECMO increases myocardial oxygen demand by increasing LVEDP and volume, could adversely impact recovery; needs perfusionist: brilakis p.252 (14.2).
- ECMO-run lesson opening now names its evidence as three reviews, two small randomised trials of early venting (EARLY-UNLOAD, EVOLVE-ECMO) and the AHA statement as Ortoleva reports it, mostly observational data and expert opinion.
- Oxford disadvantages: larger access, continuous anticoagulation, higher systemic thromboembolism risk, poor coronary perfusion, ventricular distension, more complex management, dedicated ECMO team. Complications: bleeding, limb ischaemia, vascular trauma, compartment syndrome, AKI, haemolysis, thrombus and air embolism, infection, neurological injury. Contraindications include contraindication to anticoagulation: oxford p.297.
- Mechanism of LV loading (retrograde flow raises afterload; preserved LV raises LVEDP and contractility; failing LV dilates; MR, LA pressure, pulmonary congestion; aortic valve may not open with every beat; stasis and thrombus in LV or aortic root; arrhythmias, subendocardial ischaemia; hinders recovery and weaning): ref lusebrink-ecmo-venting-2023.
- Titrate flow to lowest acceptable level; fluid management with diuretics, haemodialysis or CVVH: ref lusebrink-ecmo-venting-2023.
- Low pulsatility: aggressive management at pulse pressure 10 mmHg or less; standard pulsatility goal >10–15 mmHg; PA pressure monitoring discerns hypovolaemia from LV failure: ref ortoleva-vaecmo-review-2026.
- VA-ECMO and IABP can be used without anticoagulation for a short period in active bleeding: brilakis p.251 (14.1.6).
- Signs: ECLS-SHOCK protocol venting triggers (lack of arterial pulsatility, no aortic valve opening on echo, LVOT VTI <10 cm, LV distension); expert consensus triggers (non-pulsatile flow, progressive LA or LV distension, raised PAP or PCWP, radiographic congestion, persistent valve closure, intracavitary stasis): ref lusebrink-ecmo-venting-2023.
- Ortoleva: unloading indicated if significant pulmonary oedema not addressable less invasively, pulsatility ≤10 mmHg despite inotropes and volume optimisation, or arrhythmias thought due to distension; hemodynamic triggers unknown; PCWP 18–20 mmHg reasonable after less invasive manoeuvres; increasing flow reduced PCWP in most, increase in only 6% in one study: ref ortoleva-vaecmo-review-2026.
- Step 2 'Inotrope and PEEP': Ortoleva states directly that arterial and PA catheter monitoring can prompt volume removal, inotrope initiation, PEEP and LV mechanical unloading: ref ortoleva-vaecmo-review-2026 (no longer an inference).
- Increasing circuit flow reduces PCWP in most patients on VA-ECMO with or without IABP (reduced preload), only 6% had a rise in one study: ref ortoleva-vaecmo-review-2026 (two sentences in the review; the clinical checker's claim that the review does not say 'most' was not upheld).
- Distension and afterload in a non-ejecting ventricle: ref lusebrink-ecmo-venting-2023; excessive retrograde flow raises LV afterload: ref torre-harlequin-ecmo-2025.
