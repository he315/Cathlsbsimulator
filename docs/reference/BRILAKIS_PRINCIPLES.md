# Brilakis / Manual-of-PCI teaching principles (cross-cutting design layer)

Source: the *Manual of PCI*, *Manual of CTO*, and PROGRESS‑CTO body of work, and the
Brilakis YouTube channel (https://www.youtube.com/@manosbrilakis). Videos can't be embedded;
this file records the *teaching philosophy and canonical frameworks* to bake into the engine.
A human should refresh it against current channel content periodically.

These are **cross‑cutting** — they apply to every Track B (PCI) feature and several Track A ones.

1. **Algorithmic & stepwise decomposition.** Model/teach every procedure as an explicit sequence:
   **Steps → Equipment → Complex subgroups → Complications → Post‑procedure.** The coach names the
   current step, its goal, and what can go wrong here.
2. **Modular skills.** Hard skills are separable, individually trainable modules — for CTO especially:
   collateral crossing, subintimal tracking / dissection re‑entry, parallel wiring, reverse CART.
3. **Complication doctrine: prevent → recognise early → treat decisively.** Every complication has
   triggers, pre‑event warning signs, a visible event, and an algorithmic management pathway
   (incl. team communication). Mantra: *be prepared, have a plan, communicate, know your bailout.*
4. **Complication taxonomy the engine tags every event with:** coronary / cardiac / other.
5. **Intracoronary imaging (IVUS/OCT) by default** — sizing, vessel prep, stent optimisation, and
   diagnosing complications; reward using it where it changes a decision.
6. **Radiation ALARA + contrast minimisation as first‑class scored metrics** (collimation, low mag,
   fewer/shorter runs, ultra‑low‑contrast pathways in CKD).
7. **Plan before you touch the patient** — access, target, equipment, bailout, dose/contrast budget.
8. **Evidence/registry mindset** — J‑CTO, ACC/AHA A/B/C, Medina, Rentrop/CC, NHLBI, Ellis, SCAD types
   as the vocabulary of scoring.
9. **Prepare the vessel before stenting** — under‑expansion of an unprepared lesion is penalised.
10. **Know when NOT to intervene and when to STOP** — non‑occlusive SCAD, RV marginals, reversible spasm;
    abort on dose/contrast/time thresholds. Restraint is a scorable skill.
11. **Team & closed‑loop communication** at critical steps.
12. **Named danger flags** the engine enforces: conus over‑selection → VF; injecting on a
    damped/ventricularised pressure; stenting into poor distal runoff → thrombosis; epicardial‑collateral
    perforation in prior‑CABG is worse, not benign; catheter‑tip spasm mimicking fixed ostial disease;
    anomalous interarterial course as a sudden‑death substrate.
