# S6 facts: automatic dose-rate control and dose physics

Every constant in `src/render/xray/adrc.ts` and `src/simulation/dose.ts` is listed here with its source. A source is listed only if it was opened and read in this stream. Quotes are short and verbatim. Values not covered here are named as model parameters in code comments.

## Regulatory

**21 CFR 1020.32**, opened at https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1020.32

| Use in code | Paragraph | Quoted value |
|---|---|---|
| `AKR_LIMIT_MGY_MIN = 88` | (d)(2)(ii) | "shall not be operable ... resulting in an AKR in excess of 88 mGy per minute" |
| `AKR_LIMIT_HIGH_MGY_MIN = 176` | (d)(2)(iii)(C) | High-level control "shall not be operable ... result in an AKR in excess of 176 mGy per minute" |
| Cine exempt from the AKR limit | (d)(2)(iii)(B) | The limit is lifted "during the recording of images from the fluoroscopic image receptor for the purpose of providing the user with a recorded image(s) after termination of the exposure" |
| `IRP_FROM_ISO_MM = 150` | (k)(4)(ii) | C-arm reference location "15 cm from the isocenter toward the x-ray source" |
| `AKR_WINDOW_S = 1` | (k)(1) | Display updated "at least once every second" |
| `FLUORO_SIGNAL_INTERVAL_S = 300` | (h)(2)(ii) | Audible signal "for each passage of 5 minutes of fluoroscopic irradiation time" |
| Fluoro time shown in minutes and tenths | (h)(2)(i)(A) | "in minutes and tenths of minutes" |
| Display accuracy (documentation only) | (k)(6) | "±35 percent over the range of 6 mGy/min and 100 mGy" |

**Measurement point for the AKR limit (`FDA_POINT_FROM_DETECTOR_MM = 300`).** The limits apply at the point in § 1020.32(d)(3). AAPM TG-125 section III.C.2(iii) reproduces the rule: "In a C-arm type of fluoroscope, the AKR shall be measured at 30 cm from the input surface of the fluoroscopic imaging assembly". With the simulator's SID of 1060 mm and SOD of 760 mm, this point is the isocentre.

## AAPM Report 125 (TG-125, Rauch et al. 2012)

Opened at https://www.aapm.org/pubs/reports/RPT_125.pdf; the full text was extracted with `pdftotext`.

**Detector target (`FLUORO_NORMAL_NGY_AT_30CM`)**, section III.A:
- An image intensifier at 30 fps with a 30 cm FOV starts from a nominal 30 µR/s, "(263 nGy/s)".
- "flat-panel detectors will demand from 2 to 4 times the EERD required of a conventional image intensifier".
- "During single-frame or serial acquisition imaging ... the flat-panel detector can operate at a slightly lower EERD than that of an image intensifier."
- Used: 2 × 263 / 30 = 17.5 nGy per pulse at 30 cm. The lower end of the 2-4× range is a choice.

**Unit conversion.** TG-125 quotes SI equivalents: 20 µR/s = 175 nGy/s and 90 µR/s = 788 nGy/s, that is 8.75 nGy per µR. The same conversion is applied to the Report 70 cine values.

**Control priority (`solveNominal`)**, section VI step (8): "Preference is generally given to keeping the kVp and PW constant, if possible, and therefore it is the tube current that will change first, followed by a change in PW (up to an established limit), and finally a change in kVp. The amount of spectral filtration can also change under program control."

**Fluoroscopy trajectory (`FLUORO_PROGRAM`)**, section VII.C.1, one representative variable-filter system. The text, as a function of PMMA thickness:
- 0.9 mm Cu at 60 kVp, then 60 to 63 kVp.
- 0.6 mm Cu from 63 to 67 kVp, then 67 to 75 kVp, as PW rises "from 6 to 12.5 ms".
- 0.3 mm Cu from 75 to 81 kVp.
- 0.2 mm Cu from 81 to 91 kVp.
- 0.1 mm Cu from 91 to 120 kVp.
- "the kVp was never allowed to drop below 60 kVp".
- Tube current from 3 to 20 mArms at 15 pps (Table 1: "Default Pulse Rate (pps) 15").
- In code: stage break points 63/75/81/91 kVp; PW 6 ms in 0.9 mm Cu and 6-12.5 ms after. Pulse current is 33-107 mA, calculated as mArms / (PW × pps): 3/(0.006 × 15) ≈ 33 and 20/(0.0125 × 15) ≈ 107. The UI and code name no vendor.
- "for PMMA thicknesses from 0 to 11 in., the PW was allowed to vary only in the range from 6 to 12.5 ms". This is the acceptance range for fluoroscopy pulse width.

**Section VI.** The 30 pps example is "68 kVp, 10.5 mArms, and 7.5 ms PW". Acquisition starting factors are "usually being derived from the previous fluoroscopy segment". This is why the loop carries the equivalent thickness from fluoroscopy into cine.

**PAKR doubling thickness, comparison only (not a code constant).** Section VII.C.2(ii) gives "Td = 1.72 in. acrylic" (15 pps) and "Td = 1.64 in. acrylic" (30 pps). See the limitations in wave1/S6.md.

**Limit plateau, comparison only.** "above 12 in. of PMMA the PAKR remains constant due to the regulatory limits imposed by the FDA".

**Total filtration (SpekPy inherent filtration of 2.5 mm Al).** "the nominal 2.5 mm Al total filtration that is required by regulatory requirements".

## AAPM Report 70 (Cardiac Catheterization Equipment Performance, TG-17, 2001)

Opened at https://www.aapm.org/pubs/reports/RPT_70.pdf; text extracted with `pdftotext`.

| Use in code | Quoted value |
|---|---|
| `CINE_NGY_AT_23CM = 87.5` | "In the cine film mode, the measured IIIER ... should be, 10 to 20.0 µR/frame in the 23 cm (9 inch) FoV". The lower end is used (10 µR = 87.5 nGy), per TG-125's "slightly lower" for flat panels |

## Field of view (magnification)

**Nickoloff EL.** AAPM/RSNA physics tutorial for residents: physics of flat-panel fluoroscopy systems. RadioGraphics 2011;31:591-602, doi 10.1148/rg.312105185. Opened as PDF at the radcommons.org copy of the tutorial; text extracted.
- `FOV_DOSE_EXPONENT = 1`: "most vendors of FPD fluoroscopy systems increase the radiation to the image receptor at a rate of approximately 1/FOV".
- For image intensifiers, "dose rates into the image intensifier usually increase at a rate of 1/(FOV)2". Comparison only.

**Anthony G, Liang Y, Zhao X.** Performance evaluation of two interventional fluoroscope suites for cardiovascular imaging. J Appl Clin Med Phys 2022;23:e13741, doi 10.1002/acm2.13741 (PMC9588266). Full text via Europe PMC.
- "SNR and air kerma rates both increased with smaller FOVs". This is the direction the magnification test checks.
- kVp maximum of 125: "despite the Artis Q's higher maximum tube voltage (125 kVp)". The code uses this as a generic ceiling and names no vendor in the UI.
- One system "maintains a minimum kVp of 70 while increasing mAs up to approximately 6 in. of PMMA". Comparison only.

## Spectra (tube output, transmission, quanta, contrast weighting)

**SpekPy v2.** Poludniowski G, Omar A, Bujila R, Andreo P. Technical note: SpekPy v2.0, a software toolkit for modeling x-ray tube spectra. Med Phys 2021;48:3630-3637, doi 10.1002/mp.14945. The citation was confirmed via Europe PMC; the package is spekpy 2.5.4 from PyPI.

**Method.**
- Tungsten anode, 10° anode angle (model choice), 2.5 mm Al plus the added Cu.
- kVp from 50 to 125 in 5 kV steps; Cu of 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9 mm (TG-125's filter set).
- Water from 0 to 45 cm in 2.5 cm steps.
- Detector: 0.6 mm CsI, energy-integrating (model choice).
- Material coefficients come from SpekPy's NIST-based attenuation data, obtained by thin-filter ratios.

**Stored per node.**
- Air kerma per mAs at 1 m.
- ln(air-kerma transmission) as a quartic in t.
- ln(effective quanta per µGy), `(Σφη E)² / (Σφη E²) / K`, as a cubic.
- Signal-weighted mean mass attenuation of iodine, 316L steel, ICRP cortical bone and water, as cubics fitted over 5-45 cm.
- HVL.

**Maximum relative fit error** (kVp ≥ 60, over the fitted range):

| Quantity | Error |
|---|---|
| Transmission | 4.3% (worst at the lowest transmissions, below 1e-5) |
| Quanta | 0.9% |
| Iodine | 0.3% |
| Steel | 1.7% |
| Bone | 1.2% |
| Water | 0.5% |

**Spot values.**
- 75 kVp with 0.1 mm Cu: 32.75 µGy/mAs at 1 m, HVL 4.18 mm Al.
- Transmission through 20 cm water is 6.5e-3 (75 kVp, 0.1 mm Cu) and 1.1e-2 (70 kVp, 0.6 mm Cu).

**Script.** Run with `python gen_spectra.py spectra.json` in a venv with `pip install spekpy`. Generation took 46 s. The TS table at the end of `adrc.ts` was written from `spectra.json` for kVp of 55 and above.

```python
import json, sys, warnings, copy
import numpy as np
import spekpy as sp
warnings.filterwarnings("ignore")
KV = list(range(50, 130, 5)); CU = [0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9]
TS = np.arange(0, 45.01, 2.5); CSI_MM = 0.6
base = sp.Spek(kvp=130, th=10, dk=0.5); base.filter("Al", 2.5)
kE, phiE = base.get_spectrum(); mask = phiE > 0
def coef(matl, rho, tmm):
    s2 = copy.deepcopy(base); s2.filter(matl, tmm); _, p1 = s2.get_spectrum()
    mu = np.zeros_like(kE); mu[mask] = -np.log(p1[mask] / phiE[mask]) / (rho * tmm / 10.0); return mu
MU = {"I": coef("I", 4.93, 0.001), "steel": coef("Steel, Stainless (Type 316l)", 8.0, 0.002),
      "bone": coef("Bone, Cortical (ICRP)", 1.92, 0.02), "water": coef("Water, Liquid", 1.0, 0.05),
      "csi": coef("Cesium Iodide", 4.51, 0.002)}
eta = 1.0 - np.exp(-MU["csi"] * 4.51 * CSI_MM / 10.0)
out = {"kv": KV, "cu": CU, "nodes": []}
for kv in KV:
    row = []
    for cu in CU:
        s = sp.Spek(kvp=kv, th=10, dk=0.5); s.filter("Al", 2.5)
        if cu > 0: s.filter("Cu", cu)
        Y = s.get_kerma(); lnT, lnq, mI, mS, mB, mW = [], [], [], [], [], []
        for t in TS:
            st = copy.deepcopy(s)
            if t > 0: st.filter("Water, Liquid", t * 10.0)
            K = st.get_kerma(); k, phi = st.get_spectrum(); n = len(k)
            sig = phi * eta[:n] * k; sig2 = sig * k; S1, S2 = sig.sum(), sig2.sum()
            lnT.append(np.log(K / Y)); lnq.append(np.log(S1 * S1 / S2 / K))
            for arr, m in ((mI, "I"), (mS, "steel"), (mB, "bone"), (mW, "water")):
                arr.append((sig * MU[m][:n]).sum() / S1)
        t = TS; A = np.vstack([t, t**2, t**3, t**4]).T
        cT = np.linalg.lstsq(A, np.array(lnT), rcond=None)[0]
        sel = t >= 5.0; B = np.vstack([np.ones_like(t), t, t**2, t**3]).T[sel]
        fit = lambda v: np.linalg.lstsq(B, np.array(v)[sel], rcond=None)[0]
        row.append({"Y": Y, "T": list(cT), "q": list(fit(lnq)), "I": list(fit(mI)), "S": list(fit(mS)),
                    "B": list(fit(mB)), "W": list(fit(mW)), "hvl": s.get_hvl1()})
    out["nodes"].append(row)
json.dump(out, open(sys.argv[1], "w"))
```

## Table and mattress, skin dose

**Krajinović M, Kržanović N, Ciraj-Bjelac O.** Vendor-independent skin dose mapping application for interventional radiology and cardiology. J Appl Clin Med Phys 2021, doi 10.1002/acm2.13167 (PMC7882120). Full text via Europe PMC.
- `TAF_TABLE`: Table 4 (10 × 10 cm field, table plus mattress), rows 60-120 kV and columns 0-0.9 mm Cu. For example 60 kV gives 0.694, 0.745, 0.769, 0.783, 0.808, 0.825, and 120 kV gives 0.756, 0.794, 0.811, 0.823, 0.834, 0.839.
- Oblique factor: "F θ = e log TAF · (sec θ - 1)", that is TAF^(sec θ − 1), so the total is TAF^sec θ.
- The range of other tables, quoting DeLorenzo et al.: "TAF values at 0° for table plus pad ranged from 0.59 to 0.89".

**Jones AK, Pasciak AS.** Calculating the peak skin dose resulting from fluoroscopically guided interventions. Part I: Methods. J Appl Clin Med Phys 2011;12(4), doi 10.1120/jacmp.v12i4.3670 (PMC5718743). Full text via Europe PMC.
- Method: inverse square to the skin, then "ESAK = K a,table × t" (table correction), then "PSD = ESAK × BSF × f".
- `F_FLUORO` and `F_ACQ` are from Table 4, "Values of the f-factor for ICRU soft tissue as a function of kVp":

| kVp | Fluoroscopic mode | Digital acquisition mode |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | 1.061 | 1.056 |
| 65 | 1.063 | 1.058 |
| 70 | 1.065 | 1.059 |
| 75 | 1.066 | 1.061 |
| 85 | 1.068 | 1.063 |
| 95 | 1.069 | 1.066 |

- BSF appears only as a figure (Fig. 3), so `BACKSCATTER_FACTOR = 1.35` is a model parameter.

**Trigger levels (`DOSE_TRIGGERS`).** From docs/SIM_FACTS.md item 23: peak skin dose 3 Gy, Ka,r 5 Gy, KAP 500 Gy·cm² and fluoroscopy time 60 min. Sources there are Jaschke 2017 Table 2, PMC5489635, and Ahmed and Taha 2017. These were not reopened by this stream; the values are taken from SIM_FACTS as the plan specifies.

## Angulation, habitus and operator scatter

**Kuon E, Dahm JB, Empen K, Robinson DM, Reuter G, Wucherer M.** Identification of less-irradiating tube angulations in invasive cardiology. J Am Coll Cardiol 2004;44:1420-1428, doi 10.1016/j.jacc.2004.06.057. Abstract via Europe PMC.
- "The mean patient DAP/s and operator personal dose ... rose significantly: 3.7 and 10.6 times the PA 0° baseline values toward LAO 100° and 3.7 and 2.4 times toward RAO 100°".
- "Patient and operator values ... increased approximately 2.5 times toward 30° craniocaudal angulations".
- Used:
  - Fit targets for `PATH_MODEL.LAT_DENSITY` and `CC_EXTRA_CM`: a ratio of 3.7 at ±100° and 2.5 at ±30°. The fit gives LAT_DENSITY 0.767 and CC_EXTRA_CM 1.15 cm, and dose.test.ts checks it.
  - `OPERATOR_SIDE`, operator dose per unit DAP: 10.6/3.7 = 2.86 at LAO 100 and 2.4/3.7 = 0.65 at RAO 100.
- Not fitted, used as a check: "Caudal PA 0°/30° - angulation instead of caudal LAO 60°/20° ... enable 2.6-fold dose reductions to the patient". The model gives 1.97.

**Agarwal S et al.** Relationship of beam angulation and radiation exposure in the cardiac catheterization laboratory. JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2014;7:558-566, doi 10.1016/j.jcin.2013.12.203. Abstract via Europe PMC.
- Comparison only: "Median air kerma during DCs and PCIs was 677 and 2,188 mGy".
- Comparison only: "Fluoroscopy contributed to 66.3% of total dose during PCIs compared with 39.7% during DCs".

**Crowhurst JA et al.** Impact of ceiling suspended shield size on primary operator radiation dose during coronary angiography and intervention. J Med Radiat Sci 2026, doi 10.1002/jmrs.70042 (PMC13238651). Full text via Europe PMC.
- `SHIELD_TRANSMISSION.ceiling = 0.15`, from the midpoint of: "a Phantom study by Jia et al. demonstrated that the addition of the ceiling suspended shield reduced the exposure to the operator by 76.8%-93.5%". This is a secondary citation; Jia et al. was not opened.
- Its table skirt is "0.5 mm lead equivalence". The skirt transmission itself is a model parameter.

**Miller DL et al.** Occupational protection in interventional radiology: a joint guideline of CIRSE and SIR. Cardiovasc Intervent Radiol 2026, doi 10.1007/s00270-025-04195-4 (PMC13021820). Full text via Europe PMC.
- `SHIELD_TRANSMISSION.apron = 0.05`: "Typical 0.5 and 0.35 mm nominal thickness garments transmit 5% and 10% of fluoroscopic scatter".
- Comparison: "approximately 10 times more intense on the tube side of the patient than on the image receptor side".

## Grid

**Partridge J et al.** Radiation dose reduction without compromise of image quality in cardiac angiography and intervention with the use of a flat panel detector without an antiscatter grid. Heart 2006;92:507-510, doi 10.1136/hrt.2005.063909 (PMC1860862). Abstract via Europe PMC.
- Mean DAP with grid in and grid out: "26.2 (14.7) Gy.cm2 with the grid in to 16.1 (12) Gy.cm2 with the grid out" in diagnostic studies (−39%). In PTCA it fell "from 48.2 (36.2) to 37 (27.5)" (−23%).
- `GRID_SIGNAL_FACTOR` is a model parameter calibrated so that the steady-state reduction (27% at 20 cm) falls inside this range.

## Renderer calibration constants (not literature)

`LEGACY_AP_IODINE_GAIN` = (75/65)^2.2 = 1.370 and `LEGACY_AP_METAL_GAIN` = (75/65)^1.4 = 1.222 in `adrc.ts` reproduce the pre-ADRC sceneBuilder formula (kv = clamp(62 + (path - 220) * 0.12, 65, 105); iodineGain = (75/kv)^2.2; metalGain = (75/kv)^1.4) for the standard AP patient. They are calibration values of the old renderer, used only by `imageParamsFromAdrc(..., { calibration: "legacy-ap" })`, and carry no physical source.
