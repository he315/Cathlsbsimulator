# access-cx-forearm: model parameters

The forearm haematoma after radial access, graded and managed by its grade, and compartment syndrome escalated at once. The sourced rules are cited in `src/simulation/access/forearm.ts` from `src/content/manual/complication-management/06-access-complications.ts`: the grades and the chart's treatment by grade (the chart Brilakis reproduces from Bertrand, Fig. 29.10, lines 633-642: I local within about 5 cm, II up to about 10 cm, III below the elbow, IV above it, V compartment syndrome; analgesia, an extra band and ice for every grade, the cuff added for III and IV, the doctor informed from II and called at once for V), the chart's remarks (646: control the pressure, pain control helps it, consider stopping infusions, measure the forearm and arm circumference repeatedly, ice, a light compressive bandage after the band), Oxford's ward treatment (647: bandage, ice, sling), the cuff (623-627: Bertrand 20 mmHg below systolic let down every 15 minutes with the finger saturation watched; Oxford up to 15 mmHg below systolic; Brilakis's text above systolic for haemostasis at the puncture; never left up without a plan to let it down) and Oxford's compartment syndrome table (694-703: risk factors, signs, prevention once a haematoma is seen, management) with the tip access-cx-compartment-call (1934-1943). The EASY thresholds are closure.ts `easyGradeFromSize` (Garg 2019). The cangrelor regimen of forearm-grade-4 is pci-manual/03-antithrombotics.ts:1017. Accepting the two below-systolic cuff schemes (and not the above-systolic one) for a forearm haematoma is the package design (docs/curriculum/lab-build-plan.json, access-cx-forearm). Everything below is a MODEL PARAMETER: a simulator setting that gives the right direction, not a clinical value.
