# C4 facts: team, conscious patient and audio

Each fact below was read at the address given during this track (5 October 2026). Quotations are short and verbatim. Code comments cite the fact id (for example "fact C4-F3"). Every other number in `src/audio/**`, `src/simulation/team/**` and `src/ui/team/**` is a model parameter; they are listed at the end with where they live.

## Browser behaviour

**C4-F1. Audio needs a user gesture.** MDN, "Web Audio API best practices", https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices
- "If however, you create the context outside of a user gesture, its state will be set to `suspended` and it will need to be started after user interaction."
- Used in `AudioEngine`: no context exists until `unlock()`, which the lab calls from a click or key press; `unlock()` resumes a suspended context.

**C4-F5. Speech recognition may leave the device.** MDN, "SpeechRecognition", https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechRecognition
- "On some browsers, like Chrome, using Speech Recognition on a web page involves a server-based recognition engine. Your audio is sent to a web service for recognition processing, so it won't work offline."
- The page also lists `processLocally`, which "Specifies whether speech recognition must be performed locally on the user's device." It is not used yet (see the notes, remaining issues).
- Used in `speechInput.ts`: push-to-talk is off by default and the palette shows `SPEECH_DISCLOSURE` before it can be used.

**C4-F10. Local speech-synthesis voices.** MDN, "SpeechSynthesisVoice: localService property", https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechSynthesisVoice/localService
- The property is a boolean "indicating whether the voice is supplied by a local speech synthesizer service (`true`), or a remote speech synthesizer service (`false`.)"
- Used in `speech.ts`: only `localService === true` voices speak, as in the S11 attending's voice.

## Monitor sounds

**C4-F2. Pulse-oximeter pitch.** Source opened: "Are Anesthesiology Providers Good Guessers? Heart Rate and Oxygen Saturation Estimation in a Simulation Setting" (Riveros Perez, Jimenez, Albo et al.), Anesthesiology Research and Practice 2019, doi:10.1155/2019/5914305, full text read through Europe PMC (PMC6679872, https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6679872/fullTextXML).
- "changes in oxygen saturation values are perceived as variable pitch tones."
- Citing Loeb, Brecknell and Sanderson, "The sounds of desaturation", Anesth Analg 2016;122:1395-1403 (doi:10.1213/ane.0000000000001240; the primary paper was not opened): "Loeb et al. studied 21 pulse oximeters evidencing fundamental frequencies at 100% oxygen saturation ranging between 479 and 921 Hz, and between 38 and 404 Hz at 1% oxygen saturation".
- "different manufacturers use different algorithms to define pitch steps as saturation declines".
- Used in `monitorTone.ts`: the model tone is 880 Hz at 100% with a 1.84% frequency step per point (145 Hz at 1%), inside the measured range at both ends. The step size and the 880 Hz are model choices. A search snippet attributed a 1.84% step and 655-950 Hz to Paterson et al.; that paper could not be opened (HTTP 403), so it is not cited as the source.

**C4-F3. Alarm sounds by priority.** Source: "Capturing the attentional response to clinical auditory alarms: An ERP study on priority pulses" (Ribeiro Ferreira, Pereira, Vieira et al.), PLOS One 2023, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0281680, full text through Europe PMC (PMC9934403). The paper describes the high- and medium-priority pointers of IEC 60601-1-8:2020, which it tested.
- High priority: "The HP pointer burst is composed of two identical five-pulse blocks separated by 190 ms, totaling 1110 ms of duration." "HP pulses of 50 ms duration". "The HP pulse shows a rise time and fall time of 5 ms". Harmonics: "equally weighted components at 440 Hz, 880 Hz, 1320 Hz, 1760 Hz, and 2200 Hz".
- Medium priority: "The MP pointer burst has a total duration of 595 ms." "MP pulses of 155 ms duration". "rise time and fall time of 10 ms". Harmonics "equally weighted components at 220 Hz, 440 Hz, 660 Hz, 880 Hz, and 1100 Hz".
- The figure shows a "schematized rhythm pattern"; the text gives no pulse spacing inside a block and no medium pulse count. `ALARM_PATTERNS` spaces pulses evenly and uses three medium pulses (3 × 155 ms plus two 65 ms gaps = 595 ms). Both are model choices that reproduce the stated totals. The 2020 pointers are described as syncopated in the PUI Audio application note (https://puiaudio.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/IEC-60601-8-Application-Guide.pdf, Table 3: "Rhythm: Regular ... Syncopated"); the even spacing is therefore a simplification.
- Same paper: a recorded generic SpO2 beep had "a duration of 200 ms" and a fundamental "at 1450 Hz". The simulator's beep is shorter (120 ms, model) so beeps stay separate at high heart rates.
- Low priority and the inter-burst intervals are model parameters: the standard itself was not available.
- General constraints opened in the CUI Devices note (https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/7432/CE_IEC60601-1-8_WhiteNotes_092925.pdf): "Primary Frequency: 150 Hz to 1,000 Hz"; "Frequency Components: 4 frequency peaks between 150 Hz and 4,000 Hz". The 440 Hz and 220 Hz complexes satisfy both.

## X-ray signals

**C4-F4. Audible fluoroscopy signals.** 21 CFR 1020.32, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1020.32 (opened through WebFetch).
- (h)(2)(ii): "A signal audible to the fluoroscopist shall sound for each passage of 5 minutes of fluoroscopic irradiation time during an examination or procedure. The signal shall sound until manually reset or, if automatically reset, for at least 2 second."
- (d)(2)(iii)(C): "A continuous signal audible to the fluoroscopist shall indicate that the high-level control is being employed."
- Used in `FiveMinuteSignal` (sounds until reset; at least 2 s when reset automatically) and `ExposureTone` (an added continuous tone while high-level fluoroscopy is on). The five-minute event itself comes from S6 (`dose.fluoro-5min`, facts S6).
- The exposure tone while any pedal is down is a simulator feature asked for by the plan; no regulation requiring it was opened, and its pitch and level are model parameters.

## The patient

**C4-F6. Warmth with intra-arterial contrast.** Patient discomfort associated with the use of intra-arterial iodinated contrast media: a meta-analysis of comparative randomized controlled trials (McCullough, Capasso et al.). BMC Med Imaging 2011;11:12, doi:10.1186/1471-2342-11-12, full text through Europe PMC (PMC3125247).
- "Injection-related warmth was reported by more than 90% of patients, irrespective of the CM used."
- "More than a third of patients in controlled clinical trials have been known to report CM-injection-related discomfort, particularly local pain and an intense, unpleasant sensation of warmth".
- Used in `roles/patient.ts`: an LV or root injection of at least `warmthMinMl` (15 mL, model) is felt as warmth with probability `warmthP` 0.9. The trials pooled were mostly peripheral and other intra-arterial procedures; the volume threshold for a coronary lab and the absence of warmth with small selective coronary injections are model choices.

**C4-F8. Burning with intra-arterial verapamil.** Heme effects in lowering patient discomfort in radial artery verapamil injection (Livesay, Baljepally, Tahir et al.). Cardiol Res 2021, doi:10.14740/cr1313, full text through Europe PMC (PMC8510659).
- "The injection of verapamil via the radial artery is associated with a temporary 'burning' discomfort."
- Used in `roles/patient.ts`: the patient's words at the S9 `patient.stimulus` "cocktail" event. The intensity comes from the S9 engine, not from this source.

**Symptoms.** Everything else the patient reports (chest pain 0-10, nausea, dyspnoea, flushing, itch, anxiety, drowsiness, conscious level, vagal reactions, contrast reactions, adenosine symptoms) is read from the S9 engine (`vitals().symptoms` and its telemetry); their sources are in facts S9. The team adds only the words and the thresholds at which the patient speaks (model parameters below).

## Communication

**C4-F7. Closed-loop communication and complete verbal orders.** Completeness and Audibility of Verbal Orders for Medications and Blood Products during Trauma Resuscitation (Ryan, Williams, Aranda et al.). West J Emerg Med 2025, doi:10.5811/westjem.18585, full text through Europe PMC (PMC12698164).
- Key measures included "medication name/dose/route".
- "El-Shafy defined closed-loop as 'audible, directed to a team member, check-back by the team member, and acknowledgment by team leader.'"
- "the key to effective closed-loop communication is stating the medication or blood product that is being administered prior to administration."
- Used in `comms.ts` (a full closed loop is named, read back and confirmed; a complete drug order states drug, dose and route) and `roles/drugs.ts` (the nurse reads back before giving, and asks when the dose, unit or route is missing).

## Laboratory tests

**C4-F9. The ACT result is the clotting time.** Activated clotting time in inpatient diagnostic and interventional settings (Dalton, Martin, Garcia-Filion et al.). J Thromb Thrombolysis 2022, doi:10.1007/s11239-022-02672-y, full text through Europe PMC (PMC9618483).
- "As clotting begins to occur, the movement of the blood decreases below a pre-determined rate which indicates clot formation. The time in ACT seconds required to reach this endpoint is calculated and the results are displayed".
- Used in `roles/scrub.ts`: the scrub nurse reports the ACT when the sample has clotted, that is the measured ACT (S9 `measureAct()`) in seconds after the sample is loaded. The sampling delay (`actSampleS` 25 s) is a model parameter.
