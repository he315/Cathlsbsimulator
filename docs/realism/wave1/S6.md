### Revision after review (2026-10-05)

- **Meter fallback.** A meter reading whose `frame` does not match one of the controller's last 16 exposures (wrong counter, stale, or after a reset) no longer freezes the loop. The controller meters the analytic path instead, reports `meterSource: "analytic-fallback"` and counts `meterMisses`. `meter.frame` is now optional; without it the reading is taken to belong to the exposure `meterLatency` steps back. Step 2 and step 4 below say which counter to pass.
- **Grid and pulse width reach the renderer.** `imageParamsFromAdrc` now returns `gridIn` and `pulseWidthMs`, and the step 1 diff puts both in the ImageParams literal. XrayRenderer already reads `params.gridIn` (GRID_OUT_SPR_FACTOR) and carries `params.pulseWidthMs` for S2b.
- **AKR clamp between nodes.** Linear interpolation between two trajectory nodes on the limit could exceed it by up to 0.02% in the dense scan (88.018 against 88 mGy/min). `techniqueFor` now scales the tube loading down to exactly the limit in fluoroscopy (current first, then pulse width at minimum current) and tags the frame `akr`. A dense test (0-60 cm in 0.37 cm steps, every level, pulse rate and 4 fields) holds the limit to 1e-6.
- **Calibration shift.** Switching from the old formula changes the AP contrast the renderer was tuned against. `imageParamsFromAdrc(st, { calibration: "legacy-ap" })` keeps the old AP gains and applies only the ADRC's relative changes. See "Contrast calibration" under step 1.
- **Dose snapshot.** `DoseSnapshot` carries `tPatient`, so events emitted after a restore carry the restored patient time.
- **Snapshot of cine state.** `Adrc.snapshot()` no longer round-trips the last state through JSON, which turned the cine AKR limit (Infinity) into null.

## What it does

### ADRC (`adrc.ts`)

The ADRC is a closed loop.

- **Measurement.** Each frame, a metered detector signal (or the analytic patient path) gives an equivalent water thickness D. Two kinds of reading are accepted: the detector signal relative to target (`signalRel`), or the metered water-equivalent path (`waterCm`). The reading is interpreted with the technique of the exposure that produced it, named by `frame` (the AdrcState.frame of that exposure) or, when `frame` is omitted, the exposure `meterLatency` steps back. The default latency is 1 frame. An unmatched or stale reading falls back to the analytic path (`meterSource: "analytic-fallback"`), so the loop cannot freeze.
- **Lag.** A first-order lag (2.5 frames) moves D along a programme curve.
- **Programme curve.** The curve maps D to kVp, tube current, pulse width and Cu filter, following TG-125's priority order: current first, then pulse width, then kVp, with the filter stepping down.
- **Fluoroscopy programme.** It uses the TG-125 filter sequence: 0.9, 0.6, 0.3, 0.2 and 0.1 mm Cu, breaking at 63, 75, 81 and 91 kVp. kVp runs from 60 to 125, PW from 6 to 12.5 ms and pulse current from 33 to 107 mA.
- **Cine programme.** kVp is held near 65-80 (AAPM Report 70), PW runs from 3 to 8 ms, current is at most 800 mA and power at most 80 kW.
- **Modes.** Fluoroscopy has low, normal and high levels (×0.5, ×1, ×2 detector dose; Report 70). Cine is separate. Pulse rates are 7.5, 15 or 30 per second.
- **Detector targets.**
  - Fluoroscopy, normal level: 17.5 nGy per pulse at a 30 cm field (TG-125: 2× the image-intensifier guideline).
  - Cine: 87.5 nGy per frame at 23 cm (Report 70).
  - Both scale as 1/FOV (Nickoloff 2011).
- **Limits.**
  - The 21 CFR 1020.32(d) air kerma rate limit applies at the C-arm measurement point (30 cm in front of the detector, which is the isocentre at SID 1060): 88 mGy/min, or 176 mGy/min at the high level.
  - Cine is exempt under (d)(2)(iii)(B).
  - At the limit the programme trades current for kVp. When even 125 kVp cannot meet the target, the detector dose falls and the image gets noisier.
  - The tube, power and pulse-width limits do the same in cine (`limit: "tube"`).
- **Physics.** Tube output, water transmission, detected quanta and the signal-weighted attenuation of iodine, steel, bone and water come from SpekPy spectra, fitted per (kVp, Cu) node. They are stored as a 90-node table at the end of `adrc.ts`. Table and mattress attenuation uses the Krajinović 2021 table, with the oblique factor. The grid is either in or out (air gap).
- **Outputs for the renderer.**
  - `counts`: detected quanta at the meter, which fall when a limit binds.
  - `iodineGain`, `metalGain`, `boneGain`, `softGain`: relative to 75 kVp, 0.1 mm Cu and 20 cm water. This keeps the existing ImageParams meaning, "1 ≈ 75 kV".
  - `spr`, scaled by thickness, and the `grid` flag.
  - `pulseWidthMs`, for motion blur.
  - `noiseRel`.
- **Dose outputs.**
  - Ka,r per pulse and rate at the IRP, free in air.
  - The AKR at the regulatory point.
  - HVL.
- **Analytic path.** `patientPath(cArm, { habitus | width | depth | body })` gives the water-equivalent path for a body ellipse with lungs. Its two parameters were fitted to Kuon 2004: 3.7× from PA to LAO/RAO 100, and 2.5× at 30° cranial or caudal. It also reports whether the table is in the beam and the secant of incidence.

### Dose (`dose.ts`)

`DoseTracker` is snapshotable. Each exposure step adds:
- Ka,r, from the ADRC per-pulse kerma × pulses.
- KAP, as Ka,r × the collimated area at the IRP. Shutters are used, in the same convention as ImageParams.
- Fluoroscopy time and pulses; cine time, frames and runs. Fluoroscopy and cine are kept separate everywhere.
- A skin-dose map (72 × 54 cells, 5° × 10 mm) on the body ellipse. Each cell inside the collimated pyramid on the entrance side gets ESAK × BSF × f. ESAK uses the inverse square to that cell and table/mattress attenuation (with the oblique factor) when the ray crosses the table. The method is Jones and Pasciak 2011, and f comes from their Table 4.
- Relative operator scatter for the eyes, the legs and the trunk under the apron. It is KAP × the Kuon 2004 side factor × inverse square from the beam entrance to the operator point × shielding: ceiling shield 0.15 (Jia via Crowhurst 2026), table skirt 0.1 (model) and apron 0.05 (Miller 2026). Table height and femoral or radial position move the operator point.
- A live air kerma rate, KAP rate and operator rate over a 1 s window (21 CFR (k)(1)). The operator rate is relative to PA normal fluoroscopy of a standard patient, unshielded.

`drainEvents()` returns these telemetry events:

| Kind | Data |
|---|---|
| `dose.fluoro-5min` | `{ fluoroMin }` |
| `dose.trigger` | `{ metric: "kar" \| "kap" \| "fluoroTime" \| "peakSkin", value, threshold }`, once each, at SIM_FACTS item 23 levels: 5 Gy, 500 Gy·cm², 60 min, 3 Gy |
| `dose.cine-run` | `{ frames, durationS, karMGy, kapGyCm2, meanKvp, cArm }` |
| `dose.akr-limit` | `{ akrFdaMGyMin, limitMGyMin, mode }`, once per beam-on period |

### Display (`DosePanel.tsx`)

The panel uses Clinical Graphite and is presentational.

- **Regulated read-out.** Ka,r with its fluoroscopy and cine split, KAP, fluoroscopy time in minutes and tenths, and cine frames and runs.
- **Live block.** The current air kerma rate, the technique (kV, mA, ms, mm Cu) and the detector dose against its target.
- **Notices.** A warning appears when the AKR limit or the tube limit binds, saying that the image is noisier.
- **Follow-up bars.** Progress toward Ka,r, KAP, peak skin dose and fluoroscopy time.
- **Skin-dose map.** Shown on a canvas, with discrete legend swatches at 0, 1, 2 and 3 Gy.
- **Operator section.** Operator scatter with ceiling shield and table skirt toggles.
- **Optional controls.** Fluoroscopy dose level, fluoroscopy pulse rate, cine frame rate, and grid in or air gap.

Text is sentence case and at least 12 px. There are no gradients or glows, and no trace colours.

#### Contrast calibration (S5 and S2)

The grey-percentile and vessel-contrast calibration was tuned against the old formula. For the standard AP patient (20.0 cm water from `patientPath`) the old formula clamps at 65 kV and gives iodineGain (75/65)^2.2 = 1.370 and metalGain (75/65)^1.4 = 1.222 in both modes, with counts 380 (fluoroscopy) and 1500 (cine). The physical ADRC gives:

| AP, 20 cm water | kVp | iodineGain | metalGain | counts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Old formula, both modes | 65 | 1.370 | 1.222 | 380 / 1500 |
| ADRC fluoroscopy, normal, 15 pps | 66.9 | 0.982 (−28%) | 0.963 (−21%) | 419 |
| ADRC cine, 15 fps | 65.0 | 1.180 (−14%) | 1.178 (−4%) | 1516 |

The difference comes from the 0.2-0.3 mm Cu fluoroscopy filter and the 75 kVp, 0.1 mm Cu reference beam, which the old formula did not model. Two options:
1. **Keep the calibration (recommended for the first integration).** Use `imageParamsFromAdrc(exposure, { calibration: "legacy-ap" })`. It multiplies the iodine and metal gains by `LEGACY_AP_*_GAIN / standardApGains(mode)`, so AP keeps 1.370 and 1.222 in each mode while thickness, kVp, filter and the limit still change contrast as the ADRC says (for example 32 cm water gives iodine 0.855 in fluoroscopy and 0.901 in cine under "legacy-ap"). The counts change by +10% (fluoroscopy) and +1% (cine), which lowers noise SD by 5% and 0.5%; no renormalisation is applied to counts.
2. **Retune.** Switch to the physical gains (default) and re-run the grey-percentile and vessel-contrast scorecard (`docs/realism/research/`), then retune the shader's iodine scale. Fluoroscopy will then show lower iodine contrast than cine, which is what the extra copper does on real systems.

### 2. `src/render/xray/XrayRenderer.ts` and `detector.ts` (owner S2)

- **Meter.** Keep the detector in AEC-normalised mode (`uAec = 1`). `counts` is then the detected quanta at the meter, and it already drops when the ADRC is limited.
- **Which frame number.** `MeterReading.frame` is the renderer's private `frameNo`, which advances on every render, including pedal-up and last-image-hold frames. It is not the ADRC's frame and must not be passed as `meter.frame`. Use one of:
  - **Recommended:** pass the metered path without a frame, once per new reading: `meter: { waterCm: -Math.log(r.primaryTransmission) / (MU.soft * 10) }` (MU.soft = 0.02 /mm). The ADRC takes it as the exposure `meterLatency` back. A water-equivalent path is a property of the patient, not of the technique, so being one exposure off only adds a frame of latency.
  - **Exact:** S10 keeps a map from renderer frame to ADRC frame. After each pedal-down render, record `map.set(renderer.frameNo /* needs a public getter */, exposure.frame)`; when the reading arrives, pass `meter: { frame: map.get(r.frame), signalRel }` or `{ frame: map.get(r.frame), waterCm }`. A frame missing from the map (pedal-up frame) simply falls back to the analytic path.
  - Do not re-pass the same reading on every frame; pass `meter: null` (or omit it) when there is no new reading, and the ADRC meters the analytic path.
  - Watch `exposure.meterSource`: a run of `"analytic-fallback"` means the frame numbers are wrong.
- **Grid and pulse width.** Nothing to do in S2: sceneBuilder (step 1) now fills `params.gridIn` and `params.pulseWidthMs`, which XrayRenderer already consumes (grid-out SPR factor) and carries for the S2b motion-blur pass. Fluoroscopy PW is 6-12.5 ms; cine is 3-8 ms.

## Known limitations

- **PAKR doubling thickness.** The model's Td is 3.3 cm water between 15 and 30 cm. TG-125 quotes 1.64-1.72 inches of PMMA, about 5 cm water if 1 cm PMMA is taken as about 1.17 cm water (an approximation, not sourced). Dose therefore climbs a little faster with thickness than on the measured systems. The limit still binds near the right thickness: 31.5 cm water in normal fluoroscopy, against TG-125's "above 12 in. of PMMA", about 35 cm water. A fluoroscopy programme that raises kVp earlier would flatten the curve.
- **Unfitted Kuon check.** The model gives 1.97 for LAO 60 / caudal 20 against PA caudal 30, where Kuon reports 2.6. The analytic path has no shoulder girdle or spine.
- **Cine in very large, steep views.** The cine programme has no detector-target reduction. A habitus 1.3 patient at LAO 45/CRA 25 reaches 1.7 mGy per frame at 92 kV (33 cm water). Real systems may lower the reference instead (TG-125 VI mentions this), but no source gives a rule.
- **Fluoroscopy current cap at 30 pps.** The per-pulse current cap (107 mA) is the same at every pulse rate. TG-125's 20 mArms was measured at 15 pps; tube heat is not modelled.
- **Backscatter factor.** It is a constant 1.35, a model parameter: Jones and Pasciak plot BSF but give no table.
- **Model geometry and shielding.** The table skirt transmission (0.1), the operator points and the shape of the side factor between PA and ±100° are model parameters. Operator scatter is a relative index, not µSv.
- **Skin map body.** It uses the elliptic body cylinder: no arms, breasts or curvature along the long axis. Cells are scored at their centres.
- **Analytic patient path.** Until the renderer meter is wired, the ADRC meters the analytic water-equivalent path, not the rendered anatomy. The meter form of `step()` is ready and falls back to the analytic path on unmatched readings.
- **Contrast calibration.** The physical gains lower AP iodine contrast by 28% in fluoroscopy and 14% in cine against the old formula. "legacy-ap" keeps the old AP values until the scorecard is re-run (see step 1).
- **Body frame drift.** The default body frame (176 × 126 mm, centre z −46) copies S4's current `thorax.ts` bodyFrame. Integration should pass the actual BODY shape (`patientPath({ body })`, `bodyFromAnat`) so the two stay in step.
- **Display error.** The display carries no ±35% calibration error (21 CFR (k)(6) allows one). It could be added as a fixed per-session factor.
