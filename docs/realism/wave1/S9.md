# S9: patient engine, haemodynamics, 12-lead ECG and transducer

Stream S9 builds one seeded patient whose circulation, rhythm, pressures, 12-lead ECG, symptoms and drug responses react to what the operator does, all on the shared CardiacClock. A haemodynamic monitor shows it. Per the critique (A.4), S9 also builds the per-vessel coronary flow from the rendered TreeVessel tree.

Nothing is wired into the labs in this wave. The integration steps are listed below as exact code.

## How it works

### Patient

- **AngioCAD row.** One AngioCAD row is drawn as a whole, conditioned on indication (n = 376). It carries the segment grades, age, sex, LVEF, dominance and risk factors.
- **Continuous variables.** These come from named model distributions: height, weight, habitus, HR, BP, LVEDP, AF, QTc, creatinine, allergies, anticoagulation, anxiety, vagal tone, radial calibre, root size and ACT response.
- **Renal.** CrCl uses Cockcroft-Gault, eGFR uses 2021 CKD-EPI, and the Cigarroa MACD is computed from them.
- **ACS culprit.** For acute coronary syndromes, the most severe segment is the culprit. Only the culprit is thrombotic. Every other total occlusion, in any indication, is a chronic total occlusion with collaterals (23 ACS rows have more than one 100% segment). A thrombotic culprit at ≥ 99% has no flow, so the patient arrives with ischaemia, preloaded only in the regions downstream of the culprit, and ST elevation in that territory.

### Coronary flow

- **Resting flow.** Resting flow along the tree follows Huo-Kassab r^(7/3) taper. The leaks are normalised so that flow is conserved, and each ostium carries 1.0 mL/s × (r/1.5 mm)^(7/3).
- **Regions.** The myocardium is grouped into about 100 regions per tree. Each region has a demand, a territory, an RV flag and a Frank-axis direction from the heart centre, computed from the sample positions.
- **Lesions.** Each lesion is a `physiology.ts` CoronaryCase at its own diameter and flow, which gives its resting and maximal flow fractions.
- **Acute and chronic lesions.** Only a thrombotic lesion at ≥ 99% stops flow, and a thrombotic lesion gets no collaterals. A chronic subtotal or total occlusion is collateral-supplied (maximal flow 0.35 of normal). A chronic stenosis adds collateral supply rising from 0 at 70% to 0.35 at 95%. Serial lesions multiply their antegrade capacity, and the best collateral contribution is added once, distal to all of them. Chronic disease never leaves less than 1.3 times resting demand (model parameter), so a stable patient is not ischaemic at rest.
- **Supply and deficit.** Supply is min(need, CFR × lesion factor × dynamic factor × perfusion × steal), where:
  - need is the rate-pressure product ratio, against the resting heart rate and the resting systolic pressure measured after warm-up;
  - the dynamic factor covers occlusion, a wedged ostium, spasm and no-reflow;
  - steal = 1 − 0.5 × hyperaemia × collateral share (coronary steal under adenosine).

  The deficit drives ischaemia. `restDeficit` (the deficit at resting demand) decides whether it is transmural. Demand ischaemia with an intact resting supply stays subendocardial and gives ST depression.

### Engine

- **Each 0.4 ms step:**
  - clock step, then `circulation.step` with this patient's parameters, using the clock's beat time;
  - tip chamber, then transducer.
- **Every 4 ms:** a monitor sample.
- **Every 50 ms:** a slow tick that updates drugs, supply, ischaemia, rhythm and the circulation parameters. The circulation parameters are:
  - contractility from ischaemia, necrosis, stunning and drugs;
  - SVR from drugs, vagal tone, reaction and baroreflex;
  - venous tone, fluids and capillary leak.

  The slow tick also updates SpO2, NIBP, alarms and the renal thresholds.
- **ST vector.** It is Σ (mass × ischaemia × transmurality × region direction). Transmural ischaemia elevates ST in the leads facing the region and subendocardial ischaemia depresses it, so the lead patterns emerge from the anatomy. Transmurality comes from the supply failure at resting demand, not from the total deficit.
- **Resting reference.** After the 6 s warm-up, the engine takes the settled mean and systolic pressures as the baroreflex set point and the rate-pressure reference. The patient is therefore at rest on arrival, with or without calibration.
- **Rhythms:**
  - sinus with sinus arrhythmia;
  - AF (clock-irregular RR, f waves);
  - first-degree, 2:1 and complete block (dissociated P waves, escape rate 38);
  - VT; VF (coarse to fine);
  - asystole;
  - AIVR on reperfusion;
  - PVCs with a compensatory pause when the catheter touches the LV.
- **Hazards.** VF can follow an injection into the conus, a damped RCA or a wedged catheter, or prolonged ischaemia. Transient complete AV block follows adenosine with the sourced incidence.

### Transducer

- **Tip chamber.** The chamber fills through the residual gap (conductance 5 × gap²), plus side holes, and drains into the coronary bed. The bed resistance rises in systole, and intramyocardial back-pressure acts on it. This gives:
  - damping at a moderate gap;
  - ventricularisation at a small gap;
  - wedging at no gap;
  - extra diastolic run-off from the conus or an RV branch.
- **Transducer.** The response follows from the hydraulics: inertance and Poiseuille resistance of a 100 cm catheter plus 150 cm line, and compliance of the transducer plus any air. The chain also adds the ρgh hydrostatic offset, the drift offset until zeroed, and a 300 mmHg fast-flush square wave.

### Transducer chain

- Raising the transducer 10 cm lowers the reading by 7.40 mmHg (0.7395 mmHg/cm).
- An unzeroed transducer with an 11 mmHg drift reads 91 for a true 80, and reads 80 after `zero()`.
- In the engine, the displayed mean minus the true mean equals the drift.
- **Air:**
  - clean line: fn 24.3 Hz, ζ 0.25;
  - 0.005 mL air: 13.6 Hz, ζ 0.44;
  - 0.02 mL air: under 0.6 × fn, with higher ζ;
  - 0.05 mL air: 5.1 Hz, ζ 1.18 (overdamped).
- The fast-flush ringing frequency matches the damped natural frequency.

### Pharmacology

- **Heparin.** 100 U/kg gives an ACT of 290-350 s at 10 minutes, and 70 mg of protamine brings it below 160 s.
- **Adenosine IC (200 µg left):**
  - hyperaemia peaks above 0.8 at 3-25 s and is below 0.05 by 60 s;
  - first-degree AV block occurs in more than 90% of patients;
  - transient complete block occurs in 4-22% of 160 patients (sourced: 11.6%);
  - the block always clears.
- **GTN.** IC GTN reverses catheter-tip spasm (below 30% of its value within 30 s).
- **Pressors and vasodilators.** Metaraminol 1 mg raises MAP by more than 8 mmHg, and GTN 400 µg IV lowers it.
- **Vagal reaction.** It gives HR below 80% of baseline, MAP down more than 8 mmHg and nausea. Atropine 0.6 mg raises HR by more than 10.

### Arrest

- VF persists for 120 s with no countable HR and a MAP under 25 mmHg. A shock without a charge returns "not-charged".
- Shock success is above 0.85 at 30 s, and below 0.75 of that after 5 minutes without CPR. CPR slows the decline.
- Charge, clear and shock at 20 s converts, and MAP recovers above 50 mmHg. Reperfusion AIVR is allowed.
- A late shock leaves more stunning and a lower MAP.
- A shock without "clear" emits `safety.shock-without-clear`.

### Other checks

- **VF risk.** A conus injection gives far more VF than a normal one (8 mL in 120 paired patients).
- **Contrast reactions.** Over 40,000 draws, any reaction occurs in 0.82-1.24% (sourced 1.03%). With a previous reaction the rate is 6-8.5% (sourced 7.17%).
- **Contrast bradycardia.** 8 mL of RCA contrast slows the HR by more than 4 bpm, and it then recovers.
- **Snapshot.** Restoring a snapshot and advancing in a different split gives a byte-identical snapshot 12.7 s later. The engine uses an integer 0.1 µs time base.
- **Monitor.** `renderToString` of `HaemoMonitor` contains the monitor, ART, NIBP and the 12-lead key. It shows neither the rhythm diagnosis nor the tip class.
- **Visual check without a browser.** Traces were rendered offline to PNG by a scratch test (not committed): baseline, ventricularised, wedged, air bubble, fast flush, VF, AF, complete heart block, LV ectopy, and the LAD and RCA 12-leads. They show:
  - the incisura with transducer ringing;
  - the fast-flush square wave and its ringing;
  - an overdamped trace with air;
  - dissociated P waves in complete heart block;
  - a PVC with its compensatory pause and weak LV beat;
  - tombstone anterior ST elevation and inferior ST elevation with reciprocal aVL.

### 3. Contrast transport (S5)

Per-vessel flow comes from `engine.coronaryFlow(vessel.name)` (mL/s per sample, phasic, now), or from `coronaryFlowFor(tree, lesions)` with `vesselFlowProfile()` for an engine-free test.

S5's requested `coronaryFlow(territoryOrVesselId, beatPhase, patient)` maps to `engine.coronaryFlow(name, true)`. A pure version is `vesselFlowProfile(fm, name, supplies.map(s => s.q))` multiplied by `phasicFlow(beatPhase, side)`.

Delivered contrast is reported back with `engine.contrast(ml, side)`.

### 4. Device physics (S7)

Each physics tick, call `engine.setTip({ location, side, gap, runoff, conus, sideHoles, wallContact, lvContact, valveOpen })`. Here `gap` is the open fraction of the ostial lumen around the catheter (gap area / ostial area); the model's conductance scales with gap².

Until S7 lands, use `engine.setTip(tipFromEngagement(engagementState))`.

### 5. PCI devices (S14)

- During balloon inflation: `engine.setOcclusion("balloon", { vessel: lesion.vessel, param, fraction: 1 })`; on deflation, `clearOcclusion`.
- No-reflow: `setNoReflow`.
- Perforation: `setPericardialInflow`.
- Pressure wire: `coronaryCaseFor(tree, engine.flow, "LAD", lesions)` with `physiology.reading()`/`indices()`.
- Adenosine through `engine.give` or `startInfusion`.
- After stenting, rebuild with `engine.setCoronaryTree(tree, newLesions)`.

## Monitor colours

The alarm banner uses the `.notice-danger`, `.notice-warn` and `.notice-info` classes. The canvas reads its trace and background colours once at mount from hidden swatches with the `.monitor`, `text-ecg`, `text-art`, `text-spo2` and `text-ink-faint` classes, so the traces follow `tailwind.config.js`. Literal rgb values remain only as the fallback for server rendering. The 12-lead paper keeps its own cream and red colours because it depicts printed ECG paper.

## Known limitations

- **Circulation model.** The lumped model has no atria and no coronary compartment in the circulation, so coronary flow does not feed back into aortic pressure.
  - Tamponade is modelled as pressure on both ventricles only, so RA/RV diastolic collapse timing is not represented.
  - The incisura needs the optional inertial valve; legacy labs keep the old diode.
- **Ischaemia geometry.** Regions use epicardial sample positions, so septal ischaemia points from the centre to the septum, which only approximates the septal ST vector. Posterior MI shows only as ST depression in V1-V2 (there are no posterior leads).
- **Reperfusion arrhythmias.** Only AIVR; there is no reperfusion VF beyond the ischaemic hazard.
- **Model parameters.** Many magnitudes are model parameters, named in code and listed in the facts file:
  - the VF hazards per mL;
  - the vagal reaction;
  - most drug Emax/ED50 values;
  - contrast bradycardia;
  - the ischaemia time constants;
  - the BP and HR distributions.

  These should be calibrated against case series where sources can be opened.
- **Not sourced.** The baseline ACT (120 s) and the atropine response are not sourced.
- **Contrast reactions.** Incidences are from CT cohorts.
- **Statistics.** The STEMI V2-V3 age cut (< 40 y) is a model parameter. Shock success uses a survival-decline figure as a proxy.
- **Clock control.** `setRhythm` and `setHeartRate` act at the next R wave. Premature beats are fired through `CardiacClock.snapshot()`/`restore()` (public API) rather than a dedicated clock method. A `triggerBeat()` in `cardiacClock.ts` (S1) would be cleaner.
- **Default network.** Without a tree, a fixed default network is used (model regions and directions). Lesions from the profile are placed at fallback positions.
- **Engine and tree.** The engine is built per tree. `setCoronaryTree` resets ischaemia history when the region count changes. A snapshot names its tree by a fingerprint of vessel names, sample counts and reference radii; the engine remembers the last 16 trees it used, and a different engine needs `restore(snap, { tree })`. Two trees with the same reference radii (before and after stenting) share a fingerprint, which is harmless because the flow network depends only on reference radii, positions and lesions.
- **Collaterals.** Collateral recruitment, the 1.3 times resting reserve and the steal fraction are model parameters chosen so that stable patients are quiet at rest. Real collateral flow varies widely between patients; there is no per-patient collateral grade yet.
- **Not verified in a browser.** The monitor was not checked in a browser this stream (not allowed). The offline PNG renders and the SSR test cover drawing logic and markup only. The canvas sweep, ResizeObserver and the 12-lead panel need a visual check after integration.
